At the end of this surah one should say **آمين** (Amin). It means 'Accept this prayer'. More details come below. Those who hold that the 'Bismi' is an ayah of Suratul Fatihah count verses 6 and 7 together as the 7th verse. There is no disagreement that it has 7 verses in all.

## Commentary

### The name Allah, and the Bismi (verse 1)

There is more than one opinion about the root of the word **الله** (Allah). Whatever it may be, it is a proper name (**علم**) that denotes only that one Supreme Power who is the Creator, Controller, Sustainer, Protector, Master and Owner of all the worlds. All the names that have come as His holy names, in the Quran, in other scriptures or in the sayings of prophets, are attributes setting out His various glorious qualities. The name 'Allah' has not been used for anyone other than Him, nor may it be used. **اله** (Ilah) is a word used throughout in the sense of a thing worshipped, a deity. Noting that these two words correspond broadly in letters and in meaning, many state that the word **الله** was formed by adding the particle **ال** (al) to the word **اله** to make it specific. Accordingly, the meaning of that proper name would be 'the True God', that is, 'the one who truly deserves to be worshipped'. *\[p. 154\]* **الله أعلم**.

We mentioned some things above about beginning all good deeds, the recitation of the Quran included, with the Bismi, and about whether the Bismis at the beginnings of the surahs are ayahs of those surahs. Here there is room for a doubt. The Holy Quran is the word of Allah; it is He who revealed it. That being so, what is the wisdom behind His beginning it in His own name? That is the doubt. The secret behind it lies in teaching how we should begin tasks such as reciting the Quran, in pointing to truths like those indicated above, and in showing the practical form of a good beginning. The purpose of revealing the Quran is, after all, to teach man everything he needs to do. When a good book begins, there is no need to say that its beginning must be good. That being so, the beginning of the Quran, which deserves the highest place in every respect above all books, should surely be better than all others. What beginning is there more excellent than beginning in the name of Allah?!

Two attributive names are enough to make us understand the importance of beginning in the name of Allah and the wisdom in it: 'Ar-Rahman' and 'Ar-Rahim' (**الرحمن، الرحيم**). 'Ar-Rahman' is taken to mean the Most Compassionate, the author of every kind of favour, small or great, manifest or hidden, particular or general, of this world or the next; and 'Ar-Rahim' the Ever-Merciful, the giver of the great favours that those who do good are specially to attain.

Just as He is the supreme centre of compassion and grace, Allah is also the supreme centre of authority and justice. He says: **نَبِّئْ عِبَادِي أَنِّي أَنَا الْغَفُورُ الرَّحِيمُ ﴿٤٩﴾ وَأَنَّ عَذَابِي هُوَ الْعَذَابُ الْأَلِيمُ** الحجر (Inform My servants that I am indeed the Most Forgiving, the Ever-Merciful, and that My punishment is indeed the painful punishment – Hijr 49, 50). But His compassion overflows His wrath. That is why even the wretches sunk in disbelief and lawlessness constantly enjoy His countless favours. In Surah Al-A'raf Allah says: **وَرَحْمَتِي وَسِعَتْ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ** (My compassion is vast enough for all things). Continuing the same sentence, He also says: **فَسَأَكْتُبُهَا لِلَّذِينَ يَتَّقُونَ وَيُؤْتُونَ الزَّكَاةَ وَالَّذِينَ هُم بِآيَاتِنَا يُؤْمِنُونَ** (I shall ordain it for those who are God-conscious and give Zakat, and for those who believe in Our signs *\[p. 155\]* – Al-A'raf 156). It may be said that 'Ar-Rahman' is the word that holds the sense of the first of these two sentences, and 'Ar-Rahim' the word that holds the sense of the second.

When someone begins a task in His name, describing Allah by these two attributes, it is seen to contain both a remembrance of the favours he has received from Him so far and a request for the favours he hopes to receive from Him hereafter. The gist of a saying of the Prophet narrated by Imam Bukhari and Muslim (r) is this: 'When Allah created the creatures, He recorded this in a book and placed it with Him above His Arsh (Throne): "Surely, My compassion has overtaken My wrath."' The gist of what the Prophet ﷺ said in another Hadith narrated by both of them is this: 'If Allah's compassion were divided into a hundred parts, only one part of it has He sent down in this world among all living beings, jinn, humans and animals alike. It is because of this that they show kindness and mercy to one another. It is because of this, too, that wild beasts show tenderness to their young. The remaining ninety-nine parts He has kept in store to show mercy to His servants on the Day of Resurrection.' Ah! How vast is Allah's compassion!

### All praise is for Allah (verse 2)

The first verse is **الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ** (All praise is for Allah, the Lord of all the worlds). Whatever category of praise one examines, and for whatever thing the praise may be, in reality the one who causes that praise, and the source from which that thing springs, will be found to be Allah. For example, suppose we praise a person for his generosity. Who but Allah is the doer of the many things connected with it: creating him, making him generous, giving him the means to be generous, making it of use to others, and accepting it as his good deed?! So, in reality, He alone is worthy of all hymns of praise.

What is meant by **حمد** (praise) is to laud a person on account of the good deeds he does by his own intention. When the particle **ال** (al) is added to it, everything in the category of praise comes to be included in it. That is why meanings such as 'all praise, the whole of praise' are given to the word **الحمد**.(\*) *\[p. 156\]* Lauding good things that come about without one's own intention is not called **حمد** (praise); the word used for that is **مدح** (commendation). This word, however, is used for all good things, intended or not. For example, for lauding qualities like knowledge, helping others, God-fearing devotion and dutifulness, one can say both **حمد** (praise) and **مدح** (commendation). But for lauding things like beauty, a sweet voice or bodily form, one says only **مدح** (commendation), never **حمد** (praise). Lauding a person on account of some benefit received from him is called **شكر** (thanks). Thanks can be expressed not only by word of mouth but also by heart and through action. From the above one can see that these three words sometimes coincide and sometimes diverge. It is by taking the points where they coincide into account that some are seen using these three terms as near-synonyms. Because this usage sometimes causes confusion of meaning, we have explained these words at this length.

*(\*) The particle **ال** in Arabic has the place of the particle 'The' in English. Malayalam has no words standing in its place. When **ال** is joined to a common noun (**نكرة**), its generality goes and it takes on a specific meaning. For example, **قلم** means any pen, while **القلم** means a particular pen, or the class of pens. Further details should be learnt from books of linguistics and literature.*

The excellent attributes of His that suffice to make us understand the truth that all praise belongs to Allah alone are those stated next: **رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ** (Lord of the worlds), **الرَّحْمَٰنِ** (the Most Compassionate), **الرَّحِيمِ** (the Ever-Merciful) and **مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ** (Owner of the Day of Recompense). If one reflects on the breadth of meaning contained in each of these attributes, one can understand why it was said that all praise is for Allah.

### Lord of the worlds

The word **رَبّ** (Rabb) takes meanings such as 'protector, sustainer, one who raises (nurtures), master', and also 'owner, one who manages (administers)'. Where Allah is concerned, He is the Rabb in all these senses. **عالمين** (Alamin) is the plural of **عالم** (Alam), meaning 'world'. Although this plural form is generally used for rational beings, what is meant by رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ is 'the Rabb of all the worlds', without distinction between rational beings, other living beings and lifeless things. \[p. 157\] When Prophet Musa (a) and Prophet Harun (a) went to Pharaoh and said إِنَّا رَسُولُ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ ('We are messengers of the Rabbul Alamin'), Pharaoh asked: وَمَا رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ (And what is 'Rabbul Alamin'?). The reply Musa (a) gave was: رَبُّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا (the Rabb of the heavens and the earth and of whatever is between them). (Shu'ara 16, 23, 24)

### The Most Compassionate, the Ever-Merciful (verse 3)

**الرَّحْمَٰنِ، الرَّحِيمِ** (the Most Compassionate, the Ever-Merciful): these we have already explained in the commentary on the Bismi. Even if one accepts the view that the 'Bismi' is an ayah of this surah, repeating these two holy names here is not for a merely ornamental purpose. In the Bismi, those attributes pointed to the truths contained in beginning the recitation of the Quran in the name of Allah. Here, among the qualities that show the background of why all hymns of praise are due to Allah, the place of His compassion is also being brought out.

Although both forms, 'Rahman' and 'Rahim', express Allah's boundless compassion, it is the form 'Rahman' that signifies more the immense vastness of compassion. 'Rahman' is not used of anyone other than Allah. After the proper name 'Allah', of all the other names used as His attributes, 'Rahman' is the one seen given the most prominence in the Quran. Like the name 'Allah', the name 'Ar-Rahman' is also seen used for Allah without any other qualifier. (See Isra 110, Surah Rahman 1, Furqan 59 and others, with their commentary.) That is why scholars state that 'Rahman' must not be used for anyone other than Allah, whether as a name or as an attribute. History does not record anyone being called 'Rahman' except Musailimatul Kadhdhab (Musailimah the false prophet), who posed as a prophet in Yamamah in the time of the Prophet ﷺ and was killed in battle during the caliphate of Abu Bakr (r). Even that was with the title 'Rahman of Yamamah' (**رحمن اليمامة**).(\*)

*(\*) Usage such as 'A. Rahman' in place of 'Abdur Rahman' is not good. Care must be taken not to use, as names of created beings, any attributive names that befit Allah alone. All of this amounts to disrespect toward Allah.*

### Owner of the Day of Recompense (verse 4)

The next attribute mentioned is **مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ** *\[p. 158\]* (Owner of the Day of Recompense). It is the word **دين** (Din) that has been given the meaning 'recompense' here. Besides this, it takes many meanings according to the context, such as 'religion, procedure, custom, inquiry (trial), legal proceedings, obedience, habit'. What is meant by the Day of Recompense is that great day, the Day of Resurrection, on which an inquiry is held into all the deeds of creatures, judgement is passed, and each one is given due recompense. In place of **مَالِكِ** (Maaliki) there is also the reading **مَلِكِ** (Maliki).(\*) The meaning of the phrase would then be 'King of the Day of Recompense'. Either way, the import is that the supreme sovereign and sole ruler of that day will be Allah alone. In Suratul Infitar, Allah asks, 'Do you know what the Day of Recompense is?', and says: **يَوْمَ لَا تَمْلِكُ نَفْسٌ لِّنَفْسٍ شَيْئًا ۖ وَالْأَمْرُ يَوْمَئِذٍ لِّلَّهِ** – الانفطار ١٩ (Gist: A day on which no soul can do anything at all for another soul! On that day the command, that is, the authority, will be Allah's.)

*(\*) See what is explained in the Preface about differences of reading like this, found in some places in the Quran.*

If one gives attention to the depth and breadth of meaning conveyed by each of these four attributes, set out in just three short sentences, it becomes fully clear that there is none other than Allah truly worthy of all hymns of praise. When a servant sings Allah's praise remembering these four excellent qualities of His, a sense that Allah is present before him and near him arises in that servant's mind. And so, turning to face Allah, he says: **إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ** (You alone we worship (I and all believers like me), and of You alone we seek help). Yes, the import is: 'We are convinced that there is none but You possessed of the supremely excellent qualities mentioned above; therefore we firmly declare here that we offer our acts of worship and our pleas for help before You alone.'

### You alone we worship, and of You alone we seek help (verse 5)

We have used the word 'ആരാധന' (worship) as the rendering of **عبادة** (Ibadah), the root of the verb **نَعْبُدُ**, and 'സഹായം തേടുക' (seeking help) as the rendering of **استعانة** (Isti'anah), the root of the verb **نَسْتَعِينُ**. The most fundamental and principal subject in the teaching of all the prophets is Tawhid (the doctrine of One God). The very heart of Tawhid is Tawhid in worship and in seeking help. Yet among later Muslims *\[p. 159\]* it is also the subject in which a great deal of misunderstanding and deviation has come about. So many superstitions, ideologies and rituals that shake the very foundation of Tawhid have crept into the community. They have taken root in the community in a way that makes a complete purification impossible. **معاذ الله** (Refuge in Allah!)

If one consults dictionaries, many meanings are found for the word 'Ibadah' (**عبادة**): meanings such as 'obedience, act of virtue, submission, offering devotion, ritual observance, showing humility', and also 'veneration, worship, adoration (puja), service, pleasing'.(\*) In all of these meanings the element of humility and lowliness is found embedded. But Malayalam has no single word that brings out exactly its purport in the technical sense of the Shar' (religion). As the most suitable of those available, 'ആരാധന' (worship) is widely given as its rendering. The sense accepted for it in religion is 'showing the utmost degree of humility and devout reverence' (**اقصى غاية التذلل والخشوع**). Though slight differences of wording may be seen, that is the definition the commentators of the Quran and the eminent scholars of Islam have given for 'Ibadah' from early times.

*(\*) In English: "Worship, adoration, piety, servitude, godliness, submission" and the like. In Arabic: **الطاعة، الخضوع، الخشوع، التذلل، التنسك، ونحوها**.*

About its linguistic and religious meanings, Imam Ibn Kathir (r) has said this in his famous work of Tafsir: **والعبادة في اللغة من الذلة يقال طريق معبد وبعير معبد أي مذلل وفي الشرع عبارة عما يجمع كمال المحبة والخضوع والخوف**. Gist: In language, 'Ibadah' comes from lowliness, that is, humility. One speaks of a 'mu'abbad' road and a 'mu'abbad' camel, meaning 'mudhallal' (made tame and docile through much use). In the Shar', it is the term for that which brings together (unites) the perfection of love, devotion and fear. What Ibn Kathir (r) points out is that Ibadah takes shape not only from humility and lowliness but also from love and from fear. The implication is that only then does the humility and devout reverence reach the utmost degree. Those who worship other than Allah, too, come to feel humility and devotion toward the objects of their worship out of excessive love and fear of them. It is when one believes that some invisible power, beyond ordinary relations of cause and effect, resides in a thing that \[p. 160\] the love and fear regarding it become excessive. Put another way, worship, which is Ibadah, is the expression of the humility and devout reverence that arise from this belief. As Sayyid Rashid Rida and others have pointed out, the humility one person shows another out of affection or love, and the humility one person shows another out of fear of authority, power or oppression, or in hope of some benefit, however great it may be, is never called 'Ibadah'. The reason is that none of these arise from belief in an invisible power.

When the origin of Ibadah is the union of love and fearful devotion, it is clear that the expressions that arise according to the will and instruction of the person (or thing) worshipped, or with its knowledge and approval, are not the only ones that count as Ibadah. More than that, Ibadah can sometimes arise even against the counsel and instructions of the one worshipped. Many offer Ibadah to great souls who have died; those great souls neither know of it nor are pleased by it. Christians offer Ibadah to Prophet Isa (a). Not only does he not like it, it is utterly opposed to the Tawhid he preached. As for idols, they lack even the capacity to perceive their worshippers or their worship. In short, Ibadah crystallises out of the union of utmost love, reverence, fear, devotion, humility, meekness, belief in an invisible power, and the like.

Allah says: **وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَن يَتَّخِذُ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ أَندَادًا يُحِبُّونَهُمْ كَحُبِّ اللَّهِ ۖ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَشَدُّ حُبًّا لِّلَّهِ** (Among people there are some who take equals besides Allah. They love them as one loves Allah. But those who believe are strongest in their love for Allah. – Al-Baqarah 165) Again He says: **قُلْ إِن كُنتُمْ تُحِبُّونَ اللَّهَ فَاتَّبِعُونِي يُحْبِبْكُمُ اللَّهُ وَيَغْفِرْ لَكُمْ ذُنُوبَكُمْ ۗ وَاللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ** (Say: If you love Allah, follow me. Then Allah will love you and forgive you your sins – Al Imran 31). Elsewhere, concerning the polytheists, He says: **وَيُخَوِّفُونَكَ بِالَّذِينَ مِن دُونِهِ** (They frighten you with those besides Allah – Zumar 36). Addressing the believers He says: **وَخَافُونِ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ** (Fear Me, if you are believers – Al Imran 175).

Until quite recently, the explanations of meaning given above were what was generally accepted for 'Ibadah' among Muslims and in Islamic scholarly literature. Contrary to them, it is generally known that some groups now give it meanings such as 'obedience', *\[p. 161\]* and put forth many new arguments of their own on a grand scale. We do not undertake a discussion here about that meaning and its flaws; if we did, it would run very long. Many scholars and authors have already refuted and criticised it. For seekers of truth, what has been explained above will be enough to grasp the matter, **ان شاء الله**.

When giving the meaning of **عبادة** (Ibadah), some scholars are seen to give **طاعة** (Ta'ah) for it in Arabic. This word does have the meaning 'obedience'. This is one main piece of evidence that these people cite for their view. In reality, those scholars do not use it in the sense of following commands. They use it in the sense of a meritorious act, that is, a good deed done voluntarily. When the sense is 'following a command' (or following a law), its verb form is **اطاع** (Ata'a).(\*) When the meaning is doing a virtuous act, doing a good thing voluntarily, the verb customarily used is **تطوع** (Tatawwa'a). Imam Raghib (r) has specifically pointed out that its well-known meaning is doing a non-obligatory virtuous act, such as an optional (sunnah) prayer. Fairuzabadi (r), author of the famous dictionary Al-Qamus, says the same. As can be seen in 2:15\[8\] and 184, when one says **تطوع خيرا** it means 'voluntarily did a good thing that was not obligatory', not 'followed a good obedience'. Nobody says it that way, either. *\[TN: the print reads "2:15"; the verse meant is Al-Baqarah 158.\]*

*(\*) **قال فى المنجد: فيقال امره فاطاع بالالف لا غير وقال اطاعه اطاعة وطاعة انقادله** \[TN: Al-Munjid says: one says "he commanded him, and he obeyed (ata'a)", with the alif and no other way; and "ata'ahu, ita'atan wa ta'atan" means he submitted to him.\]*

A valuable dictionary prepared specially for the meanings of the words of the Quran, and accepted by all, is Imam Raghib (r)'s 'Al-Mufradat' (**المفردات**). In it he says: **العبودية اظهار التذلل والعبادة ابلغ لانها غاية التذلل ولا يستحقها الا من له غاية الافضال وهو الله تعالى ولهذا قال لا تعبدوا الا اياه** ('Ubudiyyah (servitude) is showing humility. 'Ibadah' is more intense than that, because it is showing the utmost humility. None deserves it except one capable of bestowing the utmost favour, and that is Allah. That is why He has said that none other than Him should be worshipped.) From this the difference between Ibadah and 'Ubudiyyah' (**العبادة والعبودية**) becomes clear. That is, Ibadah is narrower than Ubudiyyah and stronger than Ubudiyyah (**اخص منها وابلغ**). Moreover, the root form 'Ibadah' comes only for the verb **عَبَدَ** ('abada). 'Ubudiyyah', on the other hand, comes as the root form of two verb forms, **عَبُدَ** and **عَبَدَ** (with the vowels 'a' and 'u' on the middle letter). *\[p. 162\]* When it is the root of **عَبَدَ** (**بالفتح**, with fathah), it too has the meaning of 'Ibadah', and it is then in transitive form.

When it is the root of **عَبُدَ** (**بالضم**, with dammah), the meaning will be slavery. The verb is then intransitive (**لازم**). In short, the verb ''abada' and the words derived from it are used only in the sense of worshipping. The verb ''abuda' and the words derived from it are used only in the sense of slavery, that is, of being a slave. 'Ubudiyyah', because it comes as the root of both verb forms, takes both meanings, 'worship' and 'slavery'. It is through not understanding these differences objectively, or not heeding them, that meanings such as 'slavery', 'slave labour' and 'servitude' are given when the linguistic meanings of the word **عبادة** are stated. In reality, it is **عبودية**, not **عبادة**, that has such a meaning, even in language.

It is clear from the four attributes stated above that Allah alone is the one Supreme Power worthy of the utmost love, reverence and fearful devotion. Following on from that, this (4th) verse contains the admission that You alone are truly worthy of worship and of pleas for help, and the pledge that we will offer neither of them before anyone else. *\[TN: the author counts this as the 4th verse here, numbering without the Bismi; in the table above it is verse 5.\]* Every believer has to repeat this many times over, in prayer and outside it. In this chapter, which begins as a hymn of praise to Allah and ends as a great prayer to Him (yes, it is also called 'Suratul Hamd' and 'Suratud Du'a'), consider how weighty is this pledge that His servant offers, facing Him, between those two! It is in view of this that this surah has also been called **سورة المناجاة**, the chapter of intimate conversation, that is, of private audience.

Now, about the sentence **وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ** (and of You alone we seek help) there are some things to understand. It is not made clear here in which matters the help is being sought. The reasonable course is to take it that one asks for help in every matter in which help would be needed in that situation. This is a general principle accepted in the science of rhetoric (**علم البلاغة**), and it is the view of most commentators of the Quran too. Since worship was spoken of in the sentence just before it, one can certainly say that a plea for help to carry out acts of worship properly is *\[p. 163\]* intended in it. After all, the foremost of man's duties is to worship Allah alone, and the ability, good fortune and circumstances to fulfil it properly must all come from Allah Himself. But there is no need for that reason to restrict it, as some commentators are seen to do, by taking it as a plea for help for that alone.

Without any distinction such as worldly or otherworldly, religious or secular, Allah's help is indispensable in all matters. Even things ordinarily regarded as within one's own ability, or within human ability, are no exception. An example will make this clear. For someone who can read and write, writing a letter at first glance needs only a pen, a little ink and a scrap of paper. But think a little. He must be able to take that pen in hand, hold it and write; the intended matters must come to mind; the words for them must come to memory; he must record them without error: many, many such things. He must also be kept safe from things that might obstruct any of these. A tremor of the hand, or a pain, or some accident or other: think how many, many unexpected things he must be kept safe from! In short, for any trivial matter, all the circumstances and surroundings it needs must come from Allah Himself. So it can be understood overall that what is meant here is the help that cannot be had from creatures and comes only from Allah, unseen. That is why it says 'of You alone we seek help'. Since unseen power belongs to Allah alone, asking it of anyone else is not only futile; it is downright shirk as well. That is why Allah has said: do not call upon anyone else in prayer along with Allah (**فَلَا تَدْعُوا مَعَ اللَّهِ أَحَدًا**).

With this truth in mind, consider that sentence once more. It then becomes clear why it is fitting to say that it is a plea for help in all things desirable and good. A Hadith narrated from Ibn Abbas (r) deserves mention here. He says: One day, when I was riding behind the Prophet ﷺ, he said to me: 'O boy, I will teach you some words. Guard Allah (His commands), and He will guard you. Guard Allah, and you will find Him before you. When you ask (for anything), ask Allah. When you seek help (in anything), seek help from Allah. Know this: even if the whole community gathered together *\[p. 164\]* to do you some benefit, they could do you no benefit except what Allah has decreed for you. And even if they all gathered together to do you some harm, they could not harm you except with what Allah has decreed against you......' (Tirmidhi)

From what has been said, it should not be supposed that one need not, or must not, ask creatures for help in any matter. Allah says: **وَتَعَاوَنُوا عَلَى الْبِرِّ وَالتَّقْوَىٰ** (Help one another in what is virtuous and in God-fearing piety, that is, in being conscientious – Ma'idah 2). The gist is that in any good thing within the abilities given to man, there should be mutual help and cooperation. Not only is this not wrong; it is sometimes very necessary, and sometimes even unavoidable. Allah has stated in Suratul Kahf that even Dhul-Qarnain (a), while building the barrier to block the entry of 'Ya'juj and Ma'juj', asked the people **اعينونى بقوة** (help me with strength), and that they helped him by providing blocks of iron and other things. However, there is no doubt that in matters beyond the ability of creatures, which must come from Allah alone by unseen ways, help may be sought from Him alone. In such matters, seeking help from anyone other than Allah is not only meaningless; it is shirk, and the ascribing of divinity to creatures.

This surah is often recited in a gathering of more than one person, but sometimes one recites it alone. On such occasions one may wonder what the secret is behind using the plural: 'we worship, we seek help, guide us'. What it suggests is that all believers stand on one ideal and one goal, and that each individual must have a share and an interest in the common good and success of the community. Allah says that the believers are brothers (**إِنَّمَا الْمُؤْمِنُونَ إِخْوَةٌ**), and that all of you must hold fast to the rope of Allah and not be divided (**وَاعْتَصِمُوا بِحَبْلِ اللَّهِ جَمِيعًا وَلَا تَفَرَّقُوا**). The Prophet ﷺ also says: 'In showing compassion, love and kindness to one another, Muslims are like one body', that is, they should be so (Bu., Mu.). The import of all this is that it is not enough for each individual to care only for his own affairs; they must have a collective interest with one mind, and an organised sense of responsibility.

When the servants of Allah take the pledge before Him that they worship You alone and seek help from You alone, *\[p. 165\]* there is surely room for the question: what is the greatest help you are aiming at? The prayer **اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ** (Guide us onto the straight, upright path) is, as it were, the answer to that. The gist: this is the greatest favour we ask of You. If You grant us this favour, all our other needs will be fulfilled as well.

### Guide us onto the straight path (verse 6)

**اهد** (Ihdi) is the imperative form (**امر**) of the verb **هدى** (Hada). (In Arabic, command, request, counsel and the like all take the same form.) Its root forms are 'Hidayah' and 'Huda' (**الهدايه، والهدي**). Dictionaries give both of them meanings such as: making known gently, giving guidance, showing the way, bringing onto the way, giving direction (**الدلالة بلطف، الدلالة، الارشاد، اراءة الطريق، الايصال اليها**). They are customarily used in the sense of 'the right way, that is, the straight path'. Words from this root are mostly used for guidance toward good things. The words with the opposite meanings are **ضلال، ضلالة** (Dalal and Dalalah) and the other forms derived from them.

In the Quran 'Hidayah' is used both in the sense of giving guidance, that is, showing the way, and in the sense of putting on the right path, that is, bringing onto the straight way. If these two meanings are not understood separately, confusion of meaning can sometimes arise. An example of the first meaning is the sentence **وَأَمَّا ثَمُودُ فَهَدَيْنَاهُمْ فَاسْتَحَبُّوا الْعَمَىٰ عَلَى الْهُدَىٰ** (As for the tribe of Thamud, We gave them guidance; but they preferred blindness to the right way – Ha Mim Sajdah 17). It does not mean that Allah put them on the right path and they then abandoned it. What is meant is that, although right guidance was taught to them through the prophet and in other ways, they paid it no heed and sank into blindness, into the evil way. An example of the second meaning: **إِنَّكَ لَا تَهْدِي مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ يَهْدِي مَن يَشَاءُ** (Surely, you do not put on the right path whomever you love; but Allah puts on the right path whom He wills – Qasas 56). The gist is that the Prophet's ﷺ task is to show everyone the right path; putting them on the right path is not the Prophet's ﷺ task, it is Allah's doing.

It is not possible for us to set out precisely in how many ways people receive Hidayah (guidance) from Allah. **وَإِن تَعُدُّوا نِعْمَتَ اللَّهِ لَا تُحْصُوهَا** (If you were to count Allah's favour, you could not reckon it.) Even so, as Imam Baidawi, Imam \[p. 166\] Raghib (r) and others have pointed out, it can be divided as follows:

1. That which is received by being favoured with gifts such as special intelligence, the outer senses and the inner faculties, so as to meet the needs of life. The verse **رَبُّنَا الَّذِي أَعْطَىٰ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلْقَهُ ثُمَّ هَدَىٰ** – طه (Our Rabb is He who gave each thing its own creation, then gave it guidance) points to this.
2. That which is received by being given proofs and signs for telling truth from falsehood and good from evil. The verse **وَهَدَيْنَاهُ النَّجْدَيْنِ** – البلد (We showed him, man, the two high roads) indicates this kind.
3. That which is received through the prophets and the scriptures. The verse **وَجَعَلْنَاهُمْ أَئِمَّةً يَهْدُونَ بِأَمْرِنَا** – الانبياء (And We made them, the prophets mentioned before, leaders who give guidance by Our command) points to this.
4. That which is received through 'Tawfiq', the special help that people who walk the right path receive from Allah. What is said in **وَالَّذِينَ اهْتَدَوْا زَادَهُمْ هُدًى** – محمد (Those who have taken the right path, He increases them in guidance) and **وَالَّذِينَ جَاهَدُوا فِينَا لَنَهْدِيَنَّهُمْ سُبُلَنَا** – العنكبوت (Those who strive in Our cause, We will surely bring them onto Our paths) is Hidayah of this kind.
5. That which comes by being granted great fortunes in the Hereafter, such as the life of Paradise. What He said about those who died as martyrs in the way of Allah, **سَيَهْدِيهِمْ وَيُصْلِحُ بَالَهُمْ ﴿٥﴾ وَيُدْخِلُهُمُ الْجَنَّةَ عَرَّفَهَا لَهُمْ ﴿٦﴾** – محمد (He, Allah, will guide them on the way, set their state right, and admit them into Paradise), is an example of this kind.

When Allah states that disbelievers and evildoers do not receive Hidayah, it is the fourth and fifth of these that is meant. When it is said that it is not the Prophet ﷺ or anyone else who gives Hidayah, the first, fourth and fifth kinds are included; these three come from Allah alone. The first, second and third kinds are given to believers and others alike. Here, although what is meant by 'Hidayah' may be all the kinds explained above, it is the fourth and fifth that are chiefly intended. Linguistically, 'Huda' and 'Hidayah' (**الهدى والهداية**) are alike; *\[p. 167\]* but Imam Raghib (r) has pointed out that there is a slight difference between the two, and that in the Quran 'Huda' is used for the Hidayah that comes from Allah alone. He clearly cites verses such as **قُلْ إِنَّ الْهُدَىٰ هُدَى اللَّهِ** – آل عمران (Say: Surely, the true 'Huda' is the 'Huda' of Allah – 3:73) and **قُلْ إِنَّ هُدَى اللَّهِ هُوَ الْهُدَىٰ** – البقرة (Say: Surely, Allah's 'Huda' is the true 'Huda' – 2:120).

Differing statements by scholars are found in explaining 'Hidayah'. Although many agree in principle, some are open to criticism or lead to confusion of meaning. This fault shows most in the statements of modern commentators of the Quran. Seeing the explanation some give of this Quranic verse, one may feel that many of the most important aspects of Hidayah are not even a matter of thought for them. It is in view of such facts that this subject had to be clarified a little here. Keeping this clarification in mind will also be useful in the many places in the Quran that speak of Hidayah, **ان شاء الله**. It is precisely because of the importance of the subject that Imam Raghib (r) has devoted many pages of his Quranic dictionary to explaining 'Hidayah'. It would be good for those who know Arabic and are interested to look through those pages. **والله ولى التوفيق** (and Allah is the Granter of success).

What is meant by the straight path, that is, the way with no bend or crookedness (**الصراط المستقيم**), can generally be understood from the sentences that follow. Even so, many commentators are seen to take differing positions in explaining it. As Imam Ibn Kathir (r) has pointed out, although they appear different outwardly, in reality they all merge into one meaning. That is, all those statements arrive at 'the way that Allah and His Messenger have prescribed'. Imam Ibn Jarir (r) has pointed out this fact. After quoting the explanations of many, he points out that the best of all of them is this, and says: 'What is meant is: (O Allah!) grant us Tawfiq to stand firm in the words and deeds for which You granted Tawfiq (enabled) those with whom You are pleased and whom You have favoured among Your servants. That is the "straight path". For whoever is granted Tawfiq to do the things for which the prophets, the truthful (siddiqs), the martyrs and the righteous (good people), whom Allah has favoured (as stated in Surah Nisa 69), were granted Tawfiq, has surely been granted Tawfiq to practise Islam, *\[p. 168\]* to accept the truth of the messengers, to hold fast to the scriptures, to follow what Allah commanded and shun what He forbade, and to follow the way of the Prophet ﷺ, of the Caliphs and of the righteous. All of this is the "straight path".' In this explanation, Ibn Jarir (r) has brought together and reconciled the differing opinions given on what the straight path means.

Believers, after all, are those who have adopted the straight way. That being so, one may wonder why they repeat this prayer again and again, in their prayers and elsewhere, for something they have already attained. Similar doubts may occur to some people on other occasions too. Ibn Kathir (r) gives here an answer to this doubt that helps on all such occasions. Its gist is this: 'This prayer is not for attaining once more the very thing already attained. Man needs to receive Hidayah from Allah at every moment. That is why He has commanded us to pray like this. To stand firm on His path with steadfastness, without straying from it, and to keep receiving increase and growth in it, the help and Tawfiq of Allah must be obtained. Man cannot do benefit or harm even to his own body unless Allah wills. So this makes him realise that he must constantly beseech Allah to keep extending His helping hand toward him at all times. It is for this very reason that Allah has taught believers to pray thus: **رَبَّنَا لَا تُزِغْ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَيْتَنَا وَهَبْ لَنَا مِن لَّدُنكَ رَحْمَةً ۚ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ الْوَهَّابُ** (Our Rabb! Do not let our hearts swerve after You have put us on the right path! And grant us mercy from Your presence! Surely, You alone are the great Bestower – Al Imran 8).'

### The path of those You have favoured (verse 7)

What is meant by **الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ** (the straight path), and what its positive and negative sides are, Allah has pointed out in brief words. **صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ** (the path of those whom You have favoured): this is its positive side. Who these favoured ones are, and what we must do to walk the path they walked, can be understood from another verse. Allah says:

> **وَمَن يُطِعِ اللَّهَ وَالرَّسُولَ فَأُولَٰئِكَ مَعَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِم مِّنَ النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَالشُّهَدَاءِ وَالصَّالِحِينَ ۚ وَحَسُنَ أُولَٰئِكَ رَفِيقًا** – النساء:٦٩

*\[p. 169\]* (Gist: Whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger, they will be with those whom Allah has favoured: the prophets, the truthful (siddiqs), the martyrs and the righteous. And what excellent companions they are. (4:69))

What we read a little earlier in the quotation from Ibn Jarir (r) is itself an explanation of this. It also seems fitting to know the essence of some statements Baidawi (r) made in explaining this sentence. They may be quoted thus: 'Although Allah's favours are countless, they can be divided into two: those of this world and those of the Hereafter. The first are of two kinds: those attained by divine gift and those attained through effort. Those attained by divine gift are also of two kinds: spiritual and bodily. The soul, special intelligence, the power of comprehension, the power of thought and the like are the spiritual ones. Physique, form, health, beauty and the like are the bodily ones. Good character, ability, learning, leadership, culture, wealth, influence and the like, and being purified of vices, are all favours attained through effort. The second, those of the Hereafter, are favours such as the forgiveness of sins, attaining Allah's pleasure and approval, and attaining everlasting success and the life of Paradise in the company of the virtuous. What is meant here by "favour" is the favours of this last category, and the things in the other category that help in attaining them.'

### Not of those under wrath, nor of those gone astray

The negative side of that path is this: **غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ**, that is, in gist, a way other than the one adopted by people who are subject to wrath and who have gone astray. What remains to consider now is whom it means by 'those struck by wrath' and 'those gone astray'. In some Hadiths narrated by Ahmad, Tirmidhi, Ibn Jarir, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Hibban (r) and others, it is reported that the Prophet ﷺ declared that 'those struck by wrath' means the Jews, and 'those gone astray' means the Nasara (Christians). Narrations to that effect are also found from the Companions. Ibn Abi Hatim (r) is even seen to state that he knows of no other explanation of these two words than this. It is clear that while this explanation of the Messenger ﷺ exists, there is no need to look for other explanations. But one must certainly consider why the subjects of wrath came to be the Jews and those gone astray the Christians, and what the way is to be saved from the things that bring about Allah's wrath and going astray.

To explain briefly, as Ibn Kathir (r) indicated, it may be put thus: the way of the favoured is to seek out and know the truth, and to put it into practice. To know the truth and yet *\[p. 170\]* cast it aside without bringing it into practice is the way of those under wrath. To miss the true path without understanding the truth is the way of those gone astray. Imam Ibn Taimiyyah (r) too has pointed out that the Jews were called those under wrath because they acted against the truth despite knowing it, and the Christians were called those gone astray because they went astray without knowing the truth. (See **اقتضاء الصراط المستقيم**.)

All in all, those under wrath are the Jews and those gone astray are the Christians. Even so, it is clear from Allah's own words that apart from them there are others too who are subject to wrath and who have gone astray. About those who deliberately abandon faith after accepting it, He says **فَعَلَيْهِمْ غَضَبٌ مِّنَ اللَّهِ** (upon them will be wrath from Allah... – Nahl 106); about disbelievers who turn people away from the way of Allah, **قَدْ ضَلُّوا ضَلَالًا بَعِيدًا** (they have strayed far astray – Nisa 167); and about the hypocrites and the polytheists He has said **وَغَضِبَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَعَنَهُمْ** (Allah is angered with them and has cursed them – Fath 6). The import of the Hadiths mentioned above would be that, as communities, the Jewish community stands foremost in incurring curse and wrath, and the Christian community in going astray. **الله أعلم**. About the Jews Allah has declared **مَن لَّعَنَهُ اللَّهُ وَغَضِبَ عَلَيْهِ** (those whom Allah has cursed and with whom He is angered – Ma'idah 60) and **لَّعَنَهُمُ اللَّهُ بِكُفْرِهِمْ** (Allah has cursed them for their disbelief – Al-Baqarah 88). About the Christians He has declared **قَدْ ضَلُّوا مِن قَبْلُ وَأَضَلُّوا كَثِيرًا وَضَلُّوا عَن سَوَاءِ السَّبِيلِ** (They went astray before; they led many astray, and strayed from the right way – Ma'idah 77). May He include us all among the good people who become worthy of His favours, without becoming subject to Allah's curse and wrath and without straying from the true path. Amin.

In a Hadith narrated by Imam Muslim, Nasa'i (r) and others from Abu Hurairah (r), the Prophet ﷺ said: 'Allah has declared: "The prayer has been divided into two halves between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks." When the servant says **الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ**, Allah says: "My servant has praised Me." When he says **الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ**, Allah says: "My servant has extolled Me." When he says **مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ**, Allah says: "My servant has glorified Me." When he says **إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ**, Allah says: "This is *\[p. 171\]* between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asks." Then when he says **اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ ... الى وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ**, Allah says: "This is for My servant, and he shall have what he asks."'

### Saying Amin

We said earlier that on finishing the recitation of this surah one should say 'Amin' (**آمين**) along with it, and that its meaning is 'Accept this prayer'. Hadiths recorded by Ahmad, Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi (r) and others report that the Prophet ﷺ used to say it. Some narrations report that he drew it out, and others that he said it aloud. In prayer it is a strong sunnah. The Prophet ﷺ says: 'When the imam says Amin, you should say Amin. For if a person's saying Amin coincides with the angels' saying Amin, his past sins will be forgiven' (Bu., Mu.). There are many more Hadiths that could be quoted about 'Amin'.

**اللهم لك الحمد والمنة والفضل وانت الموفق والمعين لما تحب وترضى**

(O Allah, Yours is the praise, the grace and the bounty, and You are the One who grants success and help toward what You love and are pleased with.)
