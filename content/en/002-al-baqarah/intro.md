## 2. Suratul Baqarah

**سورة البقرة** · Revealed in Madinah – Verses 286 – Sections (Ruku') 40 *\[p. 173\]*

This is the largest surah in the Holy Quran. Most of it was revealed in the early periods of the Madinan life of the Prophet ﷺ. A few ayahs are ones revealed in the last periods. You will remember what was explained in the Preface: that the revelation of the Quran, from the very beginning to the end, was not in order, nor surah by surah; that the practice was for it to be revealed at various times according to the occasion and the need; and that the Prophet ﷺ used to instruct the scribes that the portions revealed from time to time should be added at such-and-such a place in such-and-such surahs; and so on.

In fields such as fundamental principles, doctrines of belief, practical commands and prohibitions, legal directions, moral exhortations, parables, illustrative examples, historical events, ethical values, glad tidings and warnings, more is to be seen in this surah than in other surahs. It may be for that very reason that in some sayings of the Prophet this surah has been called **سنام القرآن وذروته** (the hump of the Quran and its summit) (Ah., Tw.) *\[TN: the hadith references are printed as abbreviations. In this part: അ = Ah. (Ahmad), മു = Mu. (Muslim), തി = Ti. (Tirmidhi), ന = Na. (Nasa'i), ഹാ = Ha. (Hakim), ബു = Bu. (Bukhari), ത്വ = Tw., ജ = Ja.\]* *\[DOUBT: ത്വ is most probably Tabarani, and ജ most probably Ibn Majah or Ibn Jarir; the book's list of abbreviations (in the Preface) was not available to confirm.\]*. The hump, as is known, is the swelling that stands out most on the back of the camel, the ox and the like. Likewise, among the parts of a mountain, what appears highest to the eye is its summit. The gist is that, in the same way, among the subjects treated in the Quran, Suratul Baqarah stands out more prominently.

During the period of the Prophet's ﷺ life in Makkah, it was the dominance and influence of the polytheists (mushriks) that held sway there. That is why the main discussions in the surahs revealed in the Makkan phase were concerned with Tawhid (the Oneness of God) and Shirk (associating partners with Him). By the time the Prophet and the Companions (Sahabah) went to Madinah – even before they arrived there – a large section of the Arabs there had already accepted the true faith. A clear atmosphere for Islam had also come into being there. Therefore the commands and prohibitions, and the counsels and directions, to be accepted *\[p. 174\]* positively and negatively in the personal and social spheres of man were the need of that situation. In this surah, revealed in the opening phases of the Madinan life, this matter can be seen to have been specially taken into account.

At the same time, a new community of enemies, the most bitter of all for Islam and the Muslims, had to be faced there. Yes, the Jews. They, who claimed a monopoly over the divine religion and the prophetic heritage, had in reality become so corrupted that they had not even a nominal connection with either of the two. The hatred, hostility, rancour and envy that the Jews adopted against Islam are well known. That is the reason why a good part of this surah concerns them. Another hostile group, which had set out to hatch conspiracies against Islam by making use of every loophole it found, was also on the scene there: the Munafiqs (the hypocrites). These people, who with their selfish interests and temporary gains in mind had outwardly put on the garb of Islam, were in reality not Muslims. Many things are stated in this surah about them as well. In addition, it contains some discussions, parables, accounts of events and the like that are not found in other surahs.

The reason it came to be named Suratul Baqarah (the chapter of the cow) is that an incident of a cow that took place among the Israelites is described in it. Most surahs are known by names that point to some particular subject described in each surah, or that point to some particular words that occur in it. A few will have names that point to the subject treated. On the whole, their names have only the standing of an easy means of identifying the surahs. That is why the same surah sometimes comes to have more than one name. In the handling of subjects, in their arrangement and in everything, the Quran has particular methods of its own. Since all this has been clarified in the Preface, there is no need to state anything more.

Abu Hurairah (r) has reported that the Prophet ﷺ said thus: 'Do not make your houses graves. Satan surely does not enter the houses in which Suratul Baqarah is recited.' (Ah.; Mu.; Ti.; Na.) The gist of saying that they should not be made graveyards is that houses should not be made silent and empty, without doing things such as reciting the Quran. Other Hadiths like this can also be seen. Once, when sending a group of people out in a certain direction, the Prophet ﷺ examined the ability of each one of them to recite the Quran. Among them, a man of young age said that he knew such-and-such parts of the Quran and Suratul Baqarah. Then the Prophet said: 'You have Suratul Baqarah with you? *\[p. 175\]* Then go. You are their Amir (leader).' (Ti.; Na.; Ja.; Ha.) Nawwas ibn Sam'an (r) has stated thus: 'I have heard the Prophet ﷺ say: "The Quran and its people, who used to act according to it, will be brought (on the Day of Qiyamah). Suratul Baqarah and Al Imran will come before them, arguing on behalf of their people (pleading their case)." The Prophet had also described three similitudes for them. I have not forgotten them to this day. That is, it was that the two of them would be like two clouds, or like two black shades, or like two flocks of birds drawn up in ranks.' (Ah.; Mu.; Ti.; Bu. – in the Tarikh.)

In the same way, there are many Hadiths and reports (riwayahs) to be quoted that describe the excellences of Suratul Baqarah and of some particular ayahs in it, such as 'Ayatul Kursiyy'. We may become acquainted with some of them in their respective places, as occasion arises, **إن شاء الله**. All of them point out the importance and greatness of the subjects contained in this surah and in the ayahs concerned.
