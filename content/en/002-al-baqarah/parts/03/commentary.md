*\[Verses 17–18\]*

*\[p. 197\]* It is some parables of the hypocrites that are described in these verses. In the Quran's method of presentation, parables have been given a good place. It is useful for grasping the vital point and the details of the subject being presented, and for ordinary people to understand the subject quickly. The custom of narrating parables among the Arabs is one of long standing. On some occasions, it is a short parable, rather than a long explanation, that will be of more use. Some special references concerning the citing of parables do indeed come in the verses below.

Many riwayahs (narrations) can be seen quoted as an explanation, as it were, of this parable. Among them, what seems clearer and more pleasing is a riwayah that Ibn Jarir (r) quoted from some Companions such as Ibn Mas'ud (r). Its gist is this: 'When the Messenger ﷺ came to Madinah, some people entered Islam. Later they changed into hypocrites. Their parable became like that of a man: he was in darkness. Therefore he lit a fire. That fire spread light on the pits and hollows and on the harmful things in his *\[p. 198\]* surroundings. He came to be able to see all that. He was able to know what all the things were that he should beware of. While things were so, his fire went out. Because of that, he could no longer recognise the harmful things that had to be guarded against. The hypocrite too is like this. He was at first in the darkness that is shirk. Then he accepted Islam. With that, he recognised halal and haram (what is permitted and what is not permitted), and good and bad. While things were so, he became a disbeliever (again). He came to be unable to know halal from haram and good from bad. Thus they (the hypocrites) are deaf, dumb and blind. Henceforth, they will not return to Islam.'

It is because they are people who do not hear and understand the things that matter, or open their mouths and speak the realities, or look and see with their eyes, that it was said, in a figurative form, that the hypocrites are deaf, dumb and blind. In Surat al-Hajj Allah says: 'Do they not travel through the land? Then there should come to be for them hearts with which they would think, applying their intellect, or ears with which they would hear and know! But (in reality) it is not the eyes that blindness afflicts. *\[TN: a full stop is printed after കണ്ണുകൾക്കല്ല ("not the eyes"), in the middle of this sentence; translated as one sentence.\]* But it is the hearts that are in the breasts that blindness afflicts.' (Hajj; 46) Allah points out one more parable for the hypocrites:–

*\[Verses 19–20\]*

*\[p. 199\]* This parable is somewhat more extensive than the first parable. This parable is usually interpreted through two kinds of viewpoints.

(1) On the basis that what is contained in it is a general portrayal of the state of affairs of the hypocrites.

(2) On the basis that some aspects of their state of affairs are portrayed in it, each separately. It is not that interpretation on these two bases has not been adopted for the first parable too. But it is in the interpretation of this parable that the comparison between those two conclusions becomes more evident.

According to the first viewpoint, its interpretation is roughly like this: A great rain keeps pouring from the sky. Besides the darkness of the night, there are the darknesses due to the heaviness of the rain and due to the piling up of the rain clouds: all together, a vast pitch darkness. The way to move forward cannot be seen. Unable even to grope one's way along, the balance of the mind too has been upset. For, when they hear the incessant thunder and lightning and the cracking and hissing of the thunderbolts, people, fearing death, will thrust their fingers into their ears and stop up their ears. As for the profusion of lightning, so that the sight of the eyes would be lost, it is *\[p. 200\]* terrible too. When the light of the lightning comes, they will try to walk on a little. By then, darkness again. With that, they stand stock-still. It is only that, because Allah guarded and protected them, they luckily escaped without the eardrums bursting and the hearing being destroyed, and without the eyes bursting and the sight being destroyed. The state of affairs of the hypocrites too is like the condition of being caught in such a rain. That is, they never have peace of mind or calm. Doubts and confusions on one side. Agitation and fear on another side. The gains that come from joining the side of the Prophet ﷺ and the true believers, and along with them the responsibilities that may have to be faced because of it, on yet another side. The selfish gains that are obtained by joining with the disbelievers, and its consequences, besides. In short, the gist is: the very condition that arises when caught in the rain described above.

The second method of interpretation is roughly like this: It is the verses of the Quran, the messages, the signs, the commands and prohibitions and the like, which keep being revealed from Allah for their good, that have been likened to the rain. Just as rain makes the earth full of life, they, surely, produce life for man too. When heavy rain pours down, thunder and lightning are natural. It is the hypocrites' doubt, hypocrisy, confusion, evil desire and the like that have been compared to the darkness. The warnings, forewarnings and the like contained in the divine messages have been likened to thunderclaps, and the signs, glad tidings and the like in them to flashes of lightning. It is their turning away like deaf people, unable to bear hearing the warnings and reprimands, that has been likened to stopping up the ears for fear of death on account of the thunderbolts. But the purport of saying 'Allah is One who encompasses the disbelievers' is that they are not going to get safety by stopping up their ears. Sometimes an awakening comes to them about the good things that are due to be obtained, materially and in the next world, if they accept the way of truth. Thus their course moves forward a little. By then, selfish thoughts and the stages of trial come to mind. With that, it comes to a standstill. That is what was said: that they will walk forward in the light of the lightning and will stop when it becomes dark. While they have misused the ability Allah gave them to grasp things by seeing and hearing, it is not at all difficult for Him to take those away entirely. But it is only that it has not happened because He has not willed it.

Of these two methods of interpretation, in the overall form and in the detailed form, as far as ordinary minds are concerned, it is the second form that will seem more worth following and clearer. However, it is the first form that deserves more place in the field of literature. It is this that will be of more help for each person to search out and discover, according to his own development of intellect and sphere of thought, the principles and secrets contained in each parable. **الله أعلم** (Allah knows best).

*\[p. 201\]* There are two opinions among the commentators: that both these parables are about the same type of Munafiqs, and that they are not. Many, such as Imam Ibn Jarir (r), are seen to have adopted the first opinion, and many, such as Ibn Kathir (r), the second – that they are not – opinion. The parable of the fire in the 17th verse is about the out-and-out hypocrites, within whom belief has not touched at all. The parable in this verse is about the second kind of hypocrites, who, although they have belief, are unable, on account of its weakness, to stand firm in Islamic discipline, and who at times face doubt and confusion, and in whom, in between, awakening and the light of true faith become manifest. This is the second opinion. Ibn Kathir (r) can be seen to have established this opinion in his Tafsir, quoting and citing as examples many verses of the Quran and other things.

Just as it was said at the end of the 19th verse **وَٱللَّهُ مُحِيطُۢ بِٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ** (Allah is One who encompasses the disbelievers), and at the end of the 20th **إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيۡءٖ قَدِيرٞ** (Allah is capable of every matter), it is very common for some sentences to be seen in the Quran that have no apparent connection, in structure or in subject, with the preceding sentences. Most ayahs will end with some sentence of this kind. Although an apparent connection with the preceding sentences may not appear for them – if one reflects a little against the background of the subject of discourse and of the context, it can be seen that, while those appended sentences are very small in size, they are very pregnant with meaning. Those sentences will be ones that point out the gist of the subject of discourse, or the principle contained in it, or its wisdom, or some reality that is the basis of it – many things like that. And those who reflect with regard to the context will be able to find many meanings in them. **و الله الموفق** (And Allah is the One who grants success.)

### Section – 3

After speaking about the God-fearing in the first few verses of the surah, then about the disbelievers in some verses, and after that about the hypocrites in the verses up to here, Allah says in the following verses, addressing the human community as a whole:–

*\[Verses 21–22\]*

*\[p. 202\]* Recall what was explained in Surah Fatihah about the meanings and intent of 'ibadah', to which the meaning 'worship' was assigned, and of 'Rabb', to which the meaning 'Lord' was given, and at the beginning of this surah about 'taqwa', to which the meaning 'observing caution' was assigned.

Addressing mankind all together – those who were in the time when the Quran was being revealed and those who come into being after it – without the distinction of believer, disbeliever and hypocrite, Allah commands that they should worship Allah, who is the Creator that gave existence to all of them and the Lord who has been protecting and sustaining them, and that, while knowing the reality that He alone is the Creator and the Lord, they should not set up equals and partners to Him. Along with inviting the human world to the doctrine of one God, which is tawhid, Allah has also pointed out its undeniable necessity *\[p. 203\]* in these verses. In saying 'who created those before you too', there is contained an indication that your forefathers too were obliged to accept this tawhid, and that therefore it is not right for you to follow them in the tradition of shirk that they kept practising contrary to it. It is pointed out not only that tawhid is a duty that is yours by nature, but also that only through it can you be those who observe caution. It was said earlier, surely, that it is those who observe caution who stand on right guidance, and that it is they alone who attain success.

What Allah reminds of here are some great blessings that are known even to the most elementary intelligence, that each individual keeps experiencing, and that are the basis of man's existence and life. In none of them is there any other share or working that is not His. That being so, also on the basis of being grateful and submissive to Him, the sole Author of them, man must not worship anyone else. Yes, it becomes clear from this that, just as the right to be the one worshipped belongs to Allah alone, man too has the obligation to be the worshipper of Him alone.

It is with reference to Allah's having made the earth fit for man to sit, to lie down, to walk, to run, to jump – in short, to move about as he pleases – that it was said that He made it a spread (**فِرَٰشٗا**). The intent is not that He made it flat like a spread. From this it is clear that there is no contradiction between this word and the fact that the earth is spherical in shape. Further, there is no objection even to taking it as meaning 'He made it flat like a spread'. Along with its being a sphere, the expanse and vastness of the earth are not unknown, surely. If the whole earth had been rock, or water, or mud, or sand, or the like, or else if it had been entirely level, or full of hills and hollows, what would the situation be? Consider it! Only when one thinks – not on the basis of the position of any particular individual, but on the basis of the general position of man – can one guess at the greatness of the great blessing contained in this. Imagine the earth as a huge house. And suppose that its floor has been furnished. Then a roof too is needed on its upper part, surely. That is what was said: that He made the sky a building (**بِنَآءٗ**) – or a roof. It is worth mentioning that in another place it has been said: **وَجَعَلۡنَا ٱلسَّمَآءَ سَقۡفٗا مَّحۡفُوظٗاۖ** (We have made the sky a protected roof. (21:32).

Right. Now, the residents of this house need water for thirst and food for hunger, surely. For that, rain keeps pouring from the sky – from the upper region. Not only for drinking; it is also used for other needs such as agriculture. By that means He produces many kinds of fruits and produce in the earth. What Allah says about this in another place *\[p. 204\]* is as follows: 'Allah is the One who has made for you the earth a dwelling place and the sky a building. He has given you form, and then made your forms good. And He has given you food from excellent things. Such a One is Allah, your Lord, (40:64)

Now, the earth is a house. Its owner and the one who built it is Allah. Human beings, the residents in it, are His creation. The one who has been sustaining them, giving them food and water, is also He Himself. The fact being this, and this being known to everyone, is there any justification for worshipping or bowing in submission to anyone other than Him, or for not offering worship before Him?! **فَلَا تَجۡعَلُواْ لِلَّهِ أَندَادٗا وَأَنتُمۡ تَعۡلَمُونَ** (Therefore, do not make equals to Allah while you know.)

What is meant by setting up equals (**أَندَادٗا**) to Him is imagining that some other thing has any kind of equality or share in Allah's exalted attributes, or in His works, or in His authority and rights, or else in His essence. Word, deed and belief which arise from this notion, and which end in this notion, all belong to the kinds of shirk. And shirk – as Allah and the Messenger have made clear in a way that leaves no room for doubt of meaning – is the most severe of sins, and unforgivable. From what was said above one can understand, surely, the reason why making vows to other than Allah and swearing oaths in the name of other than Allah become shirk. When one remembers that the Prophet ﷺ forbade saying that it rained because of such-and-such a star – or zodiac sign – and, when saying about some matter 'what Allah willed' (**مَا شَاءَ اللّٰهُ**), also adding to it 'and what so-and-so willed' (**وَشَاءَ فُلَانٌ**), consider how vast the extent of shirk is! (Some more explanation will come at the 165th verse. **إن شاء الله** (if Allah wills).

As seen in these verses, first establishing Allah's creatorship (**خَالِقِيَّة**) and lordship (**رُبُوبِيَّة**), and then on that basis establishing His being the One worshipped (**أُلُوهِيَّة**) – or divinity – is a custom that can often be seen in the Quran. Only pure materialists – atheists – will be found who do not concede the first quality to Him. With conceding that quality, the intellect will be compelled to concede the second quality too. Although there may be differences of opinion in the explanation of the second quality – lordship – none of those who profess a scripture *\[DOUBT: വേദവാദികൾ; വേദം here is taken as "scripture" (as in വേദക്കാർ, People of the Scripture), i.e. those who hold to a revealed religion, in contrast to the atheists just mentioned; a narrower reading, "adherents of the Vedas", is also possible.\]* will fail to concede it. For one who concedes those two qualities, there is no justification for not conceding that it is necessary for the third quality too (being the One worshipped) to belong to Allah alone. One matter needs to be specially noted. Instead of debates based on logic, it is turning their attention to realities that are very familiar to human minds and that contain eternal truths, *\[p. 205\]* and to the great unseen Power that stands behind them, that will be more practical. This is also the policy of the Quran. As far as the prejudiced, arrogant ones are concerned, who will believe only what they have seen with their eyes and will not think beyond what they have already approved as right – as Allah stated in the sixth and seventh verses above – the only thing to be done is to give them up.

It would be good to know some things that Imam Razi (r) stated in his Tafsir (in the explanation of the 21st verse), after explaining that, if the principle that Allah must be worshipped – that He alone may be worshipped – is to be accepted, the existence of Allah must first be known, and that the ways the Quran adopted for that are such-and-such. The gist of that statement is as follows: 'If you have understood what has been said, we say: Allah adopted proofs of this kind as proof in His Book for two reasons:

(1) It is that which serves people to understand the matter, and that which suits the intellect more. The proofs in the Quran must be such as are grasped quickly, even without minute reflection. Only then will they benefit both the general public and those of special standing. Truly, it is that which He adopted at the very beginning of Allah's Book, too.

(2) The aim of the Quranic proofs is not to carry on disputation. It is to establish true belief firmly in hearts. It is this kind that is strong and fitting, more than all other kinds of proofs. For, just as proofs of this sort give certain knowledge about the existence of the Creator, they also remind of His blessings. And remembrance of blessings produces love, removes dispute, and brings about obedience. Therefore, it is proofs of the kind the Quran adopted that are the best.'

Continuing, Razi (r) has also quoted ten or eleven examples of how great men of the earlier generations used to give proof for establishing the existence of Allah in a form that was – not argumentative or scientific – pleasing and fitting. Some of them can be seen quoted from him by Ibn Kathir (r) in his Tafsir too. Their summary is this:

(1) When the Caliph Harun Rashid (r) asked Imam Malik (r) what the proof is for establishing the Creator, the proof he gave was the diversity of languages, of voices and of melodies.

(2) When some atheists asked Imam Abu Hanifah (r) for proof of the existence of the Creator, he said: 'Forgive me, I am thinking about a piece of news I heard. (So you must give me a little time.) The news is this: 'There is a ship in the sea. It has been filled with many kinds of goods. There is no one to guard it or to steer it. Even so, it goes *\[p. 206\]* and comes by itself. It pushes through the waves and escapes safely.' When they heard this, they said: 'Hey! No one with sense would say this.' He said: 'Shame! The countless things that exist in the heavenly world above and in the earthly world below, and the very many most ingenious matters connected with them! That none of these has a Maker?!' Their mouths were shut. (\*) They accepted Islam.

> **Note:** (\*) Whether rationalists or scientists, whatever arguments they may have to state as proof of atheism, which bewilder and attract listeners, the total gist of all of them will be one of two: either this entire universe gradually took shape by itself, just like that; or it was self-made because of a great explosion. Either way, they can only kneel before the reply of Imam Abu Hanifah (r) – unless one may give irrationality the name 'reason'.

(3) When Imam Shafi'i (r) was asked about it, he gave this reply: 'Here is the leaf of the amarathi *\[DOUBT: അമറാത്തി; the tree could not be identified. In the Arabic of this report as usually transmitted (e.g. Ibn Kathir) the leaf is ورق التوت, "mulberry leaf", and silkworms feed on mulberry; but whether അമറാത്തി is the author's word for the mulberry is not certain.\]*. Its taste is one and the same. The worm eats it. From the worm, silk comes out. The honeybee eats it. From it, honey comes out. Goats, cattle and livestock eat it. They drop dung and droppings. The deer too eats it. From it musk is produced. The food is all one and the same.'

(4) The proof Imam Ahmad (r) gave is the hen's egg. He said: 'A fortress, strong and smooth. It has no door or opening. Its outside is like white silver. Its inside is like pure gold. Suddenly, one day, its walls burst open. A living creature comes out, beautiful in form and with a lovely voice, having hearing and sight!'

All these, surely, are quotations from some great men who were lions among scholars. It would be good to know also a proof from an ordinary man. Ibn Kathir (r) quotes that, when asked what the proof is that there is a Rabb, an A'rabi (desert-dweller) said this: 'Ya Subhanallah! (How wonderful!) Dung makes known the camel. The footprint makes known the one who walked by. Then the sky with its zodiacal constellations, the earth with its wide pathways, the sea with its waves – do none of these make known that there is One who is subtly knowing?!'

After establishing the necessity of tawhid with undeniable and indisputable proofs, Allah establishes, through the following verses, the superhuman nature of the Quran too. With that, the prophethood of the noble Prophet ﷺ too becomes established. That there is none worthy of worship except Allah, and that Muhammad ﷺ is the Messenger of Allah (**لا اله الا الله محمد رسول الله**), is, surely, the watchword of Islam. Allah says:–
