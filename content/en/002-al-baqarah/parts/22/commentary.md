*[p. 392]* In this verse many subjects are contained. They may be explained thus:

**(One)** Just as Allah has put you on the straight way in the matter of the qiblah, He has also made you an excellent community. ( **وَكَذَٰلِكَ جَعَلۡنَٰكُمۡ أُمَّةٗ وَسَطٗا** ) – thus Allah informs the true believers. For the word **وَسَط** (wasat), the literal meaning is: middle, what is in the middle – thus. It is used in senses such as what is excellent, what is superior, what is moderate. As Sayyid Qutb has pointed out, whatever meaning is assigned to that word, the Muslim community are indeed deserving of that description. In beliefs and observances, and in thoughts, feelings and the like – in all indeed – they are, compared with the other communities, ones of the middle position, superior ones and ones having moderation. As Ibn Jarir (r) has opined, the Muslim community are not ones who, like the Christians, tied things onto the religion and went beyond bounds, nor ones who, like the Jews, cut the religion short. There is, surely, no need to say specially that what is meant here by the Muslim community is not nominal *[p. 393]* Muslims, and that it is the Muslims who have accepted the real Islam that Allah and the Rasul have shown us.

**(Two)** What Allah described as the outcome of having made this community a middle community excellent community – is: in order that you may become ones who bear witness over mankind and the Rasul the person who bears witness over you(**لِّتَكُونُواْ شُهَدَآءَ عَلَى ٱلنَّاسِ** .......) *[DOUBT: on p. 393 the printed "....." may belong before or after this Arabic phrase (it stands at the left end of the Arabic); placed after, as on p. 388.]*. About what is meant by this testimony, it can be seen interpreted in more than one way. There are opinions that it is testimony in this world, that it is testimony in the Hereafter, and that it is testimony in this world and in the next. Their brief substance may be summarised thus:

(1) In beliefs and observances, in practices relating to deeds and in all, giving model and leadership to others, you must be witnesses to the communities of mankind. Giving you model and leadership as to how your life and procedures must be, the Rasul ﷺ will be a witness for you too.

(2) As to whether others are on right guidance or on misguidance, you have to bear witness. Or, it is by making your moral life the standard that the good and the evil of others will be measured. Your good and evil, for their part, will also be measured on the basis of what the Rasul ﷺ has shown and taught you.

(3) Because you have received sufficient proofs through signs and scriptures, and through prophets, you will be witnesses against them in the Hereafter that the prophets who passed before have properly explained to their communities what the true way is, and that Allah has not shown stinginess to any community in explaining the true way. Or, you are ones who have to bear witness. As to whether you have properly kept the teachings of the Prophet and of the scripture or not, the Rasul too will bear witness concerning you. If one reflects a little, it can be understood that the basic principle contained in all these interpretations, which outwardly seem different from one another, is indeed one in all. Along with that, one other fact too has to be understood from this. That is, this community's becoming a witness for others – along with indeed being this community's superiority – is something that also denotes a mighty liability upon this community compared with the other communities. Because, keeping the worthiness for that testimony without bringing blemish on it is the duty of the Muslim community.

On this subject, it seems fitting to the occasion to quote here the gist of a long statement that the late Sayyid Qutb has made in his tafsir, in the commentary of this verse. It may be read thus

In belief, in conception, in worship and in the qiblah – in all indeed – distinctive features of its own are indispensable for the Muslim community too. But, as in the emblems relating to worship and in the qiblah, in nothing else will this distinctiveness be seen revealed so clearly. It is from this that the need arises to maintain the outward forms of the rites of worship. To those who have not thought as needed about them and about human psychology, they may seem to be some narrow conservatisms, or some outward ceremonies. Human nature itself, formed of the visible body and the invisible soul, does demand that there must be some forms of action for expressing his thoughts and feelings outwardly. It is on this natural basis that Islam has maintained its emblems of worship. That aim will not be fulfilled by mere intention or by presence of the soul. Therefore: one must stand turned toward such-and-such a side, must bow in such-and-such a manner, must lay the head down, must carry out ihram (entry into Hajj and 'Umrah) from such-and-such a place, must wear such-and-such a garment, must recite such-and-such, *[p. 397]* must intend, must not eat such-and-such, must not do – in this way, it is not possible without determining form and manner in each one. It is from forms not being adopted with authority for expressing thoughts and feelings outwardly that the worships of stone, tree, sun, star, bird, beast and the like have taken shape.

Therefore, Islam welcomed the demand of human nature that there should be a fixed form for the emblems relating to worship. When he turns forward to Allah with his mind and body, it fixed for him toward where he must stand turned. But there is one condition. There must not be any conception of form about Allah's essence. That it has been forbidden – that one may not bear resemblance to non-Muslims and may not imitate them in features that are their own – is also indeed against this background. All this is not merely some communal matters, conservative things or ceremonies. They indeed are the bases for distinguishing a people from other peoples in intellect, thought, feeling, mind, character, orientation and the like – in all. Abu Hurairah (r) says that the Rasul ﷺ declared : The Jewish people and the Christians do not give colour (to greyed beard and hair), you must be contrary to them. Allah's servant and His Rasul – thus do say’. (Bu) For Muslims, bearing resemblance to others in everything such as outlook, dress and attire, word and work, has been prohibited. The reason behind all this is that they will become an impetus to changes of thought and feeling. (**في ظلال القرآن**)
