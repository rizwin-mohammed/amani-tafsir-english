*[Verse 282]*

*[p. 680]* After having explained many things about interest, and about interest deals, in the previous verses, Allah teaches the true believers, in this verse, which is the biggest ayah in the Quran, many counsels and directions, and injunctions and prohibitions, that are to be observed in general in property-deals, which are daily necessities in the social life of man, and that are serviceable for their good and for their security. Just as in belief, and in character, and in customs and practices, in financial deals too the true believers have to maintain a lofty and decent standard and be a model to others, surely. That, because these proprieties of dealing that Allah has commanded in clear and broad language are not observed as is needed- even though they were obliged to observe them more than anyone else- quarrel and wrangle connected with deals are common among the Muslims in these times, is indeed a lamentable plain truth. Indeed without extra interpretation or explanation, the contents in this verse are clear in such a way that more or less everyone can understand. Even so, in the light of each sentence, our religious scholars can be seen to have given in their books many rules and elucidations relating to deeds. That is the distinctive feature of both the style of statement and the manner of presentation of the holy Quran. The substance of the legal directions contained in this verse, addressing the true believers, we can summarise thus:-

(1) When carrying out debt-deals placing a set term, it must be written and recorded. In having said debt-deals, all deals are included that are not disallowed from the religious point of view, and in which the liability to be paid off on this side and that is not paid off in cash indeed. This commandment is not on the footing of a legal compulsion- stipulation-. Or, *[p. 681]* that, only because of not writing down, becoming subject to punishment, or the deal becoming invalid, will not come about is indeed the opinion of the majority of scholars. If one sees that there may come about a way for doubt, or for squabble, in the future, it will become obligatory, moreover. In the next verse, the sentence that began **فَإِنْ أَمِنَ بَعْضُكُمْ بَعْضًا** (if some among you have trusted some) is also pointed out as proof for this. We can see the meaning and the interpretation of that sentence there. **إن شاء الله** Anyhow, only because the deal is merely small. Writing down must not be abandoned. Only on the ground that there will be no committing of breach of trust towards each other may it be decided that it is not needed. Allah says further on, surely, that whether the deal is small or big, you must not be reluctant to write down.

(2) The writing and recording must be by the hand of a third person who knows writing. If it is one of the dealers, it may come about that in the writing he brings about some alteration or hint favourable to his own concern, or else that he is suspected so, surely. When it is a third person, this ill is not going to happen. He must observe justice- must not do anything from which good or ill comes about specially to one side-, moreover. Or, the writing must be properly in accordance with the nature and the determinations of the deal.

(3) He who knows writing- if the occasion comes in which he has to write out- must not refuse it. His having come to have the ability to write is, surely, a favour that Allah has done for him. Therefore his putting that ability to use for his brethren is also a gratitude that is done to Allah. Refusing it will also be ingratitude. This is to be done merely on the footing of a service. Still, in case it becomes necessary to spend time and difficulty for it in such a way as to deserve a recompense, the writer may also take a recompense. Allah has said, surely, that you must not show harm both to the writer and to the witness.

(4) When preparing the record, the right to tell the statement that is to be written in it belongs to him in whose name the debt-liability comes in that deal. When he indeed tells the statement, it will also be an avowal that he takes up that liability. This does not mean that anything may be written into it at his will. Any artificial words that do not agree either with the determinations between both parties or with the religious laws must not be included in it. It is that which having said ‘And let him guard against Allah, his Rabb’ points out. The other party may also inquire whether any flaw is coming in the statements.

(5) It will not be possible for everyone, surely, to tell the records and evidences concerning deals and have them written. For example: the fool, the indiscreet, the madman, the child, one who does not know the language, the dumb, the sick, one who lives abroad *[p. 682]* all of these will not be able for it. Therefore, for such persons, the guardians or representatives who conduct their affairs must perform that matter. These too must specially attend to the matter of justice. Contrary both to the determination and to what is real, nothing of their own must be mixed into it.

(6) It is not enough only if it is written and recorded; two persons must also be set up as witnesses. The witnesses must be both Muslims and men who have reached maturity of age. This can be understood from the attribute ‘those who belong to your men’. Although it speaks further on about making women witnesses, there is a hint in this that in matters of deals it is men who are more deserving than women to bear witness. It is men, surely, who will have more discretion and experience in such matters. The convenience for performing testimony in court and elsewhere will also be more for them. The witnesses must be both persons of propriety and generally accepted. It is that which the attribute **مِمَّنْ تَرْضَوْنَ** (from those with whom you are pleased) points out.

(7) If, by circumstance, two men have not become convenient, the witnesses may be one man and two women. Although a special place has been accorded to the testimony of women in some matters that have a special connection with women, what is understood from this is that in the matter of deals it is not enough if only women are witnesses.

(8) The reason for laying down that two women are needed in the place of one man. Allah also reminds that it is that lapse of memory and error may often occur to them. If a mistake occurs to one, this will serve, surely, for the other to recall it to memory and correct it. When looking with reference to individuals, although some women may surpass many men in intellect and in power of memory and so on, it is the intellectual difference that is in general between woman and man that Allah hints at by means of this. Once the Prophet ﷺ counselled women thus: ‘You must both do almsgivings and seek forgiveness of sins abundantly. It is with women in the majority among the inmates of Hell that it has been shown to me; then a woman asked the reason for it: the noble Prophet said: ‘You both show ingratitude to kinsmen, and increase cursing. Than one among you, who are deficient in intellect, and religion, defeating the intellect of a firm-minded man, I have not seen anyone else (having ability for it).’ Then that woman asked: ‘What is the deficiency for us in intellect and in religion?’ The noble Prophet said: ‘That the testimony of two women stands in the place of one man is on account of the deficiency of her intellect. Her getting by for days together both without praying, and without observing fasting in Ramadan (in the menstrual seasons) is also the deficiency in her religion.’ (Muslim.)

(9) When the occasion of taking up testimony is faced, the witnesses must not re*[p. 683]*fuse it. If it is only some set individuals who are present where the deal takes place, they indeed will have to perform it. If many who are fit for testimony are ready, it must also be performed by someone among them. This is also a social duty. When need comes, the witnesses' performing their testimony before both judges and arbitrators is their responsibility. In a saying of the Prophet it has come thus: ‘Shall I tell you about the best one among witnesses? He is the one who comes with his (which he took up) testimony before the asking’. (Mu.) One who fulfils testimony without reluctance when it becomes needed is the gist. In another saying of the Prophet it has also come thus: ‘Shall I tell you about the bad one among witnesses? That is the one who bears witness before the asking to stand witness.’ (Bu; Mu.) This is, surely, the sign of false witnesses.

(10) Whether the deal is small or big, unwillingness must not be shown to write down the debt-bond. Or, laying down that it is enough if only big deals are recorded is not right. It is about debt-deals, surely, that the talk is. Therefore, until the term of the deal is over and it comes to an end, guarding its record is indeed needed. To whom the right to guard and keep the record belongs has not been made clear. Still, because the right to tell the statement belongs to him who takes on the debt-liability, it is to the person who is to get the debt settled that the right to guard the record belongs. Not only that, if it becomes needed, it is he, surely, who will have to prove both the determinations and the stipulations in the deal.

(11) Allah has also pointed out the reasons for so emphasising writing and recording. **One:** It is that which is more just with Allah. It serves to conclude matters in accordance with the determinations in the deal without ill happening to the two sides. **Secondly:** it will also be a support for the testimony of the witnesses. When there is both the spoken word of the witnesses and the written record together, the proof will grow stronger. Even if any forgetfulness or doubt comes upon the witnesses through the oldness of time or otherwise, what is real can be understood, surely, by looking at that writing. **Thirdly:** there is a possibility of doubts coming about for the deals *[DOUBT: the print has ഇടപാടുകൾക്ക് "for the deals"; it may be a misprint for ഇടപാടുകാർക്ക് "for the dealers". The English follows the print.]* indeed too, concerning some provision in the deal or about the details. It will be very helpful for settling that doubt too. Reflect and see how much concern Allah has in the good of men!

(12) All that has been stated so far was concerning debt-deals. Now, what if the deal has been a cash deal that ends at hand and spoken word- in which the giving of the article and the taking of the recompense finish together-? It need not be written and recorded. In the ordinary course, the necessity of it too will not arise. It need not be specially said either that, when, because of its being a deal on a large scale, or because of seeing that a stage will come in the future in which ownership has to be proved, or otherwise, it comes about that recording it is needed, writing it down is what is needed.

*[p. 684]* (13) Even so, in cash deals too, setting up two persons as witnesses is needed. In case any dispute and claim is raised either between the parties or from outside, this will be of benefit for making it come to nothing. It need not be supposed that in all the petty takings and givings that keep taking place daily among the people there must be two witnesses. Witness need be set up only in deals that are of consequence and in which it seems that the need for a witness may arise in the future. In cash deals the liability of both parties is finished then and there. In debt-deals the liability ends only when their time-limit ends. This is the reason for this difference between the two. It may be because of that, too, that, just as it was said that whether a debt-deal is small or big it must be written down, Allah did not say that whether cash deals are small or big witness must be set up. **اللّه اعلم**

(14) There is a possibility for the verb-form **يُضَارَّ** in the sentence **وَلَا يُضَارَّ كَاتِبٌ وَلَا شَهِيدٌ** to be both of known doer ( **مَعْرُوف** ) and of unknown doer (**مَجْهول**). In accordance with this, the two nouns standing next to it ( **كاتب، شهيد** ) can also be its doer or object ( **الفاعل او المفعول** ), therefore meaning can come to that sentence in two ways. According to the first, ‘Be it a writer, be it a witness, must not cause harm’, and, according to the second, ‘Be it to a writer, be it to a witness, harm must not be done’ will be the meaning. It is this second meaning that we have accepted in the translation. Either way, the intention is clear. That harms such as carrying out artifice in the writing, or in the spoken word of testimony, being reluctant to write out when need arises, not performing testimony, taking wage or travel expense exceeding propriety, must not come about from the side of both the writer and the witness will be its intention according to the first meaning. That harms such as compelling to carry out artifice in the writing or in the testimony, not giving the wage-expenses that are to be given according to propriety, putting those who have no leisure and convenience to difficulty both to write and to speak as witness, must not come about from the side of the dealers will be its import according to the second meaning.

(15) Allah warns that, in case harm and injustice come about, from whosesoever side it may be, it will be defiance and licentiousness from them. Continuing, it is by reminding both that everyone must guard against Allah in every subject, and that the explaining of matters in so much detailed form is for you to learn and know your religious laws, and that Allah knows about all matters such as both what the laws needed for your good and for your success are, and how far you are observing them, that Allah concludes this great verse. How grand are both the concern that Allah has in making successful the worldly and otherworldly lives of the true believers, who are His servants, and the mercy that He shows towards *[p. 685]*them! We have also read before, surely, the hadith that the Prophet ﷺ declared on one occasion, that it is ever so much more compassion that Allah has towards His servants than that which its mother has towards a suckling infant. **اللهم فلك الحمد حتى ترضى** *[DOUBT: the author gives no Malayalam for this Arabic; its likely meaning is "O Allah, so to You is the praise, until You are pleased".]*

*[Verse 283]*

*[p. 686]* What is in this verse too are indeed subjects connected with the subjects in the previous verse. It is mainly three matters, indeed, that have been stated in this.

(1) It is that when carrying out a debt-deal while on journeys, in case a person is not got to write the debt-bond, he who takes the debt (the obligor) must give some article as a pledge into the possession of him who gives the debt (the obligee) and give assurance for the deal. It is not that being on a journey, and not getting a writer, is a stipulation for the giving of a pledge- that it must not be given when not on a journey and when a writer is in existence-. It is when on a journey that both the hardship of getting a writer, and the necessity of a pledge, will be felt more. Therefore it is only that it has been said so. Both the scarcity in former times of those who knew writing and that, as far as the Arabs were concerned, journey was a daily occurrence are worth stating. That, when the noble Prophet ﷺ passed away, his coat of mail was in pledge in the hand of a Jew, and that it was on account of having acquired grain as debt for the food of the household, is indeed historically famous. From this it can be understood, surely, that journey is not a stipulation for a pledge. The aim of a pledge is making sure that the obligee will get his sum settled. If this aim is to be achieved, the pledged article has to be obtained by him into possession. It is therefore that ‘pledge that is given into possession’ has been said.

(2) It is that, once trust has been placed in each other, observing that trustworthiness properly is the duty of the one who has been trusted; that he must guard against Allah in carrying out artifice or deception in it. It is as a general principle that this has been stated. That the dealers, including both him who takes a pledge and him who gives it, have been specially targeted here can be understood by the occasion. All matters such as returning the pledged article without reluctance when the debt-sum is paid off, guarding it without causing any damage until then, when it is a deal carried out on mutual trust both without pledge or witness and without writing and recording a debt-bond, fulfilling the determinations in it completely, making good the article that is owed to be paid off without doing any artifices such as adding adulteration to it, are included in this. Both the Quran verses and the sayings of the Prophet reprehending breach of trust are famous, surely.

(3) Testimony must not be covered up. That is the character of those whose heart has become corrupt. It need not be said both that the word and works of one whose heart has become corrupt will become corrupt, and that he will become both an offender and a sinner. Not performing testimony when need arises, giving a spoken word of witness contrary to what is real, and so on, are all indeed concealing the testimony. Both that, as for irregularities contrary to what is just, and contrary to truth, no matter who indeed does them, no matter what indeed it may be, all of them *[p. 687]* Allah will know, and that answer will have to be given for it before Him, Allah warns at the end.
