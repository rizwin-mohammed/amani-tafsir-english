*[Verse 255]*

*[p. 603]* Because of stating about Allah's ‘Kursiyy’ in this verse, *[p. 604]* this verse is known by the name ‘Ayatul Kursiyy ( **آيَةُالكُرْسِى** )’. Many most excellent names and noble qualities of Allah are contained in it. Many hadiths can be seen that indicate both the superiority and the importance of this verse. In a hadith reported from Ubayy ibn Ka'b (r), the Prophet ﷺ has described this as the greatest ayah in the Kitab of Allah ( **اعظْم اية في كتاب الله** ) (Mu.). That, when lying down to sleep at night, in case of reciting this ayah, the ill of Satan will not befall until daybreak, has come in more than one hadith that Bukhari (r) and others have quoted. In some hadiths, it can also be seen that reciting this verse after the prayer and so on has been encouraged. That Ayatul Kursiyy is a safekeeping from the harm of Satan is indeed a generally known matter. But, by repeating it with the mouth- like a parrot- that result will not be seen. The reciting must be both while grasping the content and the idea in it, and while touching the heart.

It was stated, surely, in the 253rd verse that, even though the prophets kept on preaching along with clear proofs, mankind both divide, and, as one party true believers and one party disbelievers, keep on quarrelling with one another. Many fundamental realities that are the basis of the tawhid that all those prophets preached are contained in this verse, which comprises merely ten short sentences.

(1) There is no object of worship at all except Allah ( **اللَّهُلاإِلَهَإِلاهُوَ** ). About this sentence, which is the first and foremost watchword of Islam, nothing in particular needs to be explained here. The idea that each sentence that follows represents is such as to establish its necessity.

(2) He is both the One who is living, and the Controller of all. ( **الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ** ) Words such as ‘life-force, vitality, life’ are customarily used in many meanings. It is in relation to the classes of things that that difference becomes evident. For example: the power of growth of plants, the power of perception of living creatures, the capacity of motion, the productive power of the earth, the prosperity of a community – in all these meanings they are used. But, if the life-force- or else the life- of any thing that is a creation is examined, it can be seen that both its origin and its continuance will be from another centre that is foreign to that thing. As soon as that centre is lost, or a change happens to it, that vitality will also perish. As far as Allah, who is the Creator, is concerned, His life is entirely different from this. His life is that which is connected only with His holy Essence ( **الذات المقدسة** ). His life is not one that depends on anything else that is not that, foreign to it In other words, whichever life in any of the meanings indicated above as being of the classes of creations is taken, of all of it *[p. 605]* the actual centre of origin is Allah. As for Allah, He is self-existent. His life is not one that is in dependence on anything, or in connection with anything. Yes, just like His holy Essence indeed, it is both without beginning, and without end.

So, when saying that Allah is the One who is living, or that He is One who is alive ( **الْحَيُّ** )- just as indeed in His other distinctive qualities- it is clear from this both that it does not intend, in the ordinary meaning- a life, and that it intends life in a meaning that befits Him alone. Likewise, what is meant by the Controller of all ( **الْقَيُّومُ** ) is indeed that He is both One who subsists by Himself, and One who, controlling in an ordered way, manages all other things besides Him, and all matters concerning them. While both these two qualities are His alone, the real entitlement to be the object of worship too becomes His alone.

(3) Drowsiness or sleep will not befall Him. ( **لاتَأْخُذُهُسِنَةٌ وَلانَوْمٌ** ) Drowsiness ( **سِنَةٌ** ) is the initial state of slumber. Sleep ( **نَوْمٌ** ) is its complete state. Both will not touch Him. These two, surely, are a kind of rest from the weariness, and from the fatigue, of the inner senses. There are none of such matters to take hold of Allah. He is indeed in every respect both perfect and holy. Everyone can guess the consequence if a person who is controlling a motor vehicle nods off and falls asleep just a little. That being so, what would be the story if sleep or drowsiness befell that great power that is controlling worlds upon worlds even for the space of a moment *[DOUBT: മിടിയിട is printed (so too on the site); it is not a common word. Read as മിടി “beat, throb” + ഇട “interval”: “the space of a heartbeat, a moment”; it may be a slip for ഇമയിട (“the twinkling of an eye”).]*?!

(4) All that is in both the heavens and the earth is His. ( **لَهُمَافِيالسَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَافِيالأرْضِ** ) All, what He created. All, both in His ownership and slaves. All, subject to His intention and control and authority. If, in outward sight- for mankind or others, any nominal rights and powers and managements are seen, that too is indeed what He has given. Only what is confined within the framework of the system of law He has determined. That being so, what meaning is there in accepting those other than Him as objects of worship?!

(5) Who is there as those who make intercession with Him except according to His leave?! ( **مَنْذَاالَّذِييَشْفَعُعِنْدَهُإِلابِإِذْنِهِ** ) There is no one indeed. Except for those whom He has permitted, for the sake of those whom He permits, according to how He has permitted, no one has the ability or the right to intercede with Him. Prophets or angels, no one indeed is exempt from this. Besides what has been stated on this subject in some ayahs before this, references can be seen again below in many places too. Of the intercessions that take place in the Hereafter, the greatest intercession ( **الشَفَاعَة الكُبْرَى** ) is the intercession of the noble Prophet ﷺ. Even that will be with the conditions said above. If the hadiths that all the great men such as Bukhari, Muslim (r) have recorded concerning that *[p. 606]* mighty intercession are examined, this reality can be understood clearly.

While the creations in their entirety, gathering together in the great assembly of ‘Mahshar’, are distraught with fear in a state of uncertainty, they will request many who are leading prophets that they should intercede on their behalf before Allah to have some decision made for us in our matter. Each prophet, stating a reason each, will reply that he has no entitlement to it. At last the people will request the noble Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The noble Prophet will both fall in sujud to Allah, and offer praises and glorifications and supplications that Allah then inspires in him. Thereafter, when Allah wills, Allah will give the order ‘O Muhammad, raise your head and ask, intercede’. It will be then that the noble Prophet submits that request. Allah will set a limit for him. That the noble Prophet will not ask beyond that – such things have been clearly stated in those hadiths indeed. It is plain, surely, from this both that the intercession that is made with Allah is not intercession in the ordinary meaning, and that influence of any kind has no place in it, and that His judicial legal procedures He will not alter because of it.

(6) He knows all, both what is before them and what is behind them. ( **يَعْلَمُمَابَيْنَأَيْدِيهِمْوَمَاخَلْفَهُمْ** ) Yes, all the matters of those who are in the heavens and the earth- without difference of past, present and future time, or difference of smallness and bigness- He knows. He keeps on both seeing and hearing all, and observing, and recording.

(7) At the same time, about any matter that is within Allah's knowledge, no one will know anything minutely except what He has willed, moreover. ( **وَلايُحِيطُونَبِشَيْءٍمِنْعِلْمِهِإِلابِمَاشَاءَ** ) To the word (His knowledge), meanings are customarily given such as knowledge that is His, knowledge concerning Him, matters that He knows. Whichever of these meanings is accepted, all right, no one will know indeed except what He has willed and what He has made known. As far as man is concerned- it is he, surely, as far as is known, who is the creation given more ability to acquire knowledge- the more his knowledge expands, what happens is that the vastness of his ignorance becomes evident to him.

(8) His ‘Kursiyy’ is wide so as to contain the heavens and the earth. ( **وَسِعَكُرْسِيُّهُالسَّمَاوَاتِوَالأرْضَ** ) The literal meaning of ‘Kursiyy’ is such as ‘seat, royal seat, official sitting-place’. It is from this meaning that the chair came to have the name **كُرْسِى** (kursiyy) in the Arabic language. In what is meant by it here, different opi*[p. 607]*nions can be seen among the Quran commentators. It is said both that the 'Arsh ( **العرش** ), which is the throne of Allah, and the Kursiyy ( **الكُرْسِى** ) are one and the same indeed, and that the two are separate. Except that some riwayahs and quotations favourable to each are customarily pointed out, there are no fitting proofs. Many say that it is a word of simile indicating both Allah's ruling sovereignty, and the greatness of His authority, and the vastness of His learning. Then, in brief, the idea in this sentence would be that the heavens and the earth and all that is in them are things that stay confined both within Allah's ruling authority, and within His knowledge. But, in matters concerning Allah, when clear information is not received from the Quran, or from the sayings of the Prophet, it is not permissible for us to make inference basing it only on the linguistic meaning. Therefore, what is good is for us to be content, holding that Allah knows what the reality is both of the ‘Kursiyy, and of having said that it is wide.

(9) Keeping on safeguarding both the heavens and the earth does not become a burden to Allah ( **وَلايَئُودُهُحِفْظُهُمَا** ) As far as He is concerned, both the number and bulk, and the smallness and bigness, of things, or of facts, are not a problem. He need only determine that any matter must be. It will come to be. (36: 82) In them both, even matters of an atom's measure He will not fail to know. (34: 3.) Whatever He wills, that He will do. (11: 107.) There is also no one to question Him about what He does. (21: 23) That being so, how would both the management and the control of the heavens and the earth become a burden to Him?!

(10) It is He indeed who is both the Exalted, and the One who is great, ( **وَهُوَالْعَلِيُّالْعَظِيمُ** ) In an unconditional meaning, in a language untouched by even a hint of dependence *[DOUBT: the print has പാരമ്യത്യം (so too on the site), which is not a known word. Options: പാരതന്ത്ര്യം “dependence” (taken here, as the contrast is with “unconditional”), or പാരമ്യം “supremacy, the utmost degree”, which hardly fits “untouched by even a hint of”.]*, loftiness and greatness that are supreme and perfect belong to no one else except Allah indeed. About anyone else, all right, when it is said that he is exalted or that he is great, there is no need to think much to understand that it will be entirely both relative, and conditional.

It will be good to remember a truth. When it is stated about both the distinctive qualities, and the actions, of Allah, there is a fact that must be specially noted. That is, when both name-attributes and action-attributes *[DOUBT: നാമവിശേഷണങ്ങൾ and ക്രിയാവിശേഷണങ്ങൾ are rendered by their parts (“attributes of name”, “attributes of action”), since the list holds both descriptive words and verbs; in grammar the words mean “adjectives” and “adverbs”.]* such as ‘Allah is One who sees, is One who hears, is just, is great, He became angry, was pleased, gave, sent’ are said about Him, they all are not in the same meaning that is customarily said about creations. It is only that, both because words that specially convey the distinctive qualities that are Allah's are not in human language, and because, if it is stated about them in a language with such a special meaning, it is not possible for us to grasp it, they are stated in words familiar to us. Therefore they must be evaluated in their most perfect and most holy meaning *[p. 608]* only. Because, there is not a thing like Him. ( **لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ** ) There is not one who is equal to Him. ( **وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ** ) It is like this that Imam Ibn Kathir (r) concludes the explanation of Ayatul Kursiyy: ‘In these ayahs, and in the strong hadiths in their meaning, the best is the way of the ‘Salafussalih’ (the righteous predecessors). Without interpreting that it is in such and such a manner, or likening to creations, leave them as they have come (in the Quran and in the hadith). This is what they did.’

In case of thinking about the principles and realities contained in each of the nine sentences seen above, it can be seen that each of them is both what establishes and what confirms the first sentence, There is no object of worship except Allah ( **اللَّهُلاإِلَهَإِلاهُوَ** ). **وهوالموفق والمعين وهويهدى السبيل** *[DOUBT: the Arabic is printed without a Malayalam rendering and is transcribed as printed (وهوالموفق and وهويهدى written joined). It most likely means: “And He is the One who grants success and the Helper, and He guides to the path.”]*

*[Verses 256–257]*

*[p. 609]* Allah declares that it is not permissible to make anyone enter into religion- into Islam- by compelling. He has also pointed out the reason for it. It is that from misguidance the straight way has already become clearly evident ( **قَدْتَبَيَّنَ الرُّشْدُمِنَالْغَيِّ** ). That is, proofs, and signs, such as to discern plainly both that true faith and right guidance are such and such, and that disbelief and misguidance are such and such, exist in plenty. Therefore there is no need to compel anyone. ‘Those who wish may believe. Those who wish may also disbelieve.’ 18: 29 ( **فَمَنْشَاءَفَلْيُؤْمِنْوَمَنْشَاءَفَلْيَكْفُرْ** ) For both they have freedom. But, the experience will be both salvation in the future for those who become true believers accepting the straight *[p. 610]* path, and perpetual punishment for those who do not. In Su: Yunus 99 Allah says: **وَلَوۡ شَآءَ رَبُّكَ لَأٓمَنَ مَن فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ كُلُّهُمۡ جَمِيعًا أَفَأَنتَ تُكۡرِهُ ٱلنَّاسَ حَتَّىٰ يَكُونُواْ مُؤۡمِنِينَ** (If your Rabb had willed, those who are on the earth would all have believed. That being so, are you to compel them, for mankind to be true believers?!)

In the explanation of the sentence **لاإِكْرَاهَفِيالدِّينِ** (In religion there is no applying of compulsion), Ibn Kathir (r) states: ‘That is, you must not compel anyone to enter the religion of Islam. Because, both its evidences *[DOUBT: ലക്ഷ്യങ്ങൾ usually means “aims”; beside തെളിവുകൾ (“proofs”) it is taken in its older sense “evidences, indications”. The option is “its aims and its proofs”.]* and its proofs are clear. There is no need to compel to enter it. But, in case Allah both guides someone onto the way to Islam, and gives expansion to his heart, and gives light to his inner sight, he will enter it with clear proof indeed. Whoever's inner sight Allah gave blindness to, and on whose hearing and sight He set a seal (*), he, there is no benefit either for him in entering religion being subjected to compulsion and coercion. Although the ruling in this verse is general, they (the Quran commentators) say that the cause of its sending down is some folk from among the Ansaris. ‘Continuing, Ibn Kathir (r) has also quoted some riwayahs and scholars' opinions concerning this.

> **Note:** (*) About the import of setting a seal, it has been treated in the description of the 7th verse.

The summary of those riwayahs that have come, both what Ibn Kathir (r) quoted and what he did not, is this: ‘Some among the Ansaris, who were people of Madinah, had children who had accepted the Jewish religion or the Christian religion. Those fathers, who were Muslims, both restrained them and compelled them to accept the religion of Islam. It was on this occasion that this verse came down.’ Ibn Kathir (r) has quoted two other opinions of scholars about this verse. It is concerning the People of the Scripture that it speaks in this verse; what is intended is that, in case they give tribute to the Muslims, it is not permissible to compel them. This is one opinion. Another opinion: it is also that with the commandment of war the ruling in this has become annulled. For these opinions, or for some other opinions like these, there are no acceptable proofs to present, moreover. It is indeed the explanation that Ibn Kathir (r) adopted as his opinion and quoted first that is correct. 1stly: Whatever indeed the cause of the sending down may have been, the statement in the ayah is both general and without setting a condition. That, even if the cause of the sending down is particular indeed, the ruling of the ayahs is generally applicable, is beyond dispute. 2ndly: Religion ( **الدِّين** ) is not merely only some outward customs and practices; belief is its foundation. Using compulsion and force, it is not possible both to change belief and to cre*[p. 611]*ate it. 3rdly: There is no mutual contradiction between the commandment of war, and the commandment that there must be no compulsion in religion. As for the commandment of war, it is not for joining folk to Islam by compelling, moreover.

The verb form of the term **طَّاغُوت** (Taghut) is **طَغَى** (tagha). Its meaning is such as ‘exceeded the bounds, defied’. In disobedience ‘exceeded the bounds, for all- for all worshipped things other than Allah- that term is customarily used. *[DOUBT: the print opens a quote before അതിരുവിട്ടു, (“exceeded the bounds,”) and never closes it. The sense is most likely അതിരുവിട്ട “who exceeded the bounds”: “for all who exceeded the bounds in disobedience”.]* Intending leaders who lead mankind astray, sorcerers, soothsayers, devils, evil spirits, idols and the like too, Taghut will be said. That word will also be used both in a singular meaning and in a plural meaning ( **كما فى مفردات الراغب و فى القاموس** ) *[DOUBT: the Arabic is printed without a Malayalam rendering and is transcribed as printed. It most likely means: “As in the Mufradat of al-Raghib and in al-Qamus.”]* It can be said in general that ‘Taghut’ in Arabic is a usage like some usages in Malayalam such as ‘evil deity, despot, fiend’.

Here it is as the direct opposite side of believing in Allah that disbelieving in Taghut has been stated about. So, it can be understood that what is meant by ‘Taghut’ in this verse is all the worshipped things that are other than Allah- including Satan-. The import of having said ‘held fast to a strong handhold rope that does not snap off’ is clear. It means that those who both disbelieve in all the Taghuts and believe in Allah have already adopted the everlasting way of salvation The plural of the word **وَلِى** (waliyy) is **اَوْلِيَاء** (awliya') ‘kinsman, friend, manager of the affairs, guardian, helper, neighbour, confidant, comrade *[DOUBT: ചങ്ങാതി is rendered “comrade” to keep it apart from മിത്രം “friend” in the same list; part 42 rendered it “friend”.]*, – in many meanings such as these, as befits the context, it will be used. Its actual meaning is one who has both closeness and bond.

For those who keep on both believing in Allah and worshipping Him, Allah will be their friend, and kinsman, and custodian, and aider. Rescuing them from all darknesses such as shirk, kufr, doubt, hypocrisy, confusion of ideas, evil conduct, superstitions, He also leads them to the light of both true faith and right guidance. On the contrary, for those who disbelieve in Allah, their kinsmen, and friends, and stewards and such will all be Taghuts. It is to the very darknesses from which Allah rescues the true believers that those Taghuts will lead them. In the end, perpetual punishment of Hell too, such that there is no salvation at any time!
