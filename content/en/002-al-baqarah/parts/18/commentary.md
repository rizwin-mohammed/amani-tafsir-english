*\[Verses 120–121\]*

*\[p. 354\]* The Jews and the Christians not believing in the Prophet ﷺ, although they were People of the Scripture, is not because they have doubt about his truthfulness. Nor is it because of proof not being enough. It is because of the sole reason that the Prophet ﷺ *\[p. 355\]* does not accept the ways of practice that they have been adopting. As long as the Prophet ﷺ does not follow them, they are not going to be pleased about the Prophet ﷺ either. Therefore, Allah consoles the Prophet ﷺ that there is no need for complaint or grief at their not believing. As Qatadah (r) pointed out, when argument takes place with the misguided who set no value on truth and on proof, Allah also teaches the Prophet ﷺ a final plea for the true believers to present against them. That, indeed, is what is seen in the sentence ‘Allah's guidance is the guidance’ ( **إِنَّ هُدَى ٱللَّهِ هُوَ ٱلۡهُدَىٰ** ). That is, it is Allah who shows which is the right way. It is His guidance that I have accepted. Leaving it, I am not one to accept another guidance. You need not keep such an expectation about me either – that is the gist.

That the Prophet ﷺ will not yield to the desire, or to the self-will, not only of the People of the Scripture but of anyone at all indeed, is certain. That being so, someone may feel a doubt as to what the need is for a warning in a harsh tone like this: if you, by any chance, go and follow their desires, there will not be any protector, or helper, from Allah ( **وَلَئِنِ ٱتَّبَعۡتَ أَهۡوَآءَهُم** ). Disappointing the People of the Scripture – that you need not have the delusion that the Prophet ﷺ will submit to your desire – and the warning to the true believers that one must seriously guard against anyone, knowingly or unknowingly, getting caught in their net, too, is its import.

To the 121st verse interpretation can be given in two or three ways.

(1) If those who have received a scripture read that book through according to the proper manner, they will not fail to believe in it truly. According to this interpretation, all scriptures, including the Torah and the Quran, and their respective followers too, are included in this.

(2) If the People of the Scripture read their book through as is needed, they will indeed believe in this too – in the Quran too –. Because many predictions about the Prophet ﷺ are, surely, there in those books.

(3) Those who read through truly this book which is the Quran will not fail to believe unwaveringly in the Prophet ﷺ and in the Quran. In case the reading is other than according to its proper manner, belief will also be in name only. The differing possibilities in determining what is meant by **ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ** (book) and by the pronoun in **بِهِۦ** (in it) are the cause of these interpretations. In principle, the three are not contrary, moreover. Yet, it is to the second interpretation that preponderance is seen.

*\[p. 356\]* That it was said that the reading of the scripture should be according to the proper manner is very much worthy of attention. This is a matter that the Muslims, who have acknowledged the Quran as scripture, must especially take note of. The cause of all the declines that have been afflicting the Muslim community for centuries, and that keep increasing in them as time goes on, is not the Muslims' not reading the Quran through; it is only that it is not according to its proper manner. Firstly, the meaning and the idea must be understood. With that it does not suffice. It must be with reflecting and giving the mind to it, and also with the awareness that it is the word of Allah, and that it is the sole source of every good of man. There must be complete readiness and firmness of mind to accept its injunctions and prohibitions and its counsels and directions just as they are. The strength of heart to sacrifice all one's own interests and opinions before its direct ideas is needed too. It must be with believing in Allah's promises and holding fervour, and with guarding against His warnings and fearing. It is with deep reflection and with God-fearing reverence too that the Quran must be read through. **الموفق والله والمعين** *\[DOUBT: the Arabic is printed (p. 356) in the visual order والمعين، والله، الموفق from left to right, so that read from right to left it gives الموفق والله والمعين, transcribed so. The usual saying is والله الموفق والمعين ("And Allah is the One who grants success, and the Helper"); the printed word order may be a typesetting slip. ### Section – 15

*\[Verses 122–123\]*

*\[p. 357\]* Two verses of almost this same kind (in 47 and in 48) have gone past before. Remember here too the things stated in their interpretation. After stating many things about the Israelites, and addressing them, it is again repeatedly reminding that they should return to true faith, remembering the favours that Allah has done for them, and that, otherwise, they should be watchful about the consequences that are faced. Amidst the long statements concerning the People of the Scripture, some things were stated concerning the Arab mushriks too.

What come in the next verses are some references concerning the Prophet Ibrahim (a), who is the father of the race of both groups, the eminent prophet whom all have been honouring and revering with pride, and the great personage who embraced the most sacrifice in the history of the preaching of tawhid. The Israelites, comprising the Jews and the Christians, are the line of descendants of Ishaq (a), one son of his. The Arabs are the line of descendants of Isma'il (a), another son. What each section argues, too, is that they are ones who have been following the way of the Prophet Ibrahim (a).
