The true believers are brothers, and ones who must conduct themselves like the limbs of one single frame, surely. As Ibn Jarir (r) and others have pointed out, it is because of that that, without saying ‘do not eat the wealth of others’, it said ‘do not eat your wealth’ ( **لاتَأْكُلُواأَمْوَالَكُمْ** ). A usage like this can be seen in 4:29 too. Besides, when stating about killing, (in 4:29 indeed) it has also said, ‘Do not slay yourselves indeed ( **لاتَقْتُلُواأَنْفُسَكُمْ** )’. All this indicates indeed this principle. After saying in general that one may not take into one's hands the wealth of another through any way that is not permissible and fair, it is specially prohibiting the trying to take into one's hands the wealth of others by means of those in authority, the intention of having said ‘throw the wealth over to the judges’ may be to approach the judges in order to obtain a ruling and influence such as make it possible to take possession of another's wealth – to submit a false case before the authorities – and to give them a bribe for the said purpose. Either way, in effect it is alike indeed. It is intending the taking of wealth into one's hands that ‘eat ( **تأكُلُوا** )’ has been said. What is contained in it is a hint that that is a base trait that is merely contemptible and bestial. When speaking about practising deceit or doing wrong with another's wealth, among the Malayalis too the word ‘eat’ *[p. 480]* is wont to be used, surely.

The gist of a hadith that Umm Salamah (r) quoted as the Rasul ﷺ having said is this: ‘I am a man indeed. You will submit cases before me. Perhaps some among you may have brought proof beyond the others. And so, according to what I heard from him, I may also have ruled for him. But, if I rule for anyone anything that (in reality) belongs by right to his brother, what I rule for him will be a piece from Hell.’ (Bu; Mu.) It is according to the proof, surely, that the judge will pronounce a ruling in cases. The responsibility for the falsifications in the proofs of the one who gives proof, and the punishment that comes about because of that, will be his. The judges have no responsibility in it. Therefore, taking possession of another person's wealth in the name of the judge having ruled is wrongdoing and culpable – that is the gist. If even the state of a ruling that the noble Prophet ﷺ ruled is this, is there anything to say about the ruling of the other judges – be it even that they are ones who rule according to Islamic law indeed –?! Like the courts of today, when they are rulings that adopt, in proofs and in rulings and all indeed, legal procedures not in keeping with Islam – especially! They ought to remember that what the people who unfairly take possession of another person's things on the basis of having obtained a ruling from the courts obtain through those things is pieces of fire in Hell. Ibn 'Umar (r) has stated: ‘The Rasul ﷺ has cursed the one who gives a bribe, and the one who takes a bribe.’ (Da; Jā; Ti.) *[DOUBT: the print has ജാ (with the long ā), not ജ, the Volume 1 key's abbreviation for Ibn Majah; rendered “Jā” as printed. (A; Ba.)

### Section – 24

*[Verse 189]*

*[p. 481]* Of **هِلاَل** (Hilal), which means new moon – young moon –, the plural is **أهِلَّة** (Ahillah) . That for the word **بِرّ** the meaning merit and also the meritorious one will come was seen, surely, in the 177th verse. Allah has only said that they ask about the new moons. The detailed form of the question has not been explained. That the people asked the Prophet ﷺ about such things as the reason for the moon, when the new moon is seen, going on waning and afterwards attaining growth, and the secret contained in it, has come in some riwayahs. If this is right, what would have to be given as the reply to it would be an explanation along with quite some introductions connected with astronomy. But, as far as the Quran is concerned – even though many scientific principles and wisdoms are naturally also contained in it – explaining scientific principles is not its aim. That matter has been left to man's intellect, and to observations and experiments. In the preaching of the prophets, it is not a subject at all. Besides, as far as the general public is concerned, such explanations are also troublesome for them to grasp. The subjects of preaching, for their part, also need to be graspable for all kinds of people. Therefore, Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ to give only a reply about the subject that was asked that can be understood widely and that needs to be known from the religious point of view. ‘Say: that they are period-determinations for mankind and for the Hajj.’

That is, in matters that are worldly or religious, on all occasions when the need is faced of having to determine and know *[p. 482]* the time – such and such a moment, from such and such a time until such and such a time, and so on – these new moons are a help for determining in general, and especially for reckoning the period of the matters connected with Hajj – that is the gist. Religiously indeed, in many things other than Hajj, there are needs for time-determination, surely. As far as Hajj is concerned, its months, and days and nazhikas and all, need to be known by the Muslims of the world in general, and especially by those who go for Hajj and by the Arabs. It is keeping all this in front that Allah specially singles out for mention the matter of Hajj.

After having entered into Hajj, when it became necessary to enter houses before it ended, getting inside by climbing the wall, or through window openings in the back sides, without passing inside through the proper doors, was a practice of the Jahiliyyah. They also used to reckon this as a meritorious custom. Some, after having set out on a journey from home, even if they faced some need on the way requiring them to come home, used to do so, it is said. That this is a meaningless habit, and that there is no merit at all in it, and that whenever entering the house one must enter through the straight ways of entry indeed, and that it is in being people who have caution, obeying the injunctions and prohibitions of Allah, that there is merit, and that only by that will success and salvation be obtained – the continuing part of the ayah is what makes this known. The sentence **وَأْتُوا الْبُيُوتَمِنْ أَبْوَابِهَا** (go into houses through their doors) is also a parable-sentence in the meaning that entering into each matter must be through the ways that lead straight to each – must not be through crooked ways –.

*[Verses 190–193]*

*[p. 484]* These verses are among the Quran verses that came down at Madinah in the first stages concerning war. By means of these verses, Allah teaches the true believers many basic principles that are to be followed in the righteous wars with the enemies of Islam. The content of these can be summarised thus:-

(1). ‘Wage war, in the way of Allah, against those who come to war against you’. The intention of having said those who come to war against you is those who plan to fight the true believers, and make preparations. To lay down that one may fight that way only after they have come this way and begun war is foolishness, and suicidal, surely. Therefore, Allah commands to fight those who, having got ready to oppose this way, keep watching for an opportunity. He has also specially alerted that fighting must be, not to pay off grudges against the enemies, or for name and fame, but in the way of Allah – for the safety of the din of Allah, and for exalting His sentence *[DOUBT: വാക്യം is “sentence” elsewhere in this part; here it evidently means Allah's word (kalimah). “Word” is kept for വാക്ക്, so “sentence” is kept here.]* –.

(2) ‘Do not commit transgression. Surely, Allah will not like the transgressors.’ To get ready for war against a party who are peace-loving – who are quiet because of alliance covenants – having no intention of fighting this way; to put to death the old men, children, women and the like who are not warriors. Doing outrages such as cutting off the members of those who were killed in war – that matters such as these are ones that must not come about from the side of the true believers, and that all that are matters that Allah does not like – that is the gist. Many hadiths specially prohibiting such things can also be seen.

(3) ‘You must slay them at the place where you met them.’ As pointed out above, enemies who deserve to be fought that way must indeed be put to death, making use of the opportunity one gets. The principle contained in this is that leaving them remaining will be a calamity for the true *[p. 485]* believers and for Islam.

(4) Expel them too from the place from where they expelled you.’ Unable to stay on because of the bestial wrongs and oppressions that the Makkan mushriks – they, surely, are the chief enemy – let loose against the Prophet ﷺ and the true believers, they have run away leaving land and home, surely. Likewise, you must expel them too from there – that is the gist. With the victory of Makkah, this also came into practice. The mushriks of Makkah mostly accepted Islam, and those who stood firm in shirk indeed were expelled, and henceforth entry there was prohibited to mushriks. This prohibition is, moreover, in force from that day until today.

(5). ‘Fitnah’ – disorder – is what is more severe than killing.’ The matter of a man being killed is a severe deed indeed. But, the matter of fitnah is more terrible than that. Therefore, it is in order to do away with that more calamitous matter that He commands to fight the enemies, and to slay them – that is the import. The word ‘fitnah’ ( **الْفِتْنَةُ** ) is indeed a term to which many meanings come. ‘To melt (metals such as gold) by holding them to fire, to lead astray, madness, calamity, trial, attaining satisfaction in one's own opinion, riches and progeny, division in ideals, sin, disbelief, disgrace, punishment, oppression’ – all these meanings will come to that term according to the occasion. It can be said that it stands here in a meaning that embraces most of these meanings. This fact can also be understood more or less from the 217th verse below. The explanation of the Quran commentators about what is meant here by this word is of many kinds. If they are examined, it can be seen that the reason for it is their all giving importance each to its own aspect of fitnah. On the whole, their precis is that, when one looks at the disbelief of the mushriks, and the wrongs and oppressions that, following it, they let loose against the true faith and the true believers, the consequences that come about because of war are lighter than those.

That some people asked Ibn 'Umar (r), who did not take part in the internal wars that took place in the period of the Companions, about that, and that he gave them a reply, Bukhari (r) has quoted. In it, when the questioner asked, has not Allah said (in the 193rd verse) ‘Fight until fitnah does not come about ( **وَقَاتِلُوهُمْ حَتَّى لاتَكُونَ فِتْنَةٌ** ), he can be seen to have replied thus: ‘We have fought until fitnah ceases to be, and the religion is for Allah. You are intending to fight until fitnah comes about and the religion is other than for Allah’. In another wording, it is thus that he said: ‘Yes, in the period of the noble Rasul ﷺ we did so. At that time Islam (the Muslims) was small in number. Therefore, a man used to become subject to fitnah (to trial) in the matter of his religion. Either he would be killed, or else he would be oppressed. And so, Islam (the Mus*[p. 486]*lims) increased, then fitnah ceased to be....’

(6). ‘Until they (the enemies) fight this way near al-Masjid al-Haram, do not fight that way there.’ The intention of having said near al-Masjid al-Haram is the Makkah Haram. The information that the Prophet Ibrahim (a) built and raised up the Ka'bah edifice, and prayed in order that that land be made into a land free from fear, was told earlier, surely. From that day on, that no war or encroachments at all are allowed in the Makkah Haram, the Arabs of all periods have generally come accepting and observing. Even in the period of Jahiliyyah there was no change in that. As far as the Muslims are concerned, they are more bound to accept this principle, surely. Therefore, from its having been said that the enemies who make preparations for war must be slain wherever they are seen, the Makkah Haram is naturally excepted. But, for that reason, must the Muslims stand – with hands folded – without confronting them even if the enemies make ready for war in the precincts of the Ka'bah indeed? No. That is not needed. The Muslims are not allowed to begin war that way there. On the contrary, if the enemies start war this way there, then there is no need to withdraw. That action must be taken that way too, there indeed, and such things – Allah is making known to the true believers.

(7) ‘But, if they (the enemies) desist, Allah is One who forgives much and is the Ever-Merciful.’ Yes, if the enemies change their stand and accept the true faith, and abandon wars and oppositions, Allah will give them pardon. He will not take action against them in the name of the wrongs done before.

(8). ‘Wage war with them until fitnah does not come about, and the din becomes for Allah’. Or, the two things that need to be achieved through the actions of war are fitnah ceasing to be, and religion being for Allah. That is to say, whenever the situation comes about in which disorders like those stated above come to an end, and attacks and interferences against Islam, which is the religion of Allah, cease to be, and it becomes such that it can move about freely in the world with dignity, until then the need for war will be in force – that is the import. Other than that, the meaning of this is not that war must be waged until all mankind become Muslims, without shirk and kufr remaining anywhere in the world. In Islam there is, moreover, no such ruling. That there is no objection to doing good to, and duly observing justice towards, those who do not wage war against you in the matter of religion or banish you from your dwelling places, and that there is objection only to your showing friendship to those who wage war against you in the matter of religion, and banish you from your dwelling places, and give backing to that – Allah has made clear. (See Su: Mumtahinah 8,9)

(9). ‘If they desist, then except against the perpetrators of wrong, transgression *[p. 487]* is not allowed. ‘That is, if the enemies discontinue their wrongs and oppressions and encroachments, there must not be any war or attack at all against them from the side of the Muslims. If it comes about, that will be wrongdoing and injustice. On the ground that they were wrongdoers before, it is not allowed to take any such actions against them now – that is the gist. That it is intending the taking of actions in that same manner that way too, in return for the transgressions that the wrongdoers adopt this way, that transgression ( **عُدْوَانَ** ) has been said here, can be understood from the next verse. Even though in reality this is not transgression, outwardly it is equal to transgression, surely.

*[p. 488]* **حَرَام، حُرمة** (Haram, Hurmah) are words that embrace meanings such as inviolability, sacredness, venerability, forbiddenness. That it said sacred month ( **ٱلشَّهۡرُ ٱلۡحَرَامُ** ) is intending the sacred months in which war is forbidden, and that it said sacred things ( **حُرُمَاتُ** ) is intending all sacred things that must not be disrespected. The three consecutive months named Dhul-Qa'dah, Muharram, and the month of Rajab as well – four months – are the months in which war has been prohibited. *[TN: Only two names are printed for the “three consecutive months”. The next paragraph shows that the three are Dhul-Hijjah (ദുൽഹിജ്ജഃ) with one month before it and one after it.]* Places of worship, public institutions, holy days, holy places and suchlike are all included in sacred things too.

It is in order that the people who come for Hajj from various parts may, without any fear and apprehension, fulfil the Hajj rites at ease and go back that the first three months have been made sacred months consecutively. Though the main rites of Hajj take place in Dhul-Hijjah, taking into account the convenience and so on of those who come from distant regions and of those who return, one month each before and after it has been added. The month of Rajab too has been made sacred, such that it may be convenient for those who wish to do the 'umrah rite in between to perform it and go. That in these months everyone must be in peace, without wars and attacks, is a principle that had come to be generally accepted among the Arabs. As can be seen in the 217th verse, that is a law in force in Islam too. But, in the period of Jahiliyyah the Arab mushriks had been adopting a habit of, if they found war to be very necessary in any one of the said months, waging war in that month indeed, and observing another month as a sacred month in place of it. (About this it is stated in Su: Taubah 37)

That, when the enemies make ready for war this way in the Haram, war must be waged against them there indeed, was stated in the previous verse, surely. Likewise, that, if they come to war disrespecting these sacred months, then the Muslims too must clash with them without holding back because of the sacredness of those months, and that thus they must adopt action in the same manner that way too against that transgression – this Allah commands the true believers in this verse. Not only that, Allah also makes known a general principle: that in any wrongs that they carry out disregarding any other sacred things or facts that are regarded as inviolable and venerable, it is necessary to do retribution that way too in the very same way. If this principle is not adopted – if the Muslims stand aside on the ground that we may not do so as the enemies do – its consequence need not be told, surely. But, He has also specially alerted that in this retributive action too, any lapses occurring from their side need to be specially guarded against, and that Allah will be on the side of those who guard so.

*[Verse 195]*

*[p. 489]* Some Companions who were Ansar, who had been helping Islam and the Muslims by giving land, and home, and riches – when Islam had attained glory and strength – said privately to one another that henceforth there is no need for us to spend as much indeed as before, and that we may try to make our property and wealth and so on prosper. That it was following that that this verse came down has come in a hadith of Abu Ayyub al-Ansari (r). (Mu; Ja; Na; Ti.) Even if the occasion of the sending down was that indeed, there is no doubt in the content of the ayah being generally applicable. That they must spend as much wealth as they can in the way of Allah, and that they must guard against giving occasion to fall into the pit of ruin by taking to stinginess – Allah commands the true believers in this verse. Alerting that they must do the things they do well, and sincerely, and beautifully, and that Allah will be on the side of those who do so – it is to them that His mercy and help will come –, He also fills them with zeal for it.

In having said **فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ** (in the way of Allah), all the subjects that Allah likes will be included. Matters necessary for the survival, prosperity and growth of Islam, struggles with the enemies, and suchlike stand at the forefront of them. When one remembers that it is following the explaining in the previous verses of many things concerning waging war with the enemies that in this verse He commands to spend in the way of Allah, it can be thought that it is this last-mentioned matter that is chiefly intended here. The sentence do not throw your hands into ruin ( **وَلاتُلْقُوابِأَيْدِيكُمْ إِلَى التَّهْلُكَةِ** ) points its finger to a fact that the true believers need to take to heart very serious*[p. 490]*ly. That if you do not come forward to spend wealth in subjects like those indicated above – especially in matters of struggle connected with religion –, that may become a cause for the strength of your enemies to increase, and for your future to be endangered; therefore, do not give occasion for that – this is a warning to that effect.

Some commentators have pointed out that there is an indication in that sentence not only that not spending in needful subjects may be a cause of your ruin, and that you must guard against that, but also that if you spend wealth in an unneeded subject there is a chance of calamity befalling you, and that you must not give occasion for that. When the range of meaning of that sentence is looked at, this is, moreover, right. Just as it is wont to be said, ‘Do not put your hand into calamity’ and ‘Do not borrow hard times’, this sentence, ‘Do not throw your hand into ruin’, too is a figurative usage.
