### Explanatory Note – 1

### The Story of the Prophet Adam (a) and of Iblis – Is It Only an Illustration?

The story of the Prophet Adam (a) and of Iblis is one that has been repeated in the Quran many times in clear language. But for those who keep some special purposes in their hearts, the mind will not come round, surely, to refraining from interpreting according to their own liking even a subject that the Quran has stated, making it however clear. Things and facts that are contrary to the ordinary, or that are beyond the five senses – these people are not able to concede them merely because the Quran or hadith has stated them – without getting the permission of their science and reason. Therefore these people often get into difficulty. This story too is one among those. What these people argue is that this is not a story that happened in reality, and that it is only an illustration that illustrates human life. For this, these people have had to make some misinterpretations that are mutually contradictory and childish.

Among these, the summary of what some who are counted as great progressives state here is this: 'Man is very excellent and lofty. *\[p. 238\]* He is one created with a disposition for good only (\*). If he wishes, he can make this world paradise-like; he can make it hell. All aspects of the punishment of Hell can be experienced here. He can also try to subdue and rule this universe. All things are subject to him. All must bow their heads to him... But he has another nature: because he is a progressive, if someone points out something to him, he quickly becomes subject to it; he will not think about the defects. In the end he will regret. It is this nature that is pointed out in this story... When he is lolling in an ocean of bliss, many things will occur to him. Sometimes he will eat a little something that is not wholesome. At once, his manner changing, his head will spin, he will vomit, he will babble nonsense, his clothes will come off, his nakedness will be exposed. When all is over and his senses return, he will lower his head in shame. The one whose cloth has slipped off will pluck some leaves and hold them covering himself. In this way, in many ways, he will use many means to cover up his deficiency. What is the result? In this way the story of Adam and Iblis is one that has illustrated human life. This is the nature of their description. Although in this account they have not stated decisively that this story is merely an imaginary story, it need not be said that this description is against the background that this is simply not a real event. This reality can also be seen more clearly from some of their arguments that are seen below.

> **Note:** (\*) Some things in this, beginning with the argument that what man has is a disposition for good only, are all open to criticism. Since the context is a different one, it is not touched on separately here.

It was said, surely, that this event has been stated repeatedly in many places in the Quran. It is in Su. Al-A'raf: 11–27 that it has been explained in more detail. That it is told repeatedly like this is precisely because there are some lessons we have to learn from it, no doubt. Although some aspects of the lessons of the story quoted above that these people pointed out are acceptable, it cannot be left unsaid that some other aspects in it are ones that do not fit the teachings of Islam and of the Quran. Leaving out the parts that have a materialist aim and that establish capacities that man does not have, we too may accept the remaining part in general as a lesson of the story. Let that be; Allah Himself has pointed out in the Quran, in many places, the lessons we have to learn from this story. After narrating this story in detail, He says thus in Su. Al-A'raf: **يَٰبَنِيٓ ءَادَمَ لَا يَفۡتِنَنَّكُمُ ٱلشَّيۡطَٰنُ كَمَآ أَخۡرَجَ أَبَوَيۡكُم مِّنَ ٱلۡجَنَّةِ** – **الاعراف:٢٧** (Children of Adam! Just as Satan expelled your parents from Paradise, let him not throw you into trouble).

After narrating this story briefly, He says thus in Surat al-Kahf: **أَفَتَتَّخِذُونَهُۥ وَذُرِّيَّتَهُۥٓ أَوۡلِيَآءَ مِن دُونِي وَهُمۡ لَكُمۡ عَدُوُّۢ** – **الكهف : ٥٠** (That being so, will you, leaving Me, *\[p. 239\]* make him – Iblis – and his offspring your patrons? They, for their part, are enemies to you!) Examples like this can be seen elsewhere too. The main lessons to be learned from this story are clear to us from those Quranic verses themselves.

But, for the sake of the lessons mentioned above, to concede that all that has been stated in the ayahs in clear language – in language not mixed with any impression of figures of speech – such as 'Adam (a), Adam's wife (Hawwa'), Iblis (Satan), sujud, Paradise, the tree, the exposure of nakedness, covering nakedness taking leaves', is imagination and figurative usages, no one at all who truly believes in Allah and in the Quran will have the courage. That would in reality be an arrangement that makes the Quran a story or a novel. While it describes it repeatedly in completely clear language, and states many other matters too, connecting them with it on the basis that it is a real event, to give an interpretation nevertheless taking it to be only a parable is certainly an enormous encroachment against the Quran. One has to fear whether this will not be included in what Allah stated about the People of the Scripture: **يُحَرِّفُونَ ٱلۡكَلِمَ عَن مَّوَاضِعِهِۦ** (they shift the words in the scripture from their places)!

In the Torah (in the Bible) the account of commanding to do sujud to the Prophet Adam (a) is not seen. But the event of eating from the tree and so on can be seen. Whatever portion agreeing with the Quran is seen in the earlier scriptures, it is not proper to reject it. Nor is it proper to accept any portion contrary to the Quran. On this matter there is no dispute among Muslims. But in this narration of the story, some of the words of the Torah agree completely with the sentences of the Quran, and some do not agree. That being so, what these people do is to throw out what is in the Torah on this subject, saying it is entirely a fabricated tale, and to imply that it is from it that the occasion arose for Muslims to go on thinking that this is a real event. This is not at all right. At the same time as they are not able to accept, in its actual form, what has been stated clearly alike in the Quran and in the Torah, these people take and quote, from those very words of the Torah, some portions that are seen to be a little beneficial to their argument, and accept them as proof.

We have no need to investigate which the tree was that was forbidden to the Prophet Adam (a) in Paradise. Even so, it is certain that many Quran commentators will be found to have given opinions about which it was. Among them there are those who said 'paddy, wheat, grape' and so on. The word the Quran used about this is **شَجَرَةْ** (shajarah). 'Tree, *\[p. 240\]* tree' and so on are the meanings of this word. *\[TN: the two Malayalam words are വൃക്ഷം and മരം, both meaning "tree".\]* Although this word is used in Arabic for small plants too, it is usually used for a tree that stands on a trunk. Holding up this fact, these people can be seen ridiculing the holders of the opinion indicated above, saying 'these people did not take into account even the word that the Quran used', and that word (shajarah) is not used in Arabic for plants and creepers', and so on. (\*) At the same time, these people prate that what is meant here by tree (shajarah) is 'evil. If one may say 'tree' for 'evil' and 'ate from the tree' **فَأَكَلَا مِنۡهَا** for 'did evil', why can it not be said 'tree' for paddy and for grape?

> **Note:** (\*) The meaning that the Qamus (**القاموس**) gives for this word is like this: **ما قام على ساق او ما سما بنفسه دق او جل قاوم الشتاء او عجز عنه** (means what stands on a trunk. Or else, means what rises up of itself – whether it is very small or big; whether it withstands the winter season or is unable to).

The proofs these people bring to establish that what is meant here by **شَجَرَةْ** (tree) is 'evil are stranger still.

(1) In Su: Al-A'raf 19, it is following the specific mention of Satan's disobedience and defiance that **وَلَا تَقۡرَبَا هَٰذِهِ ٱلشَّجَرَةَ** (do not approach this tree) has been said.

(2) In the Torah (in Genesis 2:17), this tree has been qualified as 'the tree of knowledge about good and evil'.

(3) In the Quran (14:24–26), good and evil have each been likened to a tree. These, if you please, are the proofs for this!

By saying 'do not approach this tree' (**وَلَا تَقۡرَبَا هَٰذِهِ ٱلشَّجَرَةَ**) following what was stated about the nature of Satan, it is not, surely, that the purport of the word 'shajarah' (tree) must change into 'evil'. Not only that: in Ta-Ha 121, without saying that the two of them (Adam and Hawwa' approached that tree, what was said is that they ate from it (**فَأَكَلَا مِنۡهَا**). It becomes clear from this that that shajarah was in reality a tree itself, and that there was on it fruit or something else fit for eating. In Surat al-A'raf Allah says: **وَيَـٰٓـَٔادَمُ ٱسۡكُنۡ أَنتَ وَزَوۡجُكَ ٱلۡجَنَّةَ فَكُلَا مِنۡ حَيۡثُ شِئۡتُمَا وَلَا تَقۡرَبَا هَٰذِهِ ٱلشَّجَرَةَ فَتَكُونَا مِنَ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ** – **الأعراف** – (O Adam! You and your wife dwell in Paradise. Then eat from wherever you wish – do not approach this tree. In that case, you will fall among the wrongdoers). *\[p. 241\]* First, after saying that they may eat from wherever they like, it is pointing to one tree that He commands: do not go near this tree. Then, the purport of saying 'do not approach the tree' must be, surely, that they must not eat from it (\*). It is precisely for that reason, too, that, when speaking about violating that command, Allah said, without saying 'they approached it' (**تقربا**) or something else, 'they ate from it' (**فَأَكَلَا مِنۡهَا**) itself. If the purport of saying 'do not approach the tree' were 'do not go near evil', what should have been said is 'they approached it'. Even if the ayahs from Ta-Ha 116 to 121 are read through calmly, this true meaning will become clear.

> **Note:** (\*) Those who have acquaintance with the rules of the literary science (**عِلْمُ الْبَلَاغَة**) in the Arabic language can understand this quickly.

Now, let us look at the name given to that tree in the Torah. What was said, surely, is 'the tree of knowledge about good and evil'. Not 'the tree of evil' or 'the tree of good'. If the meaning of 'tree' is to be changed on the basis of this usage, what should have been said is 'good and evil'. Their choosing only 'evil' from it was never just. As can be seen in Ta-Ha 120, Iblis said about that tree, surely, **شَجرَةُ ٱلخُلْدْ** (the tree of permanent dwelling). *\[TN: the Arabic is printed شَجرَةُ ٱلخُلْدْ (no fatha is printed on the ج), as a cited phrase; in Ta-Ha 120 the words read شَجَرَةِ ٱلۡخُلۡدِ.\]* Since the Quran has quoted this, these people could have said that what is meant by 'tree' is 'permanent dwelling'! That would have been better than that.

The third proof is, surely, that in the Quran (14:24–26) good and evil were likened to a tree. Yes, in Su. Ibrahim, Allah has said **وَمَثَلُ كَلِمَةٍ خَبِيثَةٖ كَشَجَرَةٍ خَبِيثَةٍ** الخ (The parable of a bad word is like a bad tree...). Before that, Allah has also said **مَثَلٗا كَلِمَةٗ طَيِّبَةٗ كَشَجَرَةٖ طَيِّبَةٍ** الخ (The parable of a good word is like a good tree...). In these two verses too, what Allah says is not that good or else evil is a tree, or that the tree is good or else evil. He has only likened good and bad words to certain special kinds of trees (of the kind described further in the ayah). That it is a parable has also been stated clearly in it itself. In Su. Al-Kahf 45 Allah has said, surely: 'The parable of the worldly life is like water (rain water).' Many other parables like this can also be seen. For this reason, will anyone say 'the worldly life means water, or the reverse? In Quran 29:41 He has likened the worshippers of many gods to the spider. Because of this, no one will say, surely, 'the worshippers of many gods are a spider' or the reverse.

The strongest proofs that these people brought in order to make out that the story of the Prophet Adam and of Iblis is an imaginary story told merely as a parable *\[p. 242\]* are what was seen above. We saw that they are childish. This story is one that has been presented repeatedly in the Quran, and for which many ayahs have been employed. In the Quran and in the hadiths, many other matters too are stated on the footing that this is a real event. But since there is not seen, on any one occasion in them, any indication showing that this event is only a parable – if there were, these people would have taken it up and held it aloft, surely – this parable theory is not acceptable at all to anyone who is a true believer. In reality, the reasons that prompt these people to bring out this parable theory are some other ones.

That is: 'Iblis' is said not as any particular being, but meaning a kind of bad nature – or else a corrupt disposition; 'the jinn and Satan' (Shaitan) and all are certain special sections among human beings themselves, that is, not any creatures beyond our sight – it is in reality from some new beliefs and modern arguments of theirs such as these that such statements come out. Like this, in many other matters too, which can be clearly understood from the Quran and from hadith, and which Muslims in general have been believing and accepting, new opinions of theirs can be seen. That being so, often there is no way out for them, surely, except to take refuge in interpretations of this sort. (About Iblis, the jinn, Satan and the like, it will be explained in the explanatory note given at the end of Su. Al-Hijr. **إن شاء الله**

May Allah include us among the good people who believe in real truths. **آمين**

What was said as 'what is with you' means their scripture, and what was said as 'what I have sent down making it true' means the Quran. 'Ayahs (**الآيَات**)' means, literally, 'signs, proofs, evidences *\[DOUBT: ലക്ഷ്യങ്ങൾ: "evidences" (the older sense of ലക്ഷ്യം) or "aims"; the same question as in parts 01–02.\]*, marks and so on. The singular is 'ayah (**آيَة**). In the Quran this word is mostly used *\[p. 246\]* for four kinds of things.

1\) Cosmic and natural signs (**الآيَات الْكَونِيَة**): rain, wind, the sun, the moon and the like are included in this.

2\) Extraordinary events that usually appear through the prophets (**خَوَارِقُ الْعَادَات**) and superhuman events (**المعجزات**).

3\) Scriptural sentences, including the verses of the Quran.

4\) Historical events.

All of these, surely, are marks and proofs for understanding the truth in one way or another.

The sentence 'Do not take a small price for My ayahs (**وَلَا تَشۡتَرُواْ بِـَٔايَٰتِي ثَمَنٗا قَلِيلٗا**) contains a rather broad idea. The gist of it is that one must not, disregarding Allah's injunctions and prohibitions, commands and directions, and evidences and signs *\[DOUBT: ലക്ഷ്യദൃഷ്ടാന്തങ്ങൾ: "evidences and signs" or "aims and signs"; the same question as ലക്ഷ്യ ദൃഷ്ടാന്തങ്ങൾ in part 01.\]*, accept any worldly gains in place of them. Taking bribes and customary perquisites in religious matters and in matters of justice; violating religious ordinances or concealing the truth because of reasons such as the love of leadership, the fear of loss of position, and selfish motive; concealing religious laws and making capital out of it – all such things are included in this. Compared with Allah's 'ayahs, all of them, surely, are of little value and worthless. (**قُلۡ مَتَٰعُ ٱلدُّنۡيَا قَلِيلٞ** – **النساء:٧٧**) (Say: the resources of this world are paltry.) The reason for saying this addressing the Israelites is that evil practices like those mentioned above were widespread among them, and that their not accepting true faith was because of such worldly selfish interests. It need not be thought that this sentence is addressed to them and is not applicable to Muslims. Abu Hurairah (r) narrates thus, as said by the Prophet ﷺ: 'A person learned a knowledge by which Allah's pleasure ought to be sought; he did not learn it except to obtain through it some resource from this world; then, on the Day of Qiyamah, he will not smell the scent of Paradise, indeed." (Da.) *\[DOUBT: the abbreviation ദാ (Da.) is not expanded in the book; it is most likely Abu Dawud. Please confirm.\]*

Knowing some matters that Ibn Kathir (r) stated, quoting this hadith, will be fitting to the context. If he does not receive it from the Bait al-Mal, and it becomes impossible for him to do other work because of the occupation of teaching, there is no objection to taking a reward.

*\[p. 247\]* Remembering the favours and fulfilling the pact are, surely, the duty of everyone. In that there is no difference of scholars and those who are not. The matter of taking a paltry price for the ayahs would affect mainly the scholars. In that situation – as Baidawi (r) pointed out – that it was said distinctively, at the end of the first verse, 'Fear Me (**وَإِيَّـٰيَ فَٱرۡهَبُونِ**)', and at the end of the second, 'Guard against Me (**وَإِيَّـٰيَ فَٱتَّقُونِ**), is especially noteworthy.
