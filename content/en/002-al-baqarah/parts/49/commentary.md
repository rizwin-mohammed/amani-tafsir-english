*[Verses 278–281]*

*[p. 673]* In the 1st verse, first, He has alerted on the whole that one must guard against Him, observing Allah's injunctions and prohibitions. Then, He commands both that you must not recover henceforth any of what remains to be collected as determined in the interest deals that you have carried out before, and that you must indeed abandon it just as it is. That for no one who claims that he is a true believer is there a way not to obey this command is indeed the import of the word ‘if you are true believers’.

Some riwayahs that have come from Zaid ibn Aslam, Ibn Jurayj, Muqatil, Suddi (r) show that this verse came down in the time near to the conquest of Makkah having come about. The gist of those riwayahs is in this manner: to some among the Banu 'Amr families belonging to the Thaqif tribe, a little interest remained to be collected from some members of the Mughirah family, who were of the Makhzum tribe. After all had acknowledged Islam, the Thaqif people demanded that sum which was due to them. After the Mughirah families held consultation among themselves, they gave the reply that with the earnings that come about while in Islam we are not ready now to pay up interest. This information 'Attab ( **عَتَاب ابن اسيد رضـ** ), whom the Prophet ﷺ had appointed as the Amir in Makkah, wrote to the noble Prophet. It was on this occasion that this verse came down. The noble Prophet also sent a reply, making this verse known. Then, the Thaqif people; also said that we both return to Allah in regret, and leave off the remaining interest.

That giving back the interest-sum taken in before the command making interest forbidden came is not obligatory becomes clear from this verse. It is, surely, only that what remains to be got must be abandoned that Allah has said. Although giving back all that was taken before as interest will be good, in case it is made obligatory, it may often come about that it is not possible to practise it. Not only that, since, when receiving it, there was no legal impediment at all in it, there is no justification either for compelling to give it back, surely.

In the 2nd verse, He warns strongly that, in case of not doing so, Allah and the Rasul will declare war against those who are not ready for it. He has also made known that, if one returns in regret about what has been done, the capital- the sum lent- will get saved. The gist of having said ‘You will neither wrong, nor be wron*[p. 674]*ged’ is indeed that He will not allow either doing wrong by taking more than the capital, or becoming subject to wrong by remitting anything from it. That those against whom Allah and the Rasul declare war, they, will be subject in the Hereafter both to Allah's curse and wrath and to punishment, and that they will be the open enemies of Islam, is clear.

The import of Allah and the Rasul having declared war on those who do not turn away and return from interest can be seen interpreted in more than one manner. The summary of the said opinions is this:

(1) That is a heavy warning both that, because they are ones who do not submit to Allah's law, they become the opposing side of both Allah and the Rasul, and that they will be subject to Allah's severe wrath. But, in front of the words in the ayah, no relevance is seen for this opinion.

(2) Both public indignation will arise against them, and struggles and clashes will come about with them. This opinion, except that it can be included in the scope of meaning of having said in the previous verse **يَمْحَقُ اللَّهُ الرِّبوا** (Allah will wipe out interest), no justification is seen for its being the intention of this declaration. Some modern scholars who accept this opinion are accustomed to portray the ‘capitalist- labourer’ clashes that are seen everywhere in these times, and the tugs-of-war that are rampant between the rich class and the poor class, as the realisation of this declaration of war. In fact, if the said clashes and tugs-of-war are examined, it can be seen that, for the most part, it is not only matters connected with interest- it is many other things too- that are the reason for it.

(3) In case they do not return even though they have been asked to desist and return from the interest deal, the ruler must declare war on them. If they are single individuals, they will also be subject to the punishment of killing. This is another opinion. It is clear that only where Islamic rule is in existence is there a way to put this into effect. That Abu Bakr (r) declared war on the people who refused to give Zakat is historically famous, surely. The Islamic government has to suppress those too who carry out injuries to the public such as robbery, killing. This too is a measure like that. This indeed is the opinion of most Quran commentators. More proper, and seen to agree more with the words in the ayah, is indeed this. Many have also pointed out here both that, in the incident quoted above as having taken place between the Thaqif tribesmen and the Makhzum tribesmen, in case the Thaqif tribe was not ready to repent, the Prophet ﷺ had made known in the letter he wrote to 'Attab (r) to wage war against them, and that the Thaqif tribesmen said ‘We have no ability to clash with Allah and with the Rasul’. When Islamic reign is not in exis*[p. 675]*tence, the Muslim community ought to show their protest towards them. From having said ‘If you repent and return, you will obtain your capital. ( **وان تبتمفلكم رؤس اموالكم** )’ it follows that, in case they have to be made to return by applying force, without returning on their own, even their capital need not be given to them. Many scholars have opined so, moreover.

In the 3rd verse, He commands both that, if straitness affects the debtor in such a way that he cannot repay the debt, one must give him exemption until the convenience for that comes about, and that one must not put him to difficulty until then. This is a command that affects all debtors. It is also a duty to which every Muslim must pay special attention. In case Allah charges that the debt-sum must be remitted entirely, it will come about, surely, that nobody is ready to help those who have difficulty by lending. But, He has also specially reminded both that making it charity and remitting it is the most excellent for you, and that, if you understand the matter as is needed, that is what you ought to do. It need not be said that, if one does not remit the whole, if one remits at least quite some part of it, that too is very good. That it has been very greatly emphasised in many hadiths concerning the pouring *[DOUBT: the print has പെയ്യുന്നത് ("pouring", as of rain), so "the pouring of relaxations" may be figurative ("showering"); it may also be a misprint for ചെയ്യുന്നത് ("doing", that is, granting relaxations). Both readings give the same sense here.]* of relaxations such as extending the term for settling the debt for those whom straitness has affected, unable to repay a debt, remitting the sum, can be seen. Quoting some only for example:-

(1) The summary of an incident that Muhammad ibn Ka'b ( **القرظى رضـ** ) quoted:- When Abu Qatadah (r) went seeking a debtor of his, the debtor took to hiding without coming outside. Once they met each other. That I am a man of straitness, I have no ability to pay up the debt, he made known. Then Abu Qatadah (r) asked: ‘By Allah, truly, is it yes?’ The debtor answered: ‘By Allah, truth! Yes.’ Then Abu Qatadah (r), while weeping, said thus: ‘If it is a joy to anyone that Allah saves him from the distresses of the Day of Qiyamah, let him either give relief to the man of straitness, or remit to him.’ I have heard the Rasul ﷺ saying so. (A; Mu.)

(2) The Prophet ﷺ says: ‘If anyone either grants a postponement to a man of straitness, or remits to him, on that day when there is no other shade except the shade of Allah, Allah will give him shade in His shade.’ (A; Mu.)

(3) Abu Sa'id (r) says: ‘In the time of the Prophet ﷺ, a calamity affected the fruits that a man had bought. Thus, his debt increased. Then the Prophet ﷺ told the people to give him charity. The people gave charity. But it was not enough to settle his debt. Then the Prophet ﷺ said to his debt-dealers: ‘Take what you have got. Other than that there is nothing for you.’ (Mu.) Both that there is need for all to help those who have no abi*[p. 676]*lity to repay a debt, and that there is need to make as much effort as can be made to settle the debt, and that, when there is no way at all to pay off, the ruler must intervene and exempt the debtor from the debt-liability, can be understood from this.

There is another side too concerning debtors. In these times especially, very great importance has to be attached to this side. After taking a debt, many are accustomed to try to get by without paying off the debt, using some tricks, or justifications, or false proofs. About this too the Prophet ﷺ can be seen to have warned in many hadiths. For example:

(1) The rich man's causing delay (without settling the debt) is wrong. If one of you is handed over, joined to one who has ability, let him follow him.’ (Bu; Mu.) That is, the meaning is that, if the real debtor both hands over his debt to another person, and the person handed over to is a person who has ability, that handing-over must be accepted’

(2) ‘If anyone, intending to repay it, acquires as (debt) the wealth of the people, Allah will repay it for him. If anyone acquires it intending to destroy it (without giving), He will also destroy it’ (Bu.)

(3) ‘The excellent one among the people is he who, among them, repays debt well.’ (Mu.)

(4) ‘If he who has causes delay (without settling the debt), both touching his esteem and punishing (him) are permissible.’ (Da; Na.) That one may both speak harshly to him, and, if it becomes needed, put him in bonds – that is the gist.

(5) Abu Qatadah (r) says: A man asked: ‘O Rasul of Allah, have you seen? (You must tell this): while I am being one who has patience, and one who intends Allah's recompense, and one who goes forward without turning away, if I have been killed in the way of Allah (in war), will Allah pardon my sin for me?’ Then the noble Prophet said ‘Yes’. Then, when the man turned and went, calling him, he said thus: ‘Yes, except debt. (Allah will not forgive debt.) Jibril has told me so.’ (Mu.)

In the 4th verse, this life is temporary, the everlasting life of the Hereafter is yet to come, it is there that befitting recompense will be given for all deeds, therefore you must take the precautions needed for that day, such and so on Allah calls to the minds of the true believers. Both that this verse is the Quran verse that came down last, and that afterwards the Prophet ﷺ had lived only a few days, has come in some riwayahs. **اللّه اعلم**
