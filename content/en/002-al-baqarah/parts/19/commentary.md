*\[p. 358\]* Which were the words – commandments – by which Allah put Ibrahim (a) to trial? Quoting a few riwayahs which show that they were some practices of Islam relating to observance, that they were some things such as the command to offer Isma'il (a) in sacrifice, that they were sacrificing endeavours for the fulfilment of the mission of tawhid, and so on, Imam Ibn Jarir (r) says: ‘What is meant by those words may be all of these together. It may also be some of them. Without a hadith or the agreed opinion of the scholars, nothing may be said definitely. It is the opinion that all may be included that Ibn Kathir (r) has endorsed.

Whichever the said commandments were, it is reasonable that fulfilling those commandments completely was something not possible for ordinary persons, and something that made him excellent such that the leadership of mankind would be given to him. It is as **وَإِبۡرَٰهِيمَ ٱلَّذِي وَفَّىٰٓ** (Ibrahim, who discharged the duties completely 53:37) that Allah has described him in one place. Then – as some are usually seen to have stated – it has to be considered that they will not be only some things like cutting the nails, trimming the moustache, performing circumcision. When we consider, keeping before us the history of that great man who was an eminent prophet, and the matters stated about him in many places in the Quran, we can more or less infer about the gravity of *\[p. 359\]* those commandments that put him to trial (\*)

**إمَام** (imam) means one who is followed – leader –. In whatever matter he is followed, in that matter he will be the foremost one and an example to others. As far as the Prophet Ibrahim (a) is concerned, it need not be said that each link of his life was an example to mankind. That is why, too, all the prophets after him and their communities have been ordered to follow his example. Each community – whether wrong or right – saying with pride that they are ones who follow his example, too. It can be seen stated about this in many places in the Quran .(See the next verses 130 and 135, and verses such as Nisa' 125, An'am 161, Nahl 123, Hajj 78) In Surah Al Imran Allah says: **إِنَّ أَوۡلَى ٱلنَّاسِ بِإِبۡرَٰهِيمَ لَلَّذِينَ ٱتَّبَعُوهُ وَهَٰذَا ٱلنَّبِيُّ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ** – **آل عمران: ٦٨** (Among mankind, those most closely connected with Ibrahim are those who followed him, and this Prophet, and those who have believed)

When Allah made known that He was making him a leader, he prayed that that leadership should remain in his progeny too. Other than leadership in preaching and propagating Allah's tawhid and messages, a material position of leadership was not, surely, his aim. Therefore he wished that that blessed position should continue in his line of descendants too. As far as the prophets are concerned, their tawhid preaching receiving acceptance among the people, and there being, even after their time, persons among their own kin who carry on that preaching, will be their boundless wish. That is why, surely, the Prophet Zakariyya (a), when he came to be without children although he had reached old age, said thus in his supplication: ‘....I fear about the families that will come after me. My wife is barren, too. Therefore, grant me from Your presence a kinsman who will become heir to me and to the family of Ya'qub ! Rabb, make him an approved person, too ! **وَإِنِّي خِفْتُ الْمَوَالِيَ مِنْ وَرَائِي** (Maryam 5)

When Allah gave the divine mission (risalah) to the Prophet Musa (a): Give me a helper from my own kin ( **وَٱجۡعَل لِّي وَزِيرٗا مِّنۡ أَهۡلِي** ) (Taha 29) – thus he too prayed. In that way the Prophet Ibrahim (a) prayed that the exemplary leadership in the preaching of tawhid should be maintained after him in his progeny too. Allah has also accepted that supplication. Allah says: **وَجَعَلۡنَا فِي ذُرِّيَّتِهِ ٱلنُّبُوَّةَ وَٱلۡكِتَٰبَ** *\[p. 360\]* –**العنكبوت: ٢٧** (In his progeny We also established prophethood and the scripture).

But Allah made him aware of a reality beforehand indeed: My pact will not be applicable to the wrongdoers ( **لا يَنَالُ عَهْدِي الظَّالِمِينَ** ) – that was it. This is an intimation that his progeny will not all be good, and that there will be wrongdoers too among them. Other than that, it is not dislike or protest towards him. It is, surely, through the prophets Isma'il and Ishaq (a), his two sons, that his line of descendants continues. Among the progeny of both of them there are well-doers and wrongdoers. ( **وَمِنْ ذُرِّيَّتِهِمَا مُحْسِنٌ وَظَالِمٌ لِنَفْسِهِ مُبِينٌ**... **الصافات:١١٣** ). Allah indeed has stated so. From this sentence it can also be understood that what is meant by wrongdoers is those who are not ones who do well. That some great men, basing themselves on Allah's having said ‘My pact is not applicable to the wrongdoers.’, have stated that wrongdoers have no entitlement to become khalifah, or judge, or ruler, or mufti (one who gives religious rulings) witness, or narrator, is indeed worth stating.

*\[p. 361\]* ‘Baitun’ means house – when one says **الْبَيْت** (al-Bait), it will mean that house, or the particular house intended. Intending the Ka'bah, which is the house of Allah ( **بَيْتُ الله** ), that word is ordinarily used. Here too, that is what is meant. A word that has meanings such as place of assembling, place of shelter, centre of safety, place one keeps going back to, place where reward is received holy place, is **مَثَابَةٗ**. In reality, there is no doubt that the Ka'bah abode is indeed a resort in which all these meanings come together. **امن** means security, peace. Just as for those who go seeking forgiveness of sins and mercy from Allah, for those who go seeking shelter from enemies and from assailants too, it is a place of security. In the Haram, which is the surrounding area of the Ka'bah, it is not permitted to assault anyone or to take punitive measures. Allah says: **وَمَنْ دَخَلَهُ كَانَ آمِنًا**.... **آل عمران: ٩٧** (Whoever entered it, he became secure (3:97)) The Arabs, who were lovers of war and lovers of strife, would not, even in the ‘Jahiliyyah’ time, carry out war or assault there. Within the boundary of the Makkah Haram, it is not permitted to hunt animals, nor to cut down trees and plants. As can be seen in the next verse, this is one among those in which Allah gave answer to the supplication that Ibrahim (a) made.

The word meaning of the word **مَقَامِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ** (Maqamu Ibrahim) is the place where the Prophet Ibrahim (a) stood. Some riwayahs can be seen which show that the whole Haram in Makkah, or all the places where the rites of Hajj take place – such as Safa, Marwa, Mina, Arafat –, will be included in Maqamu Ibrahim. But they are not trustworthy. That it is the famous stone, which is widely known by the very name Maqamu Ibrahim (Ibrahim Maqam) and which is situated near the Ka'bah, in front of its door, a little to the right side of those standing facing the door – that is indeed what the majority of Quran commentators and scholars say. There are also some strong hadiths which strengthen this opinion. About the stone, it will be described below. **إن شاء الله**

Imam Ahmad (r) quotes as stated by Anas (r): Umar (r) *\[p. 362\]* stated: I ‘have come into agreement with my Rabb in three matters (\*) (To the noble Rasul) I said: O Rasul of Allah, if only we took a place of prayer from Maqamu Ibrahim (near it)! – thus. So **وَاتَّخِذُوا مِنْ مَقَامِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ مُصَلًّى** (Take a place of prayer from Maqamu Ibrahim) was revealed. I said : O Rasul of Allah, the good and the wicked may come in to your wives, surely. Therefore, if only you would tell them to observe the veil! – thus. So the ayah of the veil was revealed. The wives of the noble Rasul united against the noble Prophet (on the issue that what they received for expenses was not enough). Then I said to them: If he (the Prophet) divorced you, his Rabb may give him wives better than you, – thus. Accordingly, **عَسَى رَبُّهُ إِنْ طَلَّقَكُنَّ**..... **التحريم: ٥** was also revealed. This statement of Umar (r), although with slight differences, has been recorded, from many Companions and through many routes, by many such as Bukhari, Muslim, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i. Ibn Majah (r). Hadiths making clear that, immediately after the circumambulation (tawaf) of the Ka'bah, behind Maqamu Ibrahim – keeping the Maqam and the Ka'bah in front – the Prophet ﷺ performed the prayer of two rak'ahs, Bukhari, Muslim (r) and others have also recorded besides. The sunnah prayer that the noble Prophet put into practice, after the tawaf is finished, everyone has been observing from that day on.

> **Note:** (\*) The import of what Umar (r) said is that Allah revealed the law as I had opined. It must not be supposed from this that the noble Prophet ﷺ had not understood those matters beforehand, or that the noble Prophet had a contrary opinion about it. All that is in it is that, without receiving a command, the noble Prophet did not come forward to put his own opinion into practice in those matters. That the noble Prophet said, I will do only what has been commanded to me, – has come in some narrations, moreover.

It will be good to know the gist of some statements that Ibn Kathir (r) made after quoting, as quoted above, many hadiths that have come concerning Maqamu Ibrahim. It can be quoted thus: ‘‘All this proves that what is meant by Maqamu Ibrahim is the stone on which Ibrahim (a) had been standing in order to build up the Ka'bah. It is what Isma'il (a) brought and gave him to stand (stepping) on when the wall rose. Thus Isma'il would bring stones, and he, standing on it, would build the wall up. When one side was completed, he would move it to the other side. In that way, the wall was completed. Both his footprints had been clearly impressed on that stone. This *\[p. 363\]* stone thereafter continued to be known. To the Arabs of the Jahiliyyah time too it was well familiar. In the famous Lamiyyah (a poem ending in the letter lam) of Abu Talib, it has been spoken of. The Muslims too came to recognise it. Abdullah ibn Wahab quotes from Anas ibn Malik (r): ‘I did see his fingers and soles in the Maqam. But the hands of the people, by touching and rubbing, have erased it.’

Ibn Kathir (r) continues: Ibn Jarir (r) quotes that Qatadah (r), quoting the verse **وَاتَّخِذُوا مِنْ مَقَامِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ مُصَلًّى** (Take a place of prayer from Maqamu Ibrahim), stated: Mankind have been commanded only to perform the prayer near it. It has not been commanded to touch and rub it. This community has ventured rashly to do a thing that the former communities did not do. Those who saw on it the marks of his – Ibrahim (a)'s – heel and fingers have told us. With this community touching and rubbing, it has worn down and faded away. I (Ibn Kathir) say: ‘In olden times this stone was lying touching the wall of the Ka'bah. Today its position, in a particular spot shifted a little to the right from directly in front of the door of the Ka'bah, is well known. When the building was finished, Ibrahim (a) had placed it close against the wall of the Ka'bah. Or, when the building ended, it may have been left at the very place where it ended. Allah knows. It may be because of this that command was given to perform the prayer near it when the tawaf is finished. The one who moved it back from near the wall of the Ka'bah was indeed Umar (r), who was one of the Khulafa' al-Rashidin, and one of those about whom the Prophet ﷺ said: Follow the two khalifahs after me – Abu Bakr and Umar –. It was, surely, in agreement with his opinion that it would be good to perform the prayer near it that the Quran too was revealed therefore, none among the Companions protested about it either (**إبن كثير**)

After the Prophet Ibrahim (a) – after that stone had lain touching the wall of the Ka'bah for thousands of years – or, as has come in some riwayahs, after someone earlier, fearing that a flood would come and wash the stone away, had moved it from its place and years had passed – that Umar (r) set it a little away from the wall out of regard for the convenience of those who do tawaf, this too can be seen in some riwayahs. Anyway, after it had remained for more than 1300 years in the place where Umar (r) had moved it, because of the ever-increasing crowding and *\[p. 364\]* the great number of those who come for tawaf, after enquiring and learning the opinions of the scholars of the Muslim world – about twelve years ago King Faisal ibn Abdil Aziz (\*) has moved that stone a little further back and set it up. For centuries it had been kept secure in a small building (in a qubbah) in such a way that it was not generally possible for anyone to see it. Now, that building having been pulled down and removed, it stands in a large crystal case, in front of the Ka'bah, in such a way that it can be seen from outside.

It will be fitting to the occasion to know some things that the scholar named Muhammad Tahir al-Kurdi – al-Makki (\*\*) has described about that stone in his book named Maqamu Ibrahim: ‘On the top part of that stone, which is roughly square in shape, three sides are 36 centimetres each and one side is 38 centimetres. The bottom part will come to a little more. Height – thickness – 20 centimetres. It has been fixed on a marble base of the same size. This base is on another base, and that is in a wooden box. All have been overlaid with silver plate. (\*\*\*) It has been recorded on it that this plate was made in Hijrah 1113. At the place where the footprints of Ibrahim (a) were impressed, because people had been touching and rubbing for a long time, there are two large hollows. Their length will come to 27 centimetres at the top surface and 25 centimetres at the bottom. The gap between the two is only one cm. With the hollows becoming larger, the distance between them has come to lessen. The finger-mark will not *\[p. 365\]* be seen at all. The colour of the stone is seen as a white colour mixed with a little red and yellow.

It seems good to know one more matter he has pointed out in that book. Its summary is this: ‘The Jahiliyyah Arabs – those in the Makkah surroundings especially – were, surely, famous for worshipping stones. Even so, it is not known that they worshipped Hajar al-Aswad and Maqamu Ibrahim. At the same time, they also used to honour these two stones. What was understood on thinking about this is this: ‘If they had been worshipped then, in case it were later commanded to touch and kiss (in tawaf) the corner of Hajar al-Aswad ( **ركن الحجر** ) and to perform the prayer at Maqamu Ibrahim, the enemies of Islam and the hypocrites would have made that a weapon and said that Islam too is not pure of honour towards idols. It may be because of that that Allah guarded and preserved both without occasion arising for them to be worshipped. This opinion of this man seems roughly reasonable **والله أعلم**

Allah's command, Establish a place of prayer from Maqamu Ibrahim ( **وَاتَّخِذُوا مِنْ مَقَامِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ مُصَلًّى** ) – that the Prophet ﷺ put it into practice through a sunnah prayer of two rak'ahs near it after the tawaf was seen, surely, in the hadith quoted above. The Muslims also continue observing it even today. But what a modern-loving scholar, who usually shows interest in stating new opinions different from what everyone says, says is that Maqamu Ibrahim means the place where Ibrahim (a) performed the prayer – the Ka'bah –, and that this is a command to make the Ka'bah a place of prayer. One feels astonishment! Allah knows where he got this interpretation from. The word Maqamu ( **مَقَام** ) does not have the meaning 'place where the prayer was performed'. Even if it has no word meaning, there is no proof at all that that is what is meant. Apart from a few, when the door of the Ka'bah is opened on some special occasions, entering inside it and at some time performing a sunnah prayer, a practice of making the Ka'bah a place of prayer is not known in Islam. Except on the occasion when the event of the conquest of Makkah took place, or in Hajjat al-Wada'(in the noble Prophet's last Hajj), it is not known that the Prophet ﷺ too performed the prayer inside the Ka'bah. Perhaps it may be said that the import of saying 'make the Ka'bah a place of prayer' is to perform the prayer in the place near it – in Masjid al-Haram –. But in Su. Al Imran 97 it can be seen that Allah, saying about the Ka'bah that there are very clear signs in it, has specially *\[p. 366\]* mentioned Maqamu Ibrahim ( **فِيهِ آيَاتٌ بَيِّنَاتٌ مَقَامُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ** ). Then, in case it is held that what is meant by Maqamu Ibrahim is the Ka'bah, its meaning would have to be, surely, that there is the Ka'bah in the Ka'bah. The mentality that prompts these people to say things like this is very amazing indeed !

Now, let us consider about its having been commanded to Ibrahim (a) and to Isma'il (a) that My house should be made clean for those who do tawaf, and for those who sit in devotion, and for those who bow down in prostration and perform the prayer. What was said as My house ( **بَيْتِي** ) is indeed with the Ka'bah in view. What that word indicates is the exalted position that Allah has ordained for that holy abode. About its importance, it is stated in the next surah, 96. It is circumambulating seven rounds, beginning from ‘Hajar al-Aswad’, which is situated in the corner on the left side of those facing the door of the Ka'bah, keeping the Ka'bah on the left side, that is called ‘tawaf’. Just as it has been prescribed that those who enter mosques should perform two rak'ahs of tahiyyah (the salutation prayer), what those who enter the Masjid al-Haram mosque should do in its place is indeed tawaf. In the Hajj rite and in the 'umrah rite it is also obligatory. Doing it whenever convenient is also good. It is to the word ( **عَاكِفِين** ) that the meaning 'those who sit in devotion' has been given. Not going apart from a thing because of honour for it is called ( **عكوف** ). ( **كما فى المفردات** ) Staying devotedly in mosques for rites such as the prayer and supplication is called, in the technical language of the Shar', **اِعْتِكَاف** (sitting in devotion). Those who gather near the Ka'bah (in Masjid al-Haram) for this same purpose are what is meant here. What was said as those who bow down in prostration ( **الرُّكَّعِ السُّجُود** ) is also intending those who perform the prayer. (About sujud and ruku' being said with the prayer in view, it has been explained in the interpretation of the 43rd verse and elsewhere)

That holy abode must be pure of all things such as idols, worship of a shirk nature, abominations, licentious doings, indecent talk, impurities, wrongdoings. None of that must have a place there. Such and the like is the import of the command that that house should be purified for them. In the time of Ibrahim (a), there is no ground for there to have been idols or idol worship there. Nor is it known that there were. Even so, from the very time of the Prophet Nuh (a), idol worship had already come into currency among the people. That the land and the home and all of the Prophet Ibrahim (a) were centres of idols, and his history of fighting against them, are, surely, well known. That being so, it must be by being entirely free from all the worship of those other than Allah, and with the worship of Allah alone *\[p. 367\]* being carried out – it may be considered that this will be what is mainly intended by that purification. In this verse is also contained a call for the Arab mushriks – who took pride in Ibrahim (a) being their patriarch, and claimed that it is his way that they have been observing, and at the same time made the Ka'bah abominable with hundreds of idols – to reflect on their stance.
