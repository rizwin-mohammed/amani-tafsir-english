*[Verses 224–225]*

*[p. 537]* In such a way as to cause a breach both to doing righteous things, and to obeying the injunctions and prohibitions of Allah, and to doing good works such as making reconciliation among the people, you must not swear in the name of Allah. Or, if any such oath has happened to be sworn, one must both desist from that oath and carry out that good thing. Swearing in the Name of Allah must not become an obstruction to such things. Such things are the gist of the first verse. Both that Allah will not take punitive action concerning sentences of oath that, not deliberately, slip out through the tongue, or about mistaken words that, not intentionally, happen to come about when swearing, and that, on the contrary, it is concerning oaths that are sworn intentionally, with the mind knowing, that He will take action, He makes known in the second verse. In the midst of speech, sometimes – for reasons such as inattention, emotion – some words or other may slip out without the mind knowing. In the speech of both some individuals, and speakers of some languages, uses of sentences of oath such as ‘It is by Allah indeed ’ will be plentiful, in place and out of place alike. Sometimes, a person, basing on his knowledge, may swear and say that such-and-such a matter is so-and-so. The fact will not be that. What the real position of oaths of this kind i*[p. 538]*s, Allah is explaining to us.

The Prophet ﷺ has said thus: ‘If a person, having sworn an oath about a matter, sees that something else was more excellent than that, let him both make atonement for his oath, and do that more excellent thing.’ (Bu; Mu.) The atonement ( **كُفَارَة** ) that needs to be made for breaking an oath is: giving food, or clothing to ten needy persons, or setting a slave free and letting him go, in case one is unable for that, keeping fast for three days – these. (Ma'idah: 92) Another hadith is thus; ‘Be it in a matter that is not possible for the son of Adam (man), be it in a matter of showing disobedience to Allah, be it in severing family ties, neither is an oath allowed, nor is a vow allowed. In case a person, having made a solemn oath in a matter, sees that something else was more excellent than that, let him leave that and take that which is more excellent. If he abandons that matter, he must also give atonement for it.’ (D.) *[DOUBT: the source is printed “(ദ.)”, which is not in the book's key to the abbreviations (Vol. 1 introduction, p. 11: ബു, മു, അ, ദാ, തി, ജ, ഹാ, ന, ബ, ത്വ). It may be a slip for ദാ. (Da = Abu Dawud) or stand for another source; kept as printed.]* Ibn Abi Hatim (r) quotes that 'A'ishah (r) used to state: ‘What is called vain word ( **لغو** ) is only in jest, and in amusement. That is, it is (in the course of speech) man's saying things such as both ‘It is by Allah indeed, no’ and ‘It is by Allah indeed, yes’. In it there is no atonement. It is in both firmly resolving with the heart that one must do, and afterwards not doing, that there is atonement.’

To the sentence that begins **وَلاتَجْعَلُوا اللَّهَعُرْضَةً** (You must not make Allah an obstacle for your oaths), some have given an interpretation like this too: For the sake of matters such as doing what is meritorious, observing caution, working what is good among the people, you must not swear making Allah like a target. That is to say, by swearing in His Name that one will do the said good things, you must not degrade the worth of His Name; the practice of swearing in the Name of Allah except when dire need is faced must be shunned – that is the gist. About making swearing excessive, it has been reproached (in 68:10) both in the Quran, and in the sayings of the Prophet, moreover.

*[Verses 226–227]*

*[p. 539]* The root of the verb **يُؤْلُونَ** (yu'luna) is **اِيلَاء** (ila'). Its meaning in the language is indeed to make a solemn oath. When some dislikes or other came about between wife and husband, a habit of making a solemn oath that he himself would not henceforth carry out union with the wife existed formerly among the Arabs. It is to this particular solemn oath that ‘ila'’ is said in the technical language of religion. This will sometimes be up to a fixed time, and sometimes for an unfixed time. This is also not customarily regarded as merely divorce (as ‘talaq’). Still, on account of not carrying out intercourse with the women, it will bring them both hardship and harm, surely. Therefore what the ruling is that needs to be followed in the Islamic shari'ah concerning ‘ila'’, Allah makes known in these verses.

In case a person makes ‘ila'’ concerning his wife, four months at the most may be given to him as respite. Until then no legal procedure may be taken against him. If, with four months becoming complete – or indeed before that – he has turned back and withdrawn from that solemn oath, good. Allah may forgive him. When four months become complete, one of two things becomes unavoidable. Either one must withdraw the solemn oath and resume intercourse with the wife. Otherwise one must divorce her and let her go. Allah knows well all such things as whether there is a fitting reason for divorce, or not. Therefore it must be done carefully – such things are the gist of these verses.

From having said ‘There is a waiting of four months, it becomes clear that it is only after four months have passed that it is permissible for the wife or anyone else to adopt legal proceedings against the husband who has made ‘ila'’. ‘Turning back’ is not enough if said with the mouth; it must be brought into effect through action. Though it is turning back from the solemn oath that is desirable – as can be kn*[p. 540]*own from the earlier verses and from the hadiths – they need to give the atonement **كفارَة** for having broken the oath, moreover. The opinion of the majority of scholars is indeed that. On the basis of having said ‘If they have resolved to divorce’, both that, in case he does not turn back from his solemn oath even when four months pass, it will not merely turn into a divorce, and that a lawful divorce needs to be carried out separately indeed – is the opinion of the majority of scholars. That with four months passing the wife will go off as talaq (as a divorced woman) is indeed the opinion of some Companions such as 'Umar, 'Uthman, 'Ali (r), and so on.

Some great men having pointed out here some things that can be understood in the light of these verses concerning ‘ila'’ is noteworthy. Even if not by means of ‘ila'’, a husband may not remain without approaching his wife for quite some lengthy time without a fitting reason. What is understood from this verse is that, at the most, in the ordinary state, it is not possible for a woman to remain patient for more than four months without the approach of the husband. Both that, following some reasons, 'Umar (r) held consultation with women about this, and that on that basis he said that he himself would not permit anyone among his armies to go away leaving home for a time of more than four months, Imam Malik (r) has narrated. That, just as wives have towards husbands, husbands too have towards wives a duty of observing some proprieties, can be seen stated in the next verse. That women must not be left and made to suffer in such a way that they are neither married nor unmarried can also be seen in verse 231 of Al-Baqarah and in 4: 129. ‘You have some duties both towards yourself, and towards your household’ – the Prophet ﷺ too has stated so. (Bu.) Even if it is without swearing, all the same, about a husband who does not observe the wife-husband relation without some just reason, the wife may lodge a grievance at the place of the judge, and the judge must make a remedy for it.

*[Verse 228]*

*[p. 541]* The singular of the word **قُرُوءٍ** (quru') is indeed **قُرْء** (‘qur'’). Giving the first letter the a-vowel ( **فَتح** ), it is also customarily used as ‘qar'’. Both that its actual meaning is entering into menstruation after purity has ended, and that it will be used intending purity and intending menstruation, Imam Raghib (r) has pointed out. Both that its meaning in the language is the time when a thing that customarily happens at a fixed time happens, or a thing that customarily ends at a fixed time ends, and that therefore the Arabs will be using that word both for the time when menstruation comes about, and for the time when purity comes about, Imam Ibn Jarir (r) too has stated. Whatever the word-meaning may be, that in both the two opposite meanings, the time of purity and the time of menstruation, that word may alike be u*[p. 542]*sed, there is no dispute. In which of those two is the intended sense here, variance of opinion can be seen among scholars from the Companions onward. It is to the opinion ‘the period of purity’ that preference is seen here. **والله اعلم**

Be it following ‘ila'’ or not, all the same, women who have been given talaq (divorce) need to wait for a period of three ‘qur'’. It is to this waiting that **عِدة** ('iddah) is said. When it is taken that what is intended by ‘qur'’ is the period of purity, the 'iddah of a woman who has been divorced in the period of purity ends with the beginning of the third menstruation that comes about after that. That is, the purity at the time the divorce takes place, and the purities that come about both after the first menstruation and after the second menstruation, together – in all three qur's. Now, if it is taken that what is intended by ‘qur'’ is menstruation, three qur' will be complete only when, the third menstruation having passed, the next purity begins. (Information such as that divorcing women in the menstrual period is wrong, and so on, has been stated both in the first verse of Su: Talaq and in its interpretation. Look there.)

Allah has made clear, in 33:49, that women with whom union has not taken place need not observe 'iddah, and in 65: 4 both that the 'iddah of women who are ones in whom menstruation has not come about, or ones whose menstruation has stopped, is three months, and that the 'iddah of pregnant women is until their delivery. They – that the ruling said here, three ‘qur'’s, is concerning the 'iddah of women who are ones with whom union has taken place, and ones in whom menstrual blood customarily comes about, and ones who have no pregnancy, can be understood. *[DOUBT: the sentence begins with അവർ (“they”), which has no clear place in its construction; it may be a slip (for example for അതിനാൽ “therefore”), or it may point ahead to the women described. Rendered literally.]*

The import of having said **وَلا يَحِلُّلَهُنَّأَنْيَكْتُمْنَ -الخ** (They may not conceal what Allah has created in their wombs.) is not to cover up both that, if they see that they have pregnancy, and that, if menstruation has come about. Because of some selfish end or motive, sometimes, it may come about both that they may feel a wish either that the 'iddah period must end quickly, or that it must go on as long as possible, and that accordingly they hide the real fact. It is therefore that Allah has specially prohibited this matter. The consequence that may occur on account of it, for its part, will also be big. Taking back and conducting a new marriage – all take place depending on the 'iddah, surely. There is a way to take legal procedures too only according to external evidence. Besides that too, those matters are, moreover, mostly ones known only to women, and ones that cannot be known widely. It is taking all this into account that, in a very serious tone, He has given the warning ‘If they believe in Allah and in the Last Day ( **إِنْكُنَّ يُؤْمِنَّبِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الآخِرِ** ) as well. What this word indicates is indeed that concealing is both a mighty wrong and deserving of punishment.

After divorcing for some reason or other, regretting that it was not needed, or else feeling that, since that reason has ended, they could henceforth have gone on in harmony, is an innate human disposi*[p. 543]*tion, surely, therefore, Allah has, in Islam, given indeed legally the opportunity for the husband who divorced to take her back again into the old marriage before the 'iddah period passes. He has also indicated both that that is better than a new marriage, and that it is good to take back as far as possible. It is that which was said as ‘In that – in the 'iddah period – to take them back, their bridegrooms are more entitled’. But, that in case one divorces a third time as well after having both carried out divorce and taken back two times, this opportunity will be lost, can be seen in the next verse. When taking back, one matter needs to be specially taken to heart: the taking back may not be with some evil intention or other in mind. Through the taking back no particular harm may come about either. Goodness must be brought about. It is with an intention of such things as that it must become good between them, that their future must become beneficial, that the taking back must be. That it is with the condition ‘If they intend to bring about goodness’ that Allah has given the right to take back, both those who take back and those who induce it need to remain mindful. They also need to remain mindful both that, in case a person takes back with some evil intention or other, though it is difficult both to prove it and to take legal procedure on account of it, it will come to light before Allah, and that Allah will adopt punitive action on account of it.

Continuing, Allah makes known: Like the liability that is upon the wives, husbands too have liability towards them ( **وَلَهُنَّمِثْلُالَّذِيعَلَيْهِنَّ** ). What is contained in this sentence is an idea that is very broad and pregnant with meaning. As far as women are concerned, in a time when, speaking generally, they were reckoned only as merely like household goods, or like slaves, or else like some creatures in the class of goats and cattle, Allah has granted to women, by this one single sentence, all the rights that they deserve. To enumerate that the liabilities that need to be fulfilled from each side towards the other side are such-and-such is difficult, surely. There will be both liabilities that are specially known from the religious point of view, and generally known in terms of good custom, and liabilities that need to be followed mutually in fields such as domestic, familial, marital, financial. So as to include all this, Allah has made do with the qualification ‘In accordance with customary propriety ( **بِالْمَعْرُوفِ** )’. (\*) Not only are all kinds of liabilities included in this word, it can be seen that an indication too is contained in this, that observing those liabilities must be according to the ability and circumstances of each one. For the liabilities that a man needs to fulfil towards women, we will get both a few *[p. 544]* examples, and a rough estimate about its extent, from the quotations seen below:-

1. Reminding that you have some obligations towards women, and women towards you, the Prophet ﷺ says: ‘But, the obligation upon women towards you: they must not permit those whom you do not like to tread on your bedding. When it was said that, just as women have towards men, men too have a duty towards women, so that one may not come to suppose either that man and woman are ones of equal level in every liability, or that man has no authority of control at all over woman, Allah says in the next sentence: ‘Men have a rank more than them, moreover ( **وَلِلرِّجَالِعَلَيْهِنَّ دَرَجَةٌ** ).’ Though what this rank is has not been made clear here, it becomes clear from Su: Nisa' 34. That because of both Allah's having by nature given men some superiorities, and its being men who spend wealth for the expenses of women, *[p. 545]* the control and guardianship of them belong to men, is indeed the gist of what Allah said there. (More information can be seen there. ( **إن شاء الله** ) Both that He is able to subdue those who do not heed the legal directions of Allah and to take fitting action against them, and that all His legal directions indeed are wholly reasonable and just only, and that there is no place in them for any amendment, He points out at the end: **وَاللَّهُعَزِيزٌحَكِيمٌ** (Allah is the Majestic and the Profoundly Knowing.)

Though, in duly granting the rights of women – looked at generally – men have at all times been showing great tardiness, it does not seem that the practice of reckoning them like goats and cattle, or like slaves, as in former times, will be seen much today. On the contrary, what is more dangerous today is both unrestrained freedom, and rights that in reality they do not deserve, being given to them. Not only in the sphere of good custom, and in the moral sphere, but in the social sphere in general indeed too, the calamities that keep coming about on account of this are terrible. Refuge is in Allah!
