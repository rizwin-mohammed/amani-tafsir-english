*[Verse 240]*

*[p. 576]* It is as a continuation of the subjects that had been spoken of in the previous ayahs concerning divorce and so on that both this verse and the next two verses stand. When husbands die, they need to make a wasiyyah both to lodge their wives there indeed for one year without making them live away from their house, and to give them the expenses they need in that period. But, if those women like both to leave that house of their own accord, and to do their future matters (such as marriage) in a way that is not contrary to good custom, one need not do hindrance to them either. They can move to their houses. This much is the summary of what has been stated in this verse. In explaining both the idea and the import of this verse, it is a differing position that the Quran commentators, both predecessors and successors, can be seen to have adopted. The two relevant opinions among them are these:-

(1) This is a ruling that existed in the early period of Islam. Afterwards, with both the law that, if husbands die, women must observe 'iddah for four months and ten days coming down (in the 234th verse), and the inheritance for wives (one fourth or one eighth of the property) being put into effect, this ruling- the ruling that one must lodge them in the house for one year and give expenses- has both become annulled (and become ‘mansukh’). (*) The greater part of the Quran commentators have adopted this position. But, it is only when both the conditions for taking a ruling to be ‘mansukh’ are fulfilled, and there comes to be no way to join together the two rulings with each other, that the question of ‘naskh’ arises. As for here, not only have the said conditions not been fulfilled as needed; as is seen in the opinion of Mujahid (r) and others quoted below, there is a possibility, moreover, to reconcile the two rulings in a way that there is no inconsistency between them.

> **Note:** (*) About ‘naskh’ (the annulling of a former ruling), it has been explained both in the Introduction and in the commentary on the 106th verse.

(2) The import of this verse is that, in case the woman, after the husband's death, likes both to stay in that house and to get expenses from there, one must make a wasiyyah to give her both lodging and expenses up to one year. What is said in that verse (in 234) is that, in case she does not like that, she must observe 'iddah for four months and ten days obligatorily. Or, both that observing 'iddah for four months and ten days is obligatory, and that observing the subsequent seven months and *[p. 577]* twenty days as well may be as she likes – that is the summary. This opinion is known as being that of Mujahid (r) and others. It is this opinion that seems more fitting **اللّه اعلم**. When she does not get the convenience for another marriage after the period of the obligatory 'iddah is over, there is no need to say, surely, that this wasiyyah will be very helpful for her to live without trouble until one year is completed.

After explaining this opinion, Ibn Kathir (r) has stated thus: Then, if both her 'iddah ends, either on account of childbirth, or on account of four months and ten days having been completed, and she intends to move from that house (belonging to the husband), one must not prevent it. Allah has, surely, said that there is no objection to their going out and doing what is needed in their matter ( **فَإِنْخَرَجْنَفَلاجُنَاحَ - الخ** ). There is justification for this opinion. In the (ayah's) statement there is also possibility for it. It is this opinion that a group of people have chosen. One of them is Abul 'Abbas Ibn Taimiyyah (r). Another party has also refuted it. One of them is Abu 'Umar Ibn 'Abdilbarr (r).

*[Verse 241]*

In this verse Allah makes known both that some resource or other (‘mut'ah’) needs to be given in accordance with good-custom propriety to all women who have been divorced, and that it is a duty upon those who observe caution. It has been made clear in the 236th verse that, when women in whose case both union has not taken place and mahr has not been fixed are divorced, giving ‘mut'ah’ to them is obligatory. It can be understood from the 237th verse that, for those in whose case union has not taken place and mahr has been fixed, it is enough to give half of the mahr- giving mut'ah is not obligatory -. Therefore, in explaining the import of what has been said in this verse, both that one must give some resource or other (mut'ah) to women who have been given divorce, and that it is a duty upon those who have caution, too, it is differing opinion that there is among scholars.

(1) Without difference as to whether they are those with whom union took place or those with whom it did not, those for whom mahr was determined*[p. 578]*, or those who are not, giving mut'ah to all women who have been divorced is obligatory. No one is exempt from this.

(2) Since it has been stated before about those with whom union has not taken place, it is only those with whom union has taken place that are intended in this verse.

(3) This verse is one that comprises two kinds of ‘mut'ah’s, which are both the obligatory and what is not (the optional). That is, this is a general ruling in which both the mut'ah that needs to be given obligatorily to those for whom both union and the fixing of mahr have not come about, and the mut'ah that needs to be given to others in a manner that is not obligatory- optionally-, are included. According to this, the intention of having said duty ( **حَقًّا** ) will be a required matter. This is the summary of those opinions. Besides, there is another opinion too, both that what is meant by having said here that one must give a resource ( **مَتَاعًا** ) is not ‘mut'ah’ of the kind stated above, and that what is meant is the expenses that need to be given while in 'iddah.(*)

> **Note:** (*) What we have seen here is the explanation that the mufassirs have given up to now to the word ‘mata'’ said in the 241st verse. The majority of the commentators have given ‘mata'’ only the meaning of a present that the man needs to give in such a way as to be of use in lightening the mental shock that befalls the woman by reason of divorce. But, it is only in whether giving it to all divorced women is obligatory or not that they have difference of opinion between them. A very few commentators say both that what is intended by the matta' in this verse is the maintenance that the man needs to give to the woman in the 'iddah period, and that giving any other present apart from that is not obligatory. Holding up the ‘maintenance’ in this explanation, and conveniently forgetting the fact that it is limited to the 'iddah period, some interested parties have lately been wont to blurt out that the Quran has commanded in 2:241 to give maintenance until the woman remarries or until she dies. In truth, it is not known that a single Quran commentator has said so as the explanation of 2:241. It is noteworthy that even those who now try to misinterpret this ayah in that way did not, in their own Quran commentary published earlier, explain ‘matta'’ as maintenance until death or until remarriage.

### Section – 32

After explaining many laws pertaining to practice and advices of principle, it is some lessons of history useful for increasing in the believers both readiness for sacrifice and the spirit of struggle that are explained in the verses that follow. In this series Allah explains two events. It is while rousing the attention of the listeners, saying **أَلَمْ تَرَ** (Have you not seen!), that both of them begin. The first is in a concise form, without making clear who the characters in it are, quoting only the vital part of the event; and the second in a more or less detailed form. Allah says:-

*[Verse 243]*

*[p. 581]* More than one riwayah about this event can be seen to have taken a place in the books of tafsir. None is strong in a way that it can be accepted. Therefore, we do not venture both to lengthen by quoting them, and to give an explanation based on them. For learning the lesson that needs to be learned from the event, there is no need of that either. If there had been, Allah indeed would have given the explanation needed for it. Therefore, we too may make do with the very form in which Allah has said it briefly. A people of thousands, fearing death, ran away leaving their dwelling-place. The cause of the fear may have been either enemies, or, as can be seen in some riwayahs, some deadly contagious disease or other. **اللّه اعلم** It can be understood from the next verse that what is intended by quoting this event is giving both motivation and encouragement to struggle in the way of Allah without fear of death and loss of courage. That being so, there is justification for inferring that the fear of this people was about their enemies. In any case, there was no result from their running away on account of the fear of death. Allah commanded them to meet death. Yes, they did indeed meet death. Was the death at one stroke, or was it gradual? Was it at the hands of enemies, or on account of disease? Allah knows. Nothing can be said definitely.

Just like that indeed, the real im*[p. 582]*port too of having said that Allah gave life, we do not know. As is said in some riwayahs, it is not that it could not have come about so once, suddenly, following the supplication of some prophet or other, or the like. That, surely, is among the things within the capacity of Allah. As some opine, it may also be that it is a word in a figurative meaning: that, after the generation that died, a later generation having more morale and firm faith came onto the scene after them and revived that community. We may leave the reality to Allah. That is the safe way.

In any case, the moral lesson to be received from the event is clear. Life and death and all are both what Allah reckons, and what is under His control. When facing war, or other stages of trial, there is no point in escaping by running away, showing cowardice and panic. When facing such stages, what is needed is to stand firm with courage, both entrusting all to Allah and being pleased with His decree. When one does so, both help and generosity from His presence will be received, and victory will come to hand. In a difficult state, being unready for sacrifice, adopting cowardice, withdrawing and going backward, is also ingratitude done to Allah. Such matters as these are the lessons to be learned from this. Running away on account of cowardice in facing enemies has, surely, been disallowed in both the Quran (8:16) and the hadiths. The Prophet ﷺ has stated both that one must not go to the land of contagious diseases such as cholera, and that, if they occur in a land, one must not run away from there. (Bu; Mu.) All this is on the basis of this principle. Allah continues and says:-

*[Verse 244]*

Just as one cannot avert the decree of Allah by running away in panic at a time of peril, just like that indeed, even if one either slips away without getting ready for war in His way, or turns back and runs at the time of war, there is going to be no result. What must happen will indeed happen. **اَيْنَمَاتَكُونُوايُدْرِكُكُمُالْمَوْتُ وَلَوْ كُنْتُمْفِيبُرُوجٍمُشَيَّدَةٍ** (Wherever you may be, death will reach you. Even if you are in lofty mansions, all the same. (4:78.) One proof that death will not draw near earlier on account of taking part in war *[p. 583]* is Khalid ibnul Walid (r), both the foremost among the Muslim army leaders and the great man known as ‘Saifullahi’ (the sword of Allah). At the time when he was near death, he is saying sorrowfully: I have taken part in so many wars. There is no limb of mine that has not got a wound, having received an arrow, or a cut, or a stab. Even so, here I am, having to meet death lying on the bed as a camel dies. May the eyes of cowards not sleep! Indicating that it is not enough only to fight with the body *[DOUBT: ദേഹം is rendered “self” where it stands for nafs (parts 08–18, 38–39); here (ദേഹംകൊണ്ട്) the sense is the physical person, so “body” is used, as part 13 did once. Please confirm.]* in the way of Allah, there needs to be spending of wealth in it too, Allah asks:-

*[Verse 245]*

About the intention of having said both ‘to lend to Allah’ and ‘a good loan’, see more in the commentary on Surah: Hadid 11. Allah will accept any donation, and gifts and charities, given with a full mind and yearning for Allah's recompense. Without anything being left out, He will both keep account of all, and keep on making it grow and prosper. At last, doubling it in plentiful measure, He will also give recompense in plentiful measure. That is the gist. That, when this verse came down, Abud Dahdah ( **أبُوالدَحْدَاح – رضـ** ) of the Ansar came to hand over to the Prophet ﷺ a garden that was both the sole property he had and one containing six hundred date palms, saying ‘Here I am lending to Allah’, Ibn Abi Hatim (r) and others have quoted.

There is no need to be reluctant to spend wealth in a good matter. Wealth and means of liveli*[p. 584]*hood and all – it is Allah who gives, and takes, and makes it wide, and makes it narrow. At last, everyone will also go back to Him indeed. Then, both the profit of what was spent and the loss of what was not spent will be experienced there. It is such things that He reminds of in the last sentences.
