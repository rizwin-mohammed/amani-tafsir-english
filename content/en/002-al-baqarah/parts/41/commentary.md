*[Verse 246]*

*[p. 585]* It is the second of the two historical events pointed out before that is described both in this verse and in some verses following. This is an event that happened, many centuries having passed after the Prophet Musa (a), in the early period of the Prophet Dawud (a). Saying ‘Have you not seen what the chiefs among the Israelites said to a prophet of their own’, Allah invites our attention to the event. For the word **الْمَلاء** (mala') meanings such as ‘chiefs, heads, elders, notables’ can be given. Sometimes it will also be used intending ‘the crowd’. It is used only in a plural meaning. There have, surely, been very many prophets among the Israelites. That, without saying the prophet's name, He said only a prophet among them will be because it is not necessary to know the prophet's name in order to understand the moral lessons in the event. From the information seen below, it is understood that it was the prophet Shamawil (**شَمَوِيل ـ او صَمُويل** or Samuel). In the Bible, in Samuel, Book One (*), this history can be seen told at very great length. In some statements it does not agree with the Quran. An explanation of many statements contained in these verses is also contained in it. When examining with the statements both of the Quran commentators, and of some authors of books of prophetic history, and of the Bible placed in front, the background of the event can be understood thus:-

> **Note:** (*) The content in this is from the birth of the prophet Samuel up to the death of Dawud (a).

After the passing of Musa (a), after the Isra'il people entered Palestine under the leadership of the prophet Joshua (**يوشع بن نون**), who had been his attendant, he both fixed the dwelling-place for each of their tribes, (**) and appointed judges among them *[p. 586]*. Until his death the Israelites were under his control. Until the middle of the fourth century after Musa (a) there were no kings among them. The judges kept on controlling affairs in accordance with the directions of the prophets. The prophets would keep on giving them guidance. On some occasions the prophet indeed would also be the judge. In course of time evil customs and acts of licentiousness, reaching up to both image-worship and installations of gods, spread and took hold among the Israelites. In these times many wars took place between both the 'Amaliqah (**عمالقة**) races, such as the Ammonites, the Midianites, the Philistines, the Moabites, and the Isra'il race. In the fourth century, in the war that occurred with the Philistines, a tragic defeat befell the Israelites. The Philistines seized and carried off both the ‘Tabut’, known as the ark of the law (*), which they had been taking along with them intending their own success and deliverance, and the Tawrat. Owing to this, they both fell into mighty lamentation, and their vigour and prestige perished. At this time there were no prophets among the Israelites. A woman in a family that had a prophetic tradition gave birth to a male child. It was indeed in the meaning that the supplication received an answer that it was named Shemuel (Shimwil). Later, prophethood was attained by him. He was both their prophet and their judge.

> **Note:** (**) The place allotted to each tribe in Palestine can be seen in map no. 2.

> **Note:** (*) It is this ark that is called **التَابُوت** (Tabut). The Bible Dictionary says: ‘It has names such as the ark of Jehovah, the holy ark, and others. This is one made of wood and covered with gold. In it there were the law-tablet (the tablet on which the Tawrat was written)s. The Israelites, guarding this with devotion and respect, carried it about with them. At last they installed it in a place of worship called Shiloh. They believed that in all the positions where this rested there was the presence of Jehovah. Of the history after king Solomon (the Prophet Sulaiman) installed this in the house of God, nothing can be known.’

It was at the time when old age had taken hold of him that the Israelites asked that prophet that he must commission a king for them, both to lead them and to give leadership in war and so on. That prophet, who had mixed with them, can, surely, conjecture that, if a king came into existence, the reaction and the character that would arise in them would be of such and such a kind. So, when, commissioning a king, you have to carry on war with the enemies under him, might you withdraw and show disobedience? – thus he asked them. We, surely, have reasons such that we will of ourselves become ready to carry on war with the enemies. The mischiefs that the enemies have brought about for us are not just a little. While they have done many things, such as chasing us away both from the land and from the home, seizing our children and putting them in bondage, if we get the leadership of a king as well, how will we withdraw? – such was their reply. What happened at last was, for the most part, indeed just as the prophet had sus*[p. 587]*pected. That is what Allah said; ‘Then, when war was prescribed upon them, except a few folk among them, the rest withdrew. More information is coming below.

The summary of what the Bible has stated of this scene is like this: ‘The elders of Yisrayel came near Samuel and said: You have become old. Your sons do not walk in your way. Therefore, you must appoint us a king to rule us, as there is for all the nations. This was displeasing to Samuel. Samuel prayed to Jehovah (to God). Both hear their request, but make known to them the manner of the king who will reign over them – thus Jehovah gave answer. So, he made them hear both the hard situations and the ruins and losses that would come about for them if a king came. They insisted that they must have a king’ (1. Samuel, 8: 4-22)

*[Verse 247]*

*[p. 588]* In accordance with their request, a person named Talut was made king and determined for them. When the prophet made this information known to them, they were not pleased with it. How will that be right? It is to us, surely, that the entitlement to it belongs, more than to him. He has no capacity by way of wealth either. – such things were their reaction. The prophet, telling them the matter, made them understand. 1st, it is Allah who appointed him, making him king, not I. It is, surely, because Allah knows that he suffices to be king for you that He did so. 2nd, both in knowledge and in condition of frame he stands excelling. What is needed both to rule and to lead is knowledge. What is needed to face critical stages such as war is strength of frame. He has both. 3rd, giving kingship and so on are matters that Allah does in accordance with His intention. In that, our opinion has no place. 4th, in favour, and in capacity, and in mercy and all, Allah is Vast to the utmost. He is also All-Knowing. Then, the good and the need of creatures are not unknown to Him. In that He will not show miserliness either. – and so on.

The Israelites were, surely, twelve tribes. Among them, both prophethood in the tribe of Levi, and judgeship in the tribe of Judah, had been continuing as a tradition. Talut was a person belonging to the tribe of Benjamin, a small tribe among them. He used to do jobs such as grazing cattle, stitching sandals. There was no wealth-capacity at all worth mentioning. It is for that reason that they said that it is surely we who are entitled to kingship more than him. The name seen in the Bible in the place of the name Talut is indeed Saul (**شاول**). (*) Whether it is that he had two names, or else that one name became contracted and a change came about, that is the cause for this difference, it is not known. In any case, we may be pleased with the name that came in the Quran.

> **Note:** (*) About Saul, this can be seen in the Bible Dictionary: ‘This one was the first king in Yisrayel. The time may be said to be B.C. 1050- 1017.’

*[p. 589]* The concise form of what the Bible has described of this scene is like this:- ‘In the tribe of Benjamin, a rich man named Qisu (**قيس**) had a son named Saul. He was both one who had youth and comeliness, and one who was taller than everyone from the shoulder up. Searching for Qis's donkeys that had gone missing, he sent the son Saul along with a servant. In the course of going about seeking and searching, Samuel's town was reached. The servant said both that there is a man of God here, and that he will tell about the donkeys, and that we can see him. Both persons went and saw Samuel. He both entertained Saul and told about his future. About Saul's coming, to him there had been a revelation beforehand indeed. Then, he both gathered everyone together and made known the news of making him king, and described the duty of the king. Saul, who had been hiding among the goods, was made to come into the midst of the people. Everyone rejoiced. They called out: O king, triumph, triumph. But some base men defied Saul, saying: how will this one save us.’ (1. Sam. Ch: 9, 10.)

In the course of speaking about many wars carried out under the leadership of Saul, it says thus in the 1st Book of Samuel: ‘After Saul took up the kingship in Yisrayel, he made war with all the peoples of the nations around, such as the Moabites the Ammonites, the Edomites, the kings of Zobah, the Philistines. Wherever he went he attained triumph. He worked valour. He overcame the Amalekites (Amalikha). He also delivered the Yisra'ilites from the hand of the plunderers.’ (1. Sam. 14:47- 49) From what the Quran has stated about king Talut, what is understood is indeed that he was both a good man and one blessed. When looking at some references in the Bible, it is understood that Saul is one who, merely like ordinary kings, did both many lapses, and acts of disobedience. That being so, it is possible for us only both to affirm only what agrees with the Quran and with other proofs, to reject all references contrary to it, and to leave aside the portion about which neither can be pronounced, without determining it as true or as untrue. That is also what the Prophet ﷺ has taught us.

*[Verse 248]*

*[p. 590]* It was understood, surely, from what was described before, that the Israelites were not pleased with Talut having been appointed king. Therefore, the prophet made known to them a sign both for his kingship and for his being a person fit to lead them. That ark (**التَّابُوتُ**), which both the enemies earlier seized from you, and owing to which you came to be in very much heartache and degradation, will come back to you without any effort at all of yours. It will be the angels who bring it. This is that sign. There is no need to say that this will produce in them fervour and morale and peace of mind. That is what was said: ‘In it there will be tranquillity that is from your Rabb’. As to the intention of having said: ‘There will also be some remnant that the people of both Musa and Harun left behind.’, many opinions are seen. A copy of the Tawrat- or else some tablets on which it was written, or pieces of it-, the staffs of both Musa (a) and Harun (a), their garments – such things and others are said. (Allah knows.)

About the sentence ‘The angels will carry it and bring it’, it has been narrated from Qatadah (r) that the angels brought it and placed it in Talut's house, and, as said by the venerable teachers of Thawri (r), from him, that the angels brought it in a cart to which one or two cows were tied. In any case, Allah has stated that the return of the box is a mark for Talut's kingship. Though the Bible has described in detail about the return of the box, it has not stated it in it as a mark of his kingship. The summary of what the Bible has described is this:-

‘The Philistines placed the ark of God in the idol called Dagon (**دَاجُون**)'s *[p. 591]* temple. Dagon kept falling over owing to it. Later, shifting it to other places was tried. In those places too, calamities such as death, a plague of rats, piles kept occurring. At last, in accordance with the advice of some, it was decided both to send it back, and to make some things of gold and send them along with it as an expiation. Loading them all into a cart, tying two milch cows, the cart was let go. There was no one either to drive the cart or to show the way. The cows went straight to Bethshemes (**بَيْتُ شَمْس**). To see where it was going from there, the lords of the Philistines too had gone behind. They went back from there. Later -for some reasons- the inhabitants of Yeyarim (**يَعَارِيم**) took it and placed it in the house of Abinadab. They entrusted his son with its attendance. For seven months it remained with the Philistines, and for twenty years in Yeyarim. Yisrayel lamented to Jehovah. At this time the prophet Samuel gathered them together and gave counsels. If you are turning to Jehovah with a whole heart, removing both the alien gods and the installations, you must serve Jehovah alone. Then He will save you from the hand of the Philistines. So they abandoned both the idols and the installations’. (1. Sam. Ch: 4-8)

If the coming of the box was like this, it can be understood both that, seeing that it travelled a very great distance without anyone either to control or to drive that cart and the cows tied to it, and came and stood at the place where it was to arrive without going astray, it will be an unseen power indeed that was carrying out its control, and that, as was narrated from Imam Thawri (r), that is the import of having said ‘The angels will carry it and bring it’. It is for that reason, too, that many commentators have adopted this opinion. (In some books, instead of ‘cow’, ‘ox’ can also be seen. The two are ones without contradiction between them) Allah knows the reality.

### Section – 33

*[Verse 249]*

*[p. 593]* The indiscipline and the weakness of faith of the Israelites are, surely, famous. There will be among them both those who set out with half a mind, and those who hold misgivings about the king, and those who are not ready for sacrifice. It is about a trial having taken place to separate out both such ones and the sincere ones that He states in this verse. The river Jordan is a famous river flowing through the dwelling-places of the Israelites. It also has very many tributary rivers. When Talut and the army went marching out, some one or other of them had to be crossed. It is there that the trial takes place. No one must drink water from the river; those who drank will not be enrolled in the army; only those who did not drink will be accepted; if very necessary, one may scoop up once with the hand and drink; more than that is not allowed. This was the commandment. As some commentators can be seen to have said, that may also have been an occasion in which heat and thirst were greater. It is then, surely, that the result of the trial will be complete. Taking those who are not ready even to bear thirst for a short while to face calamities mightier than that is what will bring about more ill than good. The trial also bore fruit. Only a few folk refrained from drinking water. That, thus, the greater portion were thrust out from the army, there is no need to say.

The Bible has described many wars that Saul (Talut) and the army carried out with the enemies. Among them, it has stated in it both that in one war he said ‘Both before evening, and until I take retribution on the enemies, the one who eats food is accursed’, and that on that day the people fell into hardship without eating food. (1. Sam. 14:24.) But an event of trial like that stated in this verse is not seen in the Bible. It is usual that many vital scenes that the Quran points out in historical events are not found in the Bible. Those who did not drink water- yes, those having real faith and a firm state of mind- were only a few: that is what Allah said. That they were only as many in number as those who took part in the war of Badr- three hundred and ten and more persons- has come in a hadith quoted by Bukhari, Ibn Jarir (r) and others.

After the trial was over, when Talut, and those who succeeded in the trial, crossed the river and drew near to the battlefield, seeing both the multitude of the enemy army and their own shortness of number, some among them: We have no capacity today, surely, to face both Jalut and his army – this they said. Those among them having more knowledge and awareness and faith reassured them, speaking to them: How many events there have been in which small, small bands, by the permission and help of Allah, gained success against big, big bands. Yes, even though we are merely *[p. 594]* a minor band, it is, surely, in accordance with the permission and commandment of Allah that we are going down to war. Then, He will not fail to help us. It is to Him, surely, that we all will go back. Therefore we must adopt patience. It is on the side of those who are patient that Allah will be – and so on. A sentence that 'Umar (r) once said is worthy of statement here: ‘Defeat will not befall us on account of fewness of persons. It is on account of sins that defeat will befall us.’

Though it may seem, from this verse and from the next verse together, that Jalut would be the commander of the enemy army, it cannot be said definitely. Perhaps, it may also be that he is a soldier, the most valiant and heroic among them. Allah knows. In the Bible, it is said thus about Jalut: ‘Then, the Philistines gathered together the armies for war... Saul and the Yisrayelites gathered together... Then, from the camp of the Philistines a champion named Goliath (*), the Gittite, came out. He was one six cubits and a span (**) in height... On Goliath's head there was a bronze helmet. He wore a bronze coat of mail weighing five thousand shekels, and leg-armour made of bronze (***), and so on’ (1. Sam. 17:1-6)
