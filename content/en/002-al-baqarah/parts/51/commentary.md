### Section – 40

*[Verse 284]*

*[p. 687]* In this great surah, the biggest in the Quran, Allah explained up to now very many matters such as counsels of principle, lessons of history, legal directions. After that, He is going to conclude this surah, reminding of some realities that are fundamentally important. All the things that are in the heavens and the earth- yes, in the entire universe- are Allah's. They are both His creation and in His ownership. They are in His authority and in His control. Without distinction as secret or open, or as hidden or clear, all matters are known to Him. All the thoughts and feelings, both those that His servants reveal through works or through words, and those that they hi*[p. 688]*de in the mind without revealing, He knows. He will also carry out reckoning about them. Then, that He will both forgive those who are to be forgiven, and punish those who are to be punished, and so on, is what Allah reminds of in this verse. That Allah will know all secrets and all open things, including the thoughts that are in the hearts, is something often stated in the Quran. But, that He will carry out reckoning with men concerning what is in the hearts has been made clear only in this verse. It will be from the breadth of meaning of this that, as can be seen in riwayahs such as are quoted below, trouble of mind also came to be felt by the Companions when this verse came down.

The summary of an incident that great men such as Ahmad, Muslim (r) have recorded is like this: ‘When this verse came down, the Companions became unable to bear it. They went to the Prophet ﷺ and were aggrieved. We had been charged to do matters that are within our ability, such as the prayer, fasting, charity, struggle. Now, here, this verse has come down. That is beyond our ability, surely. (We have no ability to abandon the thoughts in the mind, surely) they said. The Prophet ﷺ said to them: ‘Without saying, as the People of the Scripture did before, we heard, we act contrary, say: we hear and obey.’ Thus, when they had read through this verse for some time and become familiar with it, their trouble of mind went away, and Allah sent down the next ayah.’ That no one will be charged except with what each one has ability for, and so on, has been made clear in the second verse next.

It can be seen that some Companions who narrated the said incident have also stated that the next verse has made ‘naskh’ of this verse. Taking this word as basis, some Quran commentators have also said that this verse is ‘mansukh’. In reality, it is not naskh, as usually spoken of- in the technical sense- (annulling of a ruling), that they have meant by that word. That ‘naskh’ has the meaning only that the verse that came down later explains the intention of the verse that came down first. Imam 'Asqalani (r) and others have also specially pointed out this thing. (Many particulars concerning ‘naskh’, including both the meaning that the predecessors have assigned to naskh ( **نَسْخ** ) and the technical meaning that later scholars have been giving to it, have been explained in the Introduction. A few of them have also passed in the explanation of the 106th verse. [For more particulars look in those places.]

That the import of what Allah has said is not that Allah will take punitive action concerning all the thoughts and impressions that arise in the mind of man is clear. That, having examined both good thoughts and bad thoughts, He will take action in accordance with each, and that He will give pardon to those to whom pardon is to be given and punishment to those to whom punishment is to be given, and that who are deser*[p. 689]*ving of pardon- or else of punishment- Allah knows, is the import of what Allah has said. Following having said that He will carry out reckoning, Allah has also said (Then He will forgive those whom He wills, and He will punish those whom He wills), surely. From hadiths such as are seen below, what has just been said will become more detailed:-

(1) The Prophet ﷺ says: ‘Surely, Allah has remitted for my community what their minds talk (what occurred in the mind)- in case they do not act on it or speak it-.’ This is one that the principal hadith scholars have all quoted.

(2) The gist of another saying of the Prophet: ‘A man intended to do a good, then he did not do it. Then it will be recorded for him as a complete good. If, having intended a good, he does it, it will be recorded as from ten up to seven hundredfold. A man intended an evil, then he did not do it, then it will not be recorded. If he does it, it will be recorded as one evil.’ This is what both Bukhari and Muslim (r) have quoted through many routes.

(3) Muslim (r) has narrated thus: ‘A band of people came to the noble Rasul ﷺ and said thus: To one among us something (some evil promptings) is felt in his mind. Saying it (outside) also seems terrible to him. (What does the noble Prophet say about this?) The noble Prophet asked: ‘Do you feel so?’ They answered ‘Yes’. Then the noble Prophet said: ‘That is clear belief.’ It is about doubtful thoughts, both such as sometimes come to occur in the mind in a way that goes against the principles relating to belief, and such as one does not get the courage even to speak openly to others, that the Companions asked a doubt of the Prophet ﷺ. That their feeling reluctance even to say it indicates the firmness of their belief, and that, because doing away with such impressions is not within the ability of man, damage will not befall true faith on account of them, is the gist of the reply that the Prophet ﷺ gave them.

It need not be said both that in the thoughts and feelings of the mind indeed, there will be good and bad, and that some will lead to merit and some to sin. Both true faith, sincerity, God-fearing reverence, the wish to do good causes and the like, and denial of the truth, arrogance, jealousy, hypocrisy, material yearning and the like, are all the progeny of the mind, surely. One and the same work indeed becomes both good and bad according to the thought in the mind. Therefore, it can be understood on the whole both that Allah, the All-Knowing, having examined entirely not only the works of each one, but both the thoughts and feelings and the intentions and concerns in his mind, will adopt the action that is deserved on account of each one, and that He will remit those that are not subject to the ability and the control of man. Thoughts and feelings need to be controlled without letting them loose uncontrolled. Both evil thoughts and evil intentions need to be bridled as far as possible. To hold down those that are beyond control without bringing them into action *[p. 690]* care needs to be taken, and so on, is what we have to learn from this. **والله الموفق والمعين** *[DOUBT: the author gives no Malayalam for this Arabic; its likely meaning is "And Allah is the One who grants success and the Helper".]*

*[Verses 285–286]*

*[p. 692]* At the beginning of the surah, Allah stated that the holy Quran is guidance for the ‘muttaqi’s, who guard the injunctions and prohibitions of Allah. And continuing, He also explained that the muttaqis are those with qualities such as believing in unseen matters, establishing the prayer, spending from what Allah has given, believing in what has been sent down to the noble Rasul and to the former prophets, and in the Hereafter. After that, up to now, He explained and elucidated very many matters. It is both by pointing out that those muttaqis stated at the beginning are the Prophet ﷺ and the true believers who believed in the Prophet ﷺ, and by explaining what their position is in belief, God-fearing reverence, obedience and the like, that Allah is concluding this surah.

Whatever has come down to the Rasul from his Rabb, in all that he has also believed. ( **آمَنَ الرَّسُولُبِمَاأُنْزِلَإِلَيْهِ مِنْرَبِّهِ** ) That he believes in it is also natural. It is indeed therefore that, when this verse came down, the Prophet ﷺ also came to say **وحق له ان يؤمن** (and he has also become entitled to believe in it), (Baihaqi, Hakim, Ibn Jarir.) Is it only the Rasul who believes in it? No. All the true believers have believed ( **وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ** ) It being Allah who sent it down, it is in Allah, surely, that one has to believe first. It is also by the hand of the unseen messengers, who are the angels, that it has been sent down. Therefore one has to believe in the angels too. It is not only to the Prophet ﷺ, or only the Quran that was sent down to the Prophet ﷺ, that Allah has sent down. Allah has sent down before this many other scriptures too, for many Rasuls. Nor were the missions and the preachings, all indeed, of both those books and those Rasuls alien from the mission, and from the preachings, of both the Prophet ﷺ and the Quran. Therefore, they all- both the Rasul and the true believers, all indeed- have believed in Allah, and in His angels and in the whole of His scriptures, and in all His Rasuls. ( **كُلٌّ آمَنَبِاللَّهِ وَمَلائِكَتِهِ وَكُتُبِهِ وَرُسُلِهِ** )

As the disbelievers said, adopting a differing attitude between both Allah and His Rasuls, **نُؤْمِنُ بِبَعْضٍ وَنَكْفُرُ بِبَعْضٍ** (We will believe in some, and in some we will disbelieve- 4: 150), they will not say. As the People of the Scripture did, saying **نُؤْمِنُ بِمَا أُنْزِلَ عَلَيْنَا** (We will believe in what has been brought down to us- 2: 91), they will not disbelieve in the other scrip*[p. 693]*tures either. That we will not show distinction between any one belonging to the Rasuls of Allah is indeed the position they have been accepting. Or, they are those who believe both in all the Rasuls, and in all the scriptures sent down to them. In just the same way, as the Israelites said before, **سَمِعْنَا وَعَصَيْنَا** (We heard, and we show disobedience too. (2: 93) they will not say either. That we hear and obey is what they will say. ( **وقَالُوا سَمِعْنَا وَاَطَعْنَا** ) Yes, whatever Allah commands or disallows, they are those who are ready both to hear it all properly and accept it, and to act in accordance with it. Because, their sole aim is forgiveness for sins from Allah. They also have the awareness that they will have to return to Allah and render account before Him. Therefore, ‘Our Rabb, Your forgiveness- that is what we need- our return is to You, surely.’ is what they will keep saying.

Right, the true believers are both those who believe in the manner stated above, and those who obey all the injunctions and prohibitions of Allah unconditionally. But, will man be able to accept all the injunctions and prohibitions of Allah just as they are and practise them? If a doubt may occur thus to anyone, as the reply to that Allah says: ‘Allah will not charge anyone, other than with what is convenient for that individual to perform, beyond that. ( **لا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلاوُسْعَهَا** )’ This is a very noteworthy sentence. Without using any words such as ( **طَاقَـتَـهَا، مُقَدُورَهَا** , which can come to mean either ‘what is possible at the utmost’, or ‘what one is able to do even though with hardship’, or the like, it is indeed **وسعها** that Allah used as the word, in the meaning ‘that for which there is convenience and means to obey’. That Allah has commanded only matters that can be brought into practice without having to bear any great hardship- without any unbearable hurt and loss befalling- is the import of this. Allah says: **وَمَاجَعَلَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ مِنْحَرَجٍ** (In the religion He has not placed upon you any hardship. (22: 78.) That Allah charges in the religion only matters that can be performed without difficulty worth mentioning in the ordinary course is the gist. Along with that, in obstacles that arise temporarily, or occasionally, remedies and relaxations in accordance with each have been given, moreover.

But, that Allah does not permit man to live entirely free- in a way that suits liking and desire- is the plain truth. Nor does that agree either with the justice of Allah, or with the kindness that He has towards His creatures. Nor will intellect or sagacity approve it. About those who both see religion as an obstacle to living so, and to whom, on account of it, aversion and disgust towards religion *[p. 694]* occur, it need only be considered that they are merely a kind of primitive creatures like birds, beasts and the like. In real fact, the good that each individual does and lays by does good to itself indeed. The evil that it does and amasses also brings ill to itself indeed. ( **لَهَا مَاكَسَبَتْوَعَلَيْهَا مَااكْتَسَبَتْ** ) In that, there is not to be any attainment or damage for Allah. But, the precise knowledge of what all results beneficially for man, and which all results detrimentally, is with Him alone, man does not know it. It is therefore, too, that Allah charges that it is in such and such a manner that man has to live.

It is with a great supplication, which both the Prophet ﷺ and the true believers ought to keep supplicating and are used to supplicating, that Allah concludes this chapter. Ibn 'Abbas (r) is saying: ‘On an occasion when Jibril (a) was with the noble Rasul ﷺ, a sound was heard. Jibril looked upward and said: That is a gate in the heaven that had never at all been opened before, being opened. Thus, an angel came down through it and came to the Prophet ﷺ. He said: Rejoice in two lights that have been given to you. Before you it has not been given to any prophet. That is, both ‘Fatihat al-Kitab’ (Surah: Fatihah), and the final portions of Surat al-Baqarah. Whatever letter you recite from the two of them, it will not fail to be given to you.’ (Mu. Na) What is meant by those two lights is both Surat al-Fatihah, and the supplications at the end of this surah. That Allah will not fail to give answer to those supplications is indeed what the angel made known to the Prophet ﷺ. It being a supplication that He Himself taught for the true believers, who are the servants of Allah, to supplicate to Him, He will not fail to give a special consideration to those supplications, surely.

The requests in this supplication that the true believers make, calling out **رَبَّنَا** (Our Rabb), are these:

(1) ‘If we forget, or we commit a mistake, do not seize us and take punitive action!’ The Prophet ﷺ has stated that one will not be punished for wrongs that are done through forgetfulness, or in mistake, it also becomes clear from many Quran verses. That being so, one may wonder what the intention of this supplication is. Most often the reason for both forgetfulness and mistake will be both through unmindfulness and because of the deficiency of the sense of duty. Sometimes it will also be because attention has come to be absorbed in subjects that are unnecessary or reprehensible. Then, in every forgetfulness and mistake man will not be wholly innocent. Now, even if the wrongs that, through forgetfulness or in mistake, *[p. 695]* came to be done are pardoned indeed, there is room also for other consequences that are not satisfactory to happen, following it. In that situation, watch over and protect us both in this world and in the next without any of the ills befalling that can come about by forgetfulness befalling or by a mistake striking, is the import of this.

(2) ‘Do not impose upon us any burdens like what was imposed upon those before!’ Both some strict laws, and customs of deeds with more trouble, such as were upon the former communities, are what is meant by burden ( **اصر** ). Among explaining the aims of the mission of the Prophet ﷺ, it can be seen that Allah has said **وَيَضَعُ عَنْهُمْإِصْرَهُمْ وَالْأَغْلَالَ الَّتِي كَانَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ** (both their -the People of the Scripture's -burden, and the shackling bonds that were upon them, he will put down from them (A'raf: 157). That many laws that were in the former communities have been lightened for the community of the noble Prophet ﷺ is worth stating.

(3) ‘Do not make us carry any matters for which we have no possibility!’ Or, do not make matters that we are unable to sustain happen in us! Keep us, without sufferings that we cannot bear coming, both in this world and in the next! is the gist.

(4) ‘Grant us pardon!’

(5) ‘Forgive us!’

(6) ‘Have compassion on us!’ Pardon both our sins and wrongs and offences, covering them up without bringing them outside, and forgive, without entrapping us in punishment and in disgrace on account of them! In short, also bless us by bestowing mercy upon us always in both our worldly and otherworldly lives! is the import.

(7) The gist of the last sentence in the supplication is like this:- ‘Rabb! You indeed are our Master. We do not accept the mastership of anyone else. That being so, we have no one to rely on other than You, surely, both to accept our supplication and bless us, and to give help against our enemies to us, who are Your servants and true believers. Therefore, keep helping us, who are true believers, against all who are disbelievers!’

In a hadith that many great men such as Bukhari, Muslim (r) have quoted from Ibn Mas'ud (r), the Prophet ﷺ says: ‘If anyone recites two ayahs from the end of Surat al-Baqarah in a night, they will suffice for him.’ That, on the occasion when these verses came down, when the Prophet ﷺ recited them, *[p. 696]* immediately after each sentence **قَدْ فُعِلَتْ** (It has been done so.) was said, Ibn 'Abbas (r) too has stated. (Mu. Ti.) Many other hadiths too that denote the superiority of these verses can be seen. That, when finishing reciting this surah, Mu'adh (r) used to say ‘Amin’ ( **آمِين** Do give answer!), Ibn Jarir (r) and others have narrated. **رَبَّنَالَاتُؤَاخِذْنَاإِنْ نَسِينَا أَوْ أَخْطَأْنَا - الآية** *[DOUBT: the author gives no Malayalam for this Arabic; it is the opening of the verse 286 supplication, likely meaning "Our Rabb, do not seize us if we have forgotten or made a mistake - the ayah", that is, to the end of the ayah.]*
