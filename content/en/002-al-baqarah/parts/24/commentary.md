### Section – 19

*[Verse 153]*

After saying, in the last verse, to remember Allah and to show thanks *[p. 409]* to Him, in this verse He advises to hold fast to patience and the prayer, and to seek help by way of it. By saying that Allah will be on the side of those who have patience, He has also specially stressed the matter of patience. That His special help and blessings will keep coming about for them – that is the import. Just as thanks in favour and in gladness, adopting patience in trials and in difficulties must be the nature of the true believer. In a hadith the Prophet ﷺ says : The matter of the true believer is a wonder indeed! All his matters will be advantageous for him. This is not so for anyone other than the true believer. If something gladdening befalls him, he shows thanks. Then that becomes a benefit for him. If something harmful befalls him, he will also be patient. Then that too becomes a benefit for him. (Mu) Very many Quran verses and sayings of the Prophet about patience, and encouraging it, can be seen.

The prayer, for its part, is an exceedingly important deed among the bodily deeds of observance. It is a meeting with Allah, and a devotion-filled supplication conducted while prostrating before Him. There is nothing to doubt in that Allah will set a special value on that supplication. That, when some unpleasant events are faced, the Prophet ﷺ used to take refuge in the prayer – used to enter quickly into the prayer – having come in hadiths is also because of that. Remember that many of the details needed for the explanation of this verse have passed before, in the commentary on the 45th verse.

*[Verse 154]*

In the previous ayah, it was stated about patience and the prayer. Those who have patience will be ones who have forbearance, and those who have the prayer will be God-fearing, surely. Those who become ready to struggle in the way of Allah and to sacrifice life, too, will be such people. If, at the time of struggle, someone is slain and becomes *[p. 410]* a martyr, though they are outwardly ones who have died, in reality they are not ones who have died; therefore, about them one must not think as of the ordinary ones who have died; they are indeed ones living with Allah. However, you will not be able to understand it – thus and so on Allah makes known. Not that the death of the martyrs (of the shahids) is in fact not death, or that it is not permitted to say about them that they died – that the death of others and their death must not be reckoned on the same footing, indeed – is the meaning of what Allah said. In Al 'Imran verses 169–171 more detail about this can be seen. **إن شاء الله** That food will keep being given to them, and that along with it they will keep being joyful about those who are yet to come and join them, and so on, has been stated there.

That the souls of the shuhada' (of the martyrs), within the insides of some birds that have a green colour, will keep grazing in Paradise through the places they wish; that, seeing the rewards that are to be received by them, they will request Allah to return them to this world and make it so that they fight again in the way of Allah and are slain; and that Allah will reply to them that He has decided that henceforth there is no return – the Prophet ﷺ has stated. (Mu) Beyond what Allah and the Rasul have made known to us, it is not possible for us to infer anything about that spiritual, unseen life of theirs. That is what Allah also said: **وَلَكِنْ لاتَشْعُرُونَ** (yet you will not perceive it). That it is not enough merely to be slain in war or at the hands of enemies; that only if they are ones who went down onto the battlefield with a sincere mind, and not with worldly aims, and were slain, will these gains just mentioned be had – the Prophet ﷺ has made clear in many hadiths. He says again about patience:

*[Verses 155–157]*

*[p. 411]* The trial from Allah is of two kinds. By means of evil and by means of good (**وَنَبْلُوكُمْ بِالشَّرِّ وَالْخَيْرِ فِتْنَةً**)(With evil and with good We will try you, a trial. (21:35) What the true believers must do is to maintain patience and forbearance in evil, and thanks and loyalty in good. If they do so, as seen in the saying of the Prophet quoted in the commentary on the 153rd verse – both kinds of trial will culminate as advantageous indeed for them. If not? Both will not bring about anything other than ill, either. In the past verses, many things were stated about patience. Following that, in these verses Allah is stating about the trials that come about by means of disasters which may be experienced in man from time to time, and also about having to adopt patience in them.

It is five kinds of trials that Allah specifically mentioned:

(1) Fear, the fear that arises from every cause such as war, strife, enemies is included in this.

*[p. 412]* (2) Hunger: meaning starvation that comes about through famine, or through excess of poverty, or through other causes

(3) Loss of wealth : all kinds of losses that come about through destruction and loss befalling the wealth one has, and through income decreasing

(4) Loss of persons : all losses of life that come about among one's own families and among relatives and friends through any cause such as disease epidemics, accidents, wars, catastrophes

(5) Decrease of fruits: decrease of production that occurs in farm produce, fruits and produce of trees and the like. All these are, surely, disasters that often happen. Keeping in front the outward relations of cause and effect, man will often have some causes to state for all these. Yet, all of them are in fact some trials from Allah. That those who adopt patience in them will receive from Allah great favours and good things, and that it is they who are the fortunate ones who have attained right guidance – Allah announces the glad tidings. On the contrary, as far as those who live with impatience and agitation are concerned, the experience will be only the increase of the seriousness of the calamities in this world, and the earning of Allah's displeasure in the Hereafter.

Without saying that He will carry out the trial with all the five matters mentioned above, that He will carry out the trial with a little something of them ( **وَلَنَبْلُوَنَّكُمْ بِشَيْءٍ** ) – that indeed is the word that Allah said. It will come about that you at times experience some or other of these. Then, on account of it, lack of patience and despair must not afflict. On the contrary, relying on patience, one must strive for them to culminate as advantageous for you. That mightier calamities than that not happening must be regarded as generosity from Allah – such indications can be seen to be contained in that word. When a calamity is faced, imagining the bigger calamities that likewise have the possibility of happening will serve to lighten the seriousness of the calamity that happened. Note how Allah has described them, as though giving a definition of the patient. If some calamity befalls them, they will say **إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ** (We are ones who belong to Allah we are also ones who return to Him indeed).

That saying this word when some calamities are faced is good can be understood from this verse and from many hadiths. But there is no benefit merely from having uttered it with the tongue. It must be while understanding the idea contained in it, and while keeping it in mind. They are Allah's creations they are His slaves, their handling of affairs and controls are all in His hand He has the right of authority to *[p. 413]* carry out anything in them, submitting to it is their duty, we are also ones who have to go back to Him after death. For everything we do here, we will have to give answer before Him, no good at all will go to waste for us with Him, and no evil will slip away either without coming to His notice – such and so on is the idea contained in that sentence. When calamities are faced, it is not only announcing the glad tidings that there will be blessings and mercy from their Lord for the patient who, saying that word with this awareness, console themselves, that Allah does. He has also given them a certificate that it is indeed they who are the rightly guided. From this itself, it can surely be surmised that this sentence holds a very great idea. In another place Allah says: **إِنَّمَا يُوَفَّى الصَّابِرُونَ أَجْرَهُمْ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ** (To the patient their rewards will be paid in full without any reckoning at all, indeed (Zumar 10)

Whether the calamities are small or big, all right, it becomes clear from hadiths that when it happens one must say thus ( **إِنَّالِلَّهِ-الخ** ). That can be understood from the ayah itself too. Saying this word is called istirja' ( **اِسْتِرْجَاع** : showing return). That if, after some calamity has happened, when it comes to memory again after quite some time has passed, one renews the istirja', Allah will not fail to give a reward for it, has come in a hadith that Ahmad and Ibn Majah (r) have narrated. As said by Umm Salamah (r), the wife of the Prophet ﷺ, Muslim (r) has narrated thus: When a calamity befalls any servant, if he says **إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ اللَّهُمَّ اَجَرْنِى فِى مصِيبَتِى وَاخْلف لى خَيرا منها** (We are Allah's, we are also ones who return to Him indeed. O Allah, give me a reward in this calamity of mine! And give me in exchange what is more excellent than it !), Allah will not fail to give him a reward in his calamity, and to give him afterwards what is more excellent than it. Thus I have heard the Rasul ﷺ saying. So, when my husband Abu Salamah died, I said as the Rasul ﷺ had commanded. And Allah gave me in exchange a person more excellent than him. Yes, the Rasul of Allah.

*[Verse 158]*

*[p. 414]* Safa and Marwah are two mounds (small hill)s situated near the Ka'bah. Those who do the Hajj and the 'Umrah have to walk between the two of them, to there and to here, seven times. After Ibrahim (a) brought and left Hajar and Isma'il (a) in Makkah and went back, Hajar suffered distress without water for herself and for her infant. When the thirst became severe, thinking that, if she saw some wayfarers going along that way, she could try asking them for water, she laid Isma'il down at the place where the well of Zamzam is now situated, and she climbed up onto each of those hills and looked. She walked between the two, to there and to here, seven times.... This event has been explained in detail in hadiths. (For more detail, see the introductory note of Su. Ibrahim) The greater part of the rites of Hajj are, surely, matters connected with the history of the Prophet Ibrahim (a) and of the Prophet Isma'il (a). That this walking is one that recalls that walking of Hajar – Bukhari (r) has quoted that Ibn 'Abbas (r) stated. It is by the name sa'y ( **سعى** walking quickly) that this is known. It is about this walking that it has been said here, **أَنْ يَطَّوَّفَ بِهِمَا** (to circumambulate between the two of them).

Among the expansion and the reforms that the Saudi government has been carrying out in al-Masjid al-Haram for some years, it has built, *[p. 415]* including Safa, and Marwah, and the walkway between them, in full, a very long and huge building. Therefore, now, those who do the ‘sa'y’ can perform it without being exposed to sun and rain. In the Hajj and in the 'Umrah, the sa'y is obligatory. The reason for Allah saying **لا جُنَاحَ** (there is no wrong), in a way that may make it seem that it is not obligatory, is explained below. The difference between the Hajj and the 'Umrah is this: it is obligatory that the rites of Hajj take place on the fixed days in the month of Dhul-Hijjah. For the 'Umrah there is no fixing of time. Another thing: the main component of the Hajj is assembling at 'Arafah. In the 'Umrah that is not there, moreover.

In the time of Jahiliyyah, the Ka'bah and the Hajj rite and all were, surely, under the supervision of the mushriks. In that period, when the Ka'bah and its surroundings had been filled with idols, an image each had been installed: on Safa, Isaf ( **اساف** ), and on Marwa, Na'ilah ( **نائلة** ). With the conquest of Makkah, among the idols that were all wiped away, those too were removed. Even so, because those two places had been the site of idols and of idol worship, and because what the real place of those two places in Islam is had not been known, a notion arose among the Muslims that carrying out the sa'y between the two is not good, and that it would be wrong. Besides, as some commentators have stated, according to the custom of the Jahiliyyah, those who came for Hajj from Madinah were ones who performed the Hajj rite in the name of the idol called Manat ( **منات** ), and ones who were reluctant to do the sa'y between the images on Safa-Marwah. Their position was that it is enough to circumambulate only the Ka'bah.

That the said notion is not right; that Safa and Marwah are hallowed places that are among the emblems of Allah; that among the rites of Hajj put into practice from Ibrahim (a) indeed onward, the walking between them is also included; and that, because they have been purified of images, there is now no place for any misgivings such as they had supposed in performing that deed – by means of this verse Allah is teaching the true believers. That is why it was said that there is no wrong in those who do the Hajj and the 'Umrah circumambulating through both of them. That, when 'Urwat ibn Zubair (r) asked whether, when it is said that there is no wrong in it, there is surely no objection to not doing it either, 'A'ishah (r) explained to him the background (explained above) in which this verse came down, and made him understand that, since the Rasul ﷺ had done it and put it into practice, it is something that no one is permitted to give up – Bukhari (r) has recorded. Other hadiths too that strengthen this can be seen in Bukhari and Muslim and others.

*[p. 416]* Of the word **شَعِيرَة** (sha'irat), the plural is **شَعَائِرِ** (sha'a'ir). Mark, emblem – is the literal meaning. When one says emblems of Allah or emblems of Islam (**شَعَائِرِ اللَّهِ شَعَائِرُ الاِسْلَام**), all matters that are reckoned as visible marks of observing and respecting the religious commands and the religious doctrines are included in it. What is mainly meant here is the acts of worship that are the Hajj and the 'Umrah, and the places connected with them. When Allah said that these two places are among the emblems of Allah, it is clear that those two are two hallowed places, and that the sa'y between them is an important meritorious deed. Allah says: **وَمَنْ يُعَظِّمْ شَعَائِرَ اللَّهِ فَإِنَّهَا مِنْ تَقْوَى الْقُلُوبِ** (If anyone honours the emblems of Allah, surely it is from the devotion of the hearts (Hajj : 32) **وَمَنْ تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا -الخ** If anyone voluntarily does some good deed, Allah is one who shows thanks, and one who knows all) – this sentence too indeed points to the importance that Allah sets on it.

One fact must be specially borne in mind. The meaning of saying about any holy places that they are emblems of Allah, or that they must be honoured, is not that the soil or the stone or anything else of that place has a kind of divinity, or that by touching and stroking them or the like some matters can be accomplished. What is meant is to observe duly, in fear of God, the deeds appointed to be performed at those places, and not to disrespect or disparage them in any way. Supplications made at those places will also have special consideration with Allah.

In the sentence **وَمَنْ تَطَوَّعَ خَيْرًا -الخ** (If anyone voluntarily does a good deed....) is contained encouragement for all optional deeds that are done besides the obligatory deeds. Even so – when the context is looked at – it can be seen that what is mainly meant here is the Hajj and the 'Umrah done optionally. The walking between Safa-Marwah, for its part, has also not been appointed as a separate deed other than in the Hajj and the 'Umrah. Or, although for those who are able the Hajj and the 'Umrah are obligatory only once in a lifetime, it can be understood from this sentence that doing more is a meritorious deed and something that Allah likes. It is also possible to understand it clearly from hadiths. Then, that what some modern scholars with crooked interests have been saying and propagating – that even for those who are able there is no consideration in Islam for doing the Hajj and the 'Umrah more than once, and so on – is wrong, and that they must fear that the warning in the next verse may be applicable to them too, can be perceived from it. After the Hijrah to Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ is not known to have done other than one Hajj. In fact, some reasons too can be seen *[p. 417]* to have existed for that. There is no proof either as to how many Hajj he may have done during the Makkan period of life. However, it is certain that, after the Hijrah to Madinah, with the knowledge and by the command indeed of the noble Prophet ﷺ, the Sahabah did the Hajj and the 'Umrah more than once. **والله اعلم**

*[Verses 159–160]*

*[p. 418]* Although it has come in some riwayahs that this verse came down about the Jewish-Christian scholars who, without explaining to the people the information that has come in the Torah and in the Injil concerning the noble Prophet ﷺ and his missions, cover it up, there is no doubt that this warning is applicable to all who cover up the truth and do not explain it to the people. Even if the cause of the sending down is particular, that the ruling in the ayahs is applicable to all equivalent events is something beyond dispute.

As said by Abu Hurairah (r), Bukhari (r) quotes: Had it not been for an ayah that is in the Kitab of Allah, I would not have told you any information. Continuing, he also recited this verse. As declared by the Rasul ﷺ, it has been narrated thus from Abu Hurairah (r) too: If anyone is asked about a piece of knowledge and he hides it (without telling it properly), on the Day of Qiyamah a bridle of fire will be put on him (Da;Ti) In a saying of the Prophet narrated from Abu Umama (r), it has come thus. Surely Allah and His angels, and those in the heavens and in the earth – even the ant in its hole – will wish favour upon the person who teaches the people a good thing (Ti). The direct opposite fruit of this great fortune, which the Prophet ﷺ stated is received by those who teach the matters needed by the people, is what Allah has warned of in this verse for those who, despite having knowledge, cover it up. Knowing, and yet deliberately covering it up, is, surely, a severer wrong than not learning and not teaching the religious wisdoms. It is a severe treachery and injustice done to Allah, and to the scripture, to the people, and to one's own soul. Therefore, the consequence faced on account of it will also be exceedingly terrible. Yes, the curse of Allah, and the curses of all who curse !

What is called curse ( **لَعَنة** ) is driving away from kindness and mercy because of the excess of loathing. The intended sense of the curse of Allah is not receiving, in this world and in the Hereafter, His kindness and indulgence and help, and becoming subject to the punishment of the Hereafter. The import of creatures cursing a person will be their having loathing and hostility toward him, and supplicating against him. Without saying it as a single sentence, ‘Allah and those who curse will curse’, in its having been said as two sentences, ‘Allah will curse; those who curse will also curse’, an indication toward the difference between the two curses can be seen. What is meant by its saying, those who curse, is indeed the curse of all those from whom a curse may come. As can be seen in the next verse, the angels and mankind are included in it. So severe an offence is covering up the real. Yet He also promises that Allah will forgive *[p. 419]* those who return in regret. If returning in repentance is to be real and fruitful, one must, surely, unfeignedly regret what has been done, and correct the wrong done, or make clear what was covered up, – and not repeat it. The import of the sentence, except those who repented and set works right and made clear, is indeed all this.
