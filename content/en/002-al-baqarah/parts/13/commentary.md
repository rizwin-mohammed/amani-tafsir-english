*\[Verses 80–82\]*

*\[p. 301\]* That they themselves are the holders of the monopoly right of success and of salvation, that it is they who are the most excellent section with Allah, and so on – that is what the Jews, then and now, have been considering. They have many superstitions and false claims that arose from this. One of them, indeed, is their claim in this manner: the punishment of Hell will not affect us; even if it affects us, it will be there only for a few days; afterwards we will indeed obtain Paradise. Allah is refuting this claim. Allah makes clear that the criterion for reckoning the punishment of Hell and the blessings of Paradise is not racial or a matter of nobility of birth. When their claim is looked at, it may seem that Allah has made some pact with them that He will not punish them and that He will give them Paradise. If there is any such pact, there is no question at all of Allah breaking the pact. But it is, surely, certain that there is none. Then the reality is something else. It is indeed fabricating and saying, in Allah's name, a matter that is not known and that does not exist. The criterion that Allah has fixed for deserving Paradise and Hell is true faith and good deeds. Whoever has earned disbelief and evil works, for them there is the permanent punishment of Hell. Whoever has earned true faith and good works, for them there is permanent life in Paradise. In this, race or nobility of birth has no place at all.

*\[p. 302\]* From the sentence ‘earned evil, and the fault and offence encompassed.’ one matter can be understood. Each time sin is committed, the way of deliverance will keep closing little by little. And as it increases and increases and encompasses, the way of deliverance closes up completely. In a hadith the Prophet ﷺ points out this matter thus: ‘Guard against the sins that are counted as trifling. Because they gather together in a man and at last land him in ruin.’ (Ahmad)

### Section – 10

*\[Verse 83\]*

*\[p. 303\]* Although the verb form **لَا تَعۡبُدُونَ** is in the present form, what is intended here is a prohibitive command. This pact that Allah took from the Israelites is not a pact applicable to them alone. All the true believers are indeed bound to observe each of its clauses today, just as then. Only, in the details of prayer and Zakat there will be some little difference. From this it can be understood that the fundamental beliefs and practices that Allah commands to all communities are indeed one and the same. The first, and the chief of all, is indeed the tawhid that one must not worship other than Allah. Following on it, doing good to parents too. These two matters can be seen mentioned specifically in the Quran in many places in this way – first tawhid and next doing good to parents –. Afterwards it commands to behave in a good way towards close relatives, orphans and the poor in particular, and towards men in general. All these are, surely, fundamental matters of character. Afterwards it speaks about prayer and Zakat, the two most important acts among the bodily and financial acts of ritual observance. The importance the Quran has assigned to these two acts is well known. Although difference will be seen in the modes of observance, these two are, from earlier times on, two important obligatory acts in Islam. These commandments can be seen even today in the Old Testament of the Bible. Looking at the Israelites, Allah says again:

*\[Verses 84–86\]*

*\[p. 305\]* The Arabs in Madinah and its surroundings were two tribes, Aws and Khazraj (**الاَوْس وَالخَزْرَج**). All were indeed idol-worshippers. Between the two groups wars will keep taking place now and then. The Jews in Madinah, for their part, were three tribes, Qainuqa', Nadir and Quraizah (**قَيْنُقَاع، نَضِير، قُرَيْظَة**). The first two tribes had an alliance with the Khazraj, and the third tribe with the Aws. *\[DOUBT: translated as printed. On book p. 190 (part 02) the author writes that the first tribe (Qainuqa') was in alliance with the Khazraj and the last two tribes with the Aws; here the first two are with the Khazraj and the third with the Aws. The book's two statements differ; please decide which the author meant, or whether to note it.\]* When war occurs, each tribe will join the side of its ally and take part in it. Once they have engaged in war, without distinction of Jews or Arabs, they will carry out killing and plunder against each other, drive out from the dwelling-places, and put into bonds. When the war ends, to free those who were made captives from among the Jews, their brothers, they themselves will collect wealth, and, giving the price of release, will rescue them from captivity. If asked about this, their reply will be this: ‘Because betraying allies is disgraceful, we fight together with them. But we have also been commanded to free those who have been made captives.’ It is about this that these verses state.

Expressions such as ‘your blood’ ‘kill yourselves,’ ‘expel yourselves’ indicate that a community of adherents of one religion is like a single body *\[p. 306\]* and that its members are ones who must behave as brothers born of one womb. With their having believed in the Torah and in the Prophet Musa (a), they are obliged to observe all the laws in the scripture. Not only that, they are people who have taken a vow, and have accepted and agreed, that they would obey and observe the rulings of the Torah. That being so, to carry out only some rulings and to reject some others is, surely, unjustified and wrongdoing. It is because of that that Allah reproaches them: is it that you believe in some part of the scripture and disbelieve in some ? He also warns them that the result of this is disgrace in this world and a most severe punishment in the Hereafter. He also points out that the reason for these people's degenerating to this extent, and becoming people who commit paradoxes, is that they sacrificed the otherworldly good things for the sake of the worldly life.

Although these verses came down on the said matter of the Jews, the warning and reproach in this apply to all who affirm and agree to the injunctions and prohibitions of Islam and then carry out only some of them and wholly abandon some. For example, some among the Muslims wholly disregard prayer, the most important obligatory act in Islam, and some others Zakat. At the same time they may also have strictness in some other religious observances. Likewise some can be seen to have adopted the positions of the Jews in the matter of interest, and of the disbelievers in some other evil practices. Some will be attentive in acts of worship and ones who disregard social obligations. Some, the very reverse. What is a great wonder is that many do so while indeed knowing and agreeing to the religious ruling about each one and its seriousness ! May Allah grant the Muslim community the right path and firmness of mind, Amin

### Section – 11

*\[Verse 87\]*

*\[p. 307\]* The tradition of the Israelites is one of having kept defying the preaching and the training of so many prophets, from the Prophet Musa (a) to the Prophet 'Isa (a), and killing many prophets. Allah points out that the reason for this is that, because the messages the prophets bring were not suited to the desires of their selves and to their self-will, they became full of pride and arrogant. About their having killed prophets, it has been described before in the 61st verse and in the commentary.

The Torah is a scripture that has set out in detail legal directions and advice, both personal and social. They have seen many signs as proof of its truthfulness. Afterwards very many prophets also kept coming among them one after another. The aim of all was to put into practice the teachings and messages of the Torah and to invite to it. In Surah Ma'idah Allah says : **إِنَّآ أَنزَلۡنَا ٱلتَّوۡرَىٰةَ .... وَٱلۡأَحۡبَارُ** (Gist: We sent down the Torah. In it there is guidance and light. For those who are *\[p. 308\]* Jews, the prophets, who were ones who had accepted Islam, and the rabbanis – the pious men – and the scholars, used to judge according to it’ (Ma'idah : 44) When 'Isa (a), the last Rasul among the Israelites, came, although a few procedures of the Torah were amended, on the whole it was indeed the code of law of the Torah that he too followed. In the name of that amendment made in the period of his mission, there was the reproach that he was denying the Torah. But Allah gave him many clear proofs and divine signs to establish his truthfulness. (About his signs it is stated in Al 'Imran). Following on that, Allah quotes in this manner what he said to the Israelites : **وَمُصَدِّقٗا لِّمَا بَيۡنَ يَدَيَّ مِنَ ٱلتَّوۡرَىٰةِ** (Gist: It is confirming as true the Torah that is before me, and to make lawful for you some things that had been made forbidden to you, that I have come. I have also brought you a sign from your Rabb. (see Al 'Imran : 49,50)

To its having said **وَأَيَّدۡنَٰهُ بِرُوحِ ٱلۡقُدُسِ** (We strengthened him with the Holy Spirit), more than one interpretation has been given

(1) The Holy Spirit, or Ruh al-Qudus, is the scripture that is the Injil. The justification for this is that the Injil is, surely, what brings about worldly and otherworldly successes and spiritual purity.

(2) What is meant is the soul of 'Isa (a). His having been born without a father, its having been said (in 21:91) that Allah breathed life of His own into his mother, and so on, are the justification given for this.

(3) What is meant by the Holy Spirit is the angel Jibril (a). This is the strong opinion, and also the one that has the support of the Quran and of hadith. About the Quran Allah says **نَزَّلَهُۥ رُوحُ ٱلۡقُدُسِ مِن رَّبِّكَ** (The Holy Spirit has sent it down from your Rabb. (Nahl :102). There is no dispute that this means Jibril (a). That, for Hassan ibn Thabit (**حسان بن ثابت ـ رضـ**), who was the famous poet of the noble Prophet ﷺ, the noble Prophet prayed ‘O Allah, strengthen him with Ruh al-Quds!’, and ‘that the noble Prophet told him that Jibril will be with him’ has come in hadith (Bu. Mu) It can be understood on the whole that the purport of its having said that He strengthened him with the Holy Spirit will be the spiritual help that he used to receive through Jibril (a). Allah says to 'Isa (a): **إِذۡ أَيَّدتُّكَ بِرُوحِ ٱلۡقُدُسِ تُكَلِّمُ ٱلنَّاسَ فِي ٱلۡمَهۡدِ وَكَهۡلٗا** (Gist : The occasion when We strengthened you with the Holy Spirit, while you were speaking to people in the cradle and when you were middle-aged........(Ma'idah : 110)

*\[Verse 88\]*

*\[p. 309\]* Our hearts are lying encased and covered in such a way that nothing new enters into them. Therefore none of the advice that Muhammad and others give goes into our minds – so the Jews used to say mockingly. In Surah Ha-Mim Sajdah 5 Allah says about the disbelievers : **وَقَالُواْ قُلُوبُنَا فِيٓ أَكِنَّةٖ مِّمَّا تَدۡعُونَآ إِلَيۡهِ.....** (They say : Our hearts are in coverings concerning that to which you invite us. In our ears too there is a kind of heaviness. Between us and you – the Prophet – there is a kind of screen.) It is a word like this that the Jews too say. That nothing that is needed enters into their hearts is indeed right. But their being covered or anything of the sort is not the reason for that. Allah gives them the reply that in reality the reason for it is that, because of their disbelief and denial of the truth, they became objects of Allah's curse.

The disbelief of the Jews is, surely, more reprehensible than the disbelief of the Arabs. Because, as is said in the next verse, the denial of the Jews is one made while knowing the reality concerning the Prophet ﷺ, and deliberately. It is from that that their words of mockery, such as that our hearts are covered, have arisen. The reply that Allah gave, rejecting that word, here – **بَل لَّعَنَهُمُ ٱللَّهُ بِكُفۡرِهِمۡ** (But, because of their disbelief, Allah has cursed them) – and, after quoting that same word and refuting it, in Surah Nisa' 155 – **بَلۡ طَبَعَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيۡهَا بِكُفۡرِهِمۡ** (But, because of their disbelief, Allah has stamped a seal over them) – points its finger at this fact (\*) So, because they have become objects of Allah's *\[p. 310\]* curse and wrath, there is no need to expect much belief from them – they will believe only very little – thus Allah informs the Prophet ﷺ.

> **Note:** (\*) The intention of saying that Allah set a seal on hearts and so on has passed in the commentary on the 7th verse.

To its having said ‘they will believe only very little’, interpretation has been given in two ways.

(1) That only very few people from among the Jews will believe in the Prophet ﷺ. In fact, it is well known that those from among the Jews who became Muslim are only very few individuals.

(2) That complete belief cannot be expected from them – they will have only very little belief –. Because they are People of the Scripture, it is natural that they have belief in a few matters here and there. But they will not become ready to believe in every matter that must be believed. The gist is that even the belief they have will not be perfect either. Moreover, the two interpretations are not contrary to each other.
