*\[Verses 6–7\]*

*\[p. 186\]* The root meaning of the verb **كَفَرَ** (kafara) is 'concealed – or covered up'. Because the farmer covers the seed by putting it into the soil, he is called **كَافِرٌ** (kafir). Since the night conceals things with its darkness, it too may be qualified as 'kafir'. Its verbal nouns are the words **كُفْرٌ، كُفْرَانٌ، كُفُورٌ** (kufr, kufran, kufur). However, some slight differences may be seen in the field of usage. For example: in the meanings disbelief and denial of the truth, it is the form 'kufr', and in the meaning ingratitude, the form 'kufran', that are mostly used. As for 'kufur', it is used abundantly in both meanings. The word **كُفْرٌ** (kufr), as the opposite of **إِيمَانٌ** (Iman – true faith), and the word **كَافِرٌ** (kafir), as the opposite of **مُؤْمِنٌ** (mu'min – true believer), are widely used. Likewise, they will be used respectively as the opposite of **شُكْرٌ** (shukr – gratitude) and of **شَاكِرٌ** (shakir – one who is grateful). In the other word-forms that arise from the root of **كُفْرِ** too, both these meanings come. All have to be understood from the context.

All who are not **مُؤْمِنٌ** (true believer) are included in the category of **كَافِرٌ** (disbeliever). But not all 'kafirs' are of the same type. Those who deny while knowing the proofs and evidences *\[DOUBT: ലക്ഷ്യങ്ങളും തെളിവുകളും; ലക്ഷ്യം is taken here in its older sense of "proof, evidence" (as in the open doubt on ലക്ഷ്യ ദൃഷ്ടാന്തങ്ങൾ in part 01); the other possible reading is "the aims and the evidences".\]*, those who deny through ignorance, those who deny with arrogance and mockery, and those who do not believe on account of neglect or thoughtlessness: all are 'kafir' indeed. Only, they will differ in the severity and the lightness of their disbelief. It is as a word that includes all kinds of 'kafirs' that we have been giving the rendering 'disbeliever' for **كَافِرٌ** and 'disbelief' for **كُفْر**. But in each *\[p. 187\]* place, one should take note, looking at the context, on the basis of what kind of denial it has been used there.

What is meant here by **ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُواْ** (those who disbelieved) is the mushriks, deniers of the truth, who, without believing in the principles that the Prophet ﷺ and the Quran preach, cover up the truth and disregard and reject the signs. Allah informs the noble Prophet ﷺ that warning them and not warning them are alike – they are not going to believe. If it is a denial due to ignorance or due to the insufficiency of the signs, there is room to hope that through warning and through counsel a change of heart may come to them. As regards those who, having understood the truth, still deliberately conceal it, none of that will be of use, surely. This itself is the state of all disbelievers who are not ready to lend an ear to counsels and to reflect on the signs. In this notification about those deniers of the truth of the Prophet's ﷺ time, there is also contained the giving of consolation to the noble Prophet. For, the anxiety and grief of the Prophet ﷺ over their not believing were very great. Allah can be seen to have spoken about this in places such as Al-Kahf 6 and Ash-Shu'ara' 3. When he knows that, in that state, he need not expect that they may believe – Allah knows that they will not believe – the Prophet ﷺ can be at ease, surely, that he need no longer be anxious about them and that he need not bear the responsibility for it.

It is the reason for their not believing that is pointed out in the statement **خَتَمَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمۡ** (Allah has set a seal upon their hearts…). The literal meaning of **خَتَمَ** (khatama) is 'sealed'. The verb **طَبَعَ** (taba'a) too is a synonym of it. These, and the other forms from their roots, are also used, in a figurative form, in other meanings. If a vessel or a letter is sealed, it is not possible afterwards to add anything to it or to remove anything from it, surely. Or, with that, it attains security and its final form. Therefore it is usual for them to be used in meanings such as 'made secure and fixed firmly' and 'concluded; completed'. (\*) The meaning of saying that the hearts and the hearing – or the ears – of the disbelievers have been sealed, and so on, is also this very thing. That is, their hearts are filled with denial and arrogance; henceforth no warnings, counsels or signs will enter into them; they are not ready to turn their attention to any of it, or to lend an ear, or to see and understand; their organs of perception have become numb: such is the purport.

> **Note:** (\*) In figurative senses like this, in Malayalam too we say such things as 'sealed it with lac, put a seal on it', surely.

*\[p. 188\]* Some details that Imam Raghib (r) stated in his Quran lexicon, explaining the meanings indicated above of the word 'khatama', are very helpful for understanding the purport of some usages that have come like this – not only here – in many places in the Quran below and in some sayings of the Prophet. The gist of that statement may be quoted thus:–

'Then, what was said as **خَتَمَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمۡ** (Allah sealed their hearts), and **…إِنۡ أَخَذَ ٱللَّهُ سَمۡعَكُمۡ وَأَبۡصَٰرَكُمۡ وَخَتَمَ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِكُم…** (…if Allah takes away your hearing and your sights and seals your hearts … (6:46)), and so on, is an indication of a custom that Allah has put into effect. That is, when man goes to the furthest extent in vain belief and in forbidden deeds, and turning back to look toward the truth ceases, it produces in him a habit in which doing that disobedience and those wrongs seems good, and his heart ends up becoming like one that has been sealed. Like this very thing is the statement **طَبَعَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمۡ وَسَمۡعِهِمۡ وَأَبۡصَٰرِهِمۡۖ** (Allah stamped a seal on their hearts and on their hearing and on their sights (16:108)); and the making heedless in **مَنۡ أَغۡفَلۡنَا قَلۡبَهُۥ** (the one whose heart We have made heedless (18:28)), the placing of coverings in **وَجَعَلۡنَا عَلَىٰ قُلُوبِهِمۡ أَكِنَّةً** (We have made some coverings over their hearts (6:25)), and the hardening in **وَجَعَلۡنَا قُلُوبَهُمۡ قَٰسِيَةٗۖ** (We made their hearts hard (5:14)) *\[TN: the reference is printed "5: 14"; in the verse numbering of the Quran text used on this site, these words are in 5:13.\]*: all are figurative usages of this kind.' (**المفردات**) It is specially pointed out that bearing this quotation well in mind is very useful on many occasions.

Let one more matter that must be borne in mind, when such usages are seen in the Quran and in the Hadith, be pointed out here. What is meant in none of those places is that Allah, at the very beginning, seals the hearts and creates an obstacle in such a way that true faith does not enter the mind, and that because of this they become unable to take in true faith. When their denial and their arrogant nature became full-grown, for that very reason Allah sealed their hearts – or put them into a state in which belief does not pass into them – that is all. This can be understood clearly from some verses of the Quran themselves. Among the reasons that made the Israelites deserving of Allah's curse, Allah quotes their statement, 'Our hearts are wrapped in covers' (none of this preaching of yours will enter into them), and says: **بَلۡ طَبَعَ ٱللَّهُ عَلَيۡهَا بِكُفۡرِهِمۡ** – النساء (But Allah has stamped a seal upon them on account of their disbelief. (4:155)) Speaking of Allah's great indignation at perverse disputing about Allah's signs, Allah says: **كَذَٰلِكَ يَطۡبَعُ ٱللَّهُ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ قَلۡبِ مُتَكَبِّرٖ جَبَّارٖ** – المؤمن (Thus *\[p. 189\]* does Allah stamp a seal upon all the hearts of those who are arrogant and self-willed. (40:35)).

For each matter in this world, Allah has appointed some ordinary causes. When those causes come together, we see those matters coming to pass. Or, on that occasion, Allah brings it to pass. It is, surely, because it is Allah who brings things to pass that sometimes, even though all the causes known to us are present, we see some matters not coming to pass. From what has just been said, the purport of saying that Allah sealed – or that the doer of the sealing is Allah – can be grasped. In this usage there is also indicated Allah's severe aversion toward those deniers of the truth. **والله أعلم** (And Allah knows best.)

After speaking, in the opening verses of the surah, about the true believers, and then, in these two verses, about the disbelievers, who are mushriks, He speaks in a few following verses about the hypocrites. Besides the mushriks, the two particular enemies of Islam in Madinah were, surely, the hypocrites (**ٱلۡمُنَٰفِقُونَ**) and the Jews, who were People of the Scripture. Therefore He has spoken here comparatively somewhat at length about the hypocrites. As for the People of the Scripture, Allah can be seen to have devoted a great many ayahs in this very surah to them. Allah says about the hypocrites:

### Section – 2

*\[Verses 8–10\]*

*\[p. 190\]* Hypocrisy (**نِفَاقٌ**) is the disagreement that arises between word and deed, and between the secret and the open. Hypocrisy may occur in beliefs and in practices. Hypocrisy in belief is the most serious. Allah has dealt in the Quran, on various occasions, with all kinds of hypocrisy. It is about the hypocrites – who kept fostering complete hypocrisy in the very fundamental belief of Islam, having kufr within and adopting the Islamic garb outwardly – that it is spoken in these ayahs.

It is in the Madinan surahs that the references to the Munafiqs are. In Makkah there were no Munafiqs. On the contrary, those who were in Makkah were some who, while actually accepting true faith in reality, were compelled, at the same time, on account of weakness, to conceal it and display disbelief outwardly. For, the dominance and power there belonged to the mushriks, surely. When the Prophet ﷺ came to Madinah, the Arabs there were people of two tribes, namely the Aws and the Khazraj. Although formerly they too were idol-worshippers like the mushriks of Makkah, even before the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions arrived there, Islam had begun to gain propagation among them. With the coming of the Prophet ﷺ, it kept increasing further too. Another group who were in the surroundings of Madinah were the Jews. They were people who had been following their Jewish tradition. The Jews were of three tribes: Banu Qainuqa', Banu Nadir and Banu Quraizah (**قَيْنُقَاءُ، قُرَيْظَة، نَضِير**) *\[TN: the first Arabic name is printed with a final ء (قينقاء); the usual spelling is قينقاع, and the author's own Malayalam ബനൂക്വൈനുക്വാഉ് has the ع sound. Transcribed as printed.\]*. The first tribe was in alliance with the Khazraj, and the last two tribes with the Aws. Although a great many people from among the Arabs had accepted Islam, *\[p. 191\]* from among the Jews only Abdullah ibn Salam (r) and very few people had come into Islam. At this time there were no Munafiqs in Madinah. At that time the Muslims had not yet attained a power to be feared. Therefore there was no need for a hypocritical guise. The Prophet ﷺ, for his part, had made treaties of alliance with the Jews and with many Arab tribes in the surrounding regions.

Although he was of the Khazraj tribe, Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul was a leader who had come to hold common leadership among both the Aws and the Khazraj. Deliberations had been going on to make Abdullah the king of all. It was at that time that Islam came to have the influence described above. As many were attracted to Islam, that plan did not go forward. Because of this, Abdullah ibn Ubayy, who was hungry for leadership, came to feel hatred toward Islam and the Muslims. With the very famous battle of Badr over, the prestige, dominance and power of the Muslims became strong, surely. When he saw the power of Islam increasing more than before, he and his allies and sympathisers too outwardly accepted Islam and acted as Muslims. Some from among the People of the Scripture were also in this group. It is from here that the beginning of the 'Munafiqs' is. Gradually, in Madinah and among the 'A'rab' (desert-dwellers) in the surrounding regions too, their number increased. Among the Muhajirs who came in Hijrah from Makkah, the disease of 'nifaq' (hypocrisy) had not afflicted anyone. None of them were, surely, people who had accepted Islam yielding to the compulsion or the influence of others, or people who had accepted true faith for the pleasure of anyone at all other than Allah. (**كَمَا فِي إِبْنِ كَثِيرٍ**) (as in Ibn Kathir).

Although Abdullah ibn Ubayy and his men were in reality disbelievers indeed, outwardly they had accepted the religion of Islam and were Muslims, surely. In that state, taking account of their outward condition, there was a chance for the true believers to be deceived, and, unawares, for many unpleasant happenings to come about through them. Mistaking the wicked for the good paves the way for many dangers, surely. Therefore the true believers need to be always vigilant concerning the Munafiqs. Through these verses Allah alerts the true believers to this fact. He informs them that there is here also a third camp, which you must keep guarding against with great care, and that their real state is such-and-such. When they come to the Prophet ﷺ and the true believers, they will display by word and by manner that they are Muslims who have accepted true faith. The purport of the 8th verse is that in reality belief has not even touched them. In Surah Al-Munafiqun this matter is stated thus: '(O Prophet,) when the hypocrites come to you, they will say: We bear witness that you *\[p. 192\]* are indeed the Messenger of Allah. Allah knows that you are indeed His Messenger. And Allah bears witness that the hypocrites are surely liars indeed.' See the 14th verse below.

In this acting of theirs, some true believers who are pure of heart may sometimes be deceived. But the true believers in general will not be deceived by it. And it is not possible, surely, to deceive Allah. Even so, their notion is that no one will know their inner deceit. Perhaps they may have come to assume that even Allah does not know their real state. It can be seen that about them Allah has stated in 58:18: 'On the Day of Resurrection they will swear to Allah and speak, just as they swear to you.' Then, even if it turns out that they do not have that notion about Allah, the deceit they practise toward the true believers, who are Allah's party, is deceit practised toward Allah too, surely. Through this hypocrisy, so contemptible as this, their aim is only some worldly, temporary gains. But its result will be the utmost degradation and disgrace. And in the Hereafter, extremely severe punishment too! Then, in reality, their deceit does not affect anyone other than themselves. Those hypocrites do not grasp any of this – on account of their stupidity and their foolishness. They do not have the good sense for it either.

The purport of saying that there is a kind of disease in their hearts can be understood, surely, from what was said above. Hypocrisy, denial and the like are the symptoms of that disease. Whenever each verse of the Quran is revealed, and whenever each sign is seen, the reaction that arises in them is that very thing. In this way that disease keeps increasing. Allah says: **وَإِذَا مَآ أُنزِلَتۡ سُورَةٞ** – … **إلى قوله** (up to His words) **كَٰفِرُونَ** – التوبة. Gist: 'Whenever any surah is sent down, some among them will say (mockingly): Which of you has this increased in faith? But as for those who have believed, it has increased them in faith. And they will rejoice. As for those in whose hearts there is disease, it will increase them in filth on top of their filth. And they will die while they are disbelievers.' (Tawbah 124, 125) The purport of saying that Allah increased disease for them can be understood from this.

*\[Verses 11–12\]*

*\[p. 193\]* It is with reference to their various kinds of doings – denial of the truth, deceiving the true believers, creating confusion of ideas among the people, action contrary to their word, secret relations with the enemies of Islam, and so on – that they are told not to cause corruption in the land. All of these, surely, are things that produce bloodshed, loss of life and unrest in the land. When told that they should keep away from such deeds, not only are those hypocrites not ready to accept it, or even to give a favourable reply; what they will do is claim that everything they do is good for the land and necessary. They will also justify their doings, saying things such as that what they intend by their alliance with the enemy camp is to bring about reconciliation and unity between the Muslims and them. All this is sheer lies; it is only works of corruption that they keep carrying out; it is the activities of such two-faced people that in reality bring about corruption and confusion in the land; they have neither the sense to grasp any of this nor the discernment to distinguish good and corruption: such is the gist of what Allah said about their reply.

The number of those who bear the name of Muslim and with whom the marks and natures that Allah has stated, here and in many other places, as belonging to the hypocrites agree – either wholly or in part – keeps growing as time goes on – especially in this age. Because they are people who were born and grew up in a Muslim family and community, they will have reluctance or lack of courage to say openly that they are not Muslims. If need be, they will even pretend that they are well-wishers of the Muslims and of Islam and reformers of the community. Their real capital will be godless, faithless rationalist arguments that undermine Islam, and materialistic interests. Their aim will be to destroy the belief and religious devotion of the Muslim masses without their knowing it, and to make them put on a guise that keeps up with the times in the name of progress. The true believers must guard against such people more than against the open enemies of Islam (**والله المستعان**) (and Allah is the One whose help is sought).

*\[Verses 13–16\]*

*\[p. 195\]* You say, surely, that you have accepted true faith and that you are Muslims, and so on; but your actions and your conduct are diametrically opposed to it. If what you say is true, you should change this position and accept a belief like that which the real true believers have believed: such things are the purport of saying **ءَامِنُواْ كَمَآ ءَامَنَ ٱلنَّاسُ** (Believe as the people have believed). It is clear that what was spoken of here as 'the people' is the true believers who have already believed in the Prophet ﷺ. In calling them 'the people', some hints can be seen to be contained, such as: it is they who are the real human beings – you too must become people having the quality of humanity like them – this position of yours is not befitting humanity. **الله أعلم** (Allah knows best). It is the very same true believers, among whom the Prophet ﷺ too is included, that the hypocrites qualified as fools. What they indirectly suggest in their reply is such things as: it is because they are all sheer fools that they become absorbed in that belief to that extent; we have not sunk so low as that; you will not get us to accept a belief like theirs. The fact is the very opposite. Allah points out that the real fools are none other than they, but that they have not the soundness of mind and the discernment to understand that.

The hypocrites are, surely, two-faced people. When they come to the true believers, they will act as if they too were true believers. Their aim is to please the Muslims, and to get a share in such things as 'ghanimah' (the property that may be obtained in war). When they leave that place and go to their real companions and friends, they will tell them openly what is in their minds. 'You need not misunderstand on seeing us behave with the Muslims. In reality we are your partisans indeed. It is only for mockery that we approach them. Our aim too is your good': and so on. It is about this that it is spoken in the 14th verse. That Allah is not going to let this mockery of theirs go *\[p. 196\]* for nothing is pointed out in the 15th verse. In this world and in the next, Allah will indeed mock them and scorn them. For the time being, what He does is to let them loose, saying, 'Let them go about as they please, losing their senses.' He does not take action at once. They will not be able to escape without coming into Allah's grip.

What was said as **شَيَٰطِينِهِمۡ** (their devils) is their friends and associates among the Jews, who are open enemies of Islam, and among the leaders of shirk. The purport of saying **ٱللَّهُ يَسۡتَهۡزِئُ بِهِمۡ** (Allah will mock them) is that He will take punitive action on account of their acts of mockery. **وَجَزَـٰٓؤُاْ سَيِّئَةٖ سَيِّئَةٞ مِّثۡلُهَا** (The recompense of an evil is an evil like it. (42:40)) The gist is that the punitive action on account of it will be in accordance with the offence.

It is accepting another thing in exchange for a thing in one's possession that is called buying with a price, and also doing trade. It is when one sees that the thing obtained here in exchange is more profitable or more useful than what is given there that buying and selling usually takes place naturally. At the least, what was given there and what was obtained here must be ones reckoned to be of equal value. Otherwise that trade turns out to be loss-making. As regards the Munafiqs, not only was their trade not profitable; it did not even happen that the capital they had put in was saved for them. That too came to be lost. As it has been reported that Qatadah (r) stated: what they did was to slip away from right guidance to misguidance, from unity to division, from security to fear, and from good conduct to bad conduct.

Allah has given human beings the intelligence and the power of thought to discern and to search out good and bad. Allah also sent them a Messenger. He invited them to the truth face to face. He also laid open before them a scripture setting out the proofs *\[DOUBT: ലക്ഷ്യങ്ങളും ദൃഷ്ടാന്തങ്ങളും; as above, "the proofs and the signs" (older sense of ലക്ഷ്യം) or "the aims and the signs".\]* and the signs. Whatever there might be in their minds, they record by word of mouth their belief in it, and when occasion comes, they keep repeating it and acting it out. In this way, the capital that is right guidance came into their hands. What they did was not to make use of it or develop it – it was to reject it, and in exchange deliberately take up misguidance; this is the trade they did. They are people who fell into misguidance without knowing what right guidance is, or without getting an opportunity to attain it. *\[DOUBT: printed ദുർമാർഗത്തിൽ പതിച്ചവരാണവർ, "they are people who fell into misguidance" (affirmative). The next sentence ("What they did was to throw away right guidance after it had come into their hands") suggests the author may have meant the negative, പതിച്ചവരല്ല അവർ, "they are not people who fell …".
