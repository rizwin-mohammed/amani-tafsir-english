*[Verse 269]*

*[p. 653]* Both that frightening that poverty will come through spending wealth, and prompting to vile conducts, are the task of Satan, and that it is the way to attain forgiveness of sins and favour that Allah counsels, were said in the previous verse. Many matters connected with spending in the way of Allah were described before that too. They are also being described further on. But, who it is that will have the fortune both to think about and evaluate such matters as is needed, and to bring them into practice and make use of them, is indeed what Allah, in between, makes us understand in this verse. It is a principle that is very meaningful and pregnant with gist that is contained in this.

For the word **حِكْمَةَ** (hikmah), ‘wisdom, philosophy, sagacious principle,’ and the like are what are usually assigned as meaning. What is meant by it, on the whole, is the knowledge that serves both to grasp a matter in its true form and to bring it into action. What is intended by ‘hikmah’ here can be seen described by many great men in many words too. Intellect, power, learning, fright concerning Allah, Quranic wisdom, the Prophet's way of life, the power of grasping matters, word and works being in agreement with the real, and so on, are the summary of them. In reality all these go and culminate in the meaning stated above. It can be said conclusively that, as the words of some who are people with material interests insinuate, it is not tactical cleverness, or the wisdoms of philosophical science, that is meant by it. From what is stated below, that will become more clear.

Ibn Kathir (r) quotes Imam Malik (r) as having said: ‘That hikmah means both the wisdom concerning religion, and the awareness that arises in hearts on account of the favour and mercy of Allah, is indeed what seems to me. When a man thinks on a worldly subject, you see him as a man of reason. Another one you see both as weak in worldly matters, and as one who has knowledge and insight in religious matters. This is what Allah gives to this person. To the other person it has gone without being obtained. Then, ‘hikmah’ will mean the wisdom in the religion of Allah. ‘In a hadith that both Bukhari, and Muslim (r) quoted, the Prophet ﷺ says: ‘To him for whom Allah intends good, He brings about wisdom in religion. I am only one who distributes (wisdom), it is Allah who (in reality) gives.’

‘Hikmah’ does not become obtainable merely because of having acquired quite some knowledges, or education, alone; only when the fortune also to make use of them in such a manner as to become a vessel for Allah's favour and recompense comes about does it become obtainable. This matter can be understood from the holy words of Allah indeed, both ‘To those whom Allah wills He gives hikmah’ and ‘To whomever hikmah is given, to him abundant goodness has already been given’. From the contexts in which, in places such as 4:54; 2:251; 38:20, Allah has used this word *[p. 654]*, and from the hadith quoted above, and from the statement of Imam Malik (r), it will become more clear. As Ibn Kathir (r) pointed out, the most exalted rank of hikmah is prophethood. To the followers of the prophets too hikmah is given according to their fortune. That ‘The fright concerning Allah is the head of hikmah (or the main part)’ has come in a hadith. In another famous hadith the Prophet ﷺ says: ‘There must be no envy except in two matters. That is, both the man who, Allah having given wealth, employs it entirely in the subject that is required, and the man who, Allah having given hikmah, both carries out judgement according to it, and teaches it (to others),’ (Bu; Mu.) What has been intended here by envy is not feeling hatred and displeasure at what they have obtained; it is the wish that oneself too must have, like them. That can also be understood from some riwayahs of the hadith indeed.

It is by saying **وَمَايَذَّكَّرُإِلاأُولُو الألْبَابِ** (Other than those who have good intellect, none will reflect intently) that Allah ends this verse. This is a very noteworthy sentence. Only those who have obtained hikmah will both know matters by thinking as is needed, and bring it into action in the due manner. That it is such ones who are the real men of reason is what this shows. The meaning ‘those who have good intellect’ that we gave is for the word **أُولُوالألْبَابِ**. Of the term **لُب** (lubb) the plural is **ألْبَاب** (albab). ‘Kernel, core, essence, fat’ and such is its actual linguistic meaning. What is meant by it is intellect of a wholly pure course, without the admixture of conjecture or the leaning of one's own interest. That is why it is seen said thus, describing its meaning, both in ‘al-Mufradat’ of Imam Raghib (r), which is the dictionary of the Quran, and in ‘al-Munjid’, which is a modern dictionary: Then, every ‘lubb is intellect; not every intellect is ‘lubb’. It is as an indication towards this special feature that in the translation we have given it the rendering ‘those who have good intellect’. Imam Raghib (r) says, continuing: That Allah speaks of the religious rulings, which it is not possible to understand except for holy intellects, connecting them with ‘ulul albabu’ is also because of that.’ As support for this, he has also cited this verse indeed.

To put it briefly, men of reason in a special meaning, which is not contained in words like **اهلالعقول،عقلاء** that are usually used in the Arabic language in such meanings as ‘men of reason, the intelligent’, are what have been intended by **أُولُوالألْبَاب**. What is intended by ‘lubb’ is not intellect of the kind that becomes capable of scientific discoveries, theories pertaining to philosophical science, cleverness for making authority and influence one's own, and the like. More information concerning this can be seen in verses 7 and 190 of Surah Al 'Imran. **إن شاء الله**

*[Verses 270–271]*

*[p. 655]* About all expenses that are made either in the capacity of almsgiving or otherwise, and about all kinds of vows- whether they be small or big, secret or open, with purity of intention or otherwise- Allah knows. Nothing *[p. 656]* will fail to come into His account. He will also give recompense befitting each. Not spending on the subject that is required, and spending in ways that Allah does not like, and spending keeping some other aim than the recompense from with Allah, are both wrong and injustice. As for those who do wrong, on the Day of Qiyamah they will not obtain any deliverance at all, or help, moreover; such and so on is the gist of the first verse. There is no objection at all to doing almsgiving openly in such a manner that others see. It is very good indeed. Because it happened to be open, Allah will not fail to accept it. But, only that it must be both with a pure mind and intending the pleasure of Allah. However, the spending being both secret, and for the poor section, is more excellent than that. It is in that that there is more merit. Not only will Allah give mighty recompense to almsgivings- especially to those who do it privately-, it will also become a cause for forgiving many sins that have been done. Allah knows minutely all matters such as who all do what all, how much recompense each merits. He will also take action in accordance with that. Such and so on is the gist of the second verse.

Making a pledge by oneself that one will do some meritorious deed that is not obligatory is what is called **نَذْر** (vow). This also belongs to the category of worship. It is for that reason that it has been prescribed that all vows may be only to Allah. If a matter is made a vow, performing it becomes obligatory. One may not make a vow of other than meritorious matters, moreover. The Prophet ﷺ has stated in this manner: ‘If anyone makes a vow to obey Allah (to do something belonging to the category of obedience), let him obey Him (Allah). If anyone makes a vow to show disobedience to Him (to do something that is disobedience), he must not show disobedience to Him either.’ (Bu) That ‘There is no vow at all in disobedience.’ the noble Prophet has also said. (Mu) In the famous hadith that states about the seven parties to whom Allah will give shade on the day on which there is no other shade other than the shade of Allah- on the Day of Qiyamah-, it is ‘the man who spends wealth privately in such a manner that the left hand does not know what the right hand spends’ that the Prophet ﷺ has counted as one. (Bu; Mu) He who does almsgiving privately does not covet anything else other than the pleasure of Allah, surely. That is the reason for this.

Although charity being given to the rich is rare, it may come about that charity is sometimes given to those too who have no substantial straitness or difficulty. Sometimes one may also have to give without the rich- poor difference being known. Even so, that is a good matter indeed. It may be because of that that both, when speaking about making almsgivings open, Allah did not make clear whether to the poor or to the rich, and, when speaking about what is more good, along with making it secret He also added and said that it *[p. 657]* be for the poor. **اللّه اعلم** That secret charity is more excellent than open charity is a general principle indeed. On some occasions a difference may come about in that. For example, what a person spends may become a model for others. Or, on account of his either not spending, or others not knowing what he spends, misunderstanding, or ill to the common good, may come about. Scholars point out that on occasions of this kind, making it open will be what is good.

*[Verse 272]*

*[p. 658]* Preaching, counselling, showing right guidance, it is these and the like that are the responsibility of the Prophet ﷺ. Other than that, getting it acknowledged and joining to the straight way is not the liability of the noble Prophet. That is the work of Allah. Those whom He wills He will put on the straight way. Whom it is that must be put on the straight way, who it is that is deserving of that, and so on, it is Allah, surely, who will know. This point is what Allah has made clear in many places in the Quran.

That there is a reason for bringing up this matter in the midst of treating of almsgivings can be understood from many riwayahs. Their summary is this: it came about that many among the true believers both stopped the concessions that they had been giving to their relatives and own people who were disbelievers, and were reluctant to give almsgivings to them. That, before this verse came down, the Prophet ﷺ too had counselled that charity must not be given except to Muslims has also come in some riwayahs of Ibn Abi Hatim (r) and others. Not only from the hatred at their not accepting true belief; it was also in order to prompt them towards true belief, that. Through this verse He informs that that method need not be continued. It is not that doing almsgivings must be to Muslims indeed. It may be to those of any religion. To whomever it is given, that is a good matter indeed. You do not have the liability for their not having accepted right guidance. That matter will be between Allah and them. Any almsgiving that is done intending the pleasure of Allah- however trifling it may be, no matter- it is you indeed who will enjoy the fruit of doing it. Allah will not waste anything. Without bringing about any reduction at all in the recompense, He will indeed fulfil it. Such and so on is the import of what was said further on.

There is no need to suppose that the charities given to both believers and disbelievers, and the charities given to both the righteous and the wicked, will be alike in every respect. That giving is a meritorious deed indeed, of whatever kind those who take may be, is the only meaning that what was said above has. Obligatory charities like Zakat need also to be given to Muslims indeed. If one sees that the wealth given will be employed either against Islam, or in ways of misguidance, then to such people either almsgivings or donations are also what must not be given. That according to the situation of both the people to whom it is given, and the subjects on which it is spent, there will be difference in the importance of almsgivings, can also be understood from the next verse.

*[Verse 273]*

*[p. 659]* That doing almsgiving, to whomever it be, is a good matter was pointed out in the previous verse. But, who are the most deserving of it is stated in this verse. Quite some Companions who were both ones who had come on Hijrah in Makkah *[DOUBT: മക്കായിൽ is printed, literally “in Makkah”; the sense is probably “from Makkah” (മക്കായിൽനിന്ന്). Rendered as printed.]* and ones who had no dwelling-place or means of livelihood were staying on the mosque verandah of Madinah and in its surroundings. They were ones immersed in services of the din such as earning Islamic wisdom, taking part in struggles and military actions according to the direction of the noble Prophet ﷺ. They, who were known by the name ‘the Suffah people’ ( **اَصْحَابُ الصفة** ) (those who stay on the verandah), were more or less three hundred persons. Allah alerts that it is to these that preference must be given in almsgivings. Although in this age there are no Suffah people- even if only partially-, both that, like this, in all ages, preference needs to be given also to those who, like them, having engaged in services of the din such as the earning of religious wisdom, suffer difficulty without means for a way of living, and that spending wealth for them is a very important meritorious deed, can also be grasped from this verse. That Allah has brought up some exemplary characteristics of the Suffah people is specially worthy of attention:-

*[p. 660]* (1) They are ones who have been restrained in the way of Allah. Yes, they have no material intentions at all. For the sake of the religion of Allah they renounced both country and home and came away. Both earning the pleasure of Allah, and practising the sacrifices that they are able to for the sake of Islam, are the aim of their life.

(2) They do not have the circumstances and convenience for carrying on trade, cultivation and the like, travelling through the earth.

(3) At the same time, those who do not know about their real state of affairs will suppose about them that they are merely rich men. Because, they are ones who safeguard self-respect that much. In their behaviour, or in speech, they will not display indications of poverty, and of straitness.

(4) But, through some special signs- humility, composure, pale face, sunken belly, exhausted body, torn and old garment, and suchlike marks- it will be possible to understand their real situation. Because of the severity of hunger, some among them would fall down in the mosque at the time of the prayer, and the Arabs who were people of the interior, when seeing it, would suppose that they had madness. In a hadith that Bukhari (r) recorded, Abu Hurairah (r) is saying: ‘I have seen seventy persons among the people of the Suffah. Not even one among them had an upper wrap (second cloth) *[DOUBT: തട്ടം is a cloth worn over the head or shoulders, and മുണ്ട് a wrap-around cloth; “upper wrap” and “second cloth” render them. The options are “head-cloth” or “shawl” for തട്ടം.]*. Either a cloth, or a coarse blanket (woollen blanket). That they will have tied on the shoulders. Some would reach up to half the shank, and some up to the ankle. So that the private part would not be seen, they would keep holding it together (with the hand).’

(5) Even so, they will not either beg of others, or put to difficulty by asking for something. Even at an unavoidable juncture, if they request something of anyone, it will be in a decent manner and in the form of an indication. The necessity of giving preference to these is clear from all this, surely.

How very much begging, and asking others and taking, have been deemed as reprehensible in Islam can be understood from many sayings of the Prophet such as are seen below:

(1) This is the gist of a saying of the Prophet that Bukhari, Muslim (r) and others have recorded through many routes: ‘Not these who wander about, who go back if given one or two dried dates, or one or two handfuls of food, are the needy. The needy one is he who remains both without wealth with which he can manage without dependence on others, and without others being able to understand about him, so that he does not obtain almsgiving, and without asking anything of human beings.’

(2) Another saying of the Prophet that both Bukhari and Muslim (r) quoted: ‘If one of you is one who keeps on asking and taking, at the time when he meets Allah *[p. 661]* (on the Day of Qiyamah) there will not be even a pinch of flesh on his face.’

(3) ‘If anyone asks of the people and takes in order that wealth may increase, it is live coals that he is asking for. Therefore let him either try to reduce (the asking), or else try to increase (at will).’ (Mu.)

(4) Whoever tries to guard honour, for him Allah will guard honour. Whoever displays self-sufficiency *[DOUBT: ധന്യത, from ധന്യൻ “Self-Sufficient” (part 46, with an open doubt); literally “richness”.]*, to him Allah will give self-sufficiency.’ (Bu; Mu.)

(5) The gist of what the Prophet ﷺ said on one occasion to the Companion named Khabisah (r) is this: ‘Asking and taking is permitted only for three persons; for him who has taken on some liability of debt, until he obtains it, and for him whose wealth has perished, some great calamity having happened, until he obtains the means for essential living, and for the person whom poverty has seized in such a manner that three men of reason who are people of the locality say that poverty has befallen such-and-such a person, until he obtains the means for essential living. Afterwards he must stop. Asking other than this- Khabisa- is haram. He who eats it is eating haram. (Mu.)

### Section – 38

*[Verse 274]*

That for those who spend wealth on good causes without difference of night and day, both secretly and openly, according to occasion and capacity, aiming at the pleasure of Allah, Allah has prepared and kept ready mighty recompenses that are their own, and that, when they go to Allah, it will not fail to be obtained by them, and that no fear or grief of any kind will come upon them, *[p. 662]* Allah announces as glad tidings. Outlay of wealth on every subject that is required will be included in this. A saying that the Prophet ﷺ declared to Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (r) in a famous hadith is worth remembering here; ‘Any expense indeed that you spend seeking the face - pleasure- of Allah, through it rank, and elevation, will not fail to increase for oneself- up to the food that one gives into the mouth of one's wife’ (Bu; Mu.) Another saying of the Prophet that Ibn Mas'ud (r) quotes; ‘If a Muslim spends for his household, taking into account the recompense (of Allah), that will become a charity on his part.’ (Bu; Mu.)
