*[Verse 186]*

*[p. 470]* In the previous verses, many things were stated about fasting. At the end, glorifying Allah, and showing thanks to Him, were advised. Continuing, in this verse supplication is spoken about. In the next verse, it is again matters connected with fasting that are dealt with. From the matter of supplication being mentioned in between, and from its being urged, it can be understood that there is a connection between fasting and supplication – that supplication in the time of fasting has special importance. That it has come in hadiths that the supplication that the one who is fasting makes when he opens the fast will receive a special answer, and that it has been narrated as a riwayah that, for this reason, Ibn 'Umar (r), at the time of opening the fast, used to gather his household and his children together and pray, are worth stating. (See Ibn Kathir) For those who examine with attention this verse, and the verses before and after it, it will be possible to understand the special connection between fasting and supplication.

But – as pointed out earlier – in the sight of those of the new opinion that the ruling, that it is enough even if those who have ability for fasting give a ransom instead without observing fasting, is in force even today, it is natural, surely, that fasting, or the connection between fasting and supplication, should not have any very great place at all. It may be because of that that some among them are seen making a new linking between this ayah and the ayahs before it. Explaining the intended meanings of this ayah, they say: ‘The old notion that one can attain nearness to God only if one torments the self by lying in starvation is wrong. If My slaves ask you about Me (you say: they need not struggle in order to approach Me) I am near (to them)........’ That the mindset behind these sentences is anything other than a lowering a little of the place that has been assigned to fasting in Islam – its spiritual side – we do not understand. Some other usages of this sort, too, are wont to be seen from these and the like. As regards the sunnah fasts known from the practice of the Prophet ﷺ, the statements of people of this sort in a very contemptuous tone are some of them.

That, following an A'rabi (an Arab who is a desert-dweller) having asked, ‘Is our Rabb near? Then we may converse with Him softly. Is He far? Then we may call out to Him’, this verse *[p. 471]* came down, and that it is something that came down following some people having enquired, ‘Where is our Rabb?’ – some riwayahs such as these can be seen. Even if in reality such a question did not occur, it may also be that Allah taught the Prophet ﷺ that, if anyone asked thus, the answer must be said in such and such a way. (Allah knows) In any case, without saying ‘if My slaves ask (**إِذَاسَأَلَكَ عَبِيدى**)’ or something else, in the usage ‘if My servants ask (**إِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِي**)’ an idea is implied: that the people who are My slaves, who worship Me, will have a wish to know about Me – therefore tell them –. (\*) Allah is always near indeed to His servants. He will always hear and know all their supplications, He will not let their requests go to waste, therefore they need not look for a special time or place for submitting requests before Him, nor need they add to it the mediation or intercession of another person, nor need they call out loudly in order to make Him hear – Allah, by means of this verse, commands the Prophet ﷺ to tell the people these and such things. That, in the situation that I am always ready to give answer to their calling on Me in supplication, they too must answer My call by way of obeying My commands and directions, and must have believed in Me properly – this too He alerts. For the word **فَلۡيَسۡتَجِيبُواْ لِي** the meaning ‘therefore let them seek of Me that I give answer’ may also come. Even if this meaning is accepted, in the end that too will culminate indeed in the gist explained above. Because, there is no meaning in requesting Him to give answer, other than while obeying Allah and believing in Him. Even so, it is the meaning indicated first that the Quran commentators are seen to have generally accepted for that word. What seems better, too, is that indeed.

> **Note:** (\*) For the statements of Imam Raghib (r) about the differences in kind in **عَبْد** ('Abd), meaning slave, and about the difference between the words **عِبَاد، عبيد** ('Ibad, 'Abid), which are its plurals, see the explanation of the 182nd verse of Su: Al 'Imran.

‘Called, prayed, requested, begged, sought’ – the word **دُعَاء** (Du'a') is the root of the verb **دَعَا** (Da'a), to which meanings such as these come according to the occasion. Another word of this same kind is **دَعْوَة** (Da'wah) too. When one says in Malayalam ‘called Allah, sought of Him’ and the like, that will be in the meaning of ‘called Him and prayed’, surely. Likewise, when these words too are said joined with Allah, it is in the meaning of calling Him and praying that they are usually used. Supplication, for its part, is moreover one of the various forms of worship. Not only that, the core aspect of worship is indeed supplication. The Prophet ﷺ says: ‘Du'a' (supplication) indeed is 'iba*[p. 472]*dah (worship).’ Continuing, (as proof of this) the noble Prophet also recited the 60th verse of Surat al-Mu'min. (A; Ti; Da; Na.) The gist of the said verse is this: ‘Your Rabb says: Call on Me and pray. I will answer you. Surely, those who show arrogance about worshipping Me will afterwards enter Hell in the state of despicable ones.’ In this verse, it is about the supplication said first that ‘worship’ has been used the second time. In another hadith, the sentence that the Prophet ﷺ said is: ‘Du'a' is the marrow of 'ibadah.’ – thus. (Ti.) It is because of this, too, that, when speaking about tawhid in worship, and about shirk, more mention is seen made of ‘du'a'’ (calling on and praying), beyond the other rites of worship.

The import of having said **إِنِّي قَرِيبٌ** (I am One who is near) is not that I am situated in a place near the one who prays. Its import can be clearly understood from the hadiths quoted below: Many, such as Ahmad, Bukhari, Muslim (r), have recorded thus, as stated by Abu Musa al-Ash'ari (r): ‘When we were on a war journey together with the noble Rasul ﷺ, whenever we climbed to some hill, or were on top of a hill, or went down into some valley, indeed, we would not fail to say ‘takbir’ raising our voices. Then the noble Prophet said: ‘O people, show moderation to your selves. It is not one who is deaf, or one who is not present, that you are calling; it is One who hears and sees (all) that you are calling. Surely, the One you call is one who is closer to him than the neck of each one of you..........’ Imam Ahmad (r) quotes thus from Abu Hurairah (r), as having heard the Prophet ﷺ say: Whenever My servant remembers Me, and his lips keep moving about Me, I will be with him.’

Allah having said, ‘I will give answer to the supplication of the one who calls on Me and prays (**أُجِيبُ دَعۡوَةَ ٱلدَّاعِ**)’, someone may doubt why it is that answers are not seen received for all the supplications we pray. The reply to this will be obtained from the hadiths of the Prophet ﷺ. In a hadith that Tirmidhi, and Imam Ahmad's son 'Abdullah (r), quote, the Prophet ﷺ says: ‘Whatever Muslim on the face of the earth prays to Allah for anything – unless he prays for a thing that is an offence, or for cutting off kinship – Allah will not fail either to give it to him, or else to push away for him, from evil, something equal to it. The wording in a hadith that Imam Ahmad (r) quoted from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (r) is thus: ‘If a Muslim makes any supplication that has no offence, or no cutting off of kinship, Allah will not fail to do for him one thing out of three: either to hasten *[p. 473]* for him the thing he prayed for; or else to store it up for him for the Hereafter; or else to turn away from him evil as much as it. ’ Then, the Companions said: ‘Then we may increase (supplication), surely!’ The noble Prophet said: ‘Allah is One who gives still more.’ A hadith that Imam Muslim (r) recorded is thus: The Prophet ﷺ said: ‘For a thing that is an offence, or for cutting off kinship, when a servant does not pray – unless he makes haste – an answer will keep being given to him.’ Then it was asked: What is making haste?’ The noble Prophet said: ‘Saying, I prayed, I prayed, I do not see an answer received for me, and so, becoming dejected, giving up supplication.’ In another hadith the Prophet ﷺ is saying: ‘When a servant has stretched out both hands towards Allah and asked for some good into them, Allah will surely be ashamed to send both of them back (giving nothing) in a state of having met with failure.’ (Da; Ti; Ja.) When, how and to what extent the supplication of the one who prays must be accepted, and which will be of more advantage to him – it is Allah, surely, who knows these and such things more than him, and more than anyone else. Continuing again with the subject of fasting indeed, Allah says:-

*[Verse 187]*

*[p. 475]* In the fasting season, after they had slept at night, the Muslims afterwards would not eat food or approach their wives. If they fell asleep before food, they would not eat food until it became time to open the fast the next day. What the reason for this is cannot be said definitively. That it is a practice that the People of the Scripture had been observing, which the Muslims too had been following, and that they came to understand it thus from its having been said in the ayah of fasting (in the 183rd verse), ‘just as it was prescribed upon those before you (**كَمَاكُتِبَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ**)’, and other things, are seen said. (Allah knows.) Whatever the reason, it need not be said, surely, that continuing that practice is very much a hardship. Bukhari (r) has quoted that it happened that a Companion did not find it convenient to eat food at the time of opening the fast, and that he fell asleep because of the fatigue of the day's labour, and that the next day, while observing fasting, by the time it became noon, a loss of consciousness came upon him. Events too, in which, after sleeping, some came to approach their wives, and some others to eat food, and afterwards, regretting, fell into grief, can be seen to have happened, in the books of hadith. It is lightness from strictnesses such as these that Allah is giving the true believers by means of this verse.

**رَفَث** (Rafath): the literal meaning is talk that one feels to be a disgrace or a deficiency to speak openly, private amorous talk with women, and the like. The figurative meaning is intercourse with women too. That that is what is intended here is what all the chief Quran commentators say, indeed. Along with making known that there is no objection to having every kind of relations between husband and wife on the night of fasting it is permissible –, Allah has also pointed out the principle contained in having permitted it. Yes, that they are for you, and you for them, as a kind of garment (**هُنَّ لِبَاسٌ لَكُمْ وَأَنْتُمْ لِبَاسٌ لَهُنَّ**) – thus. How pregnant with meaning and how attractive a sentence?! What other figurative sentence is there that portrays the relationship of wife and husband so honourably and so beautifully?! The hints of meaning contained in this need not be explained, surely.

That it said, ‘Allah has come to know that you were working deceit against your own selves indeed’, is intending events such as those indicated above. Doing things that one has understood to be not allowed, even if it is not with an evil intent, is a deceit and an injustice done to one's own souls, surely. Even so, that Allah has accepted their regret and return, and has pardoned *[p. 476]* the wrong, Allah makes known to them as good news. He also makes known that, in such a way that wrongs like that may not occur henceforth, there is no objection, until dawn, unconditionally, to having relations with wives, or to using food and drink. Along with that, He specially alerts to another matter too: that it is not enough to regard the relationship of wife and husband as merely a bodily enjoyment of pleasure, and that through married life the gain of offspring too must be intended, and that that relationship is what Allah has determined for you as the way to it, and that, therefore, you must wish and pray for the obtaining of offspring that Allah has decreed for you – that indeed is that matter. That is what the sentence **وَٱبۡتَغُواْ مَا كَتَبَ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمۡ** (seek what Allah has determined for you) denotes. For those who reckon material comforts and conveniences, and welfare and prosperity, as the aim of life, and who fear that through children a breach may befall it, it may be that this sentence, and some other Quran sentences like this, are not to their taste. But, it is as a mighty favour, and as a natural need for the continuance and growth of the human race, that the Quran has evaluated the obtaining of offspring. The Quran's statements connected with this we will be able to see in their proper places. **إن شاء الله**

It is the time from sunset to sunrise as night, and afterwards the time until the next setting as day, that is usually reckoned in general. But, in the view of the Shari'ah of Islam, it is the time from dawn ( **فجر** ) until setting ( **غروب** ) as day, and afterwards the time until dawn as night, that is reckoned. That is why, after first saying that you may have intercourse with women at night, He fixed a boundary for it, ‘until the dawn becomes manifest’, and afterwards commanded that the fast must be continued from that until night – the next setting –. When speaking about the dawn, after a simile, **حَتَّى يَتَبَيَّنَلَكُمُ الْخَيْطُالأَبْيَضُمِنَالْخَيْطِالأَسْوَدِ** (until the white thread becomes clear from the black thread), what He has done is to explain it by saying **مِنَ ٱلۡفَجۡرِ** (which is the dawn). It is clear that what was likened to the white thread is the dawn. Then, it can be understood too that what is likened to the black thread is the darkness of the night. Until the darkness of the night moves away and the dawn breaks – that is the gist. That, following the outward meaning of this sentence, some people, at night, took and set down a white thread and a black thread and tested the distinguishing between the two, and that the Prophet ﷺ explained to them that the white thread is intended for the dawn and the black thread for the darkness of the night, has come in hadiths. (These hadiths are seen to have been subjected to the criticism of some. In that time, when there were no clocks or the like to know the time exactly as today, if some simple-minded people did so, understanding from the outward form of that sentence that distinguishing between a black thread and a white thread is the sign of the dawn breaking, there is nothing incongruous *[p. 477]* in it. It is merely natural. (Allah knows the fact)

From its having said, ‘Then complete the fast until night’, it was understood that the beginning of the fast is from dawn, and that its end is with night coming on – yes, with sunset –. Another matter too is clear from this sentence. That is, that during this time eating food and drink, or intercourse with women occurring, is not allowed, and that by that the fast will be lost.

‘I'tikaf’ ( **الإِعْتِكَاف** ) is a meritorious deed that is usually practised in the time of fasting, and to which the Prophet ﷺ has given very much encouragement by word and by deed. The meaning of that word is to spend one's time keeping around something, by its side, without parting from it. Its technical meaning in Islam is to stay confined in the mosque in the worship of Allah, and in remembrances such as meditation and supplication. I'tikaf is a meritorious deed that can be done at all times. In Ramadan, and especially in the last ten days, it has also been specially encouraged. When speaking about closeness with women in the times of fasting – as the occasion arose – Allah makes known that that is not allowed in i'tikaf too: ‘While you are ones doing ‘i'tikaf’ in the mosques too, do not have contact with them. Since food and drink and other daily needs have not been prohibited in i'tikaf, none of them need be specially mentioned, surely.

After explaining all these laws, pointing to them, He says: **تِلْكَ حُدُودُاللَّهِ فَلاتَقْرَبُوهَا** (They are the boundaries of Allah. Therefore, do not approach them) That is, all that was said above are legal boundaries that Allah has determined; crossing beyond that boundary and violating it are an offence and deserving of punishment; therefore, do not go even near those boundaries; that may lead to violating the laws – that is the gist. It is a very noteworthy advice that Allah gives the true believers by means of this sentence. Wherever the limit of permission ends, from that same point indeed the limit of prohibition also begins. The boundary ridge between the two is merely imaginary, only. Therefore, when one ventures to make use of the limit of permission entirely, it will be easy, surely, for it to land in the circle of prohibition. That one must not go that far in making use of permissions, and must stop somewhere near the boundary – that is the principle contained in this advice. This needs to be heeded not only here but in all legal boundaries. When speaking about matters such as adultery, handling the wealth of an orphan, too, the word ‘do not approach them’ ( **لاتَقْرَبُوا** ) can be seen used in the Quran. Not stopping with all this, Allah finally ends this verse thus: Thus Allah explains to mankind His ayahs – they may observe caution, *[p. 478]* surely. At first (in the 183rd verse), when speaking about the law of fasting, there, ‘It is so that you may observe caution ( **لَعَلَّهُمۡ يَتَّقُونَ** ) *[TN: The book prints لَعَلَّهُمۡ يَتَّقُونَ (“they may observe caution”), the closing words of verse 187; verse 183, to which this sentence refers, ends لَعَلَّكُمۡ تَتَّقُونَ (“you may observe caution”), as the Malayalam here (നിങ്ങൾ, “you”) also has.]* – thus Allah pointed out. With that same word indeed He has also ended the subject of fasting here.

It would be good to remember here a few sayings of the Prophet such as give more light into the contents of this verse, and into some subjects explained above. The Rasul ﷺ says:

1. ‘Eat the night meal (the food in the last watch of the night). Surely, in the night meal there is barakah. (Bu; Mu)

2. ‘The difference between our fasting and the fasting of the People of the Scripture is the eating of food at the time of the night meal.’ (Mu)

3. ‘As long as my community makes the opening of the fast quick (opens the fast as soon as it is time) and makes the night meal late (makes it at the time near dawn), they will be in good’ (A). Both of those, surely, are a display of the joy and thanks in making use of the relaxation and permission that Allah gives.

4. When it was asked, If a person reaches daybreak with major impurity (janabah), will that be a hindrance to fasting?, the Prophet ﷺ said: ‘It is wont to become time for the prayer (for the Subh prayer) for me while I am one in janabah. Even then I keep the fast’ (Bu; Mu.) That is, that if ‘janabah’ occurs, apart from having to bathe before the prayer, it will not be a hindrance to fasting – that is the gist.

5. ‘When the night comes forward through this side, and the day goes back through this side, and the sun sets, the one who is fasting has come to have no fast’ (Bu; Mu) That the fast ended with sunset – that is the gist.

6. A'ishah (r) is saying: Until the noble Rasul ﷺ died, he used to do ‘i'tikaf’ in the last ten days of Ramadan. Afterwards, after the noble Prophet, his wives too used to do i'tikaf’. (Bu; Mu.)

7. The final part of a hadith that Abu Hurairah (r) narrated is thus: ‘The Prophet ﷺ used to do i'tikaf for ten days every year (in Ramadan). In the year in which he passed away, he did i'tikaf for twenty days’. (Bu.) A'ishah (r) states: ‘When the Rasul did i'tikaf, sitting in the mosque, he would show his head towards me (outside). Then I would comb (the hair) for him. He would not enter the house except for human needs (such as relieving oneself)’. (Bu; Mu.)

8. That while in i'tikaf the Prophet ﷺ used to go to visit the sick, but that his usual way was to walk on without lingering anywhere on the way – A'ishah (r) having said this, too, has come in hadith. (Da; Ja.)
