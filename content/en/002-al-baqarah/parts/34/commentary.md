### Section – 26

*[Verses 211–212]*

*[p. 513]* Showing the Israelites how very many very clear signs, Allah has favoured them. Instead of showing gratitude for them, what they did was showing ingratitude. Allah will not fail to give the severe punishment fitting for that. That the true believers must not become like that is indeed the import of the first verse. The reason for the deniers of the truth not accepting true faith is their having been deceived by the glitter of worldly life. Their sole aim will be worldly comforts and conveniences. Therefore, about both the true believers being, naturally, seen in a tier lo*[p. 514]*wer than them in comforts and conveniences, and their striving to the utmost and spending in the way of the truth, they will keep on mocking. Do not value that, no matter what the state in this world may be, it is those who accepted true faith and lived guarding the commands and prohibitions of Allah who will be the ones in the tier above them on the Day of Qiyamah. Besides that too, to those whom Allah wills, He will also give favours without account – it is such things that are the gist of the second verse.

*[Verse 213]*

*[p. 515]* It was along the straight way that the human community kept walking from its beginning. There was no division or split. Gradually there came to be divergences of opinion and splits. Then Allah, sending both prophets and scriptures, made understood what is real about the matters in which there was division. Even then, what those who divided did was, without letting go of their defiance, and rivalry, to stand obstinately in it indeed. No one has had to stand firm in division without getting clear proofs. But, to those who, understanding the truth, were ready to believe in it, Allah gave the good fortune of attaining right guidance by that way. He made them come to be on the right course.

In Surat Yunus Allah says: **وَمَاكَانَ النَّاسُ إِلاأُمَّةًوَاحِدَةًفَاخْتَلَفُوا** (Mankind were not other than one same community. Then what they did was to divide. Yunus, 19.) As stated by Ibn 'Abbas (r), Ibn Jarir, and Hakim (r) quote: ‘Between Adam, and Nuh (a) there were ten generations. All were indeed on the real shari'ah. Then they divided. Then Allah commissioned and sent the prophets as ones who announce glad tidings, and as ones who give warning.’ Some riwayahs showing that in the early times mankind were all on the straight path, that it was afterwards that divergences of opinion arose, and that it was then that Allah came to send prophets, can be seen quoted by Ibn Kathir (r) too, from Abul 'Aliyah, Qatadah, Mujahid (r). As he has pointed out, in the early period mankind were all ones who kept accepting the religious path of the Prophet Adam (a). After quite a while idol-worship came into being. Then Allah sent the Prophet Nuh (a) as Rasul and so, as far as is known, it is he who is the first Rasul to be sent.

‘In the beginning period man did not know anything about God; gradually he came to deem certain forces and things as gods; it was at the end that man arrived at belief in God *[p. 516]* and at belief in one God.’ – some are wont to say such things and so on, by conjecture, about the history of mankind. That this is not right, and that what is in reality is the reverse, is clear, surely, from what has been explained above.

*[Verse 214]*

In the communities of the prophets who passed before too, there have been miseries and distresses such as disease, war, poverty, famine. From the side of the deniers of the truth, the true believers have also had to undergo very many oppressions, and attacks, *[p. 517]* and torments. Unable to bear it, becoming panic-stricken, and trembling with fear, ‘When now will the help from Allah arrive for us – it has become unbearable for us, surely.....’ – the Rasuls who were in the communities and the true believers along with them have even lamented thus and so on. To trials that severe were they subject. In the way of the truth you too will have to become subject to some trials of that kind. If, without any of that, you are calculating that as soon as you have accepted true faith you may slip into Paradise, that is not right – Allah is alerting the true believers. That right after such stages of trial there will be the help of Allah too, that it is not at all very far off, that it is indeed near, so as to be obtained when its time comes – with such things He also consoles them. The idea contained in this verse, Allah can be seen to have made clear in another place too, in Su: 'Ankabut, verses 2,3 and elsewhere.

The gist of a hadith from Khabbab ibn al-Aratt (r) is as follows: ‘To the Rasul of Allah – will you not seek help for us, will you not pray for us – thus we said. Then he said: Among those who were before you, placing a saw on the crown of the head of a man, he used to be split as far as his soles. Then that too, it would not turn him away from his religion. With a comb of iron, the flesh of a man used to be raked off from the bone. That too would not turn him away from his religion. Afterwards the noble Prophet said: Surely, Allah will indeed complete this matter. And a rider may well travel from San'a' to Hadramawt, having nothing else to fear except Allah, and, concerning the sheep, the wolves. But, what you are doing is making haste. (Bu.) (\*)

> **Note:** (\*) An important city in Yemen is San'a' ( **صنعاء** ) those who go from there to Hadramawt ( **حَضَرَ مَوْت** ) need to go crossing many mountain regions along the southern coasts of the Arabian Peninsula and along the northern shore of the Arabian Sea.

*[Verse 215]*

*[p. 518]* More relevant than what it is that needs to be spent is how it needs to be spent. Any thing that has some goodness – benefit – is indeed fit to be spent. It is not at all that it must indeed be a big sum, or something costly. But, when spending, preference needs to be given to parents, close families, orphans, the needy, wayfarers who are in difficulty having left their homeland – to such as these. Not only in almsgiving, any goodness in every field will not go in vain. Allah is both knowing all and keeping account. He will not fail to give recompense for each one – that is the gist. The principle that in matters of charity the most preference needs to be given to the closer ones, and to those who have more difficulty, is one that has been repeatedly stated in the Quran and in hadith. When those mentioned above are in difficulty without means for livelihood, giving that is also an indispensable duty, in accordance with the ability of each one.

*[Verse 216]*

*[p. 519]* This is a command making war obligatory. Because many kinds of straits and difficulties arise through war, it is natural for you to have aversion, and dislike, towards it. That, though some ills may arise through it for the time being, it is essential for the safety and the prosperity of Islam and of the Muslims, Allah knows. It is therefore that He commands it – Allah exhorts the true believers. Though some matters will outwardly be disliked, in reality there will be many a goodness to be obtained through them. On the contrary, though some matters will outwardly be both very much liked and desirable, in reality there will also be many ills to befall through them. Man will not be able to know any of that. Only Allah knows it. Therefore, He also alerts that you must both understand that the commands and prohibitions of Allah will be with your real goodness and future good taken into account, and accept them with a full mind. There is no need to say, surely, that this is something that gives very much peace of mind and zeal to the true believers. (Bear in mind the information – against whom war must be carried on, and so on – explained in the past verses 190- 193.)

### Section – 27

*[Verses 217–218]*

*[p. 521]* That the sending down of the first verse is in connection with a particular event becomes clear from many riwayahs. The summary of the event is this: In the second year of the Hijrah, under the leadership of 'Abdullah ibn Jahsh (r), the Prophet ﷺ sent, with a writing, a small military band consisting of seven or eight persons who were Muhajirs. The noble Prophet had also commanded that the writing might be broken open and read only after a journey of two days. It was so that none of the enemies might get the chance to scent out their aim that the Prophet ﷺ did so. On opening and looking at the letter, its content was to go to (a place between Makkah and Ta'if) Nakhlah ( **نَخْلَة** ), lie in wait, and come back having found out the movements of the Quraish. On the way, two of them, because the camel that was their mount had strayed off the way, *[p. 522]* left the company. While the rest were at Nakhlah, a small trading band of the Quraish came that way. In it there were 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami and three other persons. There, the military band, holding consultation among themselves about what should be done, at last clashed with the trading band. In the clash Ibn al-Hadrami was killed, and two persons were taken captive. The third ran away and escaped. This was at the juncture when the month Rajab ends and the month Sha'ban begins. Or else, that it was the end of Jamad al-Akhir and the beginning of Rajab is also in some riwayahs. With those they had taken captive, and the trade goods they got, they came to the Prophet ﷺ. This was the first ghanimah ( **غَنِيمَة** wealth that is obtained from the enemy side in war) that the Muslims received from the enemies. The enemies, making use of this juncture, let fly reproach and mockery. They kept propagating that in the month in which war is forbidden (in Rajab) Muhammad and party waged war and dishonoured it. I have not commanded you to fight in the month in which war is forbidden, surely – the Prophet ﷺ too said. The Muslims too protested to them. They came to be in much regret. It was following this that this verse came down.

In this, the gist of the reply that Allah gave to the accusations that arose from the side of the enemies is this: Fighting in a month in which war is haram (inviolable) is indeed a mighty wrong. But, keeping on causing disorders such as hindering the people from accepting the true religion, which is the way of Allah, disbelieving in Allah, not allowing the true believers to enter al-Masjid al-Haram, which was established for worshipping Allah alone, banishing the folk who are natives of that land without consenting to their living there, is a wrong mightier than that. When two wrongs confront each other, accepting the small wrong in order to do away with the more mighty wrong is simply both fair and practical. Not only that, they have no intention either of leaving off even with the said disorders. They also have the intention, in addition, of driving back from it, if they can, those who have acknowledged Islam. For that, their programme is to keep on continuing war against the Muslims. Then, the seriousness of the fitnah that they (the mushriks) carry on against the Muslims is very mighty. This being so, compared with that, having slain some from among them can only be deemed simply trifling. (Bear in mind what was stated about fitnah in the commentary of the 191st verse)

As the occasion arises, Allah here alerts the true believers to one more matter too: In case a Muslim who has accepted true faith – be it on his own account, be it because of the urging of the enemies – turns back and goes away from Islam, its *[p. 523]* consequence is very mighty. No deed at all that he did is acceptable, be it in this world, be it in the next. There is no consideration even for the deeds he did while he was in Islam. One will deal with them too just as indeed with the disbelievers. In the Hereafter, for its part, there is also the punishment of Hell for all time. From its having said **فَيَمُتْوَهُوَ كَافِرٌ** (and if he dies, being a disbeliever), one may understand both that if he comes back again to Islam indeed, his return will be accepted, and that it is looking at the state at the time of dying that the final decision is.

That, though what 'Abdullah ibn Jahsh (r) and the comrades happened to do is a wrong, their intention and aim were good, and that Allah has forgiven them, can be understood from the 218th verse. They are, surely, ones who accepted true faith, and went in Hijrah, and struggled in the way of Allah. That when this verse came down they became glad, and that from the ghanimah properties they had brought, the Prophet ﷺ accepted one fifth of it, has come in some of the riwayahs indicated above. And the prisoners whom they had taken captive and brought, the Prophet ﷺ released to the mushriks after the two Companions who had earlier strayed away from them got back. What one of them did was to become a Muslim and take up residence indeed in Madinah.

From the roots **جِهَاد، مُجَاهدة** (jihad; mujahadah), the verb form is **جَاهَدَ** (jahada). Its meaning is ‘strove as much as possible, laboured exceedingly, made a strenuous attempt’ and such. Except that many matters including war will come within jihad, it is not a synonym of war. Imam Raghib (r) explains thus the meaning intended by these words in the Quran and elsewhere: ‘Expending the whole ability in resisting the enemy is both ‘jihad’ and ‘mujahadat’. ‘Jihad’ is of three kinds: that against the open enemy, and that against Satan, and that against one's own body. Verses such as **وَجَاهِدُوافِي اللَّهِحَقَّ جِهَادِهِ** (In the matter of Allah, do jihad according to the due manner in which jihad must be done). (22: 78) and **وَجَاهِدُوابِأَمْوَالِكُمْوَأَنْفُسِكُمْفِيسَبِيلِاللَّهِ** (In the way of Allah, do jihad with your properties and with your bodies). (9: 41) and others contain all these three. ‘Mujahadat’ can come about both with the tongue and with the hand; the Prophet ﷺ says: Do jihad against the disbelievers with your hand and with your tongue.’ ( **المُفردات** ) From this quotation it has been understood, surely, both that for **جِهَاد** (jihad) and for the other word forms that arise from it the meaning is not taking up the sword and fighting, and that it is in the meaning ‘to struggle’ that it is used. It is on this basis that carrying on struggle against superstitions, and against improper customs, is called ‘jihad’, and those who carry on struggle are called ‘mujahids’.
