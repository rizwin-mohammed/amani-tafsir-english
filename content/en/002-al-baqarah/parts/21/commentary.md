*[Verses 133–134]*

*[p. 379]* The question seen above is one that addresses, in general, both the Arab mushriks, who are traditionalists of the Prophet Ibrahim (a), and the disbelievers who are Israelites. It is a refutation of that claim. About the wasiyyah of Ibrahim (a) and Ya'qub (a), it was stated, surely, in the previous verse. The state of affairs of the Arabs and the Israelites, for its part, is also directly contrary to it. If their stance is seen, it will come to seem that that wasiyyah was not in the manner quoted above – that it was in some other way –. That is why Allah asks them, in the form of denial, Were you present there when that wasiyyah took place?

At the time when death drew near to Ya'qub (a), he called his children and asked, Whom will you take as ilah and worship after me – will you perhaps abandon the way of tawhid and take the gods of people of other castes? –. They replied with one voice : As you and your fathers (our forefathers) used to do, it will be Allah indeed whom we worship; we will live as Muslims indeed. We will not adopt any other way – and so on. This is what actually took place. In case you claim that it was not so – that it was in another way –, were you present there at that time, so as to know it ? That you were not is certain indeed. Let that be; whatever indeed it was that happened, they all passed away so long ago. They all were ones who lived firmly grounded in the same ideal and way. There is no point in disputing about them. The fruit of their deeds will be for them only. The fruit of your deeds you indeed must experience. By claiming that you are their successors, you will not get salvation. You will not be asked about their doings. Nor will you go without being questioned about your doings. Therefore your claim of tradition has no meaning at all – that is the gist.

Ya'qub (a)'s father is Ishaq (a). Isma'il (a) is his paternal uncle. The paternal uncle too has been counted among the fathers. The paternal uncle too has, surely, roughly the very position of a father. What is meant by **كَسب** (earnings) is the deeds one does and lays by in life. That, surely, is the earnings to be gained and laid by from this world for the Hereafter. If the deeds are good, it will be good earnings, and if the deeds are bad, bad earnings – only that.

*[Verse 135]*

*[p. 380]* This is Allah quoting, in a tone of reproach, some words that the People of the Scripture are accustomed to say to the true believers. The Jews will say : If you adopt the Jewish religion like us, you can attain right guidance – thus. The Christians will say: If you adopt the Christian religion like us, you can attain right guidance – thus. That is, only if you join our group will you attain right guidance. Otherwise you will be ones who have gone astray – each party will say so, that is the meaning. Each party, surely, is one that claims that the others are astray. Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ to reply to them thus: ‘No, we are not ready for that. Right guidance is not going to be received by that route either. But let us follow the way that our forefather the Prophet Ibrahim (a), whom you and we honour and respect, adopted. That is what we have been following. He was a man who, without adopting any way relating to shirk like you, keeping away from all that, adopted the sole way of tawhid like us. Therefore we are not ready to let go of that way’.

*[Verses 136–138]*

*[p. 382]* To what the People of the Scripture said – that if you become Jews or Christians you will receive right guidance – Allah pointed out in the previous verse a reply that is pregnant with meaning, worthy of thought and, along with that, in a somewhat serious tone. In these verses Allah makes known to the true believers a reply too that is somewhat calm and, along with that, lays open the fundamental difference between the Islamic belief and the belief of the People of the Scripture. What we do is not, like you, to believe only in some prophets and some scripture and disbelieve in what remains. Our stance is this. We believe in Allah without any admixture of shirk. Without looking at difference of person or difference of time, we do believe in all the prophets, including Ibrahim, Isma'il, Ishaq (a) and those among their progeny, and in all the divine messages given to them. We have no partiality of any kind such as, We will believe in such-and-such ones, we will not believe in such-and-such ones. Along with that, we are also Muslims who live submissive to all of Allah's injunctions – such is the gist of that reply.

If the People of the Scripture are ready to adopt this doctrine of belief, which is entirely reasonable and untouched by communalism or partisanship, they too will attain right guidance. If they are not prepared, that is only their factionalism and rivalry. Allah will give you deliverance from all the crises that may be faced at their hands. Allah indeed is enough to help you against them. He, surely, is the One who hears all and knows – with such words Allah also consoles the Prophet ﷺ and the true believers.

**أَسْبَاط** (asbat) is the plural of the word **سِبط** (sibt) progeny, clans, tribes, line of grandsons – such is its meaning. The Prophet Ya'qub (a), who also had the name Isra'il, had twelve sons. The lines of descendants of each one are called sibt. In place of the Arabs' calling their tribes **قَبِيلَة** (qabilah), the Israelites say ‘sibt’. That We have divided the Israelites into twelve sibts ( **وَقَطَّعۡنَٰهُمُ ٱثۡنَتَيۡ عَشۡرَةَ أَسۡبَاطًا** ) – Allah can be seen stating thus (7:160).

*[p. 383]* It is noteworthy that, without stopping at saying in general, We have believed in the prophets, it has been commanded to mention the names of some prophets specially. First the names of Ibrahim, Isma'il, Ishaq and Ya'qub (a) were mentioned. Those four, surely, are the revered forefathers and the tribal fathers of the Israelites and of the Arabs. Following on, concerning all the other prophets who have arisen among the Israelites, it also said as a whole **وَٱلۡأَسۡبَاطِ** (to the progeny too): after that it has mentioned Musa (a), as the one whom the Jews, who are Israelites, accept as their special prophet, and then 'Isa (a), whom the Christians, who include Israelites and those who are not, have been accepting as their special prophet. Finally, including all the prophets who have come in the world besides these, it has also said, and in what has been given to the prophets ( **وَمَآ أُوتِيَ ٱلنَّبِيُّونَ** ).

The true believers are ones who believe in what has been sent down to all the prophets too. But their belief in the Quran, which was sent down to the noble Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, needs to be indeed in detailed form. That is, one has to believe indeed in each verse in it and in each principle and doctrine that it contains. What is meant by believing in what was sent down to the others is to believe, as a whole, that all of them are from Allah and that they are true. A detailed belief like that in the Quran is, surely, not possible as far as they are concerned. Because many of them have become entirely unknown. In some, because alteration and tampering have come about, it has also become impossible to discern which is the original. Even so, believing that the portion in them that is seen to agree properly with the Quran is true is, moreover, obligatory. There is no way but to reject the portion that is seen to be clearly contrary to the statement of the Quran. About the portion to which neither can be assigned – the greater part of today's Bible, which is said to contain the Tawrat and the Injil, moreover belongs to this category – there is no way but to keep silence. Bukhari (r) has quoted thus from Abu Hurairah (r): The People of the Scripture used to read the Tawrat from the Hebrew language and interpret it for the Muslims in the Arabic language. Then the Rasul of Allah said : ‘Do not declare the People of the Scripture truthful and do not declare them liars. Say also, We have believed in Allah and in what Allah has sent down – thus.’

In verse 136 Allah pointed out how the true believers must declare their belief. Allah's reply concerning the responses that arise from the People of the Scripture to that declaration was stated in verse 137 too. After that, it is as its continuation, explaining a principle that is the basis of that declaration of belief, that verse 138 stands. In the interpretation of this verse – although in the details *[p. 384]* some slight differences can be seen – the overall gist of what many great Quran commentators have stated is this: Your custom, surely, is to divide people into castes by giving them the Jewish colour and the Christian colour through the rite of baptism that you, the People of the Scripture, are accustomed to perform. The meaning of your saying that if we become Jews or Christians we will be on the straight way can be nothing other than dividing into castes by giving that colour. But what we have adopted is not a colour adopted through some ceremonies like those you perform. The colour we have adopted – yes, our religion – is the colour that is Islam, which Allah has approved and given us as suited to human nature. Who can give colour better than Allah! It is Him only, moreover, that we worship, this is the colour that the Prophet Ibrahim (a) too had adopted; that is, the religion. And not otherwise: his was not a religion that, like you, divides into castes by giving colour with baptismal water.

It was understood, surely, from what was explained above, that what is meant by the colour Allah gave ( **صِبۡغَةَ ٱللَّهِ** ) is Islam, which is His religion. It is also usually said that what is meant by it is the nature Allah gave ( **فطْرَة اللَّهِ** ). The import of this too is indeed: the nature Allah gave to man – the procedure according to that nature –. That the religion of Islam is called **دِين الفَطرة** (the religion of nature), and, with the intent of the religion suited to man's real nature, **فَطْرَةَ الاِسْلَامَ** (the nature that is Islam), and so on, is also indeed in accordance with this. That giving colour ( **صِبْغَة** ) has been used about having approved the religion of Islam as the religion of man is a usage that matches a ceremony which the People of the Scripture are accustomed to perform for giving a person formal membership in their religion. In Arabic literature this is called **مُشَاكلة** (mushakalat). Ibn Jarir (r) is saying: ‘What is meant here by giving colour is the giving of the colour of Islam. When the Christians intended to make their children Christians, they would dip them in a special kind of water of theirs. They say that this is a kind of purification like the janabah bath of the Muslims (the bath from major impurity). That is a colour-giving ceremony in Christianity.’ Some other commentators too can be seen to have said likewise. It is said that what was being used for this baptism was a kind of water of yellow colour, and that that is why the name **صِبْغَة**, meaning giving dye (colour), came to it. **والله اعلم**

These verses, surely, are addressed to the People of the Scripture, who are the Jews and the Christians. In that respect, no reason is seen for supposing – as some commentators indicate – that that word *[p. 385]* ( **صِبۡغَةَ** ) may have been used based on a rite that is not current among both those groups, that is, a rite current only among the Christians. The reason seen is for supposing that that word must be indicating some colour-giving rite that was current among both groups. (Allah knows) When the Bible is examined too, what is understood is that among both groups a baptism practice, each of its own kind, was current (\*)

*[Verses 139–140]*

*[p. 386]* Only if one adopts the Jewish religion or the Christian religion is there deliverance; it is our scripture and religion that came first. It is the religion of the Prophet Ibrahim and others, the fathers of us all, that we have been adopting; therefore it is we who are the truly rightly guided. It is the reply to such arguments and quibbles of the People of the Scripture that is in these verses. The reply is one that concerns the Jews and *[p. 387]* the Christians in general. The brief gist of the first verse can be understood thus: Allah is not the Rabb of only one party among us. He is the Rabb of both parties. Also, the fruits of everyone's deeds each one must experience indeed. The other party has no liability at all concerning the doings of one party. Whoever among us adds blemish to His worship and adds adulteration to His religious injunctions, they will be the astray ones, and whoever maintains sincerity in them, they will be the ones on the straight way. Whether in worship or in religious injunctions, we do not have the habit of mixing in blemish and hypocrisy it is you who have it. That being so, your arguments and disputes have no meaning at all.

The idea in the second verse may be explained thus : Is it that you say that all those, who were our fathers and the former prophets, were Jews or Christians? None of them were Jews or Christians. The very origin of the Israelites is from Ya'qub (a), after Ibrahim, Isma'il and Ishaq (a). As for the Tawrat of the Prophet Musa (a), it also came down later, after a very long time. Judaism, for its part, is also not one that takes the Tawrat alone as its authority. It is according to the ritual procedures devised by the council of Jewish scholars which came into being later. If one speaks about the Christians, what they claim, surely, is that they are followers of the Prophet 'Isa (a). His birth was so long after those mentioned above. The religion they have been observing, for its part, is also not one based on the teachings of him or of the Injil he brought. It is also one based on some new doctrines devised by the Christian churches that came into being after him. The fact being all this, what meaning is there to that claim? You say that all those prophets were ones observing your religion. Allah too says that they were not. Whose saying will be right?! Who will know the fact more? You or Allah?! Allah indeed; there is no doubt. Who else is there who commits wrong more than you, who, without disclosing the true testimonies received from Allah through His prophets and scriptures, aiming at selfish gains and self-wills, cover up the reality by making new laws and by misinterpreting? Do not think that Allah does not know or heed any of this. Remember that you will have to answer properly before Him!

Allah says in another place: **يَـٰٓأَهۡلَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ لِمَ تُحَآجُّونَ فِيٓ إِبۡرَٰهِيمَ** ..... **أَفَلَا تَعۡقِلُونَ** (O People of the Scripture, why do you argue in the matter of Ibrahim? The Tawrat and the Injil have, surely, not been sent down except after him! Then do you not think, using reason?! (Al Imran 65) A heavy warning that specially concerns the scholars who tie on, in the name of religion, legal rulings and doctrines that do not accord with the *[p. 388]* facts established through prophets or through scripture – without the distinction whether they are Jews or Christians, Muslims or others – is the sentence **وَمَنْ أَظْلَمُ مِمَّنْ كَتَمَ شَهَادَةً عِنْدَهُ** ..... *[DOUBT: on p. 388 the printed "....." may belong before or after this Arabic phrase; placed after, as with the Al Imran 65 quotation on p. 387.]* (Who is there more of a wrongdoer than one who conceals any testimony he has received with him from Allah…). May Allah guard and protect us, Amin.
