### Section – 21

*[Verses 168–169]*

Many things were stated in the past verses about the fact that, just as Allah alone is the Creator and the Lord, He alone is the object of worship too. After that, He reminds that the Giver of food too is He indeed, that all things among the resources of the earth that are not specially disallowed or detrimental may be eaten by mankind, and that one must not, becoming subject to the evil counsels of Satan, fabricate prohibitions that do not exist and make them narrow. At the time when the Quran was coming down, the mushriks had been reckoning as forbidden some animals that had been made vows and offerings in the name of idols. (More information about these is coming in Ma'idah : 106)It is also said that some among the Jews had made camel meat, and some others some other things, forbidden. And it was as very important religious laws that all this was being observed. None of such bans is something that Allah has ordained. They are only laws that are foisted in the name of Allah at the instigation of Satan, who is man's open enemy; *[p. 432]* Satan is one who has set out to lead man astray by way of evil counsels; therefore, you must always be watchful about falling into his trap – such and so on, Allah is alerting mankind.

It was, surely, stated in the 29th verse that all that is in the earth has been created for mankind. There are no substances in the earth that are not useful in one way if not in another. Even so, not all things are fit to eat. That is why it has been said **كُلُوا مِمَّافِي الأَرْضِ** (Eat of what is in the earth). As can be seen below, in the 173rd verse, the eating of a few certain things has also been disallowed. Some of the things that can be eaten will also be dirty or harmful. That is why it has also been said ( **حَلالا طَيِّبًا** )(what is permissible, and what is excellent). A word to which meanings such as what is good what is pure, what is pleasing, what is excellent may be assigned is **طَيِّب** (tayyib). Its opposite word is **خَبِيث** (khabith), what is bad, what is corrupt, what is filthy – such and so on is its meaning. It may be said in general that what is meant here is what naturally seems good and satisfying to the mind. In short, it can be understood in general that the ruling of Islam is that there is no objection to eating all things that have no clear ban from religion, and that are neither filthy nor harmful.

*[Verses 170–171]*

*[p. 433]* What is seen in the first verse is a reproach about the practice of imitating the beliefs and customs of the forefathers, without venturing to seek out and know the truth and the proof, and without being ready to accept it if it is explained to them. All ways of misguidance like making equals to Allah and making forbidden some permissible things are, surely, offspring of that practice. In the second verse it is pointed out that it is not possible to bring those afflicted by this blind disease of imitation onto the straight way by giving them counsel of truth.

The cause of the sending down was the mushriks of that time or the Jews, let it be; the practice of reckoning imitation of the forefathers as the criterion of truth is a great malady that is seen among mankind in all times. If it is said to them, What Allah and His Rasul show us is in such and such a manner, it is to that one must return – thus, what everyone will have to say will be indeed the very same reply that the disbelievers of that time used to say. In reality, it is not how the forefathers were – it is indeed how Allah has ordained – that must be looked at. If the conduct of the forefathers is indeed in accordance with Allah's laws, there is nothing to reproach in it ; going after them in it is also necessary. On the contrary, if what Allah has ordained and the tradition received from them are different from each other, that is a proof that they had not understood the truth, and that the way they had been *[p. 434]* adopting was not right. That is what Allah asks: Even if their fathers were not doing – be it understanding anything at all, be it attaining the straight way – following them indeed?!

What Allah points out in the second verse is that those who, in the name of imitation and tradition, being firmly rooted in disbelief and superstition, are not ready to think about the truth, are sheerly on a par with animals. When the one who grazes them calls them or drives them, other than that they hear his sound, those animals, surely, do not know what it is that he says, and what the meaning and gist are of what that one who makes the sound says. The state of these too is just like that. Other than hearing the sound of those who invite them to the truth and give them counsel, nothing beyond that will enter into their heart; nor will words of truth come out through their tongue ; nor will they, looking with the eye, understand the true state of things. Yes, as far as the truth is concerned, they will be deaf and dumb and blind – that is the gist.

*[Verse 172]*

Addressing mankind in general, it was stated in the 168th verse: Eat the permissible and excellent things from the resources of the earth – thus. Addressing the true believers specially, in this verse it has been commanded again to eat the excellent things that Allah has given them. There, along with that command, warning was also given about satanic practices like making forbidden some things that Allah has permitted to us. Here, what has been said in connection with that command is indeed: If it is Allah that you *[p. 435]* worship, you must also give thanks to Him – thus. Instead of what was said there, Eat what is permissible and excellent among the things in the earth ( **كُلُوا مِمَّافِي الأَرْضِ حَلالا طَيِّبًا** ) – thus, here what has been said is: Eat of the holy things that We have given you ( **كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَارَزَقْنَاكُمْ** ) – thus. Or, here it has not been qualified as what is permissible ( **حَلالا** ). The orientation there being toward all mankind, including both believers and disbelievers, and the orientation here being toward the true believers only, is the reason for this.

The true believers will be ones who make permissible all that Allah has made permissible, and make forbidden only what Allah has made forbidden. They will also worship Allah only. That being so, it can be seen that some indications are contained in commanding them that they must eat excellent things, and that, if it is Allah that they worship, they must show thanks to Him. That, although they may seem permissible to outward view – as can be seen in the hadith quoted below – the true believers need to take care that those things are pure also from the admixture of doubt or of sin of any kind; and that, since you are ones who have been worshipping Allah only, you need also to be always thankful to Him on account of His continuing to bless you by giving you food – these and so on are those indications.

It can be seen that, elsewhere, Allah has commanded this command, which faces mankind in general and the true believers specially, addressing Allah's Rasuls too; Allah says: **يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلرُّسُلُ كُلُواْ مِنَ ٱلطَّيِّبَٰتِ وَٱعۡمَلُواْ صَٰلِحًا إِنِّي بِمَا تَعۡمَلُونَ عَلِيمٞ** (O Rasuls, eat of excellent things. And do good deeds. Surely, I am one who knows about what you do (Mu'minun : 51) Here too it has not been qualified as what is permissible. In place of having commanded the true believers, Give thanks – thus, here what has been said is: Do good deeds – thus. Even though it is indeed the same subject, presenting it taking into account the circumstances of those whom it faces in saying it, and giving along with it some directions and indications suited to them, is the habit of the Quran.

It was, surely, indicated above that food items that, though they may be lawful when one looks at the outward state, have the possibility of some ill being mixed in them in reality, must be avoided. As far as the true believers are concerned, it is essential that this be heeded not only in food items but in all things. The Prophet ﷺ says: Surely, Allah is excellent. He will also not accept other than what is excellent. *[p. 436]* Allah has commanded the true believers too just as He commanded the Rasuls. That is, He said (to the Rasuls): **يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلرُّسُلُ كُلُواْ مِنَ ٱلطَّيِّبَٰتِ -الخ** (O Rasuls, eat of excellent things. And do good deeds...)– thus. He said (to the true believers): **يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ -الخ** (O you who have believed, eat of the excellent things that We have given you..) – thus too. (The noble Prophet continued:) A man will make a long journey, with matted hair, and covered in dust. His food, for its part, his drink, for its part, his clothing, for its part, will be haram (forbidden). His having been fed and reared will also be with haram. He will stretch out his hands toward the sky, saying, My Rabb – thus; then, how is it that he will get an answer to it?! (A:Mu:Ti.)

*[Verse 173]*

After having commanded, addressing mankind generally and the true believers specially, *[p. 437]* that they give thanks for the favour of having permitted them to eat the excellent things of this earth, and that they not, following Satan, disregard that favour, Allah makes known in this verse that in reality Allah has made unlawful only very few things, and that they are such and such. The prohibited things are four in all. Besides this verse, this subject has been stated in Surah Ma'idah 4, and An'am 145, and Nahl 115. Just as in this verse, the ayah in Surat al-Nahl too is without any special explanation. In the ayahs in Ma'idah and An'am, the subject has been explained quite some more. Therefore, let us be content here with a brief description and postpone more information to those two surahs. **ومن الله التوفيق**

(1) The things that have been made forbidden are these: **الْمَيْتَة** (carrion) All carrion that has lost life other than by slaughter – by disease or by injury – is included in this. But, since it becomes clear from Ma'idah 99 that things that are hunted and caught from the sea are exempt from this, and from hadiths that fish and locusts are exempt, it can be understood that what is meant is the carrion of land creatures. The Prophet ﷺ says: Two carrions and two bloods have been made permissible for us. That is. Fish and locusts, and the liver and the spleen (A;Ja;Ha;) In a well-known incident that Jabir (r) has quoted, Bukhari and Muslim (r) have quoted that the Companions ate a dead whale that had been washed ashore from the sea, and that the Prophet ﷺ approved of it.

(2) **الدَّم** blood That there is no objection to blood that stays clinging to flesh, and that it is blood that flows out that has been disallowed, can be understood from the 145th ayah of Surah An'am. There blood has been qualified as **مسفوح** (that which has been made to flow).

(3) **لَحْمَ الْخِنْزِيرِ** (swine-flesh) Although flesh was said because it is flesh that is usually eaten, the bone and other parts of the swine too are indeed disallowed. That the reason for making the swine forbidden is that it is a filthy thing ( **فَانَّهُ رِجْس** ) is said in An'am. Whether the swine has been slaughtered or not, it is indeed forbidden.

(4) **مَاأُهِلَّ بِهِ لِغَيْرِ اللَّهِ** (that over which the sound has been raised for those other than Allah). That which has been slaughtered in the name of anyone else who is not Allah – be it idols, or great men, or devils, whoever it is, all right – or for their pleasure, is the intended sense by this. What has been slaughtered as a vow or an offering to those other than Allah, and what has been slaughtered at places that have been appointed for sacrificial rites, immolations and the like – all are *[p. 438]* included in this. See Ibn Jarir(r) saying the gist of what the commentators of the Quran have stated about the use of the word **مَاأُهِلَّ بِهِ**(that over which the sound has been raised) in the intention of what has been slaughtered: The reason for saying **مَاأُهِلَّ بِهِ** – they (the mushriks), when they intended to slaughter things that had been made offerings to their gods, used to say the names of those gods and sound it aloud. This having become customary, in the end everyone who slaughters, whether saying the name (of the gods) or not saying it, whether sounding aloud or otherwise, came to be called **مُهِلّ** (one who raises the sound). The intended sense of the raising of the sound ( **إهْلال** ) in Allah's saying **مَاأُهِلَّ بِهِ لِغَيْرِ اللَّهِ** (that over which the sound has been raised for those other than Allah) is this. Because one who says the talbiyah (\*) in Hajj or 'Umrah usually raises the sound, the saying about him **مُهِلّ** (ones who raise the sound – thus, and the saying of **استهلال** (raising and showing the sound) for a baby's crying with a noise when it is born and for rain's making a loud sound falling on the ground, belong to this group. (\*\*)

> **Note:** (\*) It is to the dhikr **لَبَيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَيْك** being customarily recited in Hajj and in 'Umrah that talbiyah( **تَلْبِيَة** ) is said

> **Note:** (\*\*) This word is customarily used in calling out on seeing the new moon too.

From the statement of Ibn Jarir (r) we can understand some matters: What must be looked at is not whose name was said when slaughtering. What must be looked at is for whom – for whose name or pleasure – it is slaughtered. In whosesoever name other than Allah, ‘for whosesoever pleasure’, as a vow and offering to whomsoever it is slaughtered, all that is included in this word and is not permitted to be eaten. In "those other than Allah", all things are included, without distinction such as idols, and great men, and goddesses and gods, and the excellent, and the base. In the explanation of the sentence **ولعن الله من ذبح لغير الله** (Allah has cursed, too, the one who slaughtered for those other than Allah) in a saying of the Prophet that Muslim (r) has quoted from 'Ali (r), Imam Nawawi (r) can be seen to have stated thus. What is meant by slaughtering for those other than Allah is slaughtering in the name of those other than Allah. That is, like slaughtering for an idol, or for the cross, or for Musa(a), or for 'Isa(a), or for the Ka'bah and the like. All this is haram. (Eating) this slaughtered thing is not permissible either. The one who slaughtered may be a Muslim, or a Christian, or a Jew, let it be. Shafi'i(r) has stated it clearly. Our people(the scholars) have also *[p. 439]* agreed on it. If, along with that, honouring and worshipping that which is not Allah for which it was slaughtered has also been intended, it is kufr (disbelief) too. If the one who slaughtered was formerly a Muslim, he has now also become an outcast from the religion ( **من شرح مسلم** )

Continuing, Nawawi(r) can also be seen to have quoted some opinions: that, at the reception of the sultan (the ruler), slaughter intending nearness to him is haram, and that it falls among what has been slaughtered for those other than Allah ( **مَاأُهِلَّ بِهِ لِغَيْرِ اللَّهِ** ); and that, if it is as an expression of joy at his arrival, there is no objection. Then, it can be understood from all this that, even if one slaughters saying Allah's name when slaughtering, slaughtering in honour of some person or thing other than Allah is also forbidden. About some kinds that may outwardly seem to be besides the four things stated in this verse, and that in reality are included in these four indeed, it can be seen in Surah Ma'idah; and that, besides these, the Prophet ﷺ disallowed some things, in hadiths. Let us speak about the first in the explanation of Ma'idah 4, and about the second in that of An'am 145. **إن شاء الله**

Since these four things are forbidden ones, there is no need to say that using them is a sin deserving punishment with Allah. But Allah makes known that, in case a state of compulsion is faced, there is then no wrong in using them. What is meant by a state of compulsion is indeed: unbearable hunger or thirst arising, and nothing else other than one of these being obtainable. However, in this too there are some conditions. It must not be with a wish to break the law. Nor must it be exceeding the need. Or, a wish to use forbidden things must not be felt on one's own account, and it must not exceed the measure for getting release from unbearable distress. Some great men, explaining these conditions, have opined that, if it is because of engaging in activities of wrongdoing like committing treason against the Islamic government, or committing plunder and robbery, that the state of compulsion was faced, this concession is not applicable to such people. But, because such activities of wrongdoing are wrong, the wrong of a person killing his own life by starving it ceases to exist. *[TN: The book prints ഇല്ലാതാകുന്നു “ceases to exist”, translated as printed. The conclusion that follows (“therefore, this concession … is applicable to them too”) would follow from the negative, ഇല്ലാതാകുന്നില്ല “does not cease to exist”.]* And that, therefore, this concession in the state of compulsion is applicable to them too indeed, is the opinion of Ibn Jarir(r) and others. Regarding this concession and condition, the sentence in the ayah in Ma'idah is like this: **فَمَنِ اضْطُرَّ فِي مَخْمَصَةٍ غَيْرَ مُتَجَانِفٍ لِّإِثْمٍ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ** (But, if anyone, without showing an inclination toward offence, becomes compelled, being in severe hunger, then Allah surely is one who forgives and is the Ever-Merciful)

*[Verses 174–176]*

*[p. 441]* When one looks at the occasion of the sending down, it is about the scholars who are People of the Scripture – especially the Jewish scholars – who cover up, without disclosing to the people, the truths that have come in the Tawrat and the Injil establishing and foretelling the truthfulness of the noble Prophet ﷺ, that it is stated in these verses. However, when the sentences in these verses are examined too, and according to the principle that, even if the reason for the sending down is particular, the ruling will be general, it must not be thought that these verses concern them only. That these warnings are applicable to everyone who, knowing the scriptural wisdom and the religious truths – aiming at some practical gains and selfish interests – covers them up, there is no doubt about that. Look at how Allah has pointed out in these verses how great the terribleness of this evil practice is:

1) It is what Allah has sent down in the scripture that they cover up. This is, surely, defiance committed against Allah.

(2) The price they get in exchange for covering up – the gain received in exchange for it – will be merely paltry and trifling. It is only worldly profits or temporary gains that prompt them to this deed. However mighty indeed worldly gains may be, it will be merely trifling indeed. **فَمَا مَتَاعُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا فِي الآخِرَةِ إِلا قَلِيلٌ** (The comforts of the life of this world, compared with the enjoyment of the Hereafter, are not other than paltry. (Tawbah : 38)

(3) The main aim of people of this kind will, surely, be earning wealth, and yearning for leadership, and filling the stomach. In reality it is filling the belly with fire. Or, what each of the gains they receive on its account does is make them prey to the fire of Hell in the Hereafter

(4) Although people of that kind will be ones who play the part of great leaders and bigwigs here, before Allah they will be despicable and hated. Therefore, on the Day of Qiyamah Allah will not even *[p. 442]* speak to them. What other despicableness is there greater than this ?!

(5) Nor will Allah be ready, considering their pitiableness or helplessness, to make them good in any way, or to give them purity.

(6) Besides all this, a painful punishment too, or the harsh and cruel punishment of Hell too !

The error of these people is more serious than going astray without knowing, and without inquiring, that the truth is in such a manner. What these do is, even after knowing the truth, deliberately conceal it. That too, for paltry gains. Then, what in reality happens through this activity is selling right guidance and buying misguidance with a price, and slamming shut the ways to receive forgiveness of sins from Allah and, in exchange for it, receiving His punishment with outstretched hand. However harsh and cruel indeed the punishment of Hell may be, we will bear it and be patient with it. It is these temporary practical gains that are dearer to them than that – this is, surely, the attitude that comes out from these. That is what Allah asks, What is it that has given these so much forbearance about Hell? ( **فَمَآ أَصۡبَرَهُمۡ عَلَى ٱلنَّارِ** ) – thus. What Allah points out in the last verse are the crucial reasons for this matter's being so terrible a crime. That is : it is in accordance with the real proper manner that Allah has sent down the scripture. All that is in it indeed is the real. And it has been explained in it in the very proper manner in which it should be explained. Then, concealing anything from it is in reality a denial of the scripture and a throwing into disarray of Allah's aim. What these, who cause division and difference of opinion in the scripture, are doing is waging a contest against Allah, as His opposing party. This, for its part, is indeed extremely remote from the possibility of success, and from just validity!
