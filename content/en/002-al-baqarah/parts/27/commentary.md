### Section – 22

*[Verse 177]*

*[p. 444]* **بِرّ** (birr) means: merit, a good thing – such and so on is the meaning. That same word will also be used in the meanings meritorious one one who does good. In this verse and in the 189th verse below, it has been used in these two meanings. Another word like this is **عَدْل**('adl). This too is customarily used in the meanings justice – thus, and one who is just – thus.

What was said, turning toward the place of rising and the place of setting - turning to the east and to the west-, indicates the objections that the People of the Scripture put forth on account of the change of the qiblah – thus some; no, it was said in the form of a figure of simile – thus some others, can be seen to have opined. Either way, the summary of the idea contained in this verse is this; by practising some outward customs like turning toward a fixed direction, a person does not become meritorious and of virtuous conduct. When Allah commands that one must turn toward such and such a direction, other than that it becomes obligatory, it is not possible to gain merit by that alone. If a person is to become meritorious, he must both believe in all the matters that he ought to believe in, and practise all the matters that he ought to practise.

### The chief among the matters of belief are these :

(1) **Belief in Allah:** That is, believing in Allah's existence, oneness, powers and rights, sublime attributes – in all of these.

(2) **Belief in the Last Day:** That is, after the worldly life there is also a second life. About all the deeds done in this life, there will be questioning there, and recompense will be given. For the doers of good deeds protection, and for the doers of evil deeds punishment, will be the outcome – belief such as this.

(3) **Belief in the angels:** There is a kind of spiritual beings called angels. They will act only in accordance with Allah's command. They will keep on performing praises and glorifications to Allah. Allah customarily carries out many matters connected with this world by means of them. The bearers of the mission between Allah and His Rasuls, who are His prophets, are the angels. – Beliefs such as these

*[p. 445]* (4) **Belief in the scriptures:** Believing in general and as a whole in the scriptures that Allah has given to His prophets, and, since the Quran is the last book and is one that will remain until the end of the world, believing in it specially, in detail too.

(5) **Believing in the prophets:** Believing in general in all the prophets who have passed before, both those known by name and those who are not, and specially in Muhammad the noble Prophet ﷺ, who is the last prophet. These are the chief subjects among the matters of belief. The beliefs in Allah's decrees and dispositions ( **القضاءوالقدر** ), Paradise and Hell and the like are indeed ones that are included in the details of the first and the second subjects.

Among the subjects of chief importance in practice, the 1st subject that Allah has specially mentioned is spending wealth while having liking and love for it. For those who have no attachment or yearning for wealth, there will be no reluctance in spending wealth. As far as they are concerned, there is also not in it either a mind-set of sacrifice or a yearning for the earning of merit. It is in accordance with however much passion for wealth there is that the purity of intention of the one who spends it on a good matter will be assessed. That is why the Prophet ﷺ declared thus too: Among almsgiving, the most superior is the almsgiving that you do while being healthy and miserly, yearning for wealth and fearing poverty .(Bu;Mu) In another place Allah says : You will not attain merit until you spend from what you like. (3:92) A position that goes somewhat further than this is giving precedence to the need of others even when there was dire need on one's own account. This can be seen to have been stated by Allah in Surat al-Hashr as an excellent quality of the Companions who were Ansaris (59:9)

Allah has also made clear the ways in which wealth must be spent. That, just like the mind-set of the one who spends, on whom it is utilised, and to whom precedence must be given, also need to be heeded – that is what this teaches

(1) **Those who have family ties:** In a hadith, the Prophet ﷺ says: Giving charity to the poor is one charity. Giving to those who have kinship is two things : charity, and keeping up kinship (A;Ti;Na;Ja.;)

(2) **Yatimu (orphans):** That is, children who are both ones whose fathers have died; and ones who have no means for a livelihood. The matter that the Quran and hadith have repeatedly said about these in forceful language is well known, surely. In a hadith, the Prophet ﷺ is saying : The one who takes charge of a yatim, whether his own or of others, and I, will be like this in Paradise. While saying this, the noble Prophet had also shown the index finger and the middle finger, holding them apart (Mu). *[p. 446]* Whether it belongs to one's own family or belongs to another family, all right, for the one who takes on the responsibility of a yatim there is a mighty rank in Paradise – that is the gist.

(3) **Miskins (the destitute):** The Prophet ﷺ can be seen to have stated in a hadith who a miskin is. The gist of the hadith is like this: It is not these people who go wandering about so that they turn back if they get one date, or two dates, or one handful of food, or two handfuls of food, that are called miskin. But, the miskin is one who does not have in hand wealth enough not to depend on another person, and whose condition does not become understood by others so that he would get some almsgiving from others (Bu;Mu). Or, one who, though there is difficulty and urgency *[DOUBT: തിടുക്കം usually means “haste, urgency”; here it may mean “pressing need, straits”. Rendered “urgency”.]*, keeps preserving his self-respect without letting others know of it – that is the import.

(4) **The wayfarer:** That is, the traveller who is in difficulty, without means to go back to his homeland, one who has got ready for a journey for needful matters, foreigners who are without support, and the like.

(5) **Those who come asking:** The practice of begging is not becoming for respectable people. As for Islam, it is also a matter that it detests very much. On the contrary, trying to make prying enquiries about those who come begging and to send them away disgraced, and driving them back by scaring them without giving anything -without saying at least a good word- are also just like that. What has come thus in a hadith is noteworthy : The one who has come asking has a right. Even if he has come on horseback, all right (A;Da). In the course of mentioning the qualities of the virtuous, that in their properties the one who asks and the one who has faced obstruction (to asking) will have a right (51: 19, 70: 24, 25) – thus Allah too says.

(6) **In the matter of slaves:** That is freeing slaves, giving financial help in undertakings that are helpful to their freeing, and to their protection, and so on. This matter too is indeed one to which very much importance has been assigned in the Quran and in hadith.

In the matters of practice, the 2nd subject is establishing the prayer, and the 3rd is giving Zakat. The verses of the Quran and the sayings of the Prophet concerning both these matters are numerous. The importance that Islam has assigned to both-even among those who commit lapses in both-is well known. The 4th subject is fulfilling a pact if one makes it. About the importance of this too there is no need of an explanation. The very famous saying of the Prophet that the marks of the hypocrite are three – telling lies, and breaking a promise, and breach of trust – alone is enough to understand the gravity of this. The 5th is patience. Patience too indeed is a subject of reference in the Quran and in hadith in abundant measure. When stating about those who are patient, Allah's having adopted a special style (\*) that customarily carries the meaning: making a slight change in the grammatical style that had continued in the sentences before it, He *[p. 447]* reminds especially about those who are patient – that too is noteworthy. (Those who have a little familiarity with Arabic grammar will understand this quickly) Allah has also made clear when patience must be exercised. Yes, in a state of hardship, and in a state of adversity, or in difficulties such as poverty, and in adversities and losses like disease – in all of these indeed. Among them, the occasion of war is, surely, a stage that is very grave, and that the true believers have to face. Therefore it has also been specially mentioned.

It appears to mean: “That is, His word al-sabirin (those who are patient) is in the accusative by way of praise and urging; that is why it was not put in the nominative and was not joined by a conjunction of sequence (‘atf al-nasaq).” Please confirm whether this English should be shown.]*

To those who keep accepting, in the due manner, the matters of belief and practice that are chief in merit, Allah has finally given two testimonials too. (1) **أُولَئِكَ الذِينَ صَدَقُوا** (They are the ones who spoke the truth) – thus, and (2) **أُولَئِكَ هُمُ الْمُتَّقُونَ** (They indeed are the muttaqis too) – thus. Yes, it is they who have realised belief, and who have upheld as true, by way of action, the claim that they are believers. The real meritorious people, who observed caution by obeying Allah's commands and prohibitions, and became God-fearing by doing meritorious deeds, are also they – that is the gist. May Allah bless us all by including us among such meritorious people. **آمين**

*[Verses 178–179]*

*[p. 448]* The passive-usage( **مَجْهُول** ) form of the verb **كَتَبَ**(kataba) is **كُتِبَ** (kutiba). Joining one thing to another is indeed its real linguistic meaning. It is in the meaning ‘write’, in the sense of joining letters and words to one another, that it is usually used. It is from this, too, that the meaning book comes to the word **كِتَاب** (kitab). It is used in meanings such as record, prescribe, decree, determine too. It is in the meaning ‘has been prescribed’ that **كُتِبَ** (kutiba) has been used here and in places below such as 180, 183. When it comes in this meaning, most often that prescription being of an obligatory form *[p. 449]* is the usual way. Sometimes it may come without being obligatory too. All must be understood by the context, and by other proofs. The word **مَعْرُوف** (ma'ruf) can be seen used in many places in the Quran. What is known, what is familiar – such and so on is its literal meaning. As Imam Raghib (r) stated, everything that has become known as good usage by way of reason, or by way of religious laws, will be called ma'ruf. The exact opposite of this is **مُنْكَر**(munkar). Taking these facts into account, to **مَعْرُوف** good custom, custom, propriety – thus, and to **مُنْكَر** evil custom, impropriety, what is detested, what is unfamiliar – thus, meanings suited to the context have been being assigned.

The practice of killing in exchange for killing was usual earlier among the Arabs and among the People of the Scripture. But, justice and equality were not observed in it. If those of the lower rank killed those reckoned as being of the upper rank, killing more than one person in exchange; if the killer is a slave, killing his master; if it is a woman, killing her husband or some man belonging to her kin – wrongdoings and injustices such as these had been accepted. On the contrary, in case the higher ones killed those of low standing in prestige and in strength, they would also release the killer from retaliatory killing, adopting stratagems of some kind or other. Some injustices of this kind are in existence even today among some groups of people. Abolishing such unjust practices, Allah commands by way of this verse that observing equality in the retribution for the crime of killing is obligatory. The import of saying: carrying out retaliation has been prescribed on you. ( **كُتِبَ عَلَيۡكُمُ ٱلۡقِصَاصُ** ) is not that killing the one who killed is obligatory. What is intended is that equality and justice must be observed in retaliatory killing. What was said – for the free man the free man, and for the slave the slave, and for the woman the woman – is indeed the explanation of that. Giving authority to the heirs of the one put to death to be content with atonement- ransom- *[DOUBT: തെണ്ടം rendered “ransom”, as in parts 08 and 18, where the meaning of this word is an open doubt.]* without killing in exchange, and encouragement having been given to them for it, are, surely, because of that.

But, if a free man, or else the reverse, put a slave to death, a man, or else the reverse, a woman? Nothing about this is explained here. Therefore, differences of opinion can be seen among scholars in the explanation of this. Since, in case those opinions and proofs and pleas are quoted, it gets quite some lengthy, we are not entering into it here. Its place is in the books of fiqh (of jurisprudence). A few certain pieces of information can also be seen at Surah Ma'idah 48. **إن شاء الله** If it is with more than one person joining together that a person was killed, the majority opinion is indeed that all of them are liable to the death penalty. That in one *[p. 450]* incident 'Umar(r) judged thus, Bukhari (r) has also recorded.

**فَمَنۡ عُفِيَ لَهُۥ مِنۡ أَخِيهِ شَيۡءٞ**.... *[DOUBT: The printed dots “....” stand at the left end of the Arabic phrase, at the start of the line; from the layout it cannot be told whether they belong before or after it. Placed after the Arabic.]* (In case anything is pardoned to anyone from his brother, then, following the propriety of good custom, and paying off to him in a good manner, is what is needed) – in the sentence that says so, many matters that affect both parties are contained. For example:

(1) Because a murder took place, the Islamic brotherhood tie between both groups is not severed. The word ‘if anything is pardoned from his brother – thus, indicates this. That, since they are brothers to each other, something in the retaliation ought to be yielded – it indicates that too.

(2)It can also be understood from this that, in case at least some among those who handle the affairs of the one who was killed give a relaxation in carrying out retaliatory killing, the killer must not be killed in exchange. ‘If anything is pardoned,’ is, surely, what Allah said.

(3) If released from retaliatory killing, then, following the propriety of good custom, and paying off fully to him in a good manner, is what is needed. That is compensation according to the proper manner of good custom - atonement - must be paid off fully from the side of the killer to the heirs of the one put to death. There must not be any lapse, or dispute, carried out in it. It must be carried out gracefully and in a good manner. Many things like these can be understood from this sentence.

Pardoning from killing once and then killing again, after some heirs have given pardon the other heirs carrying out killing, after taking atonement killing again, killing someone other than the real killer, demanding, in the amount of the atonement, beyond customary usage, having determined to give atonement not paying it off fully properly, and the like – the warning about these injustices is the sentence **فَمَنِ ٱعۡتَدَىٰ بَعۡدَ ذَٰلِكَ** (In case anyone acts in transgression after that, for him there is a painful punishment). Since it has been made clear in the 92nd verse of Surat al-Nisa' that in killings that come about by mistake, not intentionally, it is atonement that must be given, it can be understood that it is about deliberate killing that it has been stated here. The atonement has been reckoned to be a hundred camels. Information such as how they must be paid off fully and so on can be seen explained in the books of hadith and in the books of fiqh.

With the address **يَاأُولِي الألْبَابِ** (O men of reason), Allah has pointed out, in the 179th verse, the principle contained in the law of killing in exchange for killing. Only men of reason will be able to grasp it by thinking-for ordinary people, and for those who take into account only the outward state, it will not turn out that they are able to understand it quickly- that is what *[p. 451]* that address indicates. In prescribing retaliatory killing there is a life for mankind -for their life there is protection in a way- that is what Allah said. Think it over ! It is certain that by means of this law the crime of killing will decrease in the community. People who are prompted to put another to death as soon as some hostility, or selfishness, arises-that such people are not rare in the human community is indeed undeniable-for them to withdraw from that act, this law becomes a cause. For one who has once killed a person, in case he is not subjected to a severe punishment, what happens is that his boldness to carry out killing again increases. Just as his having to undergo the death penalty will be a lesson for others like him too, the way for killing to happen again at his hand also comes to cease to exist. We often hear about cruel men who carried out enormous killings in tens and in hundreds. There is no need to say that, had the death penalty been decreed upon him in the first killing, that would not have happened. About human nature, and about the measure to restrain him from his inclination to wrongdoing and from indiscretion, it is, surely, his Creator who knows, more than man. Those who do not accept the basic principle that the way to restrain people from inclinations to wrongdoing is giving punishment in accordance with the offence- even if they were irreligious atheists - it does not seem that there will be. If anyone thinks it is not so, there is only to say that they are fools among fools. It is penal laws that are practical and fitting in accordance with this basic principle that Allah has sent down too as the laws of Islam.

It was stated before, surely, that in the time of Jahiliyyah many people had gone beyond bounds in carrying out retaliatory killing in exchange for killing. But, it is also said that some groups among them had been content, taking compensation without carrying out retaliatory killing. Today, in some lands that pride themselves as ‘civilised nations’, a way of thinking has spread that carrying out retaliatory killing is an antiquated practice, and that it is a barbaric law, and so on. Because of this way of thinking, which originated from materialist outlooks and from satanic evil promptings, in some places they have discontinued the death penalty, and have instead made do with some other punishments, its result? It is nothing else, mind you, than the increase of the crime of killing, and distorted refinements in the form of committing killing! This is a plain truth that all who have eyes can see, and all who have ears can hear. There are also nations that, after abolishing the death penalty, were compelled to implement it again. If one examines once the statistics of wrongdoings such as killing, theft, robbery that are rampant in the land where those Islamic penal laws, which have been branded antiquated and barbaric, are in force, and in the lands where penal laws that are proclaimed as civilised and modern and so on are in existence, this plain truth will become convincing to anyone. If, in the nations where Islamic law is in force, it is under ten killings that take place in a year, in the other the number of murders seen will be at the rate of ten or a hundred *[p. 452]* in an hour. Even so, it is a wonder that man does not open his eyes. (\*) But, there is no wonder in those who defy the Creator, and the Creator's laws, not being able to grasp realities of this sort.

> **Note:** (\*) In a news item from Washington, that in America, the most civilised and wealthy nation in the world, last year 12,000 people - that is, 32 persons daily- were killed, the ‘Malayala Manorama’ daily newspaper has published in its issue of 10. 07. 1978.
