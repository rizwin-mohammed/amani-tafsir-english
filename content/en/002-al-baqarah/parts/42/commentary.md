*[Verses 250–251]*

*[p. 596]* It was said, surely, in the previous verse that Allah will be with those who are patient. Therefore, when they went down to the battlefield with the enemies, those true believers kept on praying to Allah both to shower down upon them the favour that is patience, and, so as to battle bravely with the enemies and win success- to make firm the foot without cowardice and faltering of step, and for the final success to become theirs. Even if strength of men and power of arms are present in plenty, both success and help must, surely, in reality be received from Allah indeed. ( **ومالنصر الامن عند الله** ) *[DOUBT: the Arabic is printed without a Malayalam rendering and is transcribed as printed (bare, with الامن written as one word). It most likely means: “And help is not except from Allah.”]* Lo, Allah accepted their supplication. With the permission and help of Allah, they both defeated and chased off that mighty enemy army. Dawud (a), from the side of the true believers, also killed the giant Jalut, who had been their backbone.

After king Talut, Dawud (a) became the king of the Israelites. Later, prophethood too came to be received by him. The intention by wisdom ( **الْحِكْمَةَ** ) may be inclusive of prophethood as well. **اللّه اعلم** In any case, what is known is indeed that the first great man who attained the fortune of adorning together the ranks both of kingship and of prophethood is indeed the Prophet Dawud (a). Some knowledges such as making war-coats, understanding the language of birds, have been stated in the Quran as favours specially given to him. That it was said, and taught him some matters that Allah wills ( **وَعَلَّمَهُمِمَّايَشَاءُ** ), indicates matters such as these. The Quran commentators can be seen to have said that at the time he killed Jalut he was a young man who went about grazing sheep, and so on. In the quotation of the Bible quoted below too, it says so. Allah knows the reality. The summary of what the Bible has described of this scene can be quoted like this:-

‘.........He (Goliath) called out to the ranks of Yisrayel: I am a Philistine and you are the retainers of Saul, surely. Let one person come near me. In case he fights a duel and kills me, we will become slaves to you. Hearing this word, Saul and party were afraid. David (Dawud) was the son of Jesse of Bethlehem ( **بيتلحم** ) in Judah. Jesse's eldest three children had come for the war. David was both the small one and one who went to graze the sheep. Jesse sent David both to learn the news of the elder brothers, and to take some goods to them. When he went, he heard the words of Goliath. David enquired what the king would give to the one who kills him. Saul knew the information that the people said, that the king will make him rich, will give the daughter in marriage to him, and so on, had David brought and questioned him. He said that, a lion and a bear having come to seize my sheep, I have beaten them and killed them. Thus, Saul gave David a coat and a sword and so on. *[p. 597]* But, as he had not been accustomed to any of that before, David took it all off. He took a staff. He picked up five stones too and put them in the shepherd's bag. With a sling in his hand he went near Goliath. Asking David, who was both a boy and comely, am I a dog, to bring both a staff and a stone, and so on, Goliath despised him. David put a stone in the sling and swung it. It struck his forehead. He fell. Thus, with one stone he killed him. Seeing this, the Philistines ran. Both the Yisra'ilites and the Judeans pursued them, shouting aloud.’ (1. Sam. Chapter: 17)

‘Though Saul gave the daughter in marriage to David, later, seeing the love and influence he had among the people, Saul came to have enmity towards him; following that, many clashes took place between them; in all of that, a son of Saul acted in favour of David; between the prophet Samuel and king Saul indeed too, disagreement arose; Samuel had told David that the kingship after Saul is for him; in a war that occurred with the Philistines, Saul was killed; before that, Samuel had attained the course of time; after Saul, though the people of Judea accepted David as king, the others made a son of Saul king; it was after the death of that son that the Israelites in their entirety acknowledged David as king.’ – such things the Bible can be seen to describe at length in the following chapters. The larger part of this is such that there is no means to affirm it in the light of the Quran. The Quran, for its part, has here contained all the historically important portions of Dawud (a) together in the three short sentences that are both ‘Dawud killed Jalut’, and ‘Allah gave him kingship and wisdom’, and ‘He taught him some matters that He wills’ ( **وَقَتَلَدَاوُدُاِلَىقَوْلِهُ مِمَايَشَاء** ) *[DOUBT: the Arabic is printed without a Malayalam rendering and is transcribed as printed. It most likely means: “And Dawud killed … up to His saying: of what He wills.”]*. The practice of telling compressed in one or two words an event that the Bible has described across more than one chapter can be seen in the Quran in other places too, like this.

After describing this historical event, Allah is pointing out the principle contained in bringing events of this kind into effect **وَلَوْلادَفْعُ اللَّهِ النَّاسَ** (If Allah had not been warding off some among mankind by means of some, the earth would have gone off to ruin.) It is not that the terrestrial world would perish, or that material life would come to a stop- it is indeed throwing both peaceful life and moral life in the terrestrial world into disorder- that is the gist of having said that the earth goes to ruin. In case Allah, from time to time helping and making successful the mission-bearers of true faith and of right guidance against the great powers of disbelief and of misguidance, does not ward them off and hold them back, the whole terrestrial world will, surely, turn into a theatre of devilishness and of bestiality. In order to do away with that- intending the common good of the people of the world -it is one of the procedural rules that Allah, on account of His generosity and favour, brings into effect in this world – that is the import. In Su: Hajj Allah say*[p. 598]*s: **اللَّهُ وَلَوْلا دَفْعُ اللَّهِ النَّاسَ بَعْضَهُم بِبَعْضٍلَّهُدِّمَتْ صَوَامِعُ** (Gist: If Allah had not been warding off some among mankind by means of some, many monasteries, Christian sanctuaries, Jewish sanctuaries, and Muslim mosques, in which the name of Allah is extolled abundantly, would have been pulled down and destroyed. (Hajj: 40)

All that was described above are real events that need to be known. They are actual truths known to both the People of the Scripture and the seekers of history. Because you are a divine messenger sent by Allah, We are reciting all this in your hearing (by means of an angel) and describing it to you – such things Allah is alerting the noble Prophet ﷺ to.

### Section – 34 · Juz' – 3

*[Verse 253]*

*[p. 599]* In this surah, up to now, He came to make statements both about many Rasuls and about their communities. At last, in the previous verse, He said also that the noble Prophet ﷺ too is one belonging to those Rasuls. After that, this is a general statement pointing to all the Rasuls stated above. That is: they all are divine messengers commissioned and sent in order to give guidance to the people. Yet, in superiority and in ranks not every*[p. 600]*one is of equal standing. Allah has made some of them superior to some. Many ranks that are not given to some, He will have given to some others. As an example, among them there are those to whom Allah spoke directly indeed, without the medium of angels or anything else. Likewise, to some others He has also given some special qualities that the others do not have. That indeed is the gist of what He said in the first sentences.

The Prophet Musa (a) is the Rasul who attained the great fortune of Allah speaking directly. Allah indeed has made it plain that Allah spoke to him. (4: 164) Besides, the event of Allah speaking to him at the valley of Sina can be seen in the Quran in more than one place too. It is not known that this fortune came about for anyone else in this terrestrial world. It is indeed for that reason too that **كَلِيمُ اللَّهِ** (Kalimullahi = the person to whom Allah spoke) is said about him. That, in the famous ‘Mi'raj’ (the journey of ascension to heaven) of the noble Prophet ﷺ, Allah spoke to the noble Prophet has come in strong hadiths. In Surat al-Najm some indications of it too can be seen. All the Rasuls were ones commissioned to some one people and period. The rank of being both the person sent to the world's people as a whole, and the last prophet, belongs only to the Prophet ﷺ. Likewise, the noble Prophet has many other honours too that other prophets do not have. In the same way, it is clear from the Quran that to the Prophet Ibrahim (a) indeed too there are many positions of rank and superiorities that the others did not receive. That ‘Allah has made him a friend’, Allah has stated in 4: 125. That is why **خلِيلُ اللَّهِ** ( ‘Khalilullahi = the friend of Allah) is said about him. To all the Rasuls, the proofs and signs needed for the carrying out of their respective missions were not left ungiven. However, in those too the standing of the Rasuls will be different. The proofs given to the Prophet 'Isa (a) are an example of that. According to the leave of Allah, giving life to those who had died, making sparrows from soil and making them fly, curing incurable diseases, and the like, were his special signs.

The Christians have been contending about 'Isa (a) both that he is the son of God and that he is God indeed. It is the reality both that this contention is a lie, and that, though he is a person born without a father, he is only a human being born from a woman, that Allah indicates, when speaking about the Prophet 'Isa (a), mostly by means of the usage **عِيسي ابن مريم** ('Isa, son of Maryam). We have pointed out before, in the explanation of the 87th verse, both that the intention by the Holy Spirit ( **رُوحُ القُدْس** ) is the angel Jibril (a), and that the import of having said strengthened by means of him will be the spiritual helps that he used to receive by means of Jibril (a). Look there for more information. (About the Christian prattlings concerning the Holy Spirit, it will be described in the last portion of Surat al-Nisa'. **إن شاء الله**)

*[p. 601]* Though the Rasuls were of different standings in superiorities and in ranks, the principle of the preaching of them all was one. Clear proofs to establish it have also been given to all. Even so, among the people, both division persists, and, splitting into camps as true believers and disbelievers, quarrel and strife keep going on. All this is not something that happens without Allah knowing. If He had willed that it must not happen so, it would surely not have happened so. Or, everyone, all together, would have been indeed both true believers and rightly guided. But, along with having given both the discriminating intellect to distinguish good and bad and sufficient proofs, indeed, Allah has given man the freedom of will *[DOUBT: ഇച്ഛാ സ്വാതന്ത്ര്യം is the usual Malayalam for “free will”; ഇച്ഛ alone is rendered “desire” in this translation, so the literal option is “freedom of desire”.]* too to accept what he likes. What Allah has determined is to give him, in the future life, the recompense in accordance with whatever position he adopts in this life using that freedom. One party, making use of this freedom, attains right guidance. Another party, misusing it, falls into misguidance. It is this. The cause both of the said quarrels and of the divisions. Such things are the gist contained in what Allah said in the subsequent sentences. On this subject, more information will come in Su: Hud verses 118, 119 **إن شاء الله**

Some can be seen to have indicated some new ideas here, arguing that the intention by the division ( **اِخْتِلَاف** ) said above is the diverse inclinations needed for man's progress and growth in life. If it were so, elucidating that division, Allah would not have said **فَمِنْهُمْمَنْآمَنَوَمِنْهُمْمَنْكَفَرَ** (Thus, among them there are those who believed too; there are those who disbelieved too). Something else, such as ‘Among them there are both traders and cultivators’, was what would have had to be said.

Why was the freedom of will to accept both given to mankind? Why were they not created giving only the inclination to accept right guidance? – man has no entitlement to question thus. Man does not have the strength to grasp its answer either. **لايُسْأَلُعَمَّايَفْعَلُ وَهُمْيُسْأَلُونَ** (Will not be questioned about what He does; they, for their part, will be questioned too.) All is Allah's creation. His disposition of laws. Which is the form that must be given to each creature. Which is the nature – such things He knows. Only He knows that. All is His intention and will ( **وَلَكِنَّاللَّهَيَفْعَلُ مَا يُرِيدُ** ). In accordance with that He will bring everything into effect.

### Section – 34

*[p. 602]* Just as He counselled spending wealth in the way of Allah following the description of the event pointed out in the 243rd verse, here too, after telling at length the second (Talut's) event that passed among the Israelites, Allah counsels the true believers to spend in the way of Allah. For struggling in the way of the true din, wealth too, surely, is needed, just as selves *[DOUBT: ദേഹങ്ങൾ is rendered “selves”, as settled for ദേഹം; here the sense may be “bodies” (persons), as part 40's ദേഹംകൊണ്ട് “with the body”.]* are.
