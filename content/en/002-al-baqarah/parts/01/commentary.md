*\[p. 176\]* Some isolated letters like these can be seen at the beginning of 29 surahs in the Quran. Because they have no connection, either structural or of meaning, with the sentences that follow, they are called **الحروف المقطعة** (the letters standing apart). At the beginning of some surahs they have come as only one letter (like **ص، ن**), in some as two letters (like **طه، حم**), in some as three letters (like **الم، الر**), in some as four letters (like **المص، المر**), and in some as five letters (like **كهيعص، حم عسق**) – in five kinds in this way. Among these themselves, some can be seen repeated in more than one surah, and some not repeated at all.

Concerning such letters, there are mainly two opinions current.

1\) It is a secret that Allah has deposited in His Book. We believe in them. Their meaning or interpretation we do not know. This is one opinion. Eminent Companions such as Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali and Ibn Mas'ud (r), Tabi'is (Successors) who were Hadith scholars, such as Sha'bi and Sufyan ath-Thawri (r), and a section of the Quran commentators have adopted this position.

*\[p. 177\]* 2\) Some meanings and indications are contained in them. We must understand that by reflecting and examining. This is the position of another group. However, in their interpretation, or in pointing out the secrets of the indications contained in them, these people will not be found to have an agreed opinion. There are differing approaches. The summary of the more relevant of them is this:–

The Quran too is a book composed of such Arabic letters, well known to everyone. But these letters are a challenge to the disbelieving enemies, to this effect: 'Just bring a book like this, or a portion like one chapter of it.' Many Quran commentators, such as Zamakhshari and Baidawi (r), are seen justifying and explaining this opinion. Imam Razi (r) has stated that Imam Mubarrad (r) and many other careful investigators hold this opinion. Ibn Kathir (r) too has said: 'Ash-Shaikh al-Imam Ibn Taimiyyah and our eminent teacher al-Hafiz Abul Hajjaj al-Mizzi (**الحافظ المزي**), also hold this opinion.' There are 28 letters in the Arabic alphabet. It is 14 letters, half of them, that are in these isolated letters at the beginnings of the surahs, namely: **ا، ل، م، ص، ر، ك، هـ، ي، ع، ط، س، ح، ق، ز** *\[TN: the last letter of the list is printed ز; the fourteenth letter of the isolated letters (as at the opening of Surah Al-Qalam) is ن, and ز does not occur among them.\]*. Taking pronunciation and differences of sound into account, the Arabic letters are divided in many ways. In each category, half can be seen to occur in these 14. (\*) The remaining halves can be inferred from these, surely. In the same way, if words in Arabic are examined, they will be ones that contain from one up to five letters. Where more are seen, they will be ones increased for some reason beyond the actual letters in their root. In these isolated letters too, there are from one up to five letters. In addition, some varieties of noun, verb and particle, and variations of form, are contained in them.

> **Note:** (\*) Just as in Malayalam letters are usually divided as *kharam*, *atikharam*, *mridu* and *laghu* *\[TN: the fourth term is printed ലഘു (laghu); the usual Malayalam grammatical term in this series is ഘോഷം (ghosham). Translated as printed.\]*, the Arabic letters too have been classified variously as (**رخوة، شدة، همس، جهر، إطباق، فتح**) and so on. A detailed account of them can be seen in the books of 'Ilmut Tajwid' (**عِلْمُ التَّجْوِيدِ**), the science of reciting the Quran.

If all this were explained here at length, it would become very long, so we content ourselves with a partial explanation. In short, it is by using Arabic letters thus well known to everyone, and the words formed from them, that all Arabic literature, prose and verse, takes shape, surely. The Quran too is a *\[p. 178\]* collection of these same letters and words; that being so, the fact that no one is able to present a work that can match the whole of it or some part of it is a clear proof of its superhuman (miraculous) nature. Such is the gist of this approach. (\*)

> **Note:** (\*) Those who wish to know further clarification should see the tafsir works of great men such as Zamakhshari and Baidawi (r).

A fact that lends support to this opinion is pointed out here. It is at the beginning of 29 surahs that such letters occur. When those surahs are examined, some sentences pointing out the truth, the greatness and the superhuman nature of the Quran can be seen following along with them. For example: here, following the letters **الم**, it says 'There is no doubt at all in that Book' (**ذَٰلِكَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبُ لَا رَيۡبَۛ فِيهِۛ**); and in the next surah, following them, it says 'There is no object of worship other than Allah; He is the Living One and the Controller. He has sent down to you the Book according to the truth' (**ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ٱلۡحَيُّ ٱلۡقَيُّومُ نَزَّلَ عَلَيۡكَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ بِٱلۡحَقِّ**). Statements like these can be seen in other surahs too. Although there are many other opinions to be quoted concerning these letters, all of them are no more than some opinions; the backing of proofs is not seen for them.

Ibn Kathir (r) has quoted thus what a great man stated: 'That Allah has not revealed these letters in vain, without purpose, is beyond doubt. If anyone says that there is something in the Quran that is purely devotional (**تعبدي**), without any meaning at all, that is a great blunder. So it is certain that those letters have some meaning. However, if something comes to us correctly from the one free from sin (from the Messenger), we will say accordingly. Otherwise we will stop where we stand, and we will say **ءَامَنَّا بِهِۦ كُلّٞ مِّنۡ عِندِ رَبِّنَاۗ** (We have believed in it; all is from our Rabb). The scholars have not agreed on one definite opinion. They have differed. So, if any opinion becomes clear to anyone on the basis of evidence, he should follow it. Otherwise, what is needed is to remain silent until the matter becomes clear.'

It will be useful to recall some facts here. It is certain that there is nothing in the Quran without any meaning or purpose, and that there cannot be. But it can be seen in Su. Al Imran, verse 7, that there may be some parts in it that not everyone in general is able to understand. The word used for it there is **متشابه** (that which has mutual resemblance – or that which cannot be made out). We have already explained this in the Preface. If Allah helps, some details can be seen in Al Imran too, **إن شاء الله**.

*\[p. 179\]* An acceptable Hadith that has come concerning these letters is this: 'Whoever recites one letter from the Book of Allah, he has one good deed for it. And a good deed carries ten times the reward. I do not say that "Alif-Lam-Mim" is one letter. "Alif" is one letter, "Lam" is one letter, "Mim" is one letter.' This Hadith was quoted from Ibn Mas'ud (r) by Hakim, Tirmidhi, Bazzar and Darimi (r), and by Bukhari (r) in his 'Tarikh'. In this too it has not been stated what the purport of the said letters is. Nevertheless, it becomes clear from this that there is good even in reciting the Quran without knowing its meaning. For details, see the Preface.

What was said as (**ذَٰلِكَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبُ** That Book) intends the Quran itself. When a thing is situated nearby, to point to it, it is **هذا** (this) and the like that are ordinarily used in Arabic. Sometimes, indicating the greatness or the exalted rank of the thing pointed to, **ذَٰلِكَ** (that, yon) is also used in its place. It is in accordance with this that that demonstrative pronoun has been used here too. What is meant is 'that great Book'. Pointing to the Quran, Allah states two things: (1) There is no doubt at all in it. (2) It is guidance for those who observe caution. He then goes on to describe the qualities of those who observe caution, and makes known that it is those who possess those qualities who are the rightly guided and the successful.

The word **رَيۡبَ** (raib) takes meanings such as doubt, misconception and the like. The first meaning is the one here. In the subjects set forth in the Quran, in its messages, or in its *lakshya drishtantangal* *\[DOUBT: ലക്ഷ്യ ദൃഷ്ടാന്തങ്ങൾ may mean "proofs and illustrations" (ലക്ഷ്യം in its older sense of "evidence") or "aims and illustrations".\]*, there is no room whatever for doubt. That all are perfectly clear realities: such is the purport of saying **لَا رَيۡبَۛ فِيهِۛ** (there is no doubt at all in it). But, merely because it is a book full of truth and perfectly clear, its guidance will not benefit all human beings; its guidance will be of benefit by that only to those who are ready to accept it: this is what the sentence **هُدٗى لِّلۡمُتَّقِينَ** (it is guidance for those who observe caution) points out.

About the meaning of the words **هُدًى، هِدَايَة** (huda, hidayah), we explained in Su. Fatihah, surely. It is to the word **مُتَّقِينَ** (muttaqin) that the meaning 'those who observe caution' was given. What is meant by that word is: the God-fearing, who live following Allah's commands and prohibitions and guarding against the things that cause His punishment and His displeasure. It is this God-fearing reverence, which is caution, that is called **تَقْوَى** (taqwa). Although the wordings of the Quran commentators in explaining the purport of this word may be seen to differ, all of them can be seen to agree on this idea. The reason why the guidance of the Quran benefits only those who observe caution *\[p. 180\]* Allah points out in another place:

> **قُلۡ هُوَ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ هُدٗى وَشِفَآءٞۚ وَٱلَّذِينَ لَا يُؤۡمِنُونَ فِيٓ ءَاذَانِهِمۡ وَقۡرٞ وَهُوَ عَلَيۡهِمۡ عَمًى** – حم السجدة

(Gist: Say: It – the Quran – is guidance and healing – comfort-giving – for those who have believed. As for those who do not believe, there will be a kind of heaviness in their ears. It will also be a blindness for them. (41:44)) Whenever those who are not ready to believe hear the Quran, what happens is that their denial increases. Thus it ends up being more harmful to them. Allah says: **وَنُنَزِّلُ مِنَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانِ مَا هُوَ شِفَآءٞ وَرَحۡمَةٞ لِّلۡمُؤۡمِنِينَ وَلَا يَزِيدُ ٱلظَّـٰلِمِينَ إِلَّا خَسَارٗا** (Gist: Through the Quran We keep sending down that which is healing and mercy for the true believers. It does not increase the wrongdoers in anything but loss. (17:82)) Allah has here set out five things as the chief marks of those who observe caution. They are these:–

1\) **يُؤۡمِنُونَ بِٱلۡغَيۡبِ** (they are those who believe in the unseen matter). In the language, accepting that a thing is true is called **إِيمَانٌ** (Iman). In statements that join belief and action together, such as **ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ وَعَمِلُواْ ٱلصَّـٰلِحَٰتِ** (those who believed and did good deeds), the Iman meant is the one in this sense. However, in the Quran and in Islamic literature, 'Iman' and the words derived from it are usually used, when unqualified, in the sense of accepting the truth and submitting to it. According to this sense, what is meant will be believing with the mind, and making that belief real in practice by word and by deed. That is why a great many great men of the earlier generations, among them Imam Shafi'i, Ahmad and Bukhari (r), say that Iman increases through good deeds and Iman decreases through evil deeds. Imam Bukhari (r) has quoted many Quranic verses and sayings of the Prophet as evidence for this in his 'Sahih'.

The literal meaning of the word **غَيْبٌ** (ghaib) is 'the unseen, or a hidden matter'. Linguistically, everything hidden from the sight of the outer senses is 'ghaib'. What was said in (4:34) about wives who guard themselves, without doing anything undesirable, in the absence of their husbands – **حَٰفِظَٰتٞ لِّلۡغَيۡبِ** – is in this sense. What is meant by believing in the 'ghaib' is matters such as the Being of Allah, the angels, the Hereafter, the Reckoning, Paradise, Hell and the experiences in the grave: matters that one cannot discover by oneself through the outer senses, or through the inner senses, or by the intellect, and that can be known only through the statements of the scriptures and of the prophets.

*\[p. 181\]* That Allah said that the first mark of the muttaqis (those who observe caution) is believing in the 'ghaib' is very noteworthy. Those who have firmly resolved that they will believe only what they have seen and heard directly, or that there can be nothing beyond what is discovered by thinking with the intellect and by doing research, are not going to receive guidance either through the Quran or through the scriptures. It is about people like these that 'deniers of religion', 'irreligionists' and so on are said. Arrogance that they know everything, and stubbornness, will be their nature. 'We are people of progressive ideas; believing in the unseen is merely outdated and uncivilised': such will be their prattle. That being so, there is no use, surely, in counselling them about any hidden matters such as the Hereafter, the resurrection, Paradise and Hell. That is the reason why the guidance of the Quran is of no benefit to them.

The 'ghaib' (the unseen matter) can be divided into two: the absolute 'ghaib' and the relative 'ghaib'. Everything that no one other than Allah can know is the absolute 'ghaib'. Matters that some are able to know and some others are not able to know fall within the relative 'ghaib'. Although angels are able to know many of the things in the exalted worlds where they dwell, humans have no ability for that, surely. In relation to humans, this is 'ghaib' of the second kind. None of the knowledge attained by thinking with the intellect, or by using scientific methods, or by experience, or by means of indications, is included in 'ghaib'. For example: (1) Knowledge obtained with the help of instruments such as the wireless telegraph, the radio and the spacecraft. (2) Knowledge obtained by observation about things such as the speed of motion and the rotation of the stars and planets, and the temperature inside the earth. (3) Inferences that may be obtained through long experience, facial features, differences of voice, foresight, intellectual ability, indications in dreams and the like. None of these is included in 'ghaib'. At most, in the sense that some individuals are able to understand them and some individuals are not able to understand them, one may, if one wishes, speak of them as relative 'ghaib' in the linguistic sense. That much only.

(2, 3) **وَيُقِيمُونَ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَمِمَّا رَزَقۡنَٰهُمۡ يُنفِقُونَ** (and they establish the prayer, and spend from what We have given them). In the language, **صَلَاةٌ** (salat) is a word that has the meaning 'supplication'. What is meant by it is the Islamic act of prayer in a particular manner, beginning with the Takbir and ending with the Salam. What is meant by establishing the prayer is performing the five daily obligatory prayers without missing their times, completing their components, conditions and proprieties. The purport of saying 'spend from what We *\[p. 182\]* have given' is Zakat, the obligatory charity in Islam, and all the charities besides Zakat that have been commanded to be performed. It is true that Islam keeps encouraging the performance of other voluntary prayers too, besides the five daily obligatory prayers, and other charities too, besides the obligatory charities. But it was because the context is one describing the indispensable qualities of the muttaqis that it was said that it is the obligatory prayers and charities that are meant. Among man's ritual duties toward Allah, the act of prayer is a very important one. Charity, besides being a duty toward Allah, is also a social duty. It becomes clear from these verses that those who do not fulfil these two duties will not be included among the muttaqis, and that the guidance of the Quran will not bear fruit for them.

Without saying 'spend from wealth' or anything else, the word Allah used is 'spend from what We have given' (**مِمَّا رَزَقۡنَٰهُمۡ يُنفِقُونَ**). This is a very meaningful word. This usage can be seen in many places in the Quran. The earning of wealth is, after all, through production from the earth and through effort. It is Allah who gave the earth its productive power, who brings forth produce from it, and who makes it beneficial. It is Allah Himself, too, who gives man the knowledge, the ability, the means and the circumstances to strive. The gains obtained by two people who keep applying the same kind of attention and effort to one matter, the yields obtained from two crops cultivated in the same soil in the same way, and the gains of the same person labouring twice in the same manner and to the same measure, will not always be the same. In the very place where a gain of thousands was obtained once, another time the result may turn out to be zero. So it is clear that resources become available not merely according to the nature of the land or the character of the labour, and that it is Allah who determines and gives everything. Only, the resources Allah has determined are obtained through the channels He Himself has determined. Thus, all the wealth and resources in man's hand are what Allah has given. That being so, why should man be reluctant to spend from it? If he spends from what Allah has given on the matters He directs, He may give a substitute – one thing or another – if not today, then tomorrow. Such hints are contained in this usage.

(4) **وَٱلَّذِينَ يُؤۡمِنُونَ بِمَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيۡكَ وَمَآ أُنزِلَ مِن قَبۡلِكَ** (they will be those who believe in what has been sent down to you and in what was sent down before you). That is, the gist is that they will believe specifically in this scripture sent down to the Prophet ﷺ, and in general in the scriptures such as the Tawrat and the Injil revealed *\[p. 183\]* to the former prophets. What all the prophets preached was Islam itself. **إِنَّ ٱلدِّينَ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡإِسۡلَٰمُۗ** (Surely, the religion with Allah is Islam (3:19)). Only, there would be some differences suited to the times in the practical and ritual manner. **لِكُلّٖ جَعَلۡنَا مِنكُمۡ شِرۡعَةٗ وَمِنۡهَاجٗا** (For each one of you, all of you, We have appointed a course of procedure and an open way. (5:48)) That being so, not believing in any one prophet or in his scripture would in effect be denying all of them, surely. The Quran confirms the scriptures before it as true. The meaning of (**مُصَدِّقٗا لِّمَا بَيۡنَ يَدَيۡهِ**), which is often seen in the Quran, can be understood from this. (On this subject, more clarification can be seen in verses 136, 137 and 285 of this surah, and in verses such as 150–152 of Suratun Nisa'.)

The People of the Scripture, being the Jews and the Christians, are those who believe in certain particular prophets and disbelieve in the rest. As for the Arabs, who were not People of the Scripture, apart from a nominal ancestral connection with Prophet Ibrahim (a), they had no connection at all with the other prophets. So, as regards those who come into Islam from among the People of the Scripture, besides having believed in their own scriptures and prophets, they must believe once more in the Prophet ﷺ and in the Quran. As regards those from among the Arabs who accept the true faith, they must newly believe in all the prophets and scriptures, the Quran and the Prophet ﷺ included. That is why Allah has stated, concerning those who believe from among the People of the Scripture, **أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ يُؤۡتَوۡنَ أَجۡرَهُم مَّرَّتَيۡنِ** (They will be given their reward twice over (28:54)). The Prophet ﷺ says: 'Allah will give reward twice over to three kinds of people: the one who believed in his own prophet and in me; the one who fulfilled the duty to Allah and the duty to his own master; and the one who taught his own slave-woman good manners well, and after that freed her and married her: these are those three.' (Bu., Mu.).

What this saying means is not that each individual from among the People of the Scripture who becomes a Muslim will be superior to all other Muslims, however exalted the rank of those may be. It only means that they will receive a reward of two kinds in one matter – in belief in the prophets. This is not contrary to others receiving more reward than them through firmness of faith and abundance of good deeds. What is meant by believing in the former scriptures is not believing in detail in what is in the existing Tawrat and Injil and the like. Because, without having been subjected to alterations or to additions and omissions – both at the time the Quran was being revealed and after it – *\[p. 184\]* none of them exists in its actual form. Therefore, what is meant is belief in general. As regards the contents of the former scriptures, they will be in one of these three ways:

1. What agrees with the statements of Allah and of the Messenger. Affirming and believing this category is obligatory.
2. What is contrary to both of them. Rejecting and not believing this category is obligatory.
3. What is neither. It is about this category that the Prophet ﷺ said thus: 'When the People of the Scripture tell you something, do not affirm them as truthful and do not declare them liars. But say thus: We have believed in what has been sent down to us and in what has been sent down to you;' (Bu.).

(5) **وَبِٱلۡأٓخِرَةِ هُمۡ يُوقِنُونَ** (they firmly believe in the Hereafter). In reality, belief in the Hereafter too is included in what was said as believing in the 'ghaib'. Only when belief in the Hereafter is firm will there truly be belief about all the matters that are to come after this physical life. The complete belief and certainty that there is a life which is everlasting and in which recompense is given precisely and exactly for all good and evil, and that there, before Allah, the Controller of all, one will have to render account of all deeds – this is the sole basis of man's awareness of right guidance. Belief in Allah and in the Hereafter is the foundation of all the doctrines of Islamic belief. Or, the awareness of man's beginning and return. To put it in another way, the awareness of where one came from and where one is going. All other beliefs will be details of this or its indispensable aspects. That is why, in most of the places in the Quran where belief is spoken of, belief in Allah and in the Hereafter is usually singled out and mentioned.

After saying that those who possess these five distinguishing qualities are the muttaqis (those who observe caution), He describes what their gains will be: **أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ عَلَىٰ هُدٗى مِّن رَّبِّهِمۡۖ** (they will be on right guidance from their Rabb). Yes, they are those who have accepted, and who walk in, that path which is the true right guidance that Allah has directed, that He is pleased with and that He has bestowed. This is the greatest certificate that one can obtain in this worldly life. What is there to be obtained above this?! Although we have given the translation 'they will be on right guidance from their Rabb', in keeping with the style of the Malayalam language, if one looks at the meaning of the words directly, what should be said is 'they *\[p. 185\]* are situated upon right guidance from their Rabb'. The Quran commentators point out the hint contained in this usage thus: As regards them, right guidance is just a vehicle, and they are the riders sitting on top of it. Just as all who ride have a destination that they are to reach, these too have a destination. Their goal, then, is Allah's pleasure and reward. To put it in another form, right guidance has not been tied onto them or forced upon them. 'That life of right guidance which their Lord appointed for them is entirely in accordance with their liking and their wish' – this is the hint.

They are on right guidance, and right guidance is in harmony with their wish and their desire. Very well; but then what gain are they to obtain by it? Allah says: **وَأُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡمُفۡلِحُونَ** (and it is they who are the successful). Yes, from all the things they had been guarding against in order to obtain protection, they obtain protection. All the matters that they had wished to be accomplished are accomplished for them. Not only in the next world; in this world too they are the successful. In this life, which is a mixture of happiness and sorrow, it is they alone who are able to stand firm with peace and contentment, without distinction between joy and grief. Yet that is not their goal. Their goal is success in the Hereafter and, above all, the pleasure of Allah. In short, Allah brings them to that goal. Allah says: 'Your recompenses will be paid in full only on the Day of Qiyamah. Then whoever is moved away from the Fire and admitted into Paradise has attained fortune. The life of this world is nothing but an instrument of deception.' (Al Imran 185) 'And the pleasure from Allah is the greatest of all. That is the great fortune.' (Tawbah 72).

After describing the qualities and the gain of the true believers, the muttaqis, for whom the guidance of the Quran proves fruitful, He speaks about the disbelievers, the deniers of the truth, and their experience:–
