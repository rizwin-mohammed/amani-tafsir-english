*\[p. 324\]* That the Israelites cast off the teachings of their scripture was stated in the past verses. It is another misdeed of theirs that Allah points out in this verse. Sihr ( **ٱلسِّحۡرَ** ) is a word used, on the whole, in all these meanings: sorcery, black magic, conjuring, sleight of hand, illusion tricks, enchantment, jugglery, eye-binding, witchcraft All the stratagems whose cause-and-effect relationship is difficult to understand can be called sihr. Gaining protection from dangers by a secret and invisible way, bringing harm upon enemies and others, knowing hidden matters, achieving great things, uniting people with each other or setting them at odds, connecting with the souls of the dead, showing wondrous feats, and so on, are the aims of sihr. Most kinds will be without reality, sheer sham and illusion. Some, for their part, will be such as produce some mental changes, and through that some experiences become manifest.

From what has been described above it can, surely, be understood that sihr is of many kinds, and that all are not alike in seriousness and in lightness. That, on the whole, although all are indeed culpable and detested, not every kind of sihr in reality becomes shirk and kufr – that is the reality. Although some can be seen saying that all sihr is kufr, it is an opinion that does not take into account the wide range of meaning of sihr and its gradations in it. In any case, there is no doubt that all kinds of sihr are ones that do not suit true faith and the true believers, and that suit only disbelief and the disbelievers. It will be useful to know here a fact that Imam Raghib (r) has pointed out under the heading **كُفْر** (kufr). The summary of them is this : (1) It is the meaning ‘what was sent down, too’ that the majority of the commentators have assigned to the word **وَمَآ أُنزِلَ**. When the natural position of the sentence structure is looked at, too, it is that which deserves preference. According to this, this word will be joined to the sentence immediately before it, **يُعَلِّمُونَ ٱلنَّاسَ ٱلسِّحۡرَ** (they used to teach men sihr). Then the meaning will be that the devils used to teach sihr and, besides it, what was sent down to the two angels too. Or else it may be joined to the sentence **اتَّبَعُوا مَا تَتْلُو الشَّيَاطِينُ** (they followed what the devils recited and handed on). It is this that many are seen to have adopted. Then the meaning will be that, besides what the devils used to recite and hand on, the Jews followed – kept using – what was sent down to the two angels too

(2) Some commentators have also assigned to the word **وَمَآ أُنزِلَ** the meaning ‘and it was not sent down, either’. Although the term **مَا** (ma) has the meaning of negation (**نفى**), ‘not’, when the structural nature of the statement, the statements that are said further about those angels and so on are looked at, this opinion seems to be merely a weak opinion. Fearing length, we do not explain further. According to the first opinion, when it comes to mean ‘what was sent down to the angels, too’, it will, surely, follow that things belonging to the division of sihr were sent down to the angels and that they taught the people sihr. It is the doubts arising from this that have, in reality, been the cause of the second meaning, that it was not sent down to the angels, being assigned. The reply to these doubts can be understood from the explanations that come below. **إن شاء الله**

That what is meant by having said **مَلَكَيْنِ** (malakaini) is indeed two real angels is the majority opinion. Although angels appearing in human form and having dealings with people is extraordinary, there is nothing improbable in it. Angels are ones who are able to appear in a form such as they intend. Whether those two who were sent as a trial were real angels or not, their names were Harut and Marut. And the event took place in Babylon. ( **بِبَابِلَ هَٰرُوتَ وَمَٰرُوتَ** ) It is well known that, in the invasion of King Nebuchadnezzar ( **بختنصر** ), the Israelites were deported to Babylon, and that they spent a long time there as slaves. **وَمَا يُعَلِّمَانِ مِنۡ أَحَدٍ حَتَّىٰ يَقُولَآ إِنَّمَا نَحۡنُ فِتۡنَةٞ فَلَا تَكۡفُرۡ**(they used not to teach anyone without saying: we are only a trial, therefore do not become a disbeliever) Yes, they used to teach the people some things. Yes, **وَمَا هُم بِضَآرِّينَ**
*\[p. 329\]* **بِهِۦ مِنۡ أَحَدٍ إِلَّا بِإِذۡنِ ٱللَّهِ** (they are not ones who cause harm to anyone without Allah's order) Allah has determined that, if such-and-such a thing is performed, such-and-such a result will come about. The results, for their part, may be good and bad. They may be benefit and harm. It is because of that, too, that He forbids that bad and harmful things be done. For example: that, if one takes a sword and cuts at a man's neck, it may lead to death is one of Allah's determinations. It is because of that, too, that it has been forbidden. At the same time, no matter at all happens without Allah's intent and order either. That He determined causes for each thing, and that, when those causes come about, the respective matters happen, is indeed in accordance with His intent and order. There is a matter that must be specially kept in mind here : Except that Allah has said on the whole ‘the thing that produces division between husband and wife’, Allah has not explained how that thing becomes effective. Explaining that is not the intent here either. There are, thus, some evil works; they belong to disbelief; but through them some harmful experiences or other may come about ; but that is not at all because their doers have some invisible or superhuman abilities ; there exist some secrets that Allah has determined and that men are not able to discover; therefore men must not be deceived on seeing such matters. What they are doing is losing the good of the Hereafter knowingly and deliberately, and buying calamity for a price. Yes, **وَلَبِئۡسَ مَا شَرَوۡاْ بِهِۦٓ أَنفُسَهُمۡ** (That for which they have sold away their own selves is very bad indeed!) If they had understood this reality, they *\[p. 330\]* would not have fallen into so terrible a danger. Ah! **لَوۡ كَانُواْ يَعۡلَمُونَ** (If they had known, it would have been good!) **وَلَوۡ أَنَّهُمۡ ءَامَنُواْ وَٱتَّقَوۡاْ**-الخ (If they had believed and observed caution, whatever merit-reward is obtained from Allah is very excellent indeed for them. Yes, if, instead of following so destructive a practice of sihr and losing the good of the Hereafter entirely, they had stood firm in true faith and kept guarding Allah's injunctions and prohibitions, how very excellent would the merit-reward that was to be obtained for them from Allah have been!
