*[Verse 260]*

*[p. 623]* Allah's capability is boundless. For Him there is no difficulty at all in giving life to those who have died. Allah is wont to manifest in this world some events beyond the ordinary procedures that are supposed to be laws of nature. After describing in the previous verse an event that is a visible sign for matters such as these, Allah has described in this verse another event of that same kind as well. Allah has not made clear which person the eyewitness who was the hero of the story in that event was. It is only that what is understood from that ayah and so on is that it was a prophet. The person who is both the hero of the story and the eyewitness in this event is the Prophet Ibrahim (a), who is the head of the family of prophets. Both concerning him, and about the positions and ranks that Allah has given him, there is no need to tell and inform anyone who has even only a little connection with the Quran. The two verses, that Allah has made him a (close) friend. ( **وَاتَّخَذَ اللَّهُ إِبْرَاهِيمَ خَلِيلًا-النسآء: ١٣٥** ), and that Allah had shown him the sovereign dominion of the heavens and the earth. **وَكَذَلِكَ نُرِي إِبْرَاهِيمَ مَلَكُوتَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ-الانعام: ٧٥**, alone are enough, to understand about the distinctions that Allah gave him.

So that the prophets are able to carry out their mission of preaching, with cause and effect, through clear signs and through the testimonies of experience, objectively and plainly, Allah gives them some disclosures and visions that others do not receive and have no likelihood of receiving. That Paradise and Hell were displayed to the noble Prophet ﷺ -as established in the hadiths-. That the Isra' and the Mi'raj (both the night journey to Bait al-Muqaddas and the journey of ascent to the heavens) took place. That he was able to see Jibril (a) in his actual form, that Musa (a) heard the speech of Allah, the event described in the previous verse and so on: all these are examples of this. There is no need to say, surely, that there is a difference between the account given by those who have known the facts by seeing them directly, and the account given by means of signs and proofs- however strong and clear they may indeed be-.

Ibrahim (a) truly knows that Allah is able effortlessly to give life to those who have died. He also has firm belief in it. Even so, he thought that, if he could see a model of it with his own eyes, that would become a peace of mind for him. It would be of use, surely, both for his knowledge and belief to become stronger than before, and for describing to the people about the life after death, in the light of experience, at the time of preaching. Thus, he supplicated to Allah: ‘My Lord, do just show me how it is that You give life to those who have died!’ Allah knows better than he does that he has no doubt or con*[p. 624]*fusion at all in that matter. Even so, in order to record his position through his own words indeed and for the people to grasp the real, Allah asked him: ‘Have you not both known it and believed in it?’ Then why is it that you ask for this? – that is the import. He said in reply: ‘O Allah, not without! *[DOUBT: ഇല്ലാതേ (printed so here; ഇല്ലാതെ in the verse panel) is literally “without”; as at the verse panel (and parts 13, 16), “not” is added in the English to make the sense of an affirming reply. Please confirm the rendering.]* Certainly I have both knowledge and belief about it. Even so, the peace of mind that comes when one knows by seeing directly is indeed something else, surely. It is only for that that this request is.’ Allah, accepting the request, informed thus: ‘All right, then catch any four birds, tame them and bring them. Then take a portion of each of them and put it on all the mountains. Afterwards call those birds. Then you can see them, taking on life, arriving running at your side.’ It is these many things that Allah has described about this event. We can say decisively and completely both that Ibrahim (a), according to the commandment, will have caught four birds and done so, and that they, after having died, will have lived again and come running to his side.

Here may be pointed out a few matters that can be understood, and ought to have been understood, by everyone who reflects about this statement of Allah, not with twisting of any sort or keeping a prejudgement in mind, but with impartial intellect and a truth-seeking state of mind:-

(1) What Ibrahim (a) has done is not asking, in the form of wonder or in the form of doubt, how He gives life to those who have died. What he did was to request of Allah that He would just show him a form of the occurring of that matter in which he has belief with certainty.

(2) Although ‘giving life ( **احياء** )’ is wont to be said in the meaning of uplifting- making prosperous- the earth, or a country, or a community, what is intended here by ‘giving life’ is indeed giving life in the actual meaning- raising up by granting life-. Because, it is about giving life to those who have died ( **الْمَوْتَى** ) that his question is. Not about the earth or the people. The meaning that the word **مَوْتَى** has, in the language and in usage and so on, is indeed ‘those who have died’, and this word can be seen in seventeen places in the Quran. If those places are examined, it can be seen that in all of them it is indeed this meaning that is intended.

(3) His question being for peace of mind, either fulfilling his wish and request, or else refusing it unconditionally or conditionally. The reply must be of one of these two forms. Or, what is needed here is not a reply that convinces him, by means of some parable or by means of a proof, that Allah is able to give life to the dead.
As for the reply that Allah gave, it is one that grants the request *[p. 625]* unconditionally. Its summary is, surely, that, if you catch four birds and do in such-and-such ways, you can know by seeing how it is that He gives life to the dead.

(4) Allah has said only four belonging to the kind of birds. He has not said that they must be such-and-such birds. Although some statements showing that the birds he caught and brought were such-and-such ones can be seen in some books of tafsir, both because there is no trustworthy proof for it, and because there is no particular benefit at all in determining and knowing the names of the birds, we do not see the need to quote any of that here.

(5) It is necessary to understand just a little about some words in the description of what is to be done with the birds after catching them. Only then will the real form of the event become clear:-

**(One):** **صرْهُنَّإِلَيْكَ** This has been read both as ‘sarhunna’ and as ‘sirhunna’. It is the first reading that is more famous. When the books of tafsir, and the books of hadith, and the Arabic dictionaries are examined, the meanings that are seen given to this word, either as its linguistic meaning or as its intended meaning, are these: **املهن : اضمعهن : اجمعهن : قطعهن : اوثقهن** To the word **اَمِل** (‘amil’), which is the first among these, a rendering such as to tame, to win over, to create an inclination, to influence, to charm, to bend, to break, to tilt, to turn over may be given according to the context. More information is also coming below. A translation in Malayalam may be given: to the word **اضعمهن**, as to join together, and to **اجمعهن**, as to gather together, to collect, and to **قطعهن**, as to cut into pieces, to cut, and to **اوثقهن**, as to bind, to tie together *[DOUBT: this word is printed اضمعهن in the list above and اضعمهن here (its first alif standing a little apart); the two printed spellings differ. The gloss “to join together” may point to a word meaning “join them” (such as اضممهن). Both are transcribed as printed.]*. It is pointed out that, because there is also **اليك** (unto you) right after this verb, the meaning **قطعهن** (cut into pieces), which is the fourth meaning, will not fit here. But, it has been pointed out both that a meaning that fits with the particle **اِلَى** (ila) has also been included in it (*), and that then it may mean either ‘cut them into pieces and bring them’ or ‘bring them and cut them into pieces’. Both those who have stated only one of the meanings seen above, and those who have stated two or three meanings together, can be seen. When all this is examined together, the gist of the sentence **فَصُرْهُنَّإِلَيْكَ**, either catch and bring those birds and keep them won over to you, or else catch and bring them and cut them into pieces- one of these two- will be what is intended.

> **Note:** (*) In Arabic literary science ( **علم البلاغة** ) this is technically called **تضمين** (making one verb include the meaning of another verb as well).

**(Two):** No one will suppose that the import of having sa*[p. 626]*id all the mountains in the sentence **ثُمَّ اجْعَلْ عَلَى كُلِّ جَبَلٍ مِنْهُنَّ جُزْءًا** (Afterwards place on every mountain a portion each from them.) is all the mountains on the earth, rather than on all the mountains that were convenient in the surroundings. What there is to consider a little is about **جُزْء** (juz'), to which the meaning ‘portion’ was given. It is wont to be used both in the meaning a portion of some one thing- a part of it-, and in the meaning a portion from a group made up of many- some belonging to it-. In case the first meaning is accepted, the gist will be that the birds must be cut into pieces, by slaughtering or by cutting, and a portion of each of them must be taken and put on every mountain. When the second meaning is taken, the gist will also be that a section each of those four birds- each one, or else some- must be taken and placed on every mountain. For two reasons it has to be said that it is the first meaning and gist that is here. 1stly: although the word **جُزْء** will be used in both meanings, it is in the first meaning that it has more clarity and currency. 2ndly, since the number of the mountains has not been made clear, other than on all the mountains ( **على كُلّ جبل** ), there must be, surely, as many **جُزْء** (portion)s as could be placed, if needed, on quite some more mountains too. There are no proofs at all as to on how many mountains it was that he placed. It is only that some have said both that it was on four mountains and that it was on seven mountains. It is clear that, if the birds are divided without slaughtering and cutting them into pieces and are taken and placed, at most they can be placed on only four mountains. Then it would come about that Allah's having said ‘on all the mountains’ has no meaning in particular at all. In reality, that word, for its part, indicates that they may be taken and placed on every mountain that seems convenient to Ibrahim (a)- whether they be four or seven or more-. That being so, deeming them as four or three or two, by holding up some riwayahs that are not adequate to be accepted as proof, or otherwise, is not justified. Even if the word **صُرْهُنَّ** does not have the meaning cut into pieces, he will, surely, have made them into portions such that he could take and place them on those mountains.

**(Three:)** **ثُمَّادْعُهُنَّيَأْتِينَكَسَعْيًا** (Then call them. They will come running to your side.) That is, whichever birds' portions it is that he has taken and placed on many mountains, if he calls those birds, they can be seen coming running to his side. Yes, with seeing those portions, which were divided into many portions and taken and placed on many mountains, joining together with one another, life coming into them again and their coming running, both his request will be fulfilled, and his wish will be realised – that is the import.

(6) Now, did the Prophet Ibrahim (a) do all that? When he called, did they come running alive? is what remains. There is no need, surely, to specially single out and say things that become clear from the context. Therefore Allah has not stated anything about this. That, even without stating it indeed, is know*[p. 627]*able.
Adopting silence without making clear some matters that become clear, or can be known, from the words that are before or after and from the contexts, is habitual both in the Quran and in the literatures of languages. If a person says, ‘We had settled between ourselves that I would go to his house yesterday: it was then that he told me that matter.’, everyone can know both that a sentence, ‘Thus I went to his house’, has dropped out of it, and that what it intends is that the telling of that matter was on that occasion. Allah has made clear (in 21 : 68, and in 37 : 97) the information that the people decided to throw the Prophet Ibrahim (a) into the fire. He also goes on to say both that He defeated them and that He saved him. It is certain that they put him into the fire. But He has not said that word anywhere in the Quran. Because it is clear indeed without saying it. Just as, in the previous verse, He said about that great man's food and drink **لَمْ يَتَسَنَّهْ** (it has not, becoming aged, changed.), He has not said about the donkey that it died and decayed, or anything else. Because it becomes truly clear from His having afterwards said to look at the bones ( **وَانْظُرْإِلَى الْعِظَامِ** ), and so on. Like this, examples of this can be seen in the Quran in abundant numbers. Not only that, Ibrahim (a)'s request is for showing to himself the giving of life to the dead. Then, in case it is both left at saying that, if you do in such-and-such ways, such-and-such will happen, and not brought into effect and shown, it cannot be said that that request has been accepted. He is also not going to receive by that a special peace of mind besides the belief that he had received earlier by means of proofs. **اللّه اعلم**.

(7) If the word **فَصُرْهُنَّ إِلَيْكَ** has the meaning ‘join those birds together towards your side’, it may be asked what the intention of that commandment will be. To this the Quran commentators have said- as is seen below- an appropriate reply. In case, immediately indeed on catching the birds- before becoming acquainted with them by looking at their shape, size, colour and so on-, they are slaughtered and cut into pieces and taken and put on the mountain, he will not be able, surely, to understand anything as to whether the birds that come running when he calls are the former birds indeed, or whether any change has happened in them. Therefore, what is intended by this commandment is becoming acquainted with them early indeed – that indeed is it.

(8) As was seen in the previous verse- no, as can be seen in most verses-, at the end of this verse too Allah has said a concluding sentence that further strengthens its contents. That sentence, **وَاعْلَمْأَنَّ اللَّهَ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ** (Know that Allah is the Majestic, and the Profoundly Knowing).
As for **عَزِيزٌ**, its literal meaning is ‘one who has majesty, one who has vigour*[p. 628]*, a forceful one, a powerful one, an honourable one’ and so on. As for **حَكِيمٌ**, the meaning can also be stated in this way: ‘one who has profound knowledge, sagacious one, one of wisdom, philosopher’. The indication in having singled out and said here both these distinctive qualities of Allah is this: because He is the Majestic, He is able to bring into effect any matter that He intends. It is not possible for any power either to defeat Him or to cause hindrance. Because He is the Profoundly Knowing, He knows how and when each matter should be, and so on. His actions will be in accordance with that. He will not do anything irrationally or unjustly.

### Misinterpretations that some modern commentators have given to this event, and the reply to them

From what was elucidated above it has become clear to us both that what the Prophet Ibrahim (a) requested of Allah was to show how it is that He gives life to those who have died- it was not to show how it is that He uplifts and reforms human beings who are lying lifeless with their liveliness gone-, and that it was by giving life to birds that had met death and showing it that Allah accepted that request, and that what prompted him to that request was not any doubt or confusion that he had about the life after death or anything else. But some people who are of crooked trends of thought both deny the said realities, which can be understood impartially from the Quran and which the Muslims, both the predecessors and the successors,- without distinction of scholar and layman- have generally been acknowledging, and attain satisfaction by giving many misinterpretations to this verse. Most of the extraordinary events described in the Quran become subject to the misinterpretation of these. This too is only one among that lot. According to their interpretation the form of the event is this: ‘The Prophet Ibrahim (a) invited his own people to the truth for a long time, using many diplomacies. There was no result. He became subject even to the ordeal of fire. In the end, having become despairing, he left the country. It was then that he asked, how is it that You will give life to these human beings lying lifeless? How will that become possible? Then He counselled both to catch four birds and domesticate them, and, after they had properly become domesticated, to take them and place them on hills one each and to call them. Birds are ones that, without domestication towards human beings, fly away on sight. If they are caught and domesticated for quite some days, they will come to where they are called. What Allah teaches by means of this is the lesson that, if man can do this, he can surely give life to human beings lying lifeless. The lack of peace of mind that Ibrahim (a) had was about his people lying degenerate. This is a parable showing that there is no need to despair about them. This is the summary of the form they give to this event.

*[p. 629]* Quite some years ago these misinterpretations of these made a little commotion among the Kerala Muslims. It was from some who had made it a habit, even without denying superhuman signs ( **المُعْجِزَات** ) entirely, to interpret them making them into ordinary events, that they were imported. This is, surely, a time when, on seeing or hearing something new, whatever blunder it may be, more people than before are got to follow after it. There will also be those who portray all that as the opinions of ‘intellectual’s. Although it is difficult to turn back to the truth those who are like this, a few of their statements concerning this event and the replies to them are added below, both for brothers who are guileless and seekers of truth to understand the real, and for them not to be deceived by the said misinterpretations:

**(1stly:-)** Giving the sentence **فَصُرْهُنَّ إِلَيْكَ** (fasurhunna ilaika’) the meaning ‘domesticate them with yourself (well)’, they say: what the main Quran commentators such as Baidawi, Zamakhshari, Abus-Su'ud have all given, and what has been given in Imam Raghib's Mufradat and in ordinary dictionaries, is indeed this meaning. Some do give it the meaning ‘slaughter’. For that there is no backing in Arabic literature. Ibn Hajar has quoted in ‘Fath al-Bari’ what Qadi 'Iyad said, that that meaning is both unfamiliar and unusual. Before speaking about this statement, in which half-truths and untruths are mixed together, it will be good to know some facts. Not only here, sometimes in other contexts too there will also be benefit from that for seekers of truth. **إن شاء الله**

(1) It was said before, surely, that, in the midst of describing the meaning of the word ‘sur’ ( **صُرْ** ), the word ‘amil’ ( **اَمِل** ) too is wont to be used in Arabic books. It is basing on this word that these have made its meaning ‘domesticate’. In fact, the meaning of that word is not domesticating- breaking in- animals that have no domestication with human beings. Further, even if it is established that a meaning ‘domesticate’ too is included among the synonyms used in the Arabic books, it is clear from what is described below that the form these have given to the event is not right.

(2) When describing the meaning of a word or a sentence, it is ordinary to say, adding along with it, more than one word or sentence in roughly that same meaning, in order to make the intention clearer. In Arabic literatures this habit is also more frequent. Its technical name is **عطفتفسير**(adding on for making clear). Then the speaker's actual intention must be understood taking into account both the words used first and the words said joined to it afterwards.
Taking some or other of them *[p. 630]* only and holding that that is the actual literal meaning is not right.

(3) According to this, when stating the meaning of the word **صُرْ** (‘sur’ or else ‘sir’), when some other terms besides the term **اَمِل** are said joined to it, what its speaker has intended will be the meaning obtained altogether from those terms. Now, let us pay attention to the statement of these quoted above. First let us examine the wordings of those great men whom these mentioned by name:-

This is the wording in the tafsir of Baidawi (r): **إليك (فصرهن إليك)فأملهن واضممهن** *[DOUBT: the Arabic is printed without a Malayalam rendering and is transcribed in the order in which it is read on the page (the first إليك stands at the right end of the line, before the bracket); it may belong at the end, after واضممهن. It most likely means: “(So turn them to you) that is, incline them and join them (to you).”]* It is these same words indeed, in these same letters, that Mabus-Su'ud *[TN: printed മബുസ്സുഊദ്; the same name is printed അബുസ്സുഊദ് (Abus-Su'ud) on p. 629 and again on this page.]* (r) has said in his tafsir, and Zamakhshari (r) in his tafsir (Kashshaf). Even if the word **أملهن** has the meaning ‘domesticate’, as this party say, since they have also added **واضممهن**, there is no justification for that meaning. What these say, surely, is that the import of the ayah is that, if, after catching the birds and domesticating them- for this quite some days indeed will be needed-, they are taken and placed on the mountain, they will come to where they are called. Those great men who stated its meaning as **اَمِل** too have not intended this meaning at all. That is plain from their wordings indeed. Look at the wordings that those same great men said- following the words quoted above- as the reason for having commanded, besides commanding to catch the birds, also to bring them along to his side: Baidawi (r) says: **لتتأملها وتعرف شياتها لئلا تلتبس عليك بعد الإحياء** (It is in order to study well and become acquainted both about them and about their mixtures of colour, so that it does not happen that you fail to recognise them after giving them life.) Abus-Su'ud (r) too has said just so.

Zamakhshari (r), for his part, going one step further than these two persons, has said thus:

**فإن قلت : ما معنى أمره بضمها إلى نفسه بعد أن يأخذها ؟ قلت : ليتأملها ويعرف أشكالها وهيآتها وحلاها ؛ لئلا تلتبس عليه بعد الإحياء ولا يتوهم أنها غير تلك**

(If you ask what the gist is of having commanded, after catching those birds, to join them together to him and bring them, I say: both so that it does not happen that he fails to recognise them after giving them life, and so that he does not mistake that they are other birds. It is in order that he should both ponder *[DOUBT: മനക്കുകയും is printed; it is not a common word and is taken from മനനം “reflection”, rendering the Arabic يتأملها. It may be a slip for another word.]* well about them, and understand their forms and shapes and patterns.) This matter is one that many other commentators too have pointed out.

Then, it is clear both that it is not catching and bringing the birds and keeping on domesticating them and making them familiar for quite some time that any of these have intended by that word, and that it is that, just when catching and bringing them, they must be examined and their shape and *[p. 631]* colour and all understood, and that it is that, if afterwards they are slaughtered or cut and taken and placed on the mountains, they will come back alive again when called. From Zamakhshari (r)'s wordings another matter too can be understood. Although, at first, when stating the meaning of the word **صر**, he said **امل** as well, afterwards what he said was ‘the gist of having commanded to join them together ( **معنى أمره بضمها إلى نفسه** )’. He did not say, according to the claim of these, ‘the gist of having commanded to domesticate ( **بامالتها** )’. From this it is clear that ‘domesticating and joining together’ are one and the same with him. Another thing too: it can be seen that these three persons have stated both that there is a reading here both as **صُرْ** (sur) and as **صِرْ** (sir), and that either way the meaning is indeed this. (Look at their tafsirs.)

Now, let us look at what Imam Raghib (r) has said in the ‘Mufradat’ **فصر هن أي:أملهن من الصور، أي: الميل، وقيل: قطعهن وقرئ:صرهن وقيل: ذلك لغتان، وقال بعضهم:صرهن، أي: صح بهن،** (‘sarhunna means ‘amilhunna’. This is from the root **ميل** (mail). The meaning **قطع هن** (make them into pieces) too has been stated. There is also a reading here as ‘surhunna’. It has also been said that thus both can be said in the language. Some say: that sarhunna means call them loudly.) To the word **ميل** meanings such as ‘slant, bend, hanging, to swerve, inclination’ will come. What Raghib (r) pointed out is indeed that the meaning the word **صر** has is the meaning in the imperative form of the transitive verb ( **متعدى** ) from it. Although it is to that meaning that he has given preference, that he has also quoted two other meanings is noteworthy. To put it briefly, neither he, nor Baidawi, Abus-Su'ud, Zamakhshari (r)- none indeed- has stopped at stating its meaning as only **امل**. Others, for their part, have also used other words, such as **اجمعهن، اوثقهن** (gather together, bind), and so on, moreover.

Now, let us examine Qadi 'Iyad (r)'s wording that Ibn Hajar (r) quoted in Fath al-Bari. While speaking about Imam Bukhari (r) having given the word **فصرهن** the meaning **قطعهن** (cut them into pieces), Ibn Hajar (r) states that this meaning has been quoted both from Ibn 'Abbas (r) and from many Tabi'is, through many routes. He has also pointed out that, from Ibn 'Abbas (r) indeed, through another route, there has also come for it **اوثقهن ثم اذبحهن** (bind them, then slaughter). Thereafter, describing the differences of reading in that word, Ibn Hajar (r) says: **قال عياض تفسير صرهن بقطهعن غريب والمعروف ان معناها املهن** *[TN: the Arabic prints بقطهعن; the Malayalam rendering that follows has قطعهن.]* ('Iyad says: giving the interpretation **قطعهن** (cut them into pieces) to **صرهن** is rare. The very familiar meaning is **املهن**) Qadi 'Iyad (r)- as this party said- has said, about the meaning slaughter **اذبحهن**, not a single letter*[p. 632]*. About the meaning cut into pieces ( **قطعهن** ), he has said only that it is rare ( **غريب** ). ( **راجع فتح الباري —الجزء الثامن —باب واذ قَال ابراهيم لربه الخ** ) *[DOUBT: this Arabic reference is printed without a Malayalam rendering and is transcribed as printed. It most likely means: “See Fath al-Bari, the eighth part, chapter: And when Ibrahim said to his Lord, etc.”]* His word is also no obstacle to the meaning cut into pieces coming for it, even if rarely. Even if it is an obstacle, the other great men too are not seen to have stated that opinion.

Imam Ibn Jarir (r), who is foremost in every respect among the Quran commentators who are predecessors, can be seen in his famous tafsir, after quoting both the differences of reading in the word **صرهن**, and the terms **امل — قطع — اضمم** that have come in its meaning, and the opinions, to have stated thus: The opinion that we stated, quoting from the scholars of Basrah, both that **صُرْ** (sur) and **صِرْ** (sir) are two usages of the language in the same meaning, and that the meaning of both those usages here is **قطعهن** (make them into pieces), and that the particle **اليك** (towards you) is connected to the verb **خذ** (catch), is the one most entitled to be the truth. Because, whether the word **فصرهن** is read with the ‘u’ vowel or with the ‘i’ vowel ( **بالضم والفتح** ) *[TN: the Arabic prints والفتح (“and the fatha”, the a-vowel); the Malayalam before it has ‘ഇ’ കാരം (the i-vowel).]*, there is the unanimous opinion among the Quran commentators that its meaning will be either **قطعهن** (make them into pieces) or **اضممهن** (join them together towards you).’ (*)

> **Note:** (*) Fearing length, his Arabic original is not quoted here. Let those who have doubt look in his tafsir.

Some matters can be understood from this statement of Ibn Jarir (r):

1. It is to **قطعهن**, meaning ‘make into pieces’, and to **اضممهن**, meaning ‘join together’, rather than to **املهن**, to which these assigned the meaning ‘domesticate’, that he has assigned standing.

3. According to his statement, the gist of the sentence **فخذ اربعة من الطير فصرهن اليك** will be only either that you must catch and bring four birds and cut them into pieces, or else catch four birds and bring them along to your side. **(2ndly:-)** They say: ‘The Quran is Arabic. It cannot be interpreted contrary to the norm of that language. When Allah says ‘domesticate’, we have freedom only to think about the secret contained in it. What they (the Quran *[p. 633]*commentators) say is that, when that meaning is assigned, the question and the answer do not agree with each other. That is a misunderstanding that came from not heeding Allah's procedural method. Pointing out some examples for the removal of doubt is Allah's method. When, owing to the stubbornness of the Israelites, the Prophet Musa (a) asked to see Allah, it was, surely, to look towards the mountain that He commanded. In the previous verse, when he asked the doubt how that town will live, what He did was to make that man die and give him life .... ’

This statement too, indeed, is a mixture of what is and what is not together.

The Quran is Arabic, it may be interpreted only according to the norm of that language. That is right, there is no dispute. But will these going on claiming both that the word **صر** has only the meaning ‘domesticate’, and that it was indeed to domesticate that Allah commanded, be according to the norm of the Arabic language? Will none of what others say, that the meanings it has are other ones, be included in the norm of the language? Not only if Allah commanded to domesticate- whatever He commanded, whether to cut into pieces, or to join together, or to gather together, or to bind, or to call loudly, or to slaughter- there is need to think about its secret. Some secrets that many great men - the vast majority of people, more worthy and more thoughtful than these - understood by thinking, we have also pointed out before this. We also saw the wordings of the great men whom this party earlier mentioned by name as favourable to themselves. What these said, both that the Quran commentators have misunderstood that domesticating and the question and answer do not agree with each other, and that the cause of that is their not heeding Allah's procedural method, has turned out a word that oversteps quite some way! Let that be. Not only these, who are ‘subtle thinkers, but the Quran commentators too have understood that pointing out examples for the removal of doubt is Allah's method. But they know both that what is here is not a reply to a doubt of the Prophet Ibrahim (a), and that that is not the only way there is for the dispelling of doubt. Are both those examples that these pointed out, to strengthen their own claim that this event of the birds is an ‘example’ that Allah pointed out in order to bring removal to Ibrahim (a)'s ‘doubt, truly examples of that indeed? Even if they are, is this event too one of them? Was it on account of doubt that Ibrahim (a) requested that? It is these things that need to be considered here.

The Prophet Ibrahim (a) has no doubt or lack of belief in Allah's being able to give life to those who have died. It is indeed because it is a certain matter that he also wished that it would be good if he could just see it. That being so, there is no need here to describe a parable for the removal of doubt. Either accepting the request, or else both rejecting it and understanding the reason for it. If not that either, reproaching that requesting so is impropriety. This is what is needed.  Allah, for His part, what He did too was indeed to ac*[p. 634]*cept the request. Then, what there is now to examine is about the examples that these pointed out for its being that, when removal of doubt was requested, it was left at describing a parable. Of those the first is, surely, the Prophet Musa (a) requesting to see Allah. What there is to say about that too is just like this. What was said, that it was owing to the stubbornness of the Israelites that Musa (a) requested that, is not right. That it is a request that arose from a doubt- that too owing to the stubbornness of the Israelites- is not likely to occur to anyone except these. In any case, that request was not accepted. What Allah did was both to make known that you will not be able to see Me while in this world, and to convince him of it through some experiences. (Look at the further details in its proper place.) A wonder indeed! ( **أَنَّى يُحْيِي هَذِهِ اللَّهُ بَعْدَ مَوْتِهَا** )’. It is indeed because he knew both that Allah will restore that country, and that He will once again give life to those who have died in it, that he felt amazement at Allah's ability too. Now, suppose that it was indeed owing to doubt that he so *[p. 635]*happened to speak, even then, it is not removing that doubt through a parable that Allah did. ( **وَلِنَجْعَلَكَ آيَةًلِلنَّاسِ** )

It does not seem that believers having intellect and conscience will set any value on this statement that uses some words such as doubt, disquiet, peace of mind in place and out of place.

No one has either said or hinted that the Prophet Ibrahim (a) had a doubt about giving life after death, or that, because of not seeing it in experience, he had disquiet- except these. Both venturing to refute something that does not exist after fabricating it, and opening the way for their own opinions through that, are a habit of this party. (There are other examples too for this.) Everyone knows that that eminent prophet is a person who has all the worthinesses that these specifically mentioned, and a heart firmer than a rock. It is indeed because of that great personage's so exalted standing that he also felt the wish that it would be good if he had the fortune too of seeing those who have died being given life. Both without stating any hindrance or condition, and without leaving it at describing some parable, Allah also fulfilled that wish for him. Proving, by describing examples or signs*[p. 636]*, the possibility of resurrection is not a matter that was attained by Ibrahim (a) alone, or that can be attained. In the Quran itself there are plenty of proofs for this. And, moreover, among that group there are so many examples and signs that are clearer and more apt than that, catching the birds of the forest and domesticating and making familiar for quite some time, if their master calls, they will come to where called. Then, we can ask them too whether it is in order to make known that Allah pointed out to the Prophet Ibrahim (a) too an example like the one pointed out to everyone, that Allah told this story here.

Everyone knows that, even if there has been however unwavering and very firm a belief about a matter through proofs and signs, both the arising of a wish to see it in experience, and the experiencing of more peace of mind and joy on seeing with the eyes, is a quality natural to human beings. That does not mean that, before seeing and experiencing, there was doubt or disquiet in it. Knowledge through proof and sign is indeed certain knowledge ( **عِلْمُ اليَقِين** ). But, it is only that, when it is seen in experience, it rises to become certain experience ( **عين اليَقِين** ). Knowing the gist of a statement of Sayyid Rashid Rida (r) in the explanation of this verse is befitting the occasion here. He is saying: ‘Some human beings have understood from this question of Ibrahim (a) that he was disquieted in belief about resurrection. Disquietude is, surely, doubt. What an awful foolishness! How far-off an intellect! In the hadith of Bukhari- Muslim it has come thus: **نَحْنُ ولىبِالشَّكِّ مِنْ إِبْرَاهِيمَ** (We are more deserving of doubt than the Prophet Ibrahim.) *[DOUBT: The second Arabic word is printed ولى; the author's rendering “more deserving” fits أولى. The hide-and-seek contained in this statement of these is noteworthy: these said, surely, that even when he was tested by being cast into the fire, Ibrahim (a) stood fi*[p. 637]*rm like a rock without wavering.
 In reality, what these have done is to deny the very event of casting him into the fire. In fact, this is justi*[p. 638]*fying a claim with the claim itself, and making the very utterance of the claimant a proof for the claim. But, in the situation where Allah has not clearly stated for what need this event has been quoted here, let us leave it, supposing that each one may accept the opinion that seems apt and justified to him. We too know that, about those who are sunk in the darknesses of ignorance, lifeless ones, and those who have slept, and about those who have knowledge and culture, those who are living and those who have awakened and so on, are wont to be said in ordinary literatures- in the Quran itself too-. Before that, there is no way out to leave without conducting an inquiry about these having exploited the names of Razi, Qurtubi, 'Asqalani, and Alusi (r), the author of Ruh al-Ma'ani.

**(5thly:)** They continue:- ‘Another reason for doubt is the saying to put each portion (juz') on each mountain. ‘Juz'’ is wont to be used both for a portion of one thing and for a section of a community. We too agree both that the word ‘juz' ( **جُزْء** )’ may have meanings such as ‘portion, part, some, limb’, and that it may be used both for the portion of a thing, and for a section in a community. But what it is mostly wont to be used for is in the meaning the portion of a thing. That is its actual meaning too: It is right that, as in 15: 44, considering a community altogether as one thing, it is wont to be used for a section in it too. Moreover, apart from its not agreeing with this new form that these give to this event, there is no obstacle here to giving it its actual meaning itself. That being so, this justification has no great relevance. Then, on statements such as, the mountains are four in number, are seven, the birds *[p. 641]*are fowl and so on, we too do not set any great value. Only that it may be. But, the portrayal that makes it seem that all this has the general acknowledgement of the Quran commentators, or of the Muslims, was not befitting decency. The secret contained in the mathematical principle that it is enough even if the mountains are two in number for the form stated in the Quran to come right is this: it has not been commanded, surely, to put each one on each mountain. If it is two and two, or one and three, dividing, one section may be put on one mountain and the other on the other mountain. But, the word that Allah said is: **اجْعَلْ عَلَى كُلِّ جَبَلٍمِنْهُنَّجُزْءًا** (Place on all each mountain each ‘juzu'’). There is no justification for it either. What Mujahid and Dahhak (r) say is the mountains that are possible. The general opinion is those in the surroundings, or else those that were convenient. In any case it is certain indeed that the number is not fixed. Then, however many the number of mountains that were both in his surroundings and convenient for him, there must be ‘juz'’s enough to put on all of them. This is not possible, surely, without slaughtering and cutting into pieces the birds. As long as there is no proof that determines how many in number is intended by ‘all the mountains’, there is no way out to confine the mountains to two or four.

To put it briefly, the crux of our opposition does not lie in the matter of ‘domestication’. It is in the ‘estrangement’ shown towards the truth and towards the straight direct interpretation that we mainly have opposition.
