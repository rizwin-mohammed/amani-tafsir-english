*\[Verses 51–53\]*

After Fir'aun and his army had been drowned and destroyed and the Israelites had been rescued, Allah had appointed with him that He would give a scripture containing the code of law for them, *\[p. 258\]* and that for it Musa (a) must spend on Mount Sinai a total of forty days – first thirty and afterwards ten more – with observances such as meditation and fasting. (The detailed account is coming in Al-A'raf) According to the appointment, when Musa (a) was going to Sinai, he entrusted the leadership of the Israelites in his absence to his brother, the Prophet Harun (a). After the Prophet Musa (a) left the place, the Israelites, under the leadership of a man named Samiri, made a calf with gold and began to worship it. Through contact with the Egyptians for many years, they had become familiar with the practice of worshipping cows. They said that it was that God whom Musa (a) had gone to seek. Although Harun (a) tried telling them the objections he could, they did not heed it. When Musa (a) came bringing the Torah after the fixed days had passed, that was the sight he saw. Need his grief and anger then be told? Even though they had done so grave an offence as this, Allah afterwards did grant them pardon. It was intending that they should become thankful – and so, that they should attain right guidance – that Allah pardoned them for it.

What is meant by the scripture is the Torah. The Torah is a code of law containing all the teachings of principle, legal directions and lessons of history needed for the guidance of the Israelites, and a religious book that explains truth and untruth and right and wrong, separating them. In that respect, what is meant by the scripture and the discrimination (**ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ وَٱلۡفُرۡقَانَ**) is the Torah itself. Some have expressed the opinion that what is meant by the discrimination is the superhuman signs (**المُعْجِزَات**) that came about through the Prophet Musa (a). Either way, the aim is indeed their attaining right guidance.

*\[Verse 54\]*

*\[p. 259\]* The Israelites felt remorse about having made the calf and worshipped it, and they lamented: if our Rabb does not forgive us and show mercy to us, we will become losers, surely. (A'raf, 149) It may be on this occasion that Musa (a) said this to them. (Allah knows) Making into a god the image of a calf that you yourselves made, leaving Allah who created you, is a colossal wrong. It is an offence that you have done to your own selves. Therefore turn back in repentance to your Creator. As the completion of your repentance and the testimony of your sincerity, you must kill your own selves, that is, some of you some others –. Although this killing is a very grave matter, when one looks at the gravity of your offence and its consequence, it is that which will turn out beneficial for you with Allah. Such is the gist of what Musa (a) said. When they acted so, Allah accepted their repentance and also granted them pardon.

Different riwayahs can be seen about what the nature of this killing, appointed as the completion of the Israelites' repentance, was. Nevertheless, that those who had no part in the worship of the calf were to kill their close relatives who had worshipped the calf was *\[p. 260\]* the appointment: such indeed is what becomes clear from the riwayah that Ibn Jarir, Nasa'i and Ibn Abi Hatim (r) quoted from Ibn Abbas (r). It is seen stated that in this way a good many people were killed, and that afterwards, following the prayer of the Prophet Musa (a), it was stopped. **الله أعلم** The statement of the Bible too is more or less the same as this. (See Exodus: 32, at 27, 28)

But some people are seen to have stated that the purport of the sentence **اقْتُلُوا أَنْفُسَكُمْ** (kill your own selves – yourselves –) is: restrain your bodily desires and punish them. Their argument is that there is no justification at all either in appointing killing as part of repentance or in commanding the destruction of the lives of many people. Some also make a proof for this argument of what has been said in Surah Nisa' 153: .....then, after proofs came to them, they made the calf. Then We granted pardon for that. Although at first sight it may seem that there is some substance in this argument, when one looks at the words Allah has said here, the riwayahs indicated above and the general statements of the Quran commentators, no way is seen to attach value to this new interpretation. Besides that, the evaluation of this killing must also be made keeping in front some more facts that are indicated below.

The event of the Israelites worshipping the calf is simply not the event of an ordinary idol-worship. It has some special features of its own. Who the Israelites are. What their circumstances are, on what occasion they did it, what the surroundings at that time were – such things need to be considered. The Israelite people are the heirs who are obliged to maintain the tradition of the Prophet Ibrahim (a), who dedicated his life for the establishment of tawhid, and of the prophets Ishaq and Ya'qub (a), who were his children and successors. The Prophet Musa (a) is the prophet appointed both to revive in them that tradition of tawhid, which had decayed with the passage of time, and to set them free from the slavery and the oppressions that they had been experiencing for centuries. They are people who have been seeing and experiencing directly many mighty divine signs and great wonders that appeared at his hand. It is through a great wonder, such as the human world had not heard of before or after, that they escaped by crossing the Red Sea and are now staying, having come and encamped in the Sinai desert. They have not at all come to forget the sight of Fir'aun, the most mighty known in the world at that time, and his army being destroyed in a manner both unusual and extremely tragic. It was at this juncture that Musa (a) went away from them – and that too entrusting their leadership to the Prophet Harun (a) – in order to receive a scripture that is the basis of their everlasting good and renown from *\[p. 261\]* Allah, their Creator. Only days that can be counted on the fingers had passed; by then, saying that it was the God whom Musa had gone to seek, making into a god that calf which a perverse one among them had cast with gold, sitting in chanting around it and performing acts of worship – this is what they did. Let alone rational second thoughts, they did not have the patience either to listen to the restraint of the Prophet Harun (a), who was holding their leadership, or to wait until the coming of the Prophet Musa (a), who would be returning within a few days. All this on one side. On another side:–

The defiant mentality, the foolishness and the disobedience of the Israelites are well known. Their asking Musa (a), when in the midst of escaping across the Red Sea they saw some people worshipping idols, to permit them too to make gods like these. His replying that you are indeed a foolish people. (7:138) They who hesitated to confront the enemies in the land that had been promised to them and had been the goal of their journey – in Bait al-Muqaddas. Even after knowing through observers that the strength of the enemies was weak, what they said to the Prophet Musa (a) was this: 'As long as they are there, we will never enter it. You and 'your Rabb go and wage war. We are sitting here' (5:24) Examples like this of their defiance and disobedience can be seen in plenty, in this surah and in other surahs.

It is taking all this into account that the gravity and the special character of their worshipping the calf must be reckoned. And as for the punishment that they were to receive from Allah on account of it, it was the punishment of Hell for all time, too. If, for many purposes like these – in order that that punishment be remitted for them, in order to prove their real repentance for this base act that they did defiantly, in order that such vile events not be repeated hereafter among the people who remain, and at the same time in order that, for those who are killed, their great sin be forgiven because they give up their lives for Allah's pardon and pleasure – the Creator who created them commanded that those who took the lead in that heinous crime be killed, it is His compassion and generosity that thinking true believers will be able to see in it. It may be indeed hinting at all this that, along with saying that you must kill your own selves, the Prophet Musa (a) also said **ذَٰلِكُمۡ خَيۡرٞ لَّكُمۡ عِندَ بَارِئِكُمۡ** (that will be beneficial for you with your Creator). **والله أعلم**. In any case, no way is seen to state a clear decision about the nature of the said killing, either from the statement of Allah or from other strong proofs.

*\[Verses 55–56\]*

*\[p. 262\]* What many Quran commentators state is that it was they who said, we will not believe you without seeing Allah openly, at the time when, after the Israelites had made the calf and worshipped it, Musa (a) had gone to Mount Sinai choosing seventy good people from among the Israelites in order to express remorse about it and to make excuse. After describing (in verses 142–154) the fixing of forty days with Musa (a), his going (to Sinai), Allah's speaking to him, his returning with the Torah, his throwing the tablets of the Torah to the ground in anger when he returned and learned that the people had worshipped the calf, and his being harsh with Harun (a) – all of it – Allah says in Surah Al-A'raf:

> **وَٱخۡتَارَ مُوسَىٰ قَوۡمَهُۥ سَبۡعِينَ رَجُلٗا لِّمِيقَٰتِنَاۖ فَلَمَّآ أَخَذَتۡهُمُ ٱلرَّجۡفَةُ قَالَ رَبِّ لَوۡ شِئۡتَ أَهۡلَكۡتَهُم مِّن قَبۡلُ وَإِيَّـٰيَۖ أَتُهۡلِكُنَا بِمَا فَعَلَ ٱلسُّفَهَآءُ مِنَّآۖ إِنۡ هِيَ إِلَّا فِتۡنَتُكَ** (الاعراف )

Gist: “Musa chose seventy men from his people for Our appointed time. When a quaking – a trembling – seized them, he said. Rabb, had You willed, You could have destroyed them and me myself *\[p. 263\]* before. Are You destroying us because of what the fools among us did? That is nothing other than Your trial..” (A'raf: 155)

It was not the whole Israelite people that demanded: we will not believe you without seeing Allah openly – if what you say, that Allah spoke to you and so on, is to become convincing to us, we must see Him directly. It was only the seventy people mentioned above (or else, as some say, another group). Since they were the good ones among that people and representatives chosen from among them, it has been stated as a whole that they said it. In any case, this demand of theirs – especially on that occasion – is sheer childishness, folly and defiance indeed. Therefore a thunderbolt struck them. They died in it. As seen in the verse of Surat al-A'raf, Musa (a) expressed his grief to Allah. Allah brought them back to life. On account of this, that community ought, surely, always to be thankful to Allah.

Some say that they did not die because of the thunderbolt striking them, that the meaning of the sentence **ثُمَّ بَعَثۡنَٰكُم مِّنۢ بَعۡدِ مَوۡتِكُمۡ** (then, after your death, We raised you up) is that after a loss of consciousness equal to death or something else occurred, He gave relief from it, and that the words **وَأَنتُمۡ تَنظُرُونَ** (while you were looking on and seeing) are a proof of this. From the saying 'a thunderbolt seized' (**أَخَذَتهم الصَّاعِقَةُ**) it cannot be asserted with certainty that they died. Nevertheless, since Allah has made it clear by **مِّنۢ بَعۡدِ مَوۡتِكُمۡ** (after your death), no one has any right at all either to doubt whether it was death that struck them or whether it was loss of consciousness that struck them, or to say that it was not death – that it was loss of consciousness or something else – that struck them. What is to be considered next is what the purport of the words 'while you were looking on and seeing' is. If they died through the thunderbolt, how would they look on and see it? – that is what these people ask. The word that Allah said is not that death seized them while they were looking on and seeing – it is that a thunderbolt seized them. Seeing the thunderbolt coming with the eyes and dying can both happen together, surely. Or else, if, without all dying together, some died first, the others may also have been watching them dying. In any case, rather than giving to what Allah said in the clear words 'after death' some other figurative meaning than real death, it is right to give the words 'while you were looking on' an interpretation suited to the context; there is no doubt.

Another argument to be stated is this. 'Ought not those who were brought to life after death then, following on that, to experience the reward and punishment that are the result of their former deeds? For them, religious ordinances and laws would then not be binding at all, would they? The reply to this argument: although, speaking at a single glance, it may seem that this is *\[p. 264\]* right, some special events need to be excluded from what has been said. The event of Allah afterwards bringing to life a person ('Uzair (a)) after he had lain dead for a hundred years is coming below in the 259th verse. It can be seen in 3:49 and in 5:110 that one among the signs of the Prophet 'Isa (a) that were described is that he would bring the dead to life. It is not from having died – it is from the second blowing of the trumpet for raising the dead on the Day of Qiyamah – that the process of recompense begins, and what the Quran has said about the period up to then is **بَرْزَخْ** (barzakh) (see 23:100) Along with that, Allah surely has the power also to make people who are given physical life again after death in this way, for some special reasons, experience none of the experiences of the spiritual world or of the next world. To put it briefly, in none of such matters is it right to be satisfied by giving interpretations that put rational arguments or naturalist arguments in front. In all matters in which no impossibility is seen rationally, safety lies in accepting, just as they are, the things that Allah has stated in clear language **والله الموفق للصواب**

The summary of what the late Sayyid Rashid Rida quoted here as stated by the late Shaikh Muhammad 'Abduh may be described thus: 'The event of the Israelites asking to see Allah is not connected with the worship of the calf. That is a special event. It has been clearly stated in their book. They demanded of the Prophet Musa (a): if we are to believe that you have superiority over us, we must indeed see Allah. Then many calamities seized them. One of them was the thunderbolt. The thunderbolt struck one group. The others were looking on at it. Through disease and illness too, many died. Shaikh 'Abduh's interpretation of the sentence **ثُمَّ بَعَثۡنَٰكُم مِّنۢ بَعۡدِ مَوۡتِكُمۡ** (then, after your death, We raised you up): he has quoted thus. 'After they had died through the thunderbolt and other things, and it had been thought that they might perish completely, Allah increased their offspring and restored them' (**تفسير المنار**)

In this, no hint is seen in the Quran about what was said – that many other calamities too, besides the thunderbolt, struck them, and that many of them died because of that. Nor does what was said – that He increased their offspring and restored them – as the interpretation of Allah's having said that after your death We raised you up, agree with the words of the Quran. In the Bible too it is not seen made clear that the Israelites asked to see Allah. What is stated in it, too, is that when Musa (a) went to receive the Torah he had taken seventy elders with him, that they saw God, that under His feet it was like a floor paved with blue stone, and like the *\[p. 265\]* clearness of the sky, that nothing befell those nobles by the holy hand, and that they saw God and ate food and drink, and so on. (See Exodus chapter 24, 1–11) Not only is this account of the Bible contrary to the statements of the Quran, it is also one that likens Allah to creatures and that does not agree with reason. Besides that, it is also diametrically contrary to another clear statement of the Bible itself. It can be seen that in verses 20, 21 of chapter 33 of the same book, Jehovah (God) has said thus, commanding Moses (Musa): 'You cannot see My face. No man will see Me and remain alive.' Then it is clear, surely, that this statement of the Bible is not trustworthy from top to bottom.

*\[Verse 57\]*

After escaping by crossing the Red Sea, because the Israelites would not agree to go on into the land promised to them – into Bait al-Muqaddas – and expel the enemies who had been holding that land, they had to wander about for forty years *\[p. 266\]* in the Sinai valley. (See Ma'idah: 23–29) It is two mighty favours that Allah gave them in that period that are recalled in this verse.

(1) Making the cloud a shade for them. Since it was a region without vegetation and water sources, the excess of heat in the desert need not be told, surely. And the Israelites, for their part, had no house there. Only that tents and camps had gradually come into being. At this juncture Allah kept giving them there, daily, shades of cloud. Some commentators, basing themselves on the word **ٱلۡغَمَامَ**, which was given the meaning 'cloud', have expressed the opinion that it was white and gave coolness.

(2) Giving manna and salwa for food. People by the hundred thousand. There is no facility in any way to make a living by doing agriculture or anything else. They were, surely, people who had fled secretly one night as refugees. Manna and salwa indeed are the two resources that Allah gave them in this period as a special favour. It is said that manna was a gum that came out from some plants or trees, and that it was a substance like wax that rained down like dew or like drizzle and stayed on the plants, and so on. In any case, manna was a food substance that was obtained without labour and that was very sweet and delightful. It has been explained as a food substance and as a drink. As Ibn Kathir (r) pointed out, it may have been usable both as a food substance that could be eaten by itself and as a drink to be drunk mixed in water. (Allah knows) Salwa is a kind of quail, or else a small bird like the quail. Allah had given them this too in such a manner that it was obtained as much as they wished.

As the result of the prayer of the Prophet Musa (a), they kept receiving quail in the evening and manna in the morning. Manna rained down like dew. It was white, like coriander seed (coriander), and sweet. They had been commanded that they should take only what was needed for each day. It can be seen stated in the Bible that what some took and kept contrary to that became wormy and stank, and so on. (Exodus chapter 16) It is also stated in it that the people gathered manna and made bread by pounding it, powdering it and boiling it (Numbers 11, at 7) (Allah knows)

That they kept receiving manna and salwa in abundance, and that these were purely delightful food substances, can be understood from the very sentence **كُلُواْ مِن طَيِّبَٰتِ مَا رَزَقۡنَٰكُمۡ** (eat of the good things that We gave you). But that the Israelites, instead of being thankful for this favour, showed ingratitude: this indeed is what the sentence **وَمَا ظَلَمُونَا** (they did not wrong Us, but it was themselves that they were wronging) points out. That is, the gist is that because of it no harm at all happened to *\[p. 267\]* Allah – nor will it happen – it was to themselves indeed that harm came by it. Some commentators are seen saying that it was with reference to their taking and keeping more, beyond the need of each day, as seen in the quotation of the Bible, that this was said. It seems that it would not be right to hold that this sentence intends that alone **والله أعلم**
