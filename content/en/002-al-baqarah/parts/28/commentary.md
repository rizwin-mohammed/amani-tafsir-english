*[Verses 180–182]*

*[p. 453]* By **خَيْر** (good or goodness), the intended sense here is wealth. Among the Arabs, that word is customarily used meaning wealth. Among us too, who are people of the Malayalam language, ‘good’ is customarily said meaning wealth, surely. But, that this word is not customarily used meaning some nominal little thing, and that that word is customarily said only when there is something rather more, such that one would be called a rich man, and that therefore this wasiyyah is not applicable to everyone – it is obligatory only for those who have some means and ability – some scholars have opined. The meaning and intent of the word **مَعْرُوف** (propriety) have already been explained in the explanation of the previous verse.

The gist of what has been stated in these verses can be understood like this: When people who have some wealth in their possession become near to death – when a disease or something else that makes it seem that death has drawn near occurs – making wasiyyah to give from one's wealth, to their parents, and to those who have close family ties, in the measure that is reckoned in the ordinary way as propriety, is a duty upon the true believers. As far as the God-fearing, who keep being heedful of Allah's injunctions and prohibitions, are concerned, it is a duty that must not be dispensed with. After having heard the wasiyyah properly and understood it, in case anyone among the witnesses, or among those who are to receive wealth according to the wasiyyah, makes any change to its true form, the offence of it will be theirs. To the one who made the wasiyyah, no offence at all is going to attach because of it. Who the real offenders *[p. 454]* are is, surely, something that Allah knows well. Now, if it is seen that something has happened to come into a person's wasiyyah as a breach of propriety, or that something culpable has been included, there is, moreover, no objection to those who understood it setting it right, making agreement between the heirs of the wasiyyah so as to remedy that wrong and offence. That is good too. Forgiveness of sins and mercy from Allah may also accrue to them.

These verses are ones that came down before the commands in Surat al-Nisa' that explain the laws of inheritance in full detail came down. Just as it has been explained there who all the inheritors are, and that the right of each one is in such and such a manner, it has not been explained here in detail about the wasiyyah. It has also not been determined how much part of the wealth must be made as wasiyyah, or how much must be given to each one, or who all are included among the close relatives mentioned besides the parents. Only ‘in accordance with propriety (**بِالْمَعْرُوف**)’ has been said. That, because their respective rights have already been fixed for all the inheritors according to the law of inheritance, henceforth wasiyyah to inheritors is not allowed, has also come in hadiths like those quoted below. All this being the cause, about the ruling of the wasiyyah commanded in these verses there is more than one opinion among the scholars. In case one examines them properly, it can be seen that, except for some details, in all the rest they are not contrary to one another, and that those opinions are ones that originated from some technical approaches. **الله اعلم**

The opinion of the majority of scholars, and of many important Quran commentators. Is that, with the coming down of the ayahs of inheritance, the obligation of the wasiyyah has lapsed (become mansukh). Besides the facts indicated above, the chief proofs that these people have are hadiths like those seen below:

(1) That in a speech the Prophet ﷺ said thus, 'Amr ibn Kharijah (r) has quoted: ‘Surely, to all inheritors their right (share) has already been given. Therefore, there is no wasiyyah for any inheritor.’ (A; Ja; Ba; Da.) Though it is with a few slight differences of letters, many others too have quoted hadiths that make this idea clear, and riwayahs from great men. As for the hadith, it is also one without defects.

(2) When Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (r) was on his sickbed, saying that he had quite some wealth, and that as a direct heir he had only one daughter, he asked the Prophet ﷺ whether he could give two of three parts of his wealth as wasiyyah. The Prophet ﷺ said that it is not allowed. Then he asked whether half could be. The Prophet ﷺ did not permit that either. Then *[p. 455]* he asked whether one of three parts could be. Then the Prophet ﷺ said thus: ‘Then one third. One third indeed is plenty. Rather than leaving one's inheritors behind as people who hold out their hands to people, better for one is leaving them behind as rich people.’ This event is one that Bukhari, and Muslim (r) have recorded.

That, as far as those who are heirs to the property according to the law of inheritance are concerned, the law of the wasiyyah has become annulled, and that, as far as parents and close families who come to be non-heirs are concerned, the law of the wasiyyah is in force – is another opinion. Because of being of a different religion, or because of having put the owner of the property to death, it may happen that the parents do not receive inheritance according to the law. By reason of there being closer family members, it may happen that family members more distant than them, too, do not get a right. Along with that, they may also be poor, and ones who suffer hardship in life. That on such occasions wasiyyah needs to be made to these parents and families, and that for anyone who receives inheritance wasiyyah is not obligatory – is the position of the holders of this opinion. This opinion is on the basis that the command of the wasiyyah is not an obligatory law. That it is not that the law of the wasiyyah has been annulled by the law of inheritance, and that the laws of inheritance are only an elucidation of putting the law of the wasiyyah into practice – is what some others say. This does not seem to be a meaningful opinion.

A statement that Ibn Kathir (r) made after pointing out the opinions seen above, and a few of their proofs, is noteworthy. It can be quoted thus: What some say is that the law of the wasiyyah was indeed an obligatory law. That indeed is also what becomes clear from the sentences of the ayah. Then, just as most people among the Quran commentators, and the people of standing among the fuqaha', say, it becomes certain that this – this ayah of the wasiyyah – must have become ‘mansukh’ (lapsed) by means of the ayah of inheritance. Because, that the obligation of making wasiyyah to parents and close families who come as inheritors has become annulled – there is ‘ijma'’ (the unanimous opinion of the scholars). Not only that, by the hadith quoted above – ‘To all inheritors Allah has given their right. Now there is no wasiyyah for any inheritor’ – it has also been disallowed. Then, the ayah of inheritance is indeed an independent law. (It is not an elucidation of the wasiyyah) It is also an obligatory ruling that Allah has fixed for those who have the share and the lot of inheritance. By means of it, this ruling of the wasiyyah has been entirely discontinued. Now, what remains is the matter of close relatives who are not inheritors. Making wasiyyah to them from one third of the property is also a desirable matter. Besides the gist of this ayah being general, what has been recorded thus from Ibn 'Umar (r) in Bukhari *[p. 456]* and in Muslim is also a proof of this. That is: ‘If a person has anything fit to make wasiyyah of, his not passing two nights without its being kept written with him is his duty – thus the Rasul ﷺ has said.’ Ibn 'Umar (r) is saying: ‘From the time I heard the Rasul ﷺ saying this, not even one night has there been without my wasiyyah being kept written with me.’ Concerning doing goodness and good to close relatives, the ayahs and hadiths are, moreover, very many.’ (**ابن كثير**)

However little or much a person's wealth may be, who all his heirs are, and how much is what belongs by right to each one, have been stipulated according to the law of inheritance. The Quran has also made clear that, if there is a wasiyyah and a debt, only what remains after deducting both of them may be divided among the heirs. But, to whom all, and how much each, the wasiyyah must be has also not been determined in the ayah or in hadith. That at the most only one of three parts of the total property may be made as wasiyyah, the Prophet ﷺ has also commanded in the event of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (r) above. When examining all the facts and proofs pointed out above, this many matters concerning the wasiyyah are ones that we are able to understand:-

(1) To family members who receive the right to the property according to the law of inheritance, wasiyyah need not be made specially. It is also something not allowed.

(2) In case anything is made as wasiyyah to those, among those indeed who get the right to the property, who have more poverty and difficulty, taking into account that particularity of theirs, it must be with the consent of the other heirs. This has also been made clear in some hadiths.

(3) Making wasiyyah of something or other to close relatives who do not receive the right to the property is the moral duty of everyone. Though, concerning this duty, there can be no legal questioning in a court of justice, it is a liability that is not to be dispensed with. From the sentence: It is a duty upon the God-fearing who have caution (**حَقًّا عَلَى ٱلۡمُتَّقِينَ**), the seriousness of this can be understood.

(4) The wasiyyah must be taking into account the measure of the wealth, and the circumstances of the close relatives. Care must also be taken not to bring partiality into it. The wasiyyah must be in accordance with customary propriety (**بِالْمَعْرُوف**), surely.

(5) The wasiyyah must stay confined within one of three parts of the total property. That it is not allowed to exceed it for any reason at all is clear from the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ.

Although very much has been stated in the Quran and in hadith concerning the importance of the wasiyyah, in the midst of the Muslim community there is very much laxity and neglect in this matter. Even among those who make wasiyyah, *[p. 457]* some people who do it holding some evil intents, such as defeating the heirs, are also not rare. It is from practices of this kind that some reactionaries, who pose as well-wishers of Islam and in reality keep striving to alter the Islamic laws, have been taking advantage. That, if a son of a person dies during his lifetime, the right to the property not being given, like the other sons who are living, to the children of that deceased son too – to grandsons too, even while there are direct sons of the owner of the property – is an injustice in the law of inheritance, and that it is a wrongdoing done to those grandsons who are orphans, and that this law must be corrected, and so on, they prate. Those people who put out this slander in the name of Islam have forgotten a real fact. It is on the basis of who, among those living when a person dies, are the ones who have more closeness and connection with him, that inheritance has been established. Poverty or wealth, ability or inability, have not been taken into consideration in it. On the contrary, in the wasiyyah, for its part, it is those matters that have been mainly taken into consideration, too. That is why, too, it has been commanded, without reckoning so much each for such and such people, that the wasiyyah must be in accordance with propriety and good custom. That it is from the community's not handling the law of inheritance, and the law of wasiyyah, in the due manner that Allah and His Rasul have taught to us, that occasion came, and is coming, for people of this sort to raise accusations in the name of Islam – the Muslim community has to remember.
