*\[Verse 28\]*

*\[p. 217\]* By means of this verse Allah reminds that man himself is a proof of Allah's existence, oneness, power, resurrection and the like. What was he before coming onto this stage of life? Where was he? Just look back into that endless past! About some of his first stages, such as the seed, the semen and the embryo, he may be able to say something or other. And the state before that? That is what Allah asks in another place: ' **هَلۡ أَتَىٰ عَلَى ٱلۡإِنسَٰنِ حِينٞ مِّنَ ٱلدَّهۡرِ** (Has there come upon man a period of time when he was not anything worth mentioning?! – Su. Al-Insan: 1). *\[TN: a single quotation mark is printed before the Arabic, and no closing mark follows.\]* Yes, in every sense, he has passed so long a time as an unknown thing. Thereafter he came onto the stage, taking on existence as a thing – as a living being – as a human being. When a little time passes, he meets death. Who is it that gave that life? Allah. Who is it that gave that death? Allah. These three states of man he cannot deny. Then, is there any justification for saying that Allah, who made him a living being after that long lifeless state, cannot give him a life again after his death?! For the One who gave life out of pure nothingness, is there any difficulty in giving life once more out of half-nothingness?! Is not this the easier one, compared with the first? (**وَهُوَ أَهۡوَنُ عَلَيۡهِ** – Ar-Rum: 28) *\[TN: the reference is printed "28"; in the Quran text used here these words are in Ar-Rum 30:27.\]* But will everything end with this second life? No. In order that gains and losses be apportioned after the reckoning of the good and bad deeds done in the past life, man will also be returned in the end to Allah, the very same Creator who gave existence its beginning. **كَمَا بَدَأَكُمۡ تَعُودُونَ** – Al-A'raf 29 *\[p. 218\]* (As He made you at first, so you will return again).

*\[Verse 29\]*

In this verse, which contains three sentences, Allah points out, first, the countless favours He has given man; second, His boundless power; and third, His knowledge of all things.

All the things that exist in the earth Allah has prepared and kept ready for man – even before man began dwelling in it. What becomes clear from it is that there is no thing in this earthly world that is not of use to man in one way if not in another, at one time if not at another, for one group if not for another, in one field if not in another. How many, many things – things for which no need had been felt until these recent days, and which were sometimes thought *\[TN: the printed word is കരുതിവശായ, which is not a usual form; the sense "which were thought" is clear from the context.\]* to be only harmful – have today become very essential things for man! Through causes such as the growth of population, the change in the way of life, scientific progress, the experiments and observations that follow, and some chance experiences, man's needs, uses and discoveries also keep growing gradually. Then just think how full of meaning the sentence **خَلَقَ لَكُم مَّا فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ جَمِيعٗا** (He has created for you all that is in the earth) is! This sentence also gives an indication of the great position that Allah has given to the creation of man. An account of this also comes from the next verse onward.

*\[p. 219\]* The purport of saying that all that is in the earth has been created for human beings is not that anyone at all may handle any thing at all unconditionally, as they please. It is that they have been created for the general good of human society. If it is held that anyone may handle any thing without any rule or control at all, the consequences that arise from it are something anyone can guess. It is for that very reason that some conditions and limits and so on have been laid down in religion for the handling of things.

From the sentence **ثُمَّ ٱسۡتَوَىٰٓ إِلَى ٱلسَّمَآءِ**…. (Then He turned to the sky and set them right as seven skies), what can be understood on the face of it is that it was after creating the resources of the earth that He created the sky. But in Surat an-Nazi'at, after stating the account of building the sky and setting it right, He has said thus: **وَٱلۡأَرۡضَ بَعۡدَ ذَٰلِكَ دَحَىٰهَآ** (And the earth He spread out after that – 79: 30). Therefore the general opinion of the Quran commentators is that the particle 'then' here is not for indicating chronological order – it is for indicating a move to another subject after speaking about one subject. (Allah knows.) A somewhat detailed account of the creation of the heavens and the earth can be seen in verses 10–12 of Surah Ha Mim Sajdah.

That the skies are seven in number (**سَبۡعَ سَمَٰوَٰتٖ**) has been said in the Quran in eight or ten places. Because modern astronomy has not been able to find seven skies, this statement, repeated in clear words in the Quran, must not be interpreted otherwise – as some are in the habit of doing. When their Creator says repeatedly that they are seven in number, no one has the right or the competence to go beyond that and say anything. As for scientific conclusions, they are things that keep changing, and things subject to changes such that a theory believed at one time to be a hundred per cent right is judged at another time, by the very same scientists, to be a hundred per cent wrong. But it is not possible for us to pronounce on anything about the nature, the special character, the boundary and so on of each one of the seven. To believe just as it is what Allah has said in open language, and to leave the knowledge of what lies beyond that, holding that He knows it – only this is open to us. In the explanations of Surah Al-Mu'minun 17 and Su. At-Talaq 12, some further clarification on this subject can also be seen.

Allah, who created the heavens and the earth and all things in them in a supremely skilful manner, and who keeps controlling and sustaining *\[p. 220\]* them in an ordered and fitting manner, is indeed the All-Knowing, who knows about all matters without distinction of past, present and future time, and about all things without distinction of smallness or bigness. (**وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيۡءٍ عَلِيمٞ**)

### Section – 4

*\[Verse 30\]*

After reminding, in the 28th verse, of having given life to man after the lifeless state, and in the 29th of having created the resources in the earth for man, in this verse He states the high position that Allah has ordained for man even before man was born on the earth. This ayah, addressed to the Prophet ﷺ, begins, surely, with **وَإِذۡ قَالَ رَبُّكَ** (the occasion when your Rabb said). The practice of beginning a subject with the word **إِذْ** (idh = occasion) in this way *\[p. 221\]* can be seen many times in the Quran. Its purport is that the importance of the subject being described should be remembered. Here, surely, is an occasion describing the beginning of the creation of man, the superiority of the human race, some wise secrets contained in the creation of human beings, and the like.

Before entering into the contents of the ayah, it would be good to give a little thought to the word **خَلِيفَة** (khalifah). The word **خَلِيفَة** has arisen from the root (**خَلْفُ**), which is the opposite in meaning of the word **قُدَّامْ** (quddam) – that is, the front. The meaning seen given to it in dictionaries and in works of Quran commentary is: **مَنْ يَخْلُفُ غَيْرَهُ وَيَقُومُ مَقَامَهُ** (one who comes behind another person and stands in that person's place). Although some slight difference of letters and so on may be seen in the words of some, all agree in the idea. The words **خُلَفَاءُ، خَلَائِفُ** (khulafa', khala'if), which are its plural forms, forms such as **خَلَفَ** (khalafa), which are verb forms, and some other words from the root have been used here and there in the Quran. Some examples can also be seen below.

**خِلَافَة** (khilafah) is, surely, a word well known to everyone. It is to a person who has khilafah that 'khalifah (**خَلِيفَة**)' is said, too. It may be because of this that Imam Raghib, in his Quran lexicon, without giving a separate meaning for the word 'khalifah', has been content with stating the meaning of 'khilafah alone. His wording is this:

**وَالْخِلَافَةُ النِّيَابَةُ عَن الْغَيرِ إِمَّا لِغَيْبَةِ الْمَنْدُوبِ عَنْهُ وَإِمَّا لِمَوْتِهِ وَإِمَّا لِعَجْزِهِ وَإِمَّا لِتَشْرِيفِ الْمُسْتَخْلَفِ وَعَلَى هَذَا ٱلْوَجْهِ ٱلْأَخِيرِ اسْتَخْلَفَ اللَّه أَوْلِيَاءَهُ فَى الأَرْضِ**

"Khilafah means standing in place of another person. Either because of the absence of the one in whose place one stands, or because of his death, or because of his incapacity, or in order to honour the person who is made khalifah. It is in this last manner that Allah has made His 'auliya'' – the people who are His friends – khalifahs in the earth." Continuing, he has also taken and shown as examples some Quranic sentences that are quoted below. As far as Allah is concerned, absence, death and incapacity are impossible, surely. That is why he said that it is in the last manner that He has made Allah's people khalifahs in the earth. 'Khalifah' is customarily said meaning the great ruler (**السُّلْطَانُ الْعَظيمُ**). As Ibn Jarir (r) has pointed out, that is not in the meaning of ruler. It is because of being a person who, after a ruler, comes into his place and *\[p. 222\]* carries on affairs. Moreover, even if it turns out that the word has such a meaning directly, that meaning will not fit in every place either.

When the quotations like those seen above, and the statements of many great men about 'khilafah' and 'khalifah', are examined, the following points are understood from them.

(1) For the word 'khalifah', the renderings 'successor, one who comes after, representative and so on may be given in Malayalam.

(2) What has been taken into account in that word is successorship rather than representation. The reason is that the word has taken shape from a root which has that basic meaning.

(3) If it is said that Allah has made someone khalifah, it is not possible for that khalifah to be His successor or His representative in the ordinary sense. The reason is that, as far as Allah is concerned – as Imam Raghib (r) has pointed out – there will be no absence for Him in any sense, and there is no question either of His handing over or entrusting any authority, action or obligation of His to another person.

(4) Then, it has become clear that if 'Allah's khalifah' is seen used anywhere about anyone, it is not possible for it to be in the sense of Allah's successor or representative – other than in the sense of a person whom Allah has honoured by giving the position of khilafah.

Well then, let us now consider what the purport is of Allah's saying **إِنِّي جَاعِلٞ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ خَلِيفَةٗ** (I am going to make a khalifah in the earth). Ibn Kathir (r) has spoken about this in detail. Let us quote here some relevant parts of what he said: After stating **أَي قَوْما يَخْلُفُ بَعْضُهُمْ بَعْضًا قَرْنًا بَعْد قَرْنً وَجِيلاً بَعْد جِيل** *\[DOUBT: on the page this Arabic is printed as two pieces with a wide gap: قَرْنًا بَعْد قَرْنً وَجِيلاً بَعْد جِيل stands at the right, next to the Malayalam bracket, and أَي قَوْما يَخْلُفُ بَعْضُهُمْ بَعْضًا at the left, so read right to left the قرنا piece would come first. It is given here in the order of Ibn Kathir's own wording (أي … بعضا first). The second قرن is printed قَرْنً (-an, without alif). Please confirm.\]* (that is, a people of whom some keep coming behind others, one generation after another generation, and the people of one age after the people of another age), he has also quoted, as proof of this, some ayahs that contain the plural and the verb form of 'khalifah'. These are they:

**هُوَ ٱلَّذِي جَعَلَكُمۡ خَلَـٰٓئِفَ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ** – Fatir: 39. Gist: He indeed is the One who has made you 'khala'if – those who come as successors – in the earth. **وَيَجۡعَلُكُمۡ خُلَفَآءَ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ** – An-Naml: 62. He makes you the 'khulafa'' – successors who come one after another – of the earth. **وَلَوۡ نَشَآءُ لَجَعَلۡنَا مِنكُم مَّلَـٰٓئِكَةٗ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ يَخۡلُفُونَ** – Az-Zukhruf: 60. Had We willed, We would have appointed angels from among you yourselves, so as to come in the earth behind (you) as replacements. **فَخَلَفَ مِنۢ بَعۡدِهِمۡ خَلۡفٌ** – Maryam: 59. After them (those prophets), a 'khalf' – a kind of successors – came behind.

*\[p. 223\]* Ibn Kathir (r) continues: 'As a section of the Quran commentators say, what is meant here by 'khalifah' is not Adam (a) alone. Qurtubi (r) has stated this opinion as the opinion of Ibn Abbas and Ibn Mas'ud (r), and of the Quran commentators in general. In this (in saying that it is the general opinion of the Quran commentators) there is something to be considered a little. In reality there is a great deal of difference of opinion in it. Razi (r) and others have also quoted it. It also becomes clear that the Prophet Adam (a) was not intended as an individual. The reason is that, if it were so, the angels' question **أَتَجۡعَلُ فِيهَا مَن يُفۡسِدُ فِيهَا وَيَسۡفِكُ ٱلدِّمَآءَ** (Will You make in it those who cause corruption in it and shed blood?) has no fitness. What they mean, surely, is that there will be such people in the human race. It may be that the angels asked that question either because of some special knowledge granted to the angels, or because of what they understood about human action from Allah's having told them that He was creating man out of black clay that makes a sound when struck (**إِنِّي خَٰلِقُۢ بَشَرٗا مِّن صَلۡصَٰلٖ مِّنۡ حَمَإٖ مَّسۡنُونٖ**: Al-Hijr 28), or – as Qurtubi said – because they understood from the word 'khalifah' the meaning: a person who gives judgement in cases of wrongdoing and who restrains from forbidden things and from sins, or else – as the Quran commentators state – because they reckoned human beings by the standard of the community that had been in the earth before. That question was not – as some commentators are in the habit of misunderstanding – by way of an objection against Allah, or because of envy toward the children of Adam. For they will not say anything going ahead of Allah, nor will they ask a question that is not permitted. Allah has said, surely: **لَا يَسۡبِقُونَهُۥ بِٱلۡقَوۡلِ** (They will not go ahead of Him in speech. (21: 27).

(He continues) 'When Allah informed them that He was appointing a creation in the earth, they asked thus. This question is an inquiry as to what the wise secret contained in that creation is. That is, the purport is: if the aim in creating this creation, among whom there will come to be troublemakers and destroyers, is that You be worshipped, here we are, always worshipping You, surely. Then, is there any need to create another group? To that, Allah gave the reply too: I know what you do not know (**إِنِّيٓ أَعۡلَمُ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ**). That is, although the evil that you mentioned is in it, I know that, along with it, there is also contained in it a good that exceeds it; you do not know it. I will send among them prophets and messengers; among them there will be siddiqs, martyrs, the righteous, those who sacrifice, scholars, devout people and others…' (**من ابن كثير باختصار**).

Let us also quote the differences of opinion that Ibn Kathir (r) pointed out as quoted by Razi (r). *\[p. 224\]* Giving for the word 'khalifah' the meaning **من يخلف غيره ويقوم مقامه** (a person who comes behind another person and stands in that person's place), and quoting as proof of it the first and second Quranic sentences quoted above, Razi (r) says: 'There are two opinions about who is meant by 'khalifah'. (One) is that it is Adam (a). Then, what was said, 'one who causes corruption in the earth', is not about him; it will be meant for his descendants. (Two) is that it is his children. Among those who say that the Prophet Adam (a) is meant, there are two opinions about the reason for calling him khalifah.

1\) Because Allah settled Adam in the earth, removing the race of the jinn that had been in the earth, he became their khalifah (the one who came behind).

2\) He was called khalifah because, among those subject to Allah's commands, he gives judgement in place of Allah. This is what has been reported from Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas and Suddi (r):- **يَٰدَاوُۥدُ إِنَّا جَعَلۡنَٰكَ خَلِيفَةٗ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ فَٱحۡكُم بَيۡنَ ٱلنَّاسِ بِٱلۡحَقِّ** – Sad: 26 (O Dawud, We have made you a khalifah in the earth. So judge among the people according to the truth): this Quranic verse strengthens this. But what those who say that the children of Adam are meant by 'khalifah' say is that they were called khalifah because some of them come behind others. The verse **جَعَلَكُمۡ خَلَـٰٓئِفَ ٱلۡأَرۡضِ** (made you khalifahs of the earth 6: 165) also gives strength to this. 'As for the word khalifah (**خَلِيفَة**), it can be used for one person and for more persons, for male and for female. (**من الرازى**).

If the statements and explanations of many leading Quran commentators, including these two great men, are examined, it becomes clear that more than one interpretation exists of the words **إِنِّي جَاعِلٞ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ خَلِيفَةٗ** (I am appointing a khalifah in the earth). Justifications, more or less, can be seen for all of them. Nor are there proofs on which it could be said decisively that such-and-such alone among them is right and all the others wrong. What is to be considered is which among them is the more acceptable. The general form of those interpretations may be understood thus:–

1\) Allah has created the human race as the successors of another community that had been the inhabitants of this earth. That is what is meant by 'khilafah'. That earlier community was the race of the jinn. Because they caused wrongdoing and disorder in the earth, Allah destroyed them or drove them away. It was because they thought that the human race, newly coming onto the stage, may also cause wrongdoing and destruction like them, that the angels asked about *\[p. 225\]* it.

2\) Allah has created human beings as communities that keep coming in turn in the earth, one generation after another generation. This is what is meant by khilafah. It is this second opinion that is seen to fit the sentences of the Quran more closely. That is why leading Quran commentators such as Ibn Jarir and Ibn Kathir (r) have given it preference, too. Moreover, as Razi (r) has stated, according to the particular structure of the word 'khalifah', there is no objection to its being used as singular, plural, masculine and feminine – all of them. Words that can be used like this are indeed plentiful besides in the Arabic language. In addition, it was, surely, on the occasion when He was going to create Adam (a), the father of mankind, that Allah said this to the angels. In keeping with that occasion, even when 'khalifah' is said in the singular form, what is meant will be his descendants. Using the names of the first father of a tribe or of a race for the tribe and the race alike is also, indeed, a custom in the Arabic language.

3\) The khalifah whom Allah has appointed for carrying out judgement and justice among His creatures in the earth according to Allah's commands and prohibitions – His representative – is Adam (a), together with the good people among his descendants who, like him, keep observing justice and truth. According to this, what was said – that they will cause corruption and shed blood – will be meant for those who are not like that. It is certain that it is not because Allah is unable to put justice and right conduct into effect in the earth that He gives this representation. Two reasons are pointed out for it. (One) The human race in general is not of a nature fit to receive divine messages and godly commands directly. That is why He chooses prophets from among them and conveys these through them. (Two) The aim is to make the human race superior by way of giving it the position of khalifah. It is not that Allah had any need in it. It is this third opinion that is seen adopted by a section of the Quran commentators.

4\) The fourth is an opinion that has taken shape from the third opinion, widening its scope somewhat further and giving special consideration to the material side. What many of the modern Quran commentators usually show more interest in is strengthening this opinion. On the basis of the account of Sayyid Qutb (r), it may be described thus: the purport of saying 'appointing a khalifah' is creating a class of creatures fit to be entrusted with the helm of managing the moving and unmoving things of this earth, and to seek out and make use of its resources, deposits and energies. Those who are inclined to give an interpretation to the verses of the Quran with a somewhat material *\[p. 226\]* bent can be seen, naturally, going a little further and arguing even that the management of the universe is for the most part in the hands of man, and that man, progressing and progressing intellectually and scientifically, may come near to reaching almost alongside God. What we have to say for the present about this argument is a sentence that Allah has said in Su. Al-An'am 91 and elsewhere: **وَمَا قَدَرُواْ ٱللَّهَ حَقَّ قَدۡرِهِۦٓ** (They have not reckoned Allah in the manner in which He ought to be reckoned).

This fourth opinion – even if its excesses are removed – is not seen to be in agreement with some statements of the Quran. It can be seen in Surah Ta-Ha: 123 that, along with commanding the Prophet Adam (a) and Hawwa' to go down from Paradise to the earth, Allah also said that if any guidance from Me comes to you – to the human race – those who follow that guidance will not go astray or suffer hardship. Not only that: in the 38th verse, which comes next, too, Allah has made this matter clear. It should be remembered that it is when sending human beings to the earth as khalifahs that Allah says this, and that what is meant by guidance from Allah is the guidance that Allah gives to the prophets through wahy and through the scriptures. If the detailed forms and the arguments of each of the opinions seen above were all described, it would become very long. Looked at comparatively, it is the opinion quoted second among these that has preference in many respects. This truth can be understood further from Ibn Kathir (r)'s quotation.

Although it is not possible for us to determine what the wise secrets are that are contained in Allah's informing the angels of the news that He was creating man, when we look at it with the context we are able to understand some things. Making known to them that man has some special characteristics and superiorities different from other creatures; teaching that it is good to hold mutual consultation in important matters and to seek the opinion of others; turning their attention to the reason for commanding them, as stated below, to do sujud to Adam; correcting their notion about human beings according to the reaction that shows itself from them – many such things may have been intended by it. Allah knows the truth. In any case, the reply that Allah gave to the angels' doubts is very full of meaning and weighty – yes: **إِنِّيٓ أَعۡلَمُ مَا لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ** (I know what you do not know)!

By means of this *\[p. 227\]* sentence Allah has pointed out that, although some of the evil aspects you mentioned may occur in human beings, many goods and virtues surpassing that are due to come from them, and that many secrets which you cannot conceive of are contained in it. That the angels became convinced of this reality from experience itself can also be seen in the next verses. It is noteworthy that Allah did not state that the evil aspect the angels mentioned – corruption and bloodshed in the earth – will not come from human beings, or that that charge of theirs is a mistake. The reason for this is that however exalted and excellent man may be, there lies in him another side as well. The Quran reminds of this truth in more than one place, too. Allah points out the basis of man's superiority:
