*[Verse 196]*

*[p. 492]* In this verse, which is somewhat long, many particulars concerning the rites named Hajj and 'Umrah are contained. Except the assembling at 'Arafah, among the component rites of the Hajj, all the rest need to be practised in the 'Umrah too. Only in fixed months and days may the rites of the Hajj be done. For 'Umrah there is no period-determination. In the manner stated below, 'Umrah may be performed along with the Hajj too, and on its own too. For those who have ability, doing the Hajj once in a lifetime is obligatory. (See Al 'Imran: 97) It is also the fifth among the five pillars of Islam. On whether performing 'Umrah at least once is obligatory, two opinions are current among the scholars from the Companions onward. That, in the circumstance that there is no proof for stating one of the two decisively, what is needed is to refrain from saying that it is obligatory – examining the proofs of both groups – Ibn Jarir (r) has pointed out. Be it obligatory or not, Hajj and 'Umrah are indeed two important rites of worship in Islam. Those who have convenience and ability are also ones who need to try to gain the pleasure of Allah, practising both more.

There are no proofs at all showing how many Hajjs and 'Umrahs the noble Prophet ﷺ did in the period of his Makkan life. The likelihood is of having done so on many occasions. Though the law of the Hajj came down in Islam most probably in the fifth year after the Hijrah, because the situations in Makkah at that time were not safe, the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions did not happen to do the Hajj at that time. In the ninth year, under the leadership of Abu Bakr (r), a party of Companions performed the Hajj. In the tenth year, the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions went together and performed the Hajj and came back. This is the last Hajj of the Prophet ﷺ, famous by the name **حَجةُ الوِدَاع** (Hajjat al-Wida'). Though it is three times that the Prophet ﷺ did the 'umrah rite, for it he went from Madinah four times. The first was in the 6th year of the Hijrah. But, because of the obstruction of the mushriks, according to the very famous covenant that took place at Hudaibiyyah, the noble Prophet returned on that day. The second is the going the next year according to the terms of that truce, and performing the 'Umrah and coming back. The third was in the 8th year. Because the ‘ihram’ (entry) for this was from the place named Ji'ir Ranah ( **جِعْرانة** ), *[p. 493]* this is known by the name 'Umrat al-Ji'ir Rana. The fourth was in the Hajjat al-Wida' too. Now let us note the main subjects stated in the ayah. They can be summarised thus:-

(1) Complete the Hajj and the 'Umrah for Allah. Perform those two, according to the command of Allah, intending His pleasure and reward – that is the gist. The import of saying ‘do for Allah’ about any matter whatever is indeed this. Other than that, it has no meaning that it is in order to fulfil some need or good of Allah. By the worship of the creatures, or by means of any other matters, there is indeed no gain at all to come about for Allah. He is, surely, the wholly Self-Sufficient One *[DOUBT: സർവ്വധന്യൻ, literally “one wholly rich” or “one wholly blessed”; rendered “the wholly Self-Sufficient One” after the clause “who has no need of anyone's support or help”; “the All-Rich One” is the literal option.]* who has no need of anyone's support or help. The intention of having said ‘complete the Hajj and the 'Umrah’ has been interpreted in more than one manner:

1. Finish doing, from first to last, rightly and well, all the matters that need to be observed in both.

2. Whether as an obligatory duty, or as an optional one, once one has entered into either one of the two, then one must indeed do it entirely and complete it. Even if some wrongs happen to come in between, it is not allowed to stop it off in between without finishing the rest.

3. The journey for them must be with only the said rites as its aim. Or, refraining from either coming with other aims too, such as trade, or settling, in the circumstance of having come, that one will carry out those too. (\*)

> **Note:** (\*) That one section of the present-day Hajj travellers are of this kind is regrettable.

4. The wealth used for the travel expenses of both being pure, and earned through permissible ways. (\*\*) Other opinions too, besides these, can be seen. In reality, what is true is that all that has just been said is included in the gist of ‘complete for Allah’. **والله اعلم**

> **Note:** (\*\*) In this matter too, the state of many is indeed deplorable.

(2) If some hindrance befalls, do sacrifice of one animal that has become convenient. That to which the meaning to be befallen by hindrance was given is the verb **أحُْصِرْ**. As Imam Raghib (r) has pointed out, both in open hindrances like those that arise from enemies, and in other kinds of hindrances that arise through disease and the like, that word will be used. What most Quran commentators are seen to have accepted is that the hindrance faced from enemies is what is intended here. In the 6th year of the Hijrah, when the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions went for 'Umrah, the Quraish stopped them at Hudaibiyyah, surely. That it was following that event that this verse came down, and so on, is the justification that they state for this. But, both when one looks linguistically, and when one examines in the light of some hadiths, *[p. 494]* what seems right is that all hindrances that come about through enemies, or through causes such as disease, are included in it. Because the occasion of the sending down is that, there is no need, surely, to restrict the range of meaning of that word, holding that that alone is what is intended. (Allah knows the reality) It is to the word **هَدْيِ** (Hady) that the meaning sacrificial animal was given. To this word there are also meanings thus: ‘conduct, action, what is sacred’. It is usual for that word to be used, intending specially the animals named goats, cattle, camels that are taken to the Haram for the rite of sacrifice. That is what is intended here too. From its having said ‘The sacrificial animal that has become convenient must be offered in sacrifice.’, it can be understood that, when any hindrance is faced such that one cannot complete the acts of the Hajj or of the 'Umrah, doing sacrifice of an animal is obligatory, and that even if it is a goat that will suffice, and that it is not obligatory that it must be cattle or a camel.

(3) Until the sacrificial animal reaches its fixed place, it is not allowed to remove the hair of the head. After the main rites of the Hajj and of the 'Umrah have ended, the hair of the head needs to be taken off. It is with that that one desists from them. That it is not necessary that the hair of the head be indeed removed – even if one cuts the hair that will suffice – can be understood from the 27th verse of Surah Fath and from the hadiths. Therefore, it can be understood that what is intended by ‘shave the head’ is ‘take off the hair of the head’. That the taking off of the hair must be after the rite of sacrifice is over, and that the carrying out of the sacrifice must be at the place fixed for it, is clear from this verse. The place fixed for the rite of sacrifice ( **مَحِل** ) is the Makkah Haram. But, when the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions had to return following their being prevented by the enemies at Hudaibiyyah, what happened was that they offered in sacrifice, there indeed, the camels that they had brought along for the rite of sacrifice. One opinion concerning this is that it was because, on account of the obstruction of the enemies, they could not take them to the Haram that they did so, and that when such hindrances are faced, the sacrifice may be carried out at the very place where the hindrance is faced. According to another opinion, it is that the place where they offered them in sacrifice at Hudaibiyyah was indeed within the boundary of the Haram. In any case, in the ordinary course, the carrying out of the rite of sacrifice being in the Haram is obligatory. The taking off of the hair of the head must be after that.

(4) If, because of some disease, or because of other nuisances such as lice on the head, the urgent need is faced of having to remove the hair beforehand, there is no objection to that. As a remedy for that, a ransom is also needed. Fasting, or almsgiving, or a rite of sacrifice – this is the ransom. How much each of these is, or in what form it must be, Allah has not made clear. Even so, we can understand about them from the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ. When a man asked Ka'b ibn 'Ujrah ( **كَعْب بِنُ عُجْرة** ) about this, the reply he gave was like this: I was taken to the presence of the Prophet ﷺ. Lice were dropping and falling down over my face. Then *[p. 495]* the noble Prophet said: I had not thought that you had difficulty to this extent. Will you get a goat? (Will you be able to offer a goat in sacrifice?) I said: No. The noble Prophet: Then, you observe fasting for three days. Or give food to six needy persons at half a ‘sa'’: (\*) each. And do remove the hair. Though this ruling (the ruling that one of these three must be done) came about in my particular matter, it is a ruling general to all of you.’ The event is one that Ahmad, Bukhari (r) and others have recorded. That this was in the time of the Hudaibiyyah event has been made clear in some riwayahs. In short, it has become clear from this, surely, that what is intended by this ransom is either keeping the fast for three days, or offering a goat in sacrifice, or giving foodstuff to six needy persons at half a ‘sa'’ each. But, must these three things – or some among them – be indeed in the Haram? Or else, may they be anywhere? On this there is difference of opinion among the scholars. What is understood outwardly from the hadith is that there is no objection either way. Moreover, there are no adequate proofs against that. **الله اعلم**.

> **Note:** (\*) What is made up of four ‘mudd’ ( **مُد** ) is one ‘sa'’ ( **صَاع** ). A mudd is a measure about as much as one gets if one scoops with both hands full. (The expert opinion is that one sa' is 2.024 kilograms – Publ.)

(5) When in a state of peace – it is then, surely, that one can perform all the rites in due form – those who, before entering into the Hajj, finish performing the 'Umrah, and so take enjoyment such that the matters (seen below) that until then had been made forbidden become permissible, must do sacrifice of one animal that has become convenient. Because it has said ‘what has become convenient’ – as said before – it is enough even if it is a goat. Its being cattle or a camel is also better.

Once one has formally entered into the Hajj and the 'Umrah, then, until it ends and one becomes free from it, men's covering of the head, women's covering of the face, using perfume, wearing things like trousers – shirt – cap, taking off the hair, the relation of woman and man, the hunting of animals – some matters thus become forbidden. Therefore, the entering into them is called ‘ihram’ ( **اِحْرَام** or the establishing of the forbidden), and the becoming free from them is called ‘tahallul ( **تَحلّل** or becoming permissible)’. There is a stipulation that the people who come from each region must do ihram from such-and-such places. Those places are also called ‘miqat’ ( **مِيقَات** or the fixed place). With those who come from abroad first doing and completing an 'umrah rite, the prohibitions seen above will go away. Afterwards, when the days of the Hajj draw near, it will suffice if, like the residents of Makkah, without going to the miqats, each one does ihram for the Hajj at the very place where each is staying. It is about, in this interval, from the prohibitions mentioned above, being re*[p. 496]*leased and becoming free that it said ‘take enjoyment until the Hajj by doing 'Umrah’ ( **تَمَتَّعَبِالْعُمْرَةِإِلَى الْحَجِّ** ). It is thus that those who come from far away mostly have been doing, bearing the convenience in mind. The doing of ihram from the miqat for the Hajj alone is called **إِفْرَاد** (singling out), and the doing of ihram for the Hajj and the 'Umrah together is called **قران** (joining together). What is stated in this verse is about the 'Umrah of the **تَمَتَّع** (taking enjoyment) mentioned first.

(6) That those who do **تَمَتُّعْ** (taking enjoyment) must carry out a convenient sacrifice ( **هَدْي** ) in return for the concessions they obtain in between was said, surely. If that is not possible, what is needed is that they observe ten fasts. Of these, three must be finished on the days of the Hajj – before the tenth of Dhul-Hijjah. The remaining seven must be completed also after having arrived back in the land. But the concession of ‘tamattu'’ is not for the residents of the Haram. Therefore the attendant atonement too is not applicable to them.

Those who wish to know more explanation concerning each of the things stated above must have recourse to the books of fiqh and to the special books of ‘manasik’ (of the matters connected with the Hajj rites). Note specially the last part of the ayah. All the rites must be worked in each one's own true form, and with caution, and full of devotion, and must also be with only the pleasure and reward of Allah as the aim. Otherwise, one must fear concerning the punitive action of Allah. Such things are the indications in that sentence. **والله الموفق والمعين**

### Section – 25

*[Verses 197–199]*

*[p. 498]* The months of the Hajj are some months that are widely known. That is, Shawwal, and Dhul-Qa'dah, and Dhul-Hijjah. Though the rites of the Hajj end in the first half of Dhul-Hijjah, when one counts the months of the Hajj that too is counted – that is all. Because it is not allowed to do the Hajj other than in the fixed period, it is clear from this that it is not allowed to do ihram for the Hajj before Shawwal. The intention of having said make the Hajj obligatory in these months ( **فَرَضَ فِيهِنَّ ٱلۡحَجَّ** ) is indeed to enter, doing ihram for it. That, once one has entered into the Hajj, even if a hindrance befalls in between, other than that one must adopt the means of redress fixed for it, it is not allowed to desist unconditionally, has been understood from the 196th verse, surely. Then, with the entering into the Hajj, completing it becomes obligatory. It is for that reason that it said ‘make the Hajj obligatory’.

Once one has entered into the Hajj, afterwards Allah has strictly disallowed three things: amorous talk with women, and licentiousness, and dispute ( **رَفَث, فُسُوق, جِدَال** ) Of these, about the meanings and intents of the word **رَفَث**, it has been explained in the commentary of the 187th verse. What was intended by it there was intercourse with women*[p. 499]*. Here, that not only coition, but also the preliminary plays such as kissing that are connected with it, and talks too, are what is intended can be understood from the hadiths, and from the statements of the predecessors. It is for that reason that here we have given it the translation ‘amorous talk with women’. In having said licentiousness ( **فُسُوق** ), all doings without obeying the injunctions and prohibitions of the religion, and without keeping discipline, are included. All talks with quarrelling, and wrangling, and carrying on controversy with one another are included in carrying on dispute ( **جِدَال** ) too. Though avoiding the last two things is indeed obligatory at all times, they have been more strictly disallowed at the time of the Hajj. Along with commanding to avoid these three things, He has encouraged to keep doing meritorious good things, and has alerted that, however small a good thing may be, Allah will not waste it.

Afterwards, Allah advises that, when getting ready for the Hajj journey, the going must be after getting ready all the urgently needed things required for it, **زاد** (Zad) is the word that Allah has used for this. Though its literal meaning is foodstuff for the journey, what is wont to be meant by it is all the things necessary for the journey. Some in the period of Jahiliyyah used to think that, when going for the Hajj rite, going after getting ready the goods and materials for the journey is not good, and that it is not needed, and that it is against ‘tawakkul’ (that one must entrust matters to Allah), and so on. Even in this period, this notion is not absent in a few. This is a refutation to people of this kind. That those who come for the Hajj from the region of Yemen used to come without any travel belongings, saying that they are ones who entrust to Allah, and that it was that which was the reason for this to come down, Bukhari (r) and others have quoted as Ibn 'Abbas (r) having said. In this period, if one has money in hand, it will be possible to buy almost all things from Makkah. Even so, carrying some implements and such will be an urgent necessity. In former times, even if one had money in hand, it would be troublesome to get the necessary things for a price, surely. In the end, what will come about will be that they themselves either have to suffer distress, or become a burden to others. That one must not give occasion for that, and that carrying the necessary things on the journey is not contrary to the aim of the Hajj, Allah makes known by means of this command. Along with that indeed, He also alerts to another reality: that among the travel things the most excellent is taqwa ( **تَقْوَى** ) – that is indeed it. Both as guarding so as not to depend on others and not to give occasion to beg, and as guarding the injunctions and prohibitions of Allah – or God-fearing reverence –, in two manners, interpretation has been given here to this sentence.

When one looks at the occasion, the sentence may also be one that embraces the idea in both these interpretations, *[p. 500]* in a general meaning. (Allah knows.) The literal meaning of the term **تَقْوَى** is indeed to guard. Even so, it is intending the God-fearing reverence that guards the injunctions and prohibitions of Allah that, in the Quran and elsewhere, it and the other terms that originate from it are mostly wont to be used. That what is intended by the caution in having said **وَاتَّقُونِ يَاأُولِيالأَلْبَابِ** (O men of reason, you must also guard against Me) in the next sentence is what was said second, there is no doubt. Getting ready the travel materials is, surely, outwardly a worldly need. Therefore, on the basis of this command one must not cling much to worldly comforts and conveniences – the matter of guarding against Allah is what needs to be heeded more – it is indeed this that all this alerts to. Reminding of the favour of having given mankind garments for covering nakedness, and for adornment, it has said thus in Su: A'raf 26: **وَلِبَاسُالتَّقْوَىذَلِكَ خَيْرٌ** (The garment that is taqwa is indeed what is more excellent.) It too reminds of indeed this principle.

Continuing, what is seen in the 198th verse is the removing of a misunderstanding that had crept in among the Muslims. The Jahiliyyah Arabs had a habit of conducting mighty trades in the Hajj season at places such as 'Ukkaz, Majnah, Dhul-Majaz ( **عكاظ، مجنة، ذو المجاز** ). Thinking that this is not good, the Muslims used to keep away from it. That it is about this that **لَيْسَعَلَيْكُمْ جُنَاحٌ**... *[DOUBT: the dots “...” are printed touching the left end of this Arabic phrase; from the page it cannot be told on which side of the Arabic they belong.]* (There is no wrong for you in seeking the favour from your Rabb.) came down has come in riwayahs that many, including Bukhari (r), have quoted from Ibn 'Abbas (r) and others. Ibn Jarir (r) quotes thus: ‘Abu Salih (r) asked 'Umar (r): ‘Amir al-Mu'minin! Did you carry on trade in the Hajj season? He said: Were their (the Arabs') means of life (in anything else) other than in the Hajj?!’

On the 9th day of Dhul-Hijjah the hajis assemble at 'Arafah. It is intending the return from there that ‘flow forth ( **افاضة** )’ is said. That usage is one that likens the stream of the hajis to water flowing. What is called both ‘'Arafat’ and ‘'Arafah’ ( **عَرفَات، عَرْفَة** ) is indeed one. When coming down from 'Arafah in the evening, one needs to stay the night at the place called Muzdalifah ( **مُزْدَلِفَة** ). It is intending Muzdalifah – or else the hill named Quzah ( **قُزح** ) that is there – that ‘al-Mash'ar al-Haram’ ( **الْمَشْعَرِالْحَرَامِ** ) is said. Allah has specially brought out and shown the things that need to be known when flowing forth from 'Arafah:

(1) At al-Mash'ar al-Haram one needs to keep Allah specially in mind. There is no obligation at all that such-and-such ‘dhikr’ or ‘du'a'’ is needed there. Even so, *[p. 501]* dhikr, du'a', talbiyah – all these need to be increased as much as possible. Maghrib and 'Isha' too must be prayed there, making them jam' (together). It can be said that it is all this that is intended by it.

(2) In the Hajj rites and in their observances, many improper customs had crept in during the period of Jahiliyyah. It was not possible even to distinguish the right and the wrong in them. Now, explaining their true form, and giving the good fortune to accept the true faith and right guidance, Allah has favoured the Muslims. He also alerts that, as thanks for that, one needs to keep bearing Allah in mind, at all times in general, and in the midst of the Hajj rites especially.

(3) That, when flowing forth, the coming away must be indeed from the place from which mankind in general flow forth, is another matter. This is a refutation of an evil usage that the Quraish had smuggled into the practices of the Hajj in the name of their nobility and their leadership. After first explaining about some things that need to be known when coming away from 'Arafah, then, another matter too, it is by saying **ثُمَّ** that Allah alerts to this matter. From the time of the Prophet Ibrahim (a) onward, the habit is that the people who assemble in the Hajj go to 'Arafah, and return from there by way of Muzdalifah. But, the Quraish, and some connected with them, not joining the company of the other people, boasting ‘We are the worthy ones and the heroes ( **نَحْنُ الحمس** )’ and so on, used to assemble at Muzdalifah, and turn back from there. Allah has commanded that henceforth none of the Muslims must do so, and that all must go to 'Arafah and return from there indeed.

(4) Lastly, He has advised to seek forgiveness of sins from Allah, and, reminding that He is One who forgives much and is the Ever-Merciful, has encouraged it.

We did point out before about His having commanded in this very manner after the prayer and after fasting. Here, it is following His having stated about the return from 'Arafah that this advice is. It can be seen that in the next verse Allah has again advised like this that one must bear Allah in mind at the juncture of having finished performing all the rites connected with the Hajj too. The essence and the life of all worships rest, surely, in the remembrance of Allah and in the consciousness about Him. This is the reason for this. It can be seen that in having advised to seek forgiveness of sins on this occasion a special secret too is contained. They (those who do the Hajj) are now, surely, at occasions and at places in which supplications may get more answer, and in which His glances of grace may be obtained more. Therefore, it is indeed that one must take care to use this juncture for that. To the word **ذِكْر** (Dhikr) *[p. 502]* the meaning is ‘keep in mind, remember, meditate, say, extol’ and such. In places of this kind, its being carried into effect is by means of hamd, tasbih, tahlil, takbir, du'a' and the like. Presence of mind, and sincerity, and God-fearing reverence are their living side. **ومن الله التوفيق**
