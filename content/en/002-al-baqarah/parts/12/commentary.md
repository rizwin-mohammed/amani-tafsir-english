### Section – 9

*\[Verses 72–73\]*

*\[p. 290\]* As stated before, this is the first part of the event. A person came to be killed. Allah has not stated who the slayer was, or what the reason for the killing was. Narrating the story is, surely, not the aim. To understand the lesson of the story, there is no need to know any of that either. It is as an event that happened among the Israelites before, and that, following on it, was a sign of Allah's mighty power and of life after death, that it has been *\[p. 291\]* quoted here. This can be properly understood from the last two sentences of the 73rd verse. The aim of describing that past event addressing them is to warn the present Israelites, who, after seeing and knowing such signs, still, with hardened hearts, have been keeping up the tradition of misguidance, and to invite them to the straight way. This fact can be understood somewhat more from the next verse.

Not knowing who the real murderer was, they fell into mutual accusation and counter-accusation, division and quarrel. At that juncture Allah commanded them to take some portion or other of that cow, which had been slaughtered as stated in the previous verses, and to strike that slain man with it. Which that portion was has not been made clear here. It is clear that, whichever the portion was, it has no special place in giving life to the corpse, and that only Allah's intention and command is the cause of it. That when struck with that portion life came back to the dead body, and that it said that the one who killed me is such-and-such a person, is what – in the situation where there are no proofs to the contrary – becomes clear from the style of the ayah. The Quran commentators too have expressed that very opinion. Some matters seen below are noteworthy here :

(1) It is at the juncture when the people, not identifying the slayer, were evading by making accusations against one another, that Allah commands to strike the dead body with the cow's portion.

(2) It is also while saying that Allah will bring out into the open the thing that you hide (**وَٱللَّهُ مُخۡرِجٞ مَّا كُنتُمۡ تَكۡتُمُونَ**) that that command has been given; then, there is, surely, no need to say that the aim of this command is to reveal who that murderer is.

(3) Continuing, it also says that it is in that manner that Allah brings the dead to life (**كَذَٰلِكَ يُحۡيِ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡمَوۡتَىٰ** ).

(4) At the end, it also says that He shows you His signs in order for you to think using reason (**وَيُرِيكُمۡ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ**-الخ). Then it became clear from these sentences that the dead person was brought to life through it, and that it happened as a sign.

(5) It is right that Allah has not clearly stated that the slain man was struck with the cow's portion, that he came to life through it, and that he said that his slayer was such-and-such a person. But it is clear from the sentences before and after. To keep silent about some matters that are clearly known from the statements before and after is one of the Quran's methods of presentation. About this it has been explained before, in the Introduction and elsewhere.

*\[p. 292\]* Of there being a connection between the event of slaughtering the cow and the event of the killing, the sentence **ٱضۡرِبُوهُ بِبَعۡضِهَا** (strike him with its portion) is a very clear proof. Anyone who has even an elementary knowledge of the Arabic language can understand that, in this, what is meant by the pronoun (**الضَمِير**) in **ٱضۡرِبُوهُ** (strike him) cannot be anything other than that slain person, and that what is meant by the pronoun in **بِبَعۡضِهَا** (its portion) cannot be anything other than that cow which was slaughtered as stated in the previous verses. But it is, surely, natural that those who have made it their habit to misinterpret all extraordinary signs, such as life returning to the dead body when struck with a portion of the cow, shut their eyes to such facts, and try to be satisfied by saying some specious arguments or other. These are some examples of the misinterpretations and specious arguments that they are seen to have adopted, arguing that there is no connection between the slaughtering of the cow and the killing, and that the two are separate events, in order to do away with the extraordinariness of this event:

(1) What they make the sentence **كَذَٰلِكَ يُحۡيِ ٱللَّهُ ٱلۡمَوۡتَىٰ** (it is in that manner that Allah brings the dead to life) here mean is indeed that it is in that manner that matters of death are detected and that, through that, the life of men is protected. The proof they show for this is that the word **يُحْيِي** (bring to life) has come in the Quran at 8:24 in the sense of uplifting men, and at 57:17 in the sense of uplifting the earth. In this surah and elsewhere too, Allah has quoted some other events of bringing to life after death. In all those places, too, they have not failed to make misinterpretations like this. If one sees some of their statements, it will seem that there are no words in the Arabic language such that – other than in some figurative meaning (**المَعْنَى المجازى** ) – the meaning 'bring to life after death' properly comes from them !

There is no dispute that, not only in the two places mentioned above but in some other places too, Allah has used the word **إحْيَاء** (bringing to life) in the Quran in the sense of uplifting. But, in all those places, there will be some marks (**قرينة** ) showing that it is not the actual meaning (**المعنى الحقيقى** ) that is intended, and that it is the figurative meaning that is intended. It is indeed an accepted principle in the science of literature (**علم البلاغة** ) that only when there is a special mark showing that it is not its actual meaning that is intended for any word, is it permissible to assign it a figurative meaning appropriate to the context. They too will presumably agree that Allah has the power to bring the dead to life in this very world. But it is because they are events beyond the laws of nature well known to us that they are induced to misinterpret such events. But that it is as merely extraordinary special signs, beyond the generally known laws of nature, that Allah quotes such events in the Quran – *\[p. 293\]* that fact too must be remembered.

The two Quranic verses they pointed out are these: **يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱسۡتَجِيبُواْ لِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ إِذَا دَعَاكُمۡ لِمَا يُحۡيِيكُمۡ** (O you who have believed, if the Rasul calls you to the thing that gives you life, respond to Allah and to him (Anfal : 24) **أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ يُحۡيِ ٱلۡأَرۡضَ بَعۡدَ مَوۡتِهَا** (Surely, Allah gives life to the earth after its death – after its lifeless state –. (Hadid :17) The first verse is one that addresses people who are alive. Everyone knows that it is not to the matter of bringing them to life again after their death – it is to the matter necessary to uplift them in their life – that the Rasul invites them. In the same way, everyone can know that in the second verse what is meant by the death and life of the earth is not death and life of exactly the same kind as those of living creatures, that what is meant by its death is its lying barren so that produce is not produced in it, and that what is meant by its life is that state changing and vegetable and other produce being produced in it. In Su. Qaf 11 Allah has said that with rain-water We gave life to a dead – lifeless – country (**وَأَحۡيَيۡنَا بِهِۦ بَلۡدَةٗ مَّيۡتٗا**). No one has any doubt that the purport of having said 'gave life' in all these places is just that. Here, on the other hand, it is in open language that Allah has said 'bring the dead to life'. Therefore no other figurative meanings at all can be given here.

(2) These people, who argue that the slaughtering of the cow and the killing are two separate events – not connected with each other – have at last had to say that the history of slaughtering a cow comes here too. They also say that the event stated in the previous verses was in order to remove devotion to the cow from the minds of the Israelites, that the second was in order to detect the murderer, and that the word **ٱضۡرِبُوهُ بِبَعۡضِهَا** (strike him with its portion) is an indication pointing to this second one. It can, surely, be asked: whatever is meant by the cow's portion, how can the slayer be known when the slain man is struck with it? Their reply is this : The people suspected of having committed the murder must say on oath, placing their hand on the body of the slaughtered cow, 'We have not done it, we do not know about it'. Then, if the real culprit, or those who know about him, are in that group, they will confess the truth. It was a tradition of sacrificial rite that existed in that period for detecting a crime of murder in which it is not known who the murderer is. Because of devotion to the cow and the trembling produced in the mind through the sacrificial rite, the culprit will come forward to confess the crime. Although what has been said here may be counted as a principle of psychology, not even an indication of it is seen in that sentence that Allah said.

*\[p. 294\]* (3) What gave these people the impetus to speak about two events of slaughtering a cow, and what they put forward as support for themselves, are two statements in the Bible. The 1st is from Numbers 19, at 1–10. A red heifer without blemish and defect, and on which a yoke has not been placed, must be slaughtered. The priests must take a little of its blood and sprinkle it toward the tent. It must be kept for the congregation of the children of Yisrayel, for the water of purification. It is a sin-sacrifice. This is the summary of what was said there. If this is taken at face value, will what results from this sacrifice be the destroying of devotion to the cow from the minds of the Israelites, or will it be the strengthening of it? Think it over! It is clear from the 97th verse of Su. Ta-Ha that what happened was that the golden calf they had worshipped before was burnt and scattered in the sea. On the contrary, what the Bible (Exodus 32 : 20) says is that it was burnt and made into ashes, scattered on water, and the children of Yisrayil were made to drink it. We have no doubt that this too is an entirely unbelievable quotation like this.

The 2nd statement of the Bible is that in Deuteronomy 21, at 1–9. If it happens that the one who committed the crime of murder is not identified, the elders and the judges together must measure the distance to the town. The elders of the nearest town in the surroundings where the slain man lies must break the neck there of a heifer that has not been made to work and on which a yoke has not been placed. Then the priests must go and settle every litigation and brawl. All the elders of that town must wash their hands over the heifer and must say: we have not shed that blood; our eyes have not seen it either. Then that murder will be absolved. This is the summary of that statement. Other than having been described as a procedure that the Israelites are to observe, it has not been stated in it that such an event took place, or that a murderer could be identified through it. In that situation, no justification at all for these people's argument is to be had from this.

Someone may doubt: in the situation where the Quran has stated that the event of slaughtering a cow and so on in order to identify the slayer in a murder case happened among the Israelites, why is such an event not seen in the Bible? Not only this one event that the Quran has stated about the Israelites, but some other events too are not seen in the Bible. There is no way to put it down that none of that happened because it is not seen in the Bible. We can only say that it may have been included among what Allah stated: **يُحَرِّفُونَ ٱلۡكَلِمَ عَن مَّوَاضِعِهِۦ وَنَسُواْ حَظّٗا مِّمَّا ذُكِّرُواْ بِهِۦ** (they make changes to the words from their proper places, and they have forgotten a portion of what they were exhorted with. (Ma'idah : 13) **والله أعلم** From these three examples themselves the hollowness of these people's argument can, surely, be understood.

*\[Verse 74\]*

*\[p. 295\]* This verse is a protest about the hearts of them – the Israelites – not changing even after enjoying so many favours as were stated over many past verses, and seeing and experiencing very many signs. In spite of all this, your hearts have hardened firmly like rock so that no change of mind comes to you. Neither good counsels nor warnings enter your hearts. Nor do you think about the truth and right guidance. Nor do you consider your own ways of deliverance. In reality, it is not enough to say 'like rock'; it has to be said that your hearts are harder than it. Because even in rocks some slackening and movement does occur. From inside some rocks springs and streams are wont to flow. Some stones split open and through them *\[p. 296\]* water is wont to come out. Some others are wont to fall downward from above. Your hearts, on the other hand, have become entirely inert, without any kind of change occurring. Woe! One thing you must remember : none at all of your deeds goes without Allah knowing or observing it. The gist is that you will have to experience the recompense of all this from Him.

Everyone knows that streams and springs flow from rocks, that water comes out, and that huge rocks move from their places and roll down. But what is the purport of having said that they fall downward because of fearing Allah (**مِنۡ خَشۡيَةِ ٱللَّهِ** )? It may be asked: rocks, surely, do not have intellect and mind. To this, replies have been given in more than one way. In reality, in the situation where a reply to this is obtained from the Quran itself, there is no need to search for other opinions. Allah says:

**تُسَبِّحُ لَهُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتُ ٱلسَّبۡعُ وَٱلۡأَرۡضُ وَمَن فِيهِنَّ وَإِن مِّن شَيۡءٍ إِلَّا يُسَبِّحُ بِحَمۡدِهِۦ وَلَٰكِن لَّا تَفۡقَهُونَ تَسۡبِيحَهُمۡ**

“The seven skies and the earth and those in them perform tasbih (singing of praise) to Him. There is not a single thing at all that does not perform tasbih praising Him. But you do not comprehend their tasbih.” (Isra' : 44) It is, surely, clear from this that Allah has placed in each thing some awarenesses and qualities suited to each. Then it can be understood that whatever the special features are that Allah intends from rocks and that He has deposited in them, that will become manifest from them. How it is, in what manner it is – none of that can we know. Allah alone knows. In any case, it must not be disregarded as a mere figure of speech, as some understand it.

*\[Verses 75–77\]*

*\[p. 298\]* Allah is informing the true believers that there is no room for the hope that that Jewish race – whose hearts became corrupted and hardened and numb like rocks, as stated in the previous verses – will accept what you say and become true believers. They knew in plenty, from the Torah and elsewhere, about a prophet who was to come and about his qualities. They know the real principles and laws of the religion more or less. It was also their habit to tell all that openly to the Arabs. When the noble Prophet ﷺ came as a prophet, what arose in them was jealousy, enmity and hatred. This contradiction is not something that arose in them newly. Misinterpreting and altering, for selfish ends, the scriptural sentences and divine counsels that are the words of Allah, even after having heard them properly, understood them and grasped their meaning, is their habit from before. Therefore the purport is that you need not think that they will now come forward to accept the truth, setting aside that tradition.

There were some hypocrites among the Jews. When they come to the Muslims they will act as though they were people who had accepted true faith. Sometimes they will also tell the Muslims openly some truths concerning the Prophet ﷺ. When they leave there and go to their own people, these will be subjected to their reproach. Are you explaining to them these secrets that Allah has revealed to you? Do you remember what its consequence will be? It will become the cause for them to argue with you before Allah with those matters and make you culprits; do you not understand any of this? – in such words they will admonish them in private. If one sees this admonishing of theirs, it seems, surely, that Allah cannot know their secrets and covert activities at all. The question in the 77th verse, 'Are they not aware of the fact that Allah knows all their secret and open things?!', points its finger at this foolishness of theirs.

*\[Verses 78–79\]*

*\[p. 299\]* Among the People of the Scripture there are two kinds of people. One: the common people who do not know writing and reading. They will not have learned the scripture and come to know it. As far as they are concerned, religion will be only some delusions and conjectures. Only some delusions, such as that on the basis of hearsay and of the statements of the scholar-priests such-and-such good things and merits are going to be obtained for us, and some conjectures and suppositions or other that religious tenets will be in such-and-such a manner, will be in their possession. That will be their religious authority. In not having enquired and come to know, and in not having thought, these are indeed culprits. There is another group: the scholar-priests. It is the case of these that is very woeful. They know about the scripture. But in the name of Allah and of religion they will fabricate lies and injustice. Not only by mouth – they will also write books and propagate them. Through this their aim is nothing other than to gain worldly selfish benefits. That is, their work will be to exploit the people through it and to gain wealth and influence. It is as religious books and as principles from Allah that they will present before the public those writings that they wrote themselves. Each activity of theirs, and each gain obtained from it, will end for them in woe, ruin and punishment.

Some riwayahs can be seen showing that it was the habit of the Jews to write some books containing many artificial accounts and interpretations about the coming of the noble Prophet ﷺ, his characteristics, and some true realities that he *\[p. 300\]* preaches, and to propagate them among the common people among them and among the Arabs. That can be understood from Allah's statement itself too. Besides, the existing Bible itself also bears witness to the fact that, apart from the real scriptures, some individuals from among them composed books and writings, and that they came to be counted as scriptures among them.

Although this verse came down reproaching the Jews, there is no doubt that this warning applies in its very literal sense to all scholar-priests who carry on propaganda in the name of religion – through books or otherwise – and make capital of it, misinterpreting the Quran and the words of the Prophet, altering and twisting the accepted principles and laws of Islam, and presenting artificial proofs and arguments for it. May Allah protect and save us. Amin
