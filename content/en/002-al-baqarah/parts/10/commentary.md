*\[Verses 58–59\]*

*\[p. 268\]* There are opinions that what is meant by **هَٰذِهِ ٱلۡقَرۡيَةَ** (this country) is Bait al-Muqaddas (\*), that it is Ariha (\*\*), and so on. It is said in Su. Ma'idah that Musa (a) said thus to them: **يَٰقَوۡمِ ٱدۡخُلُواْ ٱلۡأَرۡضَ ٱلۡمُقَدَّسَةَ ٱلَّتِي كَتَبَ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمۡ** (My people, enter the holy land that Allah has appointed for you. Ma'idah 21) The likelihood is that this most probably refers to Bait al-Muqaddas. Therefore many of the main Quran commentators are of the view that it is Bait al-Muqaddas that is meant here. Ariha, also, is not situated on the way going from Egypt to Bait al-Muqaddas. That country (\*\*\*) is across, beyond it. Nevertheless, it is said that it was Ariha that they entered first, that it was then that it was said to enter doing sujud, and that it was afterwards that they entered Bait al-Muqaddas (Allah knows) This matter is not seen in the Bible.

> **Note:** (\*) What are called Yerushalem, Jarusalam (Jerusalem), al-Quds al-Sharif (**اورشليم، القدس الشريف**) and so on refer to Bait al-Muqaddas (**بَيْتُ المُقَدَس**).

> **Note:** (\*\*) Ariha (**أرِيحَاء**) or Yeriho (Jericho): this is a town on the western side of the valley of the river Jordan (**الاردن**), five nazhika north of the northern border of the Dead Sea (Bahr Lut). *\[DOUBT: നാഴിക is printed; as a measure of distance it is commonly taken as a "mile", but the page does not say which measure is meant. Left as "nazhika".\]* (Ve.Pu.Ni) *\[DOUBT: the source is given only as the abbreviation വേ.പു.നി; it is most likely വേദപുസ്തക നിഘണ്ടു ("Bible dictionary", cited by that name in part 06), but the page does not expand it.\]*

> **Note:** (\*\*\*) See map number 2.

When they were commanded to wage war on the ruffian race called the 'Amaliqah, who were there, in order to enter that holy land in which the prophets Ibrahim, Ishaq and Ya'qub (a), the forefathers of the Israelites, had been dwelling, and which had been promised to them, they refused it and showed cowardice. As its result they had to drift about in the Sinai desert for forty years. Until the time of the Prophet Musa *\[p. 269\]* (a) had passed, they were not able to enter there. Afterwards it was under the leadership of the Prophet Yusha' (a) (\*), his successor and the man who held their leadership, that they conquered that country and entered it. They – who had wandered about in the desert for forty years, had grown weary of eating the same food, as seen in the 61st verse, and were eager to go to a region that has fruits and streams – had been commanded that, when they entered that fertile region, their passing through its gateway must be with heads lowered, bowing, as people expressing gratitude and humility to Allah, and that they must say hittatun (**حِطَّةٌ**). Allah also informed them that then He would forgive their faults and offences, and would give more good to those who do well. The meaning of the word **حِطَّةٌ** is to lower, or to put down. What is intended is: put down – that is, forgive – our sin for us. What has been reported – that the noble Prophet ﷺ entered Makkah on the day of the conquest of Makkah with very humble devotion, and that as soon as he entered he bathed, took wudu' and prayed – is worth remembering here.

> **Note:** (\*) The name of the Prophet Yusha' (**يوشع - ع**) is found in the Bible as Joshua.

But what they did was the contrary. They – if not all of them, a section of them – entered beating the drum of victory arrogantly. Not only did they not say the word that they were commanded to say; in its place they also said another word. It has come in a hadith narrated by Bukhari (r) and others that they shuffled along on their buttocks and said **حنطة** (a grain of wheat) in place of saying **حِطَّةٌ** (forgiveness of sins). In brief, it was directly contrary to what they were commanded. For this defiance Allah sent down a severe punishment on them. What the said punishment was has not been made clear here. It is said that it was plague or some great disease like it. (Allah knows)

It is good to remember one fact. It can be seen stated here and there in the Quran thus: He sent down punishment, sent down manna and salwa, sent down iron, sent down clothing. In all these places, what can be seen used is the verb **اَنْزَلَ**, which has the meaning 'sent down'. Although its linguistic meaning is indeed 'sent down from above downwards', in all these places it has been said not in that sense – it has been said in the sense 'Allah gave, or produced and gave'. When one says the Quran was sent down, the rain was sent down, and so on, what is meant is indeed 'sent down from above to the earth'. Imam Raghib (r) has specifically mentioned this fact in his lexicon, with examples.

### Section – 7

*\[Verse 60\]*

*\[p. 270\]* Just as He sent down manna and salwa for food in the period of living in the desert, this is a great favour that Allah did for them for the need of water, and at the same time also a mighty wondrous sign. When they faced hardship for water, Musa (a) prayed. Allah commanded to strike a rock with his staff. At once, twelve springs burst out from it and flowed. The Israelites were, surely, twelve tribes. So that there would be no crowding and quarrelling among them, Allah made a spring flow separately for each tribe. It was also specifically determined which one was each tribe's.

It was on the eastern side of the Red Sea, a little distance from the town of Suez (**سويس**). Although the twelve spring-channels are not clearly seen today, *\[p. 271\]* some of them are still flowing and trickling today it is by the name **عُيُون مُوسى** (the springs of Musa) that it is known even today. Making use of this water, there is also some date-palm cultivation existing there. It is customary for travellers to go and visit that place. This too is indeed one of the superhuman signs that Allah manifested at the hand of Musa (a) through his staff. The staff that changed into a great serpent before Fir'aun, and the staff by which, when the sea was struck, its water split, are that very one. Among Allah's creatures, all that He wills will happen. In that, neither the laws of science nor the inference of reason can say anything.

*\[Verse 61\]*

*\[p. 272\]* This is another example of the ingratitude and the obstinacy of the Israelites. Without being satisfied with the manna and salwa that they had been receiving in plenty without labour, they complained to Musa (a) that it is not possible for us to live on this one food, that we must have grain, vegetables and so on produced for us, and that he must pray for that. Many things like this have, surely, become possible for them as the result of his prayer. *\[p. 273\]* The Bible quotes this matter thus: 'Then the mixed multitude among them became greedy. The children of Yisra'il, weeping again: who will give us meat to eat? We remember the fish, the cucumbers, the pumpkins, the onions, the red onions and the chuttulli (garlic) that we used to eat in Mizrayim (in Egypt) without price. But now our soul is parched. There is nothing to be seen except this manna. So they said' (Numbers 11: 4–7) *\[DOUBT: ചുറ്റുള്ളി is printed, with the author's own gloss (വെളുത്തുള്ളി, "garlic"); the English word for ചുറ്റുള്ളി itself is not certain (perhaps "leeks"), so it is left as "chuttulli".\]*

In a tone of protest Musa (a) said to them: 'Without being satisfied with this excellent food – which keeps coming to you as you like without labour, which Allah has graciously given you as a special favour, and which is tasty and delightful – is your demand that, in place of it, you must have the very food of low grade that is obtained only by toiling on the earth and that everyone generally uses? Astonishing indeed! If that is so, go from this desert to some city or other and stay there. Then all these demands of yours will be met' – and so on. But are they ready for that! Had they been ready, they would not, surely, have had to wander about in that desert.

The gist of saying **وَضُرِبَتۡ عَلَيۡهِمُ ٱلذِّلَّةُ وَٱلۡمَسۡكَنَةُ** (abasement and degradation were struck upon them) is that, as if a tent had been pitched over them enclosing them inside, abasement and degradation have covered them over without leaving them. That is, the purport is that they have degenerated into a community without courage and valour, without self-respect and dignity. The purport of saying **وَبَآءُو بِغَضَبٖ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ** (and they returned with wrath from Allah) is that all that they were able to gain in their life's journey is only the wrath of Allah. What is meant is not that they became the object of Allah's curse and wrath because they said that it is not possible for us to get along with a single food. There, Allah Himself has pointed out the reasons for it in the two sentences that follow. It is because they had been disbelieving in Allah's signs and killing prophets without justification. It is because they showed disobedience and kept committing transgression. The reason for it is that denying the signs, murdering the prophets, not obeying Allah's commandments and committing transgression all became their habit. When looked at as a religious community, the history of the Jews in these matters is crueller than the history of any other community.

Of these four reasons that Allah pointed out, the things other than murdering the prophets are ones that had already begun among them from the very time of the Prophet Musa (a), and afterwards kept increasing. Killing the prophets, on the other hand, began some time *\[p. 274\]* after the Prophet Musa (a). So it is clear from this too that it was not in the time of Musa (a), and that it was in later times, that the Jews became a community stamped – cursed – with Allah's curse and wrath. From this it can be understood that there is no inconsistency between this and Allah's having said before this about their earlier generations **فَضَّلۡتُكُمۡ عَلَى ٱلۡعَٰلَمِينَ** (We have made you superior to the people of the world). The Jewish community has not had a history of having lived in the world otherwise than as abject and base – as a community with dignity and self-respect. What there is to mention as an apparent exception to this is only the state of Isra'il that they established in Palestine a few years ago. Its founding and its continued existence – as a state, the most hated among the states of the world – do not in reality stand on its own feet; it is the selfish interests and the help of some great powers that are behind it. (We can speak more about this in the commentary on the 112th verse of Al 'Imran **إن شاء الله**)

Many examples of the disobedience, the transgression and the not believing in signs of the Israelites can be seen in the Quran. But, apart from its having been said in general that they used to kill prophets, no such event has been stated in the Quran. It is not seen in strong hadiths either. At the same time, in their scripture that exists in their hands – in the Bible – there are, moreover, many examples of it. When they are examined, it can be seen – as can be understood from the 87th verse below – that they have a veritable tradition in carrying out cruel persecutions against the prophets and in killing them brutally. Let us quote here, in brief, some examples from the Bible:

### Section – 8

*\[Verse 62\]*

The Jews and the Christians are wont to say that the heirs of Paradise are they alone. (See the 111th verse) What Allah does through this verse is to refute this monopoly claim based on sheer sectarianism. The gist is: without difference of race – as those who believed in the Prophet ﷺ, or Jews, or Christians, or Sabians, or anything else – whoever truly believes in Allah and the Last Day and does the good deeds based on that belief, it is they who have salvation and success. The criterion of salvation and success is not sectarian or communal. In brief: it is belief and good deeds.

Here there is room for a question: as far as the Jews, the Christians and so on, who do not believe in the Prophet ﷺ, are concerned, what was said is clear. But as far as those who believe in the Prophet ﷺ and keep doing *\[p. 276\]* good deeds are concerned, what is the purport of saying 'if they believe and do good deeds'? How will those who have once believed afterwards believe anew? This is the question. If one thinks a little, it can be seen that there is no substance in this question. When it is said that for anyone at all there is deliverance only through true faith and good deeds, its meaning will be that those who have been practising both must stand firm in that same state without deviating from it, and that those who have not practised both must begin practising them. The meaning is not, otherwise, that those who have once believed must again adopt a new belief.

Some people who have hidden crooked interests and un-Islamic attitudes in their minds are wont to be seen misinterpreting this verse and the 72nd verse of Su. Ma'idah, which is of the same kind. *\[DOUBT: the verse number is printed clearly as 72. In the Quran text used here, Al-Ma'idah 72 begins لَقَدۡ كَفَرَ ٱلَّذِينَ قَالُوٓاْ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ ٱلۡمَسِيحُ, while Al-Ma'idah 69 has the same wording as this verse (believers, Jews, Sabians, Christians … whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day). Translated as printed; please decide whether to note 69.\]* The summary of their interpretation is that there is no need to accept the religion of Islam itself, there is no objection whichever religion one adopts – if one believes in God and in the life of the Hereafter and does good things, anyone will obtain deliverance and salvation. What they do through this is to argue that these two Quranic verses support a doctrine of no-religion known by the name 'the doctrine of the truth of all religions'. If there were not in the Quran verses in great numbers explaining, about both the things – belief in Allah and the Last Day, and good deeds – how they must all be, and if it came about that there are in the Quran only these two verses that state about the two of them, we too could have conceded that this interpretation of theirs is right in a way. When the Quran says 'belief' and 'good deeds' in one place without conditions, it is certain that it must be in a meaning that takes into account all the explanations and conditions that have been given for those two in other places in the Quran.

So, whatever and however the belief and good deeds are that the Quran accepts and envisages – that itself is what is meant by those two, here and anywhere else in the Quran. The purport of this is not, otherwise, that the Quran accepts belief and good deeds in the sense that the people of each religion have been accepting. Even the followers of a religion founded on sheer idol-worship and on the manifest doctrine of many gods claim that they are believers in God. Although the people of most religions are wont to say that they believe in life after death, each group's belief is of its own kind. What one group considers a sin, another group observes as a meritorious act. For example, the people of some other religions count as the greatest meritorious act things like the worship of creatures, which the Quran has made clear are sheer shirk and kufr and are unforgivable. This being so, *\[p. 277\]* need it be said that that interpretation – by which it comes about that all of these will be successful and people of salvation in the view of the Quran – is entirely un-Islamic, ill-intentioned and meaningless?! To put it briefly, the purport of these verses is only that there is deliverance only for those who adopt the belief and good deeds that Islam accepts and the Quran envisages – it is not the monopoly of any community or race. He has also declared in clear language that no religion other than Islam is acceptable with Allah. **وَمَن يَبۡتَغِ غَيۡرَ ٱلۡإِسۡلَٰمِ دِينٗا فَلَن يُقۡبَلَ مِنۡهُ وَهُوَ فِي ٱلۡأٓخِرَةِ مِنَ ٱلۡخَٰسِرِينَ** (If anyone seeks a religion other than Islam, it will not be accepted from him indeed. And he will be among the losers in the Hereafter.(3:85) Belief and good deeds that are not those of Islam will not, indeed, be belief and good deeds in the language of the Quran.

There are opinions that what is meant by the Sabians is those who, having abandoned the Jewish religion and the Christian religion, have been worshipping stars or angels (goddesses and gods), and so on. There is no particular section known by this name now. The literal meaning of that word is 'those who have changed religion'.
