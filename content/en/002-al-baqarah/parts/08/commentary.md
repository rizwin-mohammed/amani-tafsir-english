*\[Verses 42–43\]*

*\[p. 247\]* The two methods that people who lead others astray use for it are: mixing together the real and the unreal – truth and untruth – and making the two unrecognisable, and covering up the real; the first will mostly be used with those who have some little knowledge, and the second with the common folk who have no knowledge. As far as the People of the Scripture are concerned, because they have a connection with the scripture, the Jews – the scholars among them especially – used to employ the first method to lead them astray, and, as far as the Arabs are concerned, because they have no acquaintance with the scripture, the second method to lead them astray. These two methods that they are wont to use to turn people back from accepting *\[p. 248\]* true faith were frauds carried out while knowing full well what the truth is. That is why it was said not to do so while knowing it full well. If it had been because of not recognising the truth, there would have been for it at least a nominal justification. Since it is deliberately hiding the truth, what happens is that the guilt doubles.

The fact that much has been foretold in the Torah about the coming and the special qualities of the noble Prophet ﷺ was widely known, in a way that the Jews could not deny. They, for their part, were also waiting impatiently for the coming of a prophet. But, for the reason that that prophet arose from among the Arabs, whom they had been counting as a people lower than themselves, what they did was to nurse envy and enmity. With that, they were prompted to interpret those truths, which they themselves had been propagating before, by twisting them, to add adulteration to them, and to cover them up without speaking out openly. It is about this that it has been stated mainly in the first verse.

Prayer and Zakat had been ordained among the Jews too. Only, there would have been some difference in the modes of performance. But in both matters – just like the Muslim community of today – their stance too was very deplorable. The acts of prayer had come to be not performed as they should be, or not at all. Even among those who performed it, it had also turned into just a ceremony. The matter of Zakat need not even be mentioned. There is no other community that has kept up a tradition of miserliness in spending wealth as severe as the Jews. Not only miserliness: greed for wealth too had become their innate qualities. It is keeping facts like these in view that they are commanded to establish the prayer and to give Zakat. Those very things which you are bound to carry out through your scripture are what Muhammad, the Prophet ﷺ, too counsels. Therefore you too, joining together with him and with those who have believed in him, keep performing the act of prayer. Such indeed is the purport of the second verse.

**رُكُوع** (ruku') means 'to bow' to bend down, to lower the head' and so on: that is the literal meaning. Ruku', sujud and standing (**الرُّكُوعْ وَالسُّجُود والْقِيَامْ**) are, surely, the main component in prayer; in that respect these three words are used in the Quran and elsewhere meaning prayer.

*\[Verse 44\]*

*\[p. 249\]* The reproach is not in counselling others to do good deeds. That is a good thing indeed. Counsellors are those who ought to show an example in the matter on which they give counsel; the reproach is about not practising oneself the thing one counsels others to do, and at the same time posing as followers of the preachers of the scripture. *\[DOUBT: printed വേദഗ്രന്ഥത്തിന്റെ പ്രബോധകരുടെ അനുയായികളുമായി ("as followers of the preachers of the scripture"), translated as printed. The -ഉം on അനുയായികളുമായി may suggest that "as preachers and followers of the scripture" (പ്രബോധകരും) was meant.\]* This verse indicates that this is a senseless practice and that it is not fitting for those who have sense. Although this reproach is aimed at the Jewish scholars, the principle it contains is indeed applicable to all religious counsellors. The true believers must, surely, keep especially free from all the things stated in the form of reproach about the People of the Scripture.

Allah says:- **يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ لِمَ تَقُولُونَ** ...**الصف** – (O you who have believed, why do you say what you do not do?! That you say what you do not do is a thing that provokes very great wrath with Allah. (Saff: 2, 3) It has come in more than one hadith, narrated by Ahmad (r) and others, that on the night of the 'Mi'raj' the noble Prophet ﷺ saw the tongues and lips of some people being cut with scissors of fire, and that when he inquired about it, Jibril (a) said thus: 'These are the orators in your community who counsel people to do good deeds and forget their own selves', there are many other hadiths too that can be seen on this subject.

*\[Verses 45–46\]*

*\[p. 250\]* That they must believe in the Prophet ﷺ and in the Quran, must stop the practice of leading people astray, must perform things such as prayer and Zakat, and must also themselves keep the counsel with which they counsel others – many such things were stated in the past verses, addressing the Israelites. It is, surely, natural that they should feel difficulty in many respects in leaving the traditional ways that they have been observing for a long time and adopting a new course of conduct different from them. It is as a remedy for this distress of mind that they are commanded to take up patience and prayer. If patience and forbearance are adopted in all kinds of distress of mind and in straits, it produces the capacity to stand against adverse circumstances, and with that the distress of mind in leading an Islamic life goes away. Making prayer a practice and a regular habit, for its part, increases awareness of Allah and God-fearing reverence, and procures spiritual and mental strength and purity. In that way this will become a cause for receiving help and aid from Allah. Such is the purport of saying to seek help through patience and prayer.

This great counsel too, although it is addressed to the Israelites, is applicable to all true believers as well. In the 153rd verse of this same surah Allah says: **يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ ٱسۡتَعِينُواْ بِٱلصَّبۡرِ وَٱلصَّلَوٰةِ** **الخ**.(O you who have believed, seek help through patience and prayer; surely Allah is with those who are patient). In another *\[p. 251\]* place He says:- **إِنَّمَا يُوَفَّى ٱلصَّـٰبِرُونَ أَجۡرَهُم بِغَيۡرِ حِسَابٖ** (To the patient their reward will be paid in full without reckoning, indeed. Zumar: 10) About prayer He says: (**وَأَقِمِ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَۖ إِنَّ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ تَنۡهَىٰ عَنِ ٱلۡفَحۡشَآءِ وَٱلۡمُنكَرِ** **العنكبوت** –.....And establish the prayer. Surely the prayer restrains from shameful conduct and from what is forbidden. (Ankabut: 45) Ibn Hatim (r) has narrated that 'Umar (r) stated: Patience is of two kinds: patience in calamities – this is good indeed. Better than that is the patience to keep away from forbidden things. *\[DOUBT: the name is printed ഇബ്നുഹാതിം (Ibn Hatim) and is translated as printed; whether another name (such as Ibn Abi Hatim) is meant cannot be confirmed from the page.\]* What has come in the hadith narrated by Ahmad, Abu Dawud (r) and others – that when any uneasiness befell the noble Prophet ﷺ, it was his habit to turn quickly to prayer – is especially worth mentioning here.

The majority of commentators say that what is meant by the pronoun 'it (**هَا**)' in the sentence **وَإِنَّهَا لَكَبِيرَةٌ** (Surely it is a great – heavy – thing indeed) is the prayer (**الصَّلاةِ**), mentioned just before it. When the outward form of the words is considered, it is that, too, which has more justification. What others are of the opinion is that what is meant by it is seeking help (**الا ستعانة**) through patience and prayer. Either way, the purport of the sentence is clear indeed.

When Allah Himself has stated that the devout (**ٱلۡخَٰشِعِينَ**) are those who have the awareness and the thought that they will have to meet Allah and return to Him, that word now needs no explanation. Those who have this awareness, for their part, will, surely, have as their sole aim the pleasure and the rewards of Allah. For that they will also be ready to bear hardships and losses and to embrace sacrifice. **ومن الله التوفيق**.

### Section – 6

*\[Verses 47–48\]*

*\[p. 252\]* Allah is inviting the Israelites to true faith and to right guidance, reminding them again, once more, of the favours that Allah has been giving them, and calling to their minds the excellences He gave them above other communities. Through this He also awakens them to the fact that it is you who ought to come forward for it, more than all other people. When the Israelites became a community as the followers of a religion, they had excellence in many respects above the other communities of that time. Ever so many prophets arose from among them. Very many kings too arose among them. In that way, religious leadership and leadership in government came into their hands. It was they who had the good fortune to be the followers of the Torah, the scripture and code of law that deserves the highest place among the earlier scriptures. They saw and experienced numerous signs and favours, such as can be seen in the verses below. To put it briefly, leadership and glory in the religious field and in the worldly field belonged to the Israelites.

By saying 'I made you superior to the people of the world', it does not mean that He made them superior to all communities of all times, or that He made them superior even to the community of the noble Prophet *\[p. 253\]* ﷺ. About the community of the Prophet ﷺ Allah has said, surely, **كُنتُمۡ خَيۡرَ أُمَّةٍ أُخۡرِجَتۡ لِلنَّاسِ**......**لخ** (You are the best community that has been raised up for mankind (3:110). Allah has also pointed out there the reasons for it.

After reminding them of the favours and the excellences given to them, He warns them that they have now thrown away all those good traditions, that in that way they have become subject to Allah's wrath and punishment, and that therefore they must beware of the consequences they are going to experience on the Day of Qiyamah. He has also reminded them especially of four things about the Day of Qiyamah.

(1) On that day no one will avail anyone.

(2) On that day no intercession whatever is acceptable.

(3) On that day no atonement will be accepted. That is, it is not possible to escape by giving something in its place so as to get free from punishment.

(4) More than that, there will be no help of any kind for them to receive on that day.

Yes, each one will experience only the result of what he himself has earned and stored up; no one will bear another's guilt (6:164, 35:18, 39:7) for each one, his own affair itself will be plenty (80:37), neither will parents avail children, nor children parents (31:33). None of the intercessions of intercessors will be of effect there (74:48). Who is there fit to intercede with Him without Allah's permission ?! (2:255) Even if people who are disbelievers had all that is on the earth, and as much again along with it, and tried to get free from punishment by giving it as ransom, it would not be accepted (5:39) *\[DOUBT: തെണ്ടം, here and in the word table of verse 48 (പ്രായശ്ചിത്തം,തെണ്ടം), is printed clearly. From the context it means something given to be let off punishment; rendered "ransom". Please confirm the meaning of this word.\]* Can one escape by giving something in exchange, or in the name of a bond of love? Not that either (14:31). But can they help one another? Not that either. All will be subject to the command (37:25, 26).

We are the people of prophethood and of the scripture, Paradise is our monopoly, at most we shall have to lie in Hell only a few days, we are the children and the favourites of Allah – such were the things the Israelites kept claiming. Through this Allah is warning them that all these claims are sheer falsehood and meaningless, and that if this state of theirs continues, there is no possibility for them to obtain any way of escape on the Day of Qiyamah. In the verses that follow, Allah reminds them of some favours that their earlier generations experienced and of which they were eyewitnesses, and which these, who are the later generations, keep repeating with pride.

*\[Verses 49–50\]*

*\[p. 254\]* The events stated in these verses and in some of the verses that follow are some of the main events that took place in the history of the Israelites. Since they are known to the People of the Scripture and are described in the books in their possession, what Allah has done here is to enumerate them without any further explanation. About some of them, in some surahs such as Al-A'raf, Ta-Ha and Al-Qasas, a little has been *\[p. 255\]* explained. We shall see about it in the respective places, **إن شاء الله**. Let us also give, as is fitting, the explanation that is most necessary about the events stated only in this surah. **ومن الله التوفيق**.

As can be seen in Surat Yusuf, when the Prophet Yusuf (a) was the head of financial affairs in Egypt (in Misr), his father – the Prophet Ya'qub (a), called Isra'il – and his brothers went to Egypt and settled there. In more or less four hundred years that family multiplied and became a vast people. Their numbers and their traditional culture became intolerable to the Qibtis (Copts), the old native stock of Egypt. They let loose oppression and persecution against them. The government of the country was, surely, in their hands. Just as the rulers in Rome were known by the name Qaisar (Caesar), the rulers in Persia by Kisra (Khosrau), and the kings in Yemen by Tubba', the rulers in Egypt were known by the name Fir'aun (Pharaoh). In the time of one Fir'aun, the hostility toward the Israelites and the racial partiality reached their peak. They thought that if they were left in this state, the name and fame of the Qibtis would gradually suffer harm, and that it would lead to the government of the country being lost to them (\*) In that way they came to count the Israelites as their slaves and to treat them entirely as if they were animals. They became the prey of countless persecutions and torments. This matter is described in the Bible (in the book of Exodus, chapter 1). (We have quoted it in the commentary on the 4th verse of Su. Al-Qasas)

> **Note:** (\*) Even in this age, which is described as the civilised age, there are, surely, plenty of people and rulers who keep up this kind of racial mentality.

One of these persecutions was Fir'aun's command to kill at once the male infants born among the Israelites, and to spare the female infants. There were also officials specially appointed for it. Ever so many infants were slaughtered in that way, brutally. It was in this period that Musa (a) was born. Allah put it into the mind of his mother, as soon as she gave birth to him, to put the infant in a box and float it along the river Nile. In that way, when that infant, who was going to be the cause of the downfall of the Qibtis' glory, reached the bank of the river that flows past the gate of Fir'aun's palace, the people of the palace found him, and he was brought up as a child of the palace. (The explanation can be seen in Su. Ta-Ha and in Al-Qasas) Even when the Prophet Musa (a), having received prophethood and divine office (nubuwwah and risalah), was carrying on the task of preaching, Fir'aun's oppressions and persecutions grew strong again. *\[DOUBT: ദിവ്യത്വം (here paired with രിസാലത്ത്, "messengership") literally means "divinity" or "divine status"; rendered "divine office". Please confirm the intended sense.\]* Among them, the practice of killing the male infants of the Israelites was going on then too (a reference to this can be seen in Surah Al-A'raf 127–129) It is the deliverance He gave them from severe torments like these *\[p. 256\]* through Musa (a) that Allah reminds the Israelites of by saying **وَإِذۡ نَجَّيۡنَٰكُم مِّنۡ ءَالِ فِرۡعَوۡنَ**.... (Remember the occasion when We rescued you from the folk of Fir'aun......).

**بَلَآءٞ** (bala') means trial. Although this word is mostly used for evil, it is also used for a trial by means of good. Allah says: **وَنَبۡلُوكُم بِٱلشَّرِّ وَٱلۡخَيۡرِ فِتۡنَةٗ** – **الانبياء : ٣٥** (We will put you to trial by evil and by good) In the one by means of evil, what is meant by trial will be patience, and in the one by means of good, gratitude. Accordingly, the sentence **وَفِي ذَٰلِكُم بَلَآءٞ** (In that there was a trial) has been given an interpretation in two ways.

(1) That Fir'aun's folk made you suffer severe punishment and slaughtered your children was a trial to test your patience and forbearance.

(2) That He rescued you from Fir'aun's people was a trial to test your gratitude and loyalty.

It is the first interpretation that most commentators are seen to accept. Either way, it is clear, surely, that that trial was very mighty.

The summary of the event recalled in the second verse is this: When the Israelites had suffered Fir'aun's persecution until they were weary of it, Musa (a), by the command of Allah, set out at night taking them along and reached near the Red Sea. Musa (a) struck the sea with his staff. Then Allah split the sea water so that they could walk through. They reached the other shore between walls of water that stood apart on both sides and were saved. When Fir'aun and his army, who, learning that they had left the place, had been pursuing them, had gone down into the sea by that same passage, the sea water that stood dammed up on both sides came together, and all of them sank in it and perished. In reality this one single event was enough to make the Israelites sit up and think. But, in the course of time, apart from repeating that event over and over and taking pride in it, gratitude for it was not shown among them. The full account of this great event can be seen in Surat Ta-Ha and in Al-Shu'ara'. (About the new portrayal that some who have ventured to interpret the Quran according to rationalist arguments and crooked interests give to this event, and the misinterpretations they have given to many Quranic verses for that purpose, sufficient criticism has been made in the lengthy explanatory note given after Surat Ta-Ha.)

It can be seen in the books of hadith that Ibn 'Abbas (r) stated thus: *\[p. 257\]* When the noble Messenger ﷺ came to Madinah, he saw the Jews fasting on the day of 'Ashura' (the tenth of the month of Muharram). The noble Prophet asked what this day was. This is a good day; this is the day on which Allah rescued the Israelites from their enemy; on that day Musa (a) fasted – so they replied. Then the noble Prophet said:- Regarding Musa, I have more claim than you. So on that day the noble Prophet fasted and commanded fasting on that day. (Ah.; Bu.; Mu.; Na.; Ja.) It is according to this hadith that Muslims fast on the day of 'Ashura'. It is also generally known that it is part of the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.
