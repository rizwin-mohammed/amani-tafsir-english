*[Verse 275]*

*[p. 663]* After describing many things about almsgivings in the past few verses, it states about interest, which is contrary to them in every respect, both in this verse and in some verses that follow. If it is good qualities such as good intention, generosity, purity of heart, sacrifice, helping others, compassion for the needy, social good, that give inspiration to almsgivings, on the contrary it is bad qualities such as evil intention, crookedness of mind, selfishness, excessive yearning, exploitation, social injury, that are the prompters of interest. He who gives charity does not expect anything else in return for it. Even the sum given is lost to him. To the interest-lender, both the sum that he gave and, over and above, a fixed amount, come back. If the charitable man, reckoning the difficulty of others too like his own difficulty, helps them, what the interest-lender does, in the name of letting the wealth that remains over after his need is deducted go to others for a short time, is earning by exploiting the difficulties of the poor people. If he helps another from what he made by toiling, turning blood into water, this one squeezes out the toil of another, and sucks up his blood. Almsgivings represent humane good character. Along with that he also becomes a vessel for the pleasure and the recompense of Allah. Interest, for its part, satanic character. It will also become a cause for the curse and wrath of Allah and for punishment. Thus, from whatever side one looks, those two are indeed mutually contrary.

The usage **الَّذِينَ يَأْكُلُونَالرِّبَا** (those who eat interest) concerning those who use interest-property indicates Allah's hatred towards them. Among the benefits that come from wealth, the most principal is eating, surely. Besides- as Ibn Jarir (r) pointed out- when this verse came down, that was the very way of livelihood of the interest-traders who were there. The usage ‘eating wealth’, as indicative of reprehen*[p. 664]*sion, concerning those who exploit the wealth of others through ways that are forbidden and wrongful, can be seen elsewhere too in the Quran. Not only those who take interest; those also who take part in it in any way are offenders indeed. Jabir (r) says: ‘The Prophet ﷺ has cursed both the one who eats interest, and the one who feeds it, and its two witnesses, and its writer. He has also said that all of them are equal.’ (A; Mu; Da; Ti). That both helping and taking part in a matter that is ‘haram’ are haram indeed is indeed a general principle. But, although all are sharers in the offence, it need not be said that the offence of the one who takes interest will be more severe than that of the others. The matter that among the seven destructive great sins it is interest that the Prophet ﷺ has counted as one is also famous. (*) In another hadith the Prophet ﷺ has declared both that interest exists in seventy ways, and that the lightest among them is equal to a person marrying his mother. (Ja: Ba.)

> **Note:** (*) Shirk, sihr, unjust killing, eating interest, eating the wealth of orphans, retreating from battle, making an accusation of adultery about chaste believing women, these are those seven (Bu; Mu.)

**ربوا** (riba) is the root word that is translated as ‘interest’. (**) Its linguistic meaning is indeed ‘growth’ or ‘increase’. Interest is, surely, the gain that is obtained by the capital increasing and growing. Interest deals are by nature divided into two kinds. That is:

> **Note:** (**) This is written both **رِبَا** and **ربوا**. In the Mushafs it is **ربوا** that is usually written.

(1) The gain that is levied in the name of allowing a term to the debtor. This is known by the name **ربا النسيئة** (term-interest). In this kind, what was in vogue among the Arabs at that time, and what they had been reckoning as interest deals, were three types of deals.

**One:** after selling some article for a sum with a term set, if he who bought it does not settle the price at that term, one will both extend the term again and give it, and increase the sum of the price. If again the term has to be extended again and given, one will increase the sum again too.

**Two:** giving a loan with the determination that gain must be added and given at such-and-such a rate for so much time.

**Three:** both setting a fixed term for the loan, and determining that on that day so much of a sum must be added and given. In all this indeed it is clear that it is in the name of giving a term for the loan that an increase over the original sum is levied. If all the interest deals that exist today are analysed- if some stipulations and conditions that suit the times are left aside- they can be seen to be included in one type among these three.

(2) Like gold for gold, and wheat for wheat, when selling one article *[p. 665]* for an article belonging to the same kind (when exchanging), the extra gain that is collected. This is known by the name **رِبَا الفَضل** (surplus-interest). This is also called **رِبا النقد** (cash-interest). Because this surplus gain can on the face of it be considered trade profit, the Arabs had not been reckoning such deals as interest deals. Not only that, the principle of levying gain in the name of allowing a term to the debtor is not clear in this either, surely. That is why- as comes below- both some individuals among the Companions at first denied this kind of interest, and later, when knowledge came about the hadiths of the Prophet ﷺ, turned back from that opinion. It may be in order to remove that notion that the second kind of interest is also seen explained more in the hadiths. Allah knows.

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (r) has quoted as having been stated by the Prophet ﷺ: ‘Gold for gold, and silver for silver, and wheat for wheat, and barley for barley, and dried dates for dried dates, and salt for salt, (the selling) must be both as equal for equal, and as hand for hand. If anyone either gives more, or demands more, it has become interest. The one who takes and the one who gives are equal in this. (Bu; Mu.) In some wordings of the hadith there has come both ‘You must not bring about increase-and-decrease’, and in some wordings ‘If these species differ- if it is hand for hand (in cash)- sell as you intend’. It is to be stated that, although letter-differences and word-differences will be seen, many hadiths making this idea clear have been quoted by hadith scholars such as Bukhari, Muslim (r) through many routes, and by way of many Companions. Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (r) has again stated in this manner: ‘Bilal (r) brought to the Prophet ﷺ some Bani (the superior kind so called) dried dates. ‘Where (did you get) this from, the Prophet ﷺ asked. He said: ‘We had with us some inferior kind of dried dates. From that, it was bought at the rate of one ‘sa'’ for two ‘sa'’s’. Then the noble Prophet said: ‘Ah! Pure interest! Pure interest! Do not do so. In case you intend to buy, sell the dried dates as a separate sale. Then buy (the other) for that.’ (Bu; Mu.) Like this the Prophet ﷺ has also disallowed many other deals that become a way to the admixture of interest. They are plentiful in hadith books and elsewhere.

What has been reckoned as interest on the face of it is, surely, levying more than the principal sum in the name of allowing a time-limit. When selling an article for an article belonging to the same kind, both the seller or the buyer receiving a greater quantity, and allowing a term to one of the two, bring about for it a resemblance to interest deals. That is why it is in the manner that all deals which make it seem that the evil aspects contained in the interest deals that were in vogue at the time the Quran was coming down are there must be eradicated from Islam, that the Prophet ﷺ declared all such deals as interest de*[p. 666]*als. What the Prophet ﷺ has done by means of this is both giving an explanation of the interest that Allah has forbidden and of the deals in which interest comes about, and disallowing deals that have similarity with interest deals. Besides, an encouragement too is contained in this, that, when it becomes necessary for brothers to exchange things belonging to one and the same kind between themselves, keeping some need or other in front, they must do mutual help and cooperation, without venturing to receive a gain in its name. If it simply must be- as the Prophet ﷺ said in the incident of Bilal (r) quoted above and elsewhere- one can also, surely, sell the article in hand for a cash price and with that price buy separately the article that is needed. That at least by doing so Muslims need to be wholly pure from the tinge of interest is what becomes clear from what the Prophet ﷺ commanded to Bilal (r).

In the matter that, in the sale of the six things seen above that were specifically mentioned in the hadiths- if they are sold otherwise than in the manner stated in the hadith- interest will come about, there is no difference of opinion among the scholars who are predecessors and successors. The opinion of one section is that interest will come about only in those six. The opinion of the majority of scholars is that interest will come about in articles beyond those six too. (Detailed information is also to be known from the proper places.) Anyway, that surplus-interest ( **رِبا الفضل** ) too is forbidden in Islam is what is known undeniably from the hadiths. For no one who acknowledges the hadiths of the Prophet ﷺ as an authoritative source is there a way not to acknowledge it.

Concerning those who eat interest, **لا يَقُومُونَإِلا كَمَا يَقُومُ** …. *[DOUBT: the printed dots touch the left end of the Arabic, so on which side of it they stand cannot be told; they are placed after it here, since the phrase is cut off.]* that they will not rise except as he whom Satan throws down on account of affliction rises, is indeed what Allah said. Meaning madness, the Arabs used to say **مَسّ** (mass). ‘Affliction, touch’ and the like are its word-meaning. It is from their notion that madness comes about on account of the affliction of Satan that that word came into usage meaning madness. That the word Satan throwing down ( **يَتَخَبَّطُهُالشَّيْطَانُ** ) is merely a usage according to that notion- Satan does not throw anyone down- some say. For many reasons, no justification is seen for endorsing this opinion just as it is.

The opinion of most of the Quran commentators is that this is the state that comes about for the interest-eaters when, after death, they rise up from the graves, coming back to life. That some particular states befitting the doings that each one had adopted in the worldly life will be seen manifested in all on the Day of Qiyamah is something generally known from the Quran and from hadith. Allah says (in 3: 161) that he who has taken wealth by cheating will come on the Day of Qiyamah with the thing that he took by cheating. That as a result of doing wudu, on the Day of Qiyamah, the face and the hands and feet of the true believers will be whi*[p. 667]*te has come in hadith too. That the belly of those who eat interest will be big like houses has also come in a hadith that Baihaqi (r) quoted. Like this, many other things too can be seen. Then, the gist is that, as a result of having gone on filling the belly with interest-property, on the Day of Qiyamah they, without being able either to rise or to walk properly, will keep falling, tilting and toppling, like madmen. Another opinion is this: the interest-lenders will conduct themselves on earth like those whom madness and intoxication have seized. Or, the gist is that, for the sake of amassing wealth, they will keep doing anything at all like madmen. **اللّه اعلم**

Allah points out the reason for the interest-lenders experiencing this abased state: ‘That is on account of their having said that trade is just like interest’. Interest is laying out property and taking gain. If that is an offence, ought not trade too to be culpable, that too is surely taking gain indeed. That being so, how is it that taking interest is wrong, and doing trade is not wrong?! Such and so on is the import of what they said. This is not an antiquated justification that arose only from those Arabs of olden times, who were uncivilised. It is an argument that can be heard even from some great champions of today, who are considered to be both civilised and progressed in every respect. Besides the wrong of taking and eating interest, this is also another wrong for the sake of justifying that wrong. But, look at the answer that Allah gives to this: **أَحَلَّ اللَّهُالْبَيْعَ وَحَرَّمَا لرِّبَوا** *[TN: the print sets the space after the alif of the article, so that the alif stands with the word before it, instead of before the alif as in the verse panel above; the Arabic is transcribed as printed.]* (Allah has made trade permissible. Interest He has also made forbidden.) Allah has not touched in this answer upon either the difference between trade deals and interest deals, or what the reason is for making one permissible and the other forbidden. There is no need for that either. The difference between the two is something that all, from common people up to experts in economic science- every one who is not interest-lovers and their collaborators- can understand if they think a little. It is a principle weightier than all that which has been pointed out in this sentence.

That is:- Allah is the Creator, and the Sustainer, and the Guardian of all things including man. All are His slaves and in His ownership. He indeed is also the One who knows more than man what all, which all are needed for every kind of good and progress of man, individual, and social, and civic. That being so, the actual authority and entitlement of law-making are only for Him. Man is also bound to obey that. In that alone is there in reality deliverance for him, too. Therefore, in any matter, enquiring whether Allah has permitted it, whether He has prohibited it, and following that, is indeed the duty of man. Understanding and investigating the sagacious principles contained in His laws is good indeed. But man may not hold that he will obey those laws only if he becomes convinced of that. Nor does he have entitlement to carry on quibbling and argument in that. Such and so on *[p. 668]* are the indications contained in that sentence.

Allah continues: ‘Therefore, whoever, good counsel from his Lord having come to him, desists from it, for him will be what has passed away before.’ That is, if anyone, as soon as information was obtained both that the practice of interest is so severe a sin, and that Allah has forbidden it, and that therefore it must be shunned, desists from it and remains both without continuing the existing deal and without carrying out a new deal, he need not give back either any sum that had been collected as interest before – that is the import. But, though he has desisted, what is his state of mind in that? Is it with a pure mind and the spirit of obedience that he desisted? Or else, is it with protest and hatred? How much has he put his dealers to difficulty? – any matters such as these need not be enquired into probingly now. Allah will examine all that and do what is needed – such and so on is the gist of the sentence **وَأَمْرُهُإِلَىاللَّهِ** (His affair is to Allah). As Sayyid Rashid Rida (r) pointed out, what is understood from the words that Allah said is indeed that, although giving back the interest that was taken and used before knowing about the command forbidding interest is not obligatory, what is more good is giving it back. Now, what if anyone, not valuing this counsel, again continues as before, or carries out a new interest deal? Allah says: **وَمَنْ عَادَفَأُولَئِكَ أَصْحَابُ النَّارِ** …. *[DOUBT: as above, the printed dots touch the left end of the Arabic, so on which side of it they stand cannot be told; they are placed after it here, since the verse is cut off.]* (In case anyone repeats, that group are the people of Hell. They will also be permanent residents in it). How very grave a misdeed Allah has reckoned the practice of interest to be can be understood from this warning. Those who not only carry out interest deals, but also justify it and, for that, misinterpret the sentences of Allah and of the Rasul too, need to keep this warning specially in mind.

Although it is famous that interest is a great sin that has been strictly disallowed in Islam- as Ibn Kathir (r) indicated- it is a subject which has many confusions and variances of opinion in the details. The economic philosophies and systems of government of today also keep making them more complicated.. A hadith that has been narrated from Abu Hurairah (r) is indeed to be stated here. It is like this: ‘A time in which the people eat interest will come upon them.’ the Prophet ﷺ said. Then it was asked: ‘All men?’ The noble Prophet said: ‘For those who have not eaten of it, it will befall from its dust too.’ (A; Da; Na; Ja) Therefore every true believer needs to be vigilant to guard their own selves, as far as possible, from interest deals, and from its dust. Withdrawing from deals in which even a slight tinge of interest may come about, and not trying to justify such deals by making them pure deals, is the remedy for that. Hadiths like those seen below will be guides for this:-

*[p. 669]* (1) Saying I have learnt thus from the Prophet ﷺ, Hasn ibn 'Ali (r) quotes: ‘Leave what causes confusion to you and turn to what does not cause confusion. Because, truth will be peace of mind and untruth will be confusion’ (A; Ti; Na.)

(2) The Prophet ﷺ once asked Wabisah (r): ‘Have you come to ask about merit and sin?’ He answered ‘Yes’. Then the noble Prophet, holding the fingers together and tapping on his chest, said three times: ‘Seek judgement from your own mind’. Afterwards he said: ‘Merit is that towards which peace comes about for the mind, and tranquillity for the heart. Sin, even if the people pronounce (whatever) judgement, no matter, will be what causes entanglement in the mind and what produces apprehension in the chest.’ (A; Darimi.)

(3) Jabir (r) quotes as said by the Prophet ﷺ: ‘Flesh that has sprung up and come about from haram will not enter Paradise. What is more deserving of flesh that has sprung up and come about from haram is Hell.’ (A; Da; Ba.)

(4) Ibn Mas'ud (r) quotes as said by the Prophet ﷺ: ‘If any servant, earning the wealth of haram, does charity from it, it will not be accepted from him; if he spends from it, barakah (prosperity) will not be given in it either: if he leaves it remaining and goes (dies), it will not fail to become for him provision for the journey to Hell.’ (A; Sharhus-Sunnah:) May Allah include all of us among the righteous who live heeding His injunctions and prohibitions. Amin.

It was, surely, following on the statements concerning almsgivings that it stated about interest in the previous verse. In this verse Allah points out the contrast between the conse*[p. 670]*quences of the two. The intention of having said that He will both wipe out interest and make almsgivings grow is not both that all the wealth of the interest-lenders will perish, and that the wealth of those who do charity will keep on increasing. Although sometimes it happens so, there is no way to deny that it happens the other way too. Living with comfort and with contentment, having respectability and dignity in the midst of society, obtaining the love and respect of the people, and the like matters are, surely, the attainments to be obtained in this world through wealth. As far as the interest-lenders are concerned, there are none of these kinds of matters for them to obtain. As wealth increases, what will happen is that stinginess and greed and lack of peace increase for them. Along with that, the hatred and enmity of the people will also keep increasing. In the Hereafter, for its part, there is no use at all for them from that wealth. Even if it is spent on a subject that is required indeed- because it is haram earning- there is no merit for them to obtain through it. Matters that are carried out using haram wealth will also turn out detrimental to them. As far as the charitable man is concerned, for all the expenses that he makes he will both obtain mighty recompense from Allah, and Allah will make his charities grow and become big. Even if wealth diminishes, he will have contentment and peace of mind in what there is. The love and sympathy of the people towards him will also keep increasing. Besides, from the verses about interest and almsgivings that have passed before, and from the hadiths quoted above, some other facts too can be understood.

In a saying of the Prophet quoted from Ibn Mas'ud (r) the following has come: interest- even if it becomes much, no matter- its outcome will be towards diminution.’ (A; Ja) We have seen before this the hadith that Bukhari, and Muslim (r) recorded, that, if anyone does charity equal to one dried date from pure earnings, Allah will make it grow for him and make it big like a mountain, as one of you rears his foal fondling it. Allah says: ‘Say: The foul and the good will not be equal- even if the abundance of the foul makes you wonder, no matter’ (Ma'idah : 163) ‘If anyone has done a good thing, We will make good increase for him in it.’ (Shura: 23)

The sentence ‘Allah will not like any great sinful ungrateful one.’ is a sentence in a very grave tone. It is as a general principle that that sentence stands. Even so, it is clear from the context that the interest-lenders are specially intended in it. The interest-lender is, surely, one who, not contenting himself with the halal wealth and earnings that Allah has permitted to him, exploits the wealth of another through a foul way. Then, it need not be said that he is ungrateful and a great sinner. The import of having said that Allah will not like him is both that Allah has displeasure and hatred towards him *[p. 671]*, and that he will not obtain any help or deliverance from His presence. What other misery and loss is there beyond this?! May Allah protect us. Amin.
