*\[Verses 23–24\]*

*\[p. 207\]* To all who claim or doubt that this Quran which Muhammad, the Prophet ﷺ, has brought was not sent down by Allah, or that it was fabricated by him or by someone else for him, or that his claim to a divine mission is not right – whatever time and land they may belong to – this is a mighty challenge. Allah has not stopped even with the challenge. It has also been stated decisively, along with it, that you may, if you wish, gather anyone other than Allah to help, but even so, *\[p. 208\]* it will never at any time be possible for you. A heavy warning too has been given: that if, having become convinced that this challenge cannot be met, you still do not turn back, you must be ready for the most severe punishment of Hell. Not only here: Allah has repeated this challenge in the Quran in more than one place. Allah says:– **قُل لَّئِنِ ٱجۡتَمَعَتِ ٱلۡإِنسُ وَٱلۡجِنُّ عَلَىٰٓ أَن يَأۡتُواْ بِمِثۡلِ هَٰذَا ٱلۡقُرۡءَانِ لَا يَأۡتُونَ بِمِثۡلِهِۦ وَلَوۡ كَانَ بَعۡضُهُمۡ لِبَعۡضٖ ظَهِيرٗا** (الإسراء: ٨٨). Gist: Say: 'Even if mankind and the jinn join together to bring something like this Quran, they will not bring one like it – even if some of them are giving support to others.' (Isra': 88). **قُلۡ فَأۡتُواْ بِعَشۡرِ سُوَرٖ مِّثۡلِهِۦ مُفۡتَرَيَٰتٖ وَٱدۡعُواْ مَنِ ٱسۡتَطَعۡتُم مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ إِن كُنتُمۡ صَٰدِقِينَ** (هود: ١٣) Say: Then bring some ten fabricated surahs like this. And do call all whom you can besides Allah – if you are truthful (Hud, 13). **قُلۡ فَأۡتُواْ بِسُورَةٖ مِّثۡلِهِۦ وَٱدۡعُواْ مَنِ ٱسۡتَطَعۡتُم مِّن دُونِ ٱللَّهِ إِن كُنتُمۡ صَٰدِقِينَ** (يونس: ٣٨) 'Say: Then bring a surah that is like this. And do call all whom you can besides Allah – if you are truthful.' (Yunus, 38).

This challenge, which had been repeated openly for a long time among the mushriks in these three Makkan surahs, Allah is now repeating again in this surah, among the Jews and the Munafiqs of Madinah. In place of 'a book like the Quran' (**بِمِثۡلِ هَٰذَا ٱلۡقُرۡءَانِ**), once He said that it would be enough if they brought ten surahs (**عَشۡرِ سُوَرٖ**). Another time He also tried saying: bring a single surah (**سُورَةٖ**). Every time, there was no other response than silence. The surahs of the Quran are of various sizes, surely. Among them there are also ones that contain only three short ayahs. That being so, if they had brought even one small surah like that, they could have boasted that they had met this challenge. He even granted this too: it is enough that they do not call Allah alone to join them – besides Him they may call human beings and the race of the jinn, all of them, for company and for help. If there is any particle of justification for their denial, let it come to light. He called on them many times – for years, indeed – to just try doing it. Without waiting for any reply of theirs, there Allah states decisively, in language that leaves no room for doubt of meaning: you will never at any time do it – it is not possible for you!.

Even with all this, there is no record in history of anyone at all having ventured out to meet the challenge, either in that time or from then until today. Until the end of time this challenge will indeed remain, just as it is, on the pages of the Mushaf. *\[p. 209\]* So many literary masters, who gained fame as having reached the summit of Arabic literature, have existed then and afterwards too. It is not known either that a few of them joined together and tried making an organised effort for it. In reality, it can be seen that this verse contains two proofs that the Quran is from Allah: that no one is able to overcome this challenge, and that it was decisively foretold in advance that they will never be able to do it.

It need not be said that it is because of the superhuman nature (**اعجاز**) of the Quran that no one is able to bring a book or a chapter like it. In which field of it does this superhuman nature lie? Although many may be seen to have said many things on this subject, the plain truth is that it extends through all of its literary styles of language, its ideas, its principles and doctrines, and its presentations. Since the necessary explanation about this has been given in the Introduction (\*), it is not explained further here. Until now there has been no book accepted as fully matching it even in any single one of these fields. Not only among books that are human works, but even among the earlier scriptures, there has been no book like the Quran. That is also why the Quran stands as the everlasting sign of the truthfulness of the noble Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ says: 'There is no prophet who has not been given sufficient signs for people to believe. What has been given to me is indeed the wahy (divine revelation) that Allah has given me. Therefore I hope that on the Day of Qiyamah I will be the one having more followers than them.' (Bu. Mu.)

> **Note:** (\*) See the chapter (The Superhuman Nature of the Quran).

When the particle **مِن** (min) in the sentence **فَأۡتُواْ بِسُورَةٖ مِّن مِّثۡلِهِۦ** *\[TN: the printed Arabic here has بِسُوَرٍ ("surahs", plural) in place of بِسُورَةٍ ("a surah") of verse 23; the author's own explanation that follows ("a surah") is of the verse as it stands. The Arabic is given here from the verse.\]* is considered, its meaning comes to be: 'Bring a surah from what is like it (the Quran).' In the verse in Surah Hud it is **بِعَشۡرِ سُوَرٖ مِّثۡلِهِۦ** (ten surahs like it), and in the verse in Yunus **بِسُورَةٖ مِّثۡلِهِۦ** (a surah like it). In the verse in Al-Isra', on the other hand, it has been said: **بِمِثۡلِ هَٰذَا ٱلۡقُرۡءَانِ** (what is like this Quran). So it is clear that the overall intent of these is: bring a book like the Quran, or ten surahs like those of the Quran, or else one surah. A modern scholar is seen to have written a strange interpretation of that sentence. His words are these: 'Its meaning is: write and bring at least a chapter of a system of life sufficient to make human life successful, like the Quran. According to this, what is meant by the Quran must be 'a system of life', and what is meant by a surah must be its 'chapter', surely. *\[TN: no closing quotation mark is printed for the scholar's words; where the quotation ends is not marked.\]* As a proof of this, he has also craftily pointed to the 49th verse of Surat al- *\[p. 210\]* Qasas. But the context and the subject of that verse are different. That verse is addressed to those who objected, asking why signs like those given to the Prophet Musa (a) had not been given to the Prophet ﷺ, and who at the same time disbelieved both in the Prophet Musa (a) and in the Prophet ﷺ. Its wording is this: **قُلۡ فَأۡتُواْ بِكِتَٰبٖ مِّنۡ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ هُوَ أَهۡدَىٰ مِنۡهُمَآ أَتَّبِعۡهُ** (Gist: Say: Then bring from Allah a book that is more guiding than those two – than what the two prophets brought – I will follow it …) It is clear at first sight itself, surely, that the two are two subjects. In reality, when one considers what lies behind this twisted interpretation, what one understands is this: there are among the modern scholars of today some new advocates of claims such as that, although the literary language of the Quran is of a very high standard as far as the time in which it was revealed is concerned, that high position of it does not hold for all times; that there is no obstacle to a book like it, or in a state surpassing it, in literary language being produced by human beings; and that the science of literature has progressed very far beyond that of that time. It seems that it may be that claim which lies behind this.

If, after becoming convinced that it is not possible to bring a book equal to the Quran, or else even a chapter like a chapter in it, one still does not believe in it, it is plain, surely, that it is only obstinacy and arrogance. That is why He warns them very strongly that, if they do not do so, they must guard against Hell, that the fuel burned in it is human beings and stones, that it has been prepared for disbelievers of this kind, and so on. It need not be said that what is meant by saying that the fuel of Hell is human beings is the disbelievers. But what is meant by stones (**ٱلۡحِجَارَةُ**)? It has come in some trustworthy riwayahs from Ibn Mas'ud (r), Mujahid (r) and others that they are a kind of sulphur stones in Hell, which blaze up quickly and give off an unbearable stench. Some have stated that they are the stones that idol-worshippers have been worshipping. Whichever it may be, what it indicates is the severity of the fire of Hell and the gravity of the punishment. If it turns out that they are idols, the humiliation of their worshippers too will have been intended by it. It is known from the Quran and from the sayings of the Prophet that the fire familiar to us and the fire of Hell are not alike, and that the fire of Hell is a special kind of fire, many times more powerful.

On the basis of its having been stated concerning Hell, here and in Al Imran 131, that it has been prepared for the disbelievers (**أُعِدَّتۡ لِلۡكَٰفِرِينَ**), the *\[p. 211\]* scholars have understood that Allah has created Hell already beforehand. This is further confirmed from many hadiths too. Likewise, concerning Paradise, Allah has stated in 3: 133 that it has been prepared for those who observe caution (**أُعِدَّتۡ لِلۡمُتَّقِينَ**), and in 57: 21 that it has been prepared for the true believers (**أُعِدَّتۡ لِلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُواْ**). But those belonging to the Mu'tazilah sect do not agree with this, on account of some rational arguments. However, it needs to be remembered that rational arguments have no place in matters of this kind, and that what is required is to accept just as it is what is seen in the words of Allah and of the Messenger.

*\[Verse 25\]*

*\[p. 212\]* In the previous verse He warned about the punishment for those who denied the Quran. In this following verse He gives glad tidings about the blessings that the true believers are due to receive. We have stated earlier in the Introduction that this is one of the Quran's methods of presentation.

When the true believers are given varieties of fruit – or kinds of ripe fruit – to eat in Paradise, there are two opinions about the meaning of their saying that this is what was given before: that it means 'this is the very same fruits that were given the time before this, surely', and that it means 'this is the very same fruits that we used to get earlier, in this world, surely'. Either way, the reason for saying so is that they have resemblance to one another in such a way that the difference cannot be recognised. That is, in form, in colour and so on, they will be alike. As for taste and flavour, they will be different. What falls into sight will, surely, be the shape and the colour. A riwayah reported as stated by Ibn Abbas (r) is noteworthy here: **ليس فى الدنيا مما فى الجنة الا الاسماء – ابن جرير وغيره** (Of the things in Paradise, there is nothing in this world except the names). That is, the gist is that, apart from names such as mango, pomegranate, bed and meat, they have no resemblance at all in other matters. When speaking about the things and the circumstances of Paradise, this principle must always be kept in mind. The state of the things in Hell is also the same. None of them is to be compared with worldly things. This is a reality known from many verses of the Quran and from the sayings of the Prophet.

**أَزۡوَٰجٞ** (azwaj) is the plural of **زَوْجٌ**. Its literal meaning is 'mates'. It is used for all kinds of things that are mates. As far as human beings are concerned, it is used also in the senses of wives, husbands and wives, and husbands. All must be understood from the context. What becomes clear is that what is meant by it here is wives. *\[DOUBT: ഇവിടെ അതുകൊണ്ട് വിവക്ഷ can be read as 'what is meant by it here' (as rendered; the author often writes X കൊണ്ട് വിവക്ഷ / ഉദ്ദേശ്യം) or as 'therefore, what is meant here'. The sense (wives) is the same either way.\]* The gist of saying 'purified' (**مُّطَهَّرَةٞ**) may be understood, in all, as: those who are pure from all kinds of defects and impurities – bodily, of nature, of temperament and of beauty. The life of Paradise will be eternal – no end or break will befall it – that indeed is the meaning of saying that they are permanent dwellers in it too (**وَهُمۡ فِيهَا خَٰلِدُونَ**). In a hadith recorded by Bukhari, Muslim (r) and others, the Prophet ﷺ has said thus: 'The people of Paradise will enter Paradise, and the people of Hell will enter Hell. After that a man will come onto the scene between them and proclaim aloud: People of Hell, there is no death; people of Paradise, there is no death; all will be permanent dwellers in their respective places.'

*\[Verses 26–27\]*

*\[p. 214\]* The parables of the fire and of the rain concerning the hypocrites were explained earlier, surely. Likewise, on some occasions the Quran has made the fly, the spider and the like into parables too. The hypocrites had alleged that all this does not befit the dignity of a book from Allah. It is the reply to that which is in these verses. What is intended by parables and examples, surely, is to grasp matters by understanding the principles and the relations of resemblance contained in them. The smallness or bigness, or the rank and status, of the thing taken up and shown as a parable has no relevance in it. That is the purport of saying that Allah has no shame at all in taking as a parable some insignificant thing like a mosquito, or some other thing superior to it. As far as the true believers are concerned, they will try to grasp the principles and secrets and the items of knowledge contained in that parable, without inquiring into the thing made a parable. On the contrary, the disbelievers will mock, saying things like 'What sort of parable is this; what is meant by this?', and find satisfaction. The sentence **يُضِلُّ بِهِۦ كَثِيرٗا وَيَهۡدِي بِهِۦ كَثِيرٗاۚ** (by means of it He leads many (people) into error, and puts many (people) on the straight way) is a protest against these people and a warning.

That is, it becomes a cause for those who are hypocrites and deniers of the truth to increase in their misguidance and denial, and for those who are true believers to increase in their right guidance and belief. The indication in this is: let them consider in which of these two they ought to be included. *\[p. 215\]* Allah has pointed out in the very next sentence that the purport of that sentence is not that Allah, without any just criterion at all, will put some people on the straight way and some other people in misguidance, and that He has fixed a criterion for both. Yes: **وَمَا يُضِلُّ بِهِۦٓ إِلَّا ٱلۡفَٰسِقِينَ** (He does not lead into error by means of it except the wayward). Then it need not be stated further, surely, whom He will put on the right path.

**فَاسِقٌ** (fasiq) is the agent noun of **فِسْقٌ** (fisq), which has meanings such as 'straying, leaving the true way; defying a command; waywardness; roguery; misconduct'. Looking at the linguistic meaning, there can be fasiqs among believers, among disbelievers and among hypocrites. It can be seen used in the Quran concerning all of them too. In general its use is about those who, violating religious injunctions, do not observe the discipline of religion. Here, what is likely is to take what was said – **وَمَا يُضِلُّ بِهِۦٓ إِلَّا ٱلۡفَٰسِقِينَ** (He does not lead into error by it except the wayward) – as intending the disbelievers. This can be understood from His having said, after describing their evil qualities in the 27th verse, 'it is they themselves who are the losers', and from His having said about those with the same evil qualities (in Surat ar-Ra'd: 25) **أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ لَهُمُ ٱللَّعۡنَةُ وَلَهُمۡ سُوٓءُ ٱلدَّارِ** (It is for those people that the curse is. It is for them that the evil abode is, too.) What Allah has described are three evil practices of the wayward:

(1) Breaking Allah's order – the pact with Him.

(2) Cutting the bond that was commanded to be joined

(3) Causing corruption in the earth.

It is the word **عُهۡدُ** ('ahd) that has been translated as 'order'. According to the context and the nature of the particles adjoining it, many meanings too may come for it: 'covenant, pact, bequest, command, order, commandment, pledge, acquaintance, responsibility, time', and others. Speaking generally, what is meant here is a matter that Allah has ordered and that man is obliged to fulfil. As many great men too have stated, Allah's order (**عُهۡدُ ٱللَّهِ**) is of two kinds:

(1) The order through the special intelligence and the natural signs that Allah has deposited in human beings. Through this, man is himself *\[p. 216\]* obliged to grasp and to accept Allah's existence, oneness, being the One worshipped and the like. If he does not fulfil this obligation, man becomes one who has broken Allah's order and one who has broken the duty toward Him. All kinds of disbelievers are indeed those who have broken the order in this sense.

(2) The order through prophets, scriptures and extraordinary signs. The disbelievers among the People of the Scripture and the hypocrites are those who have broken the order in this sense. What the hypocrites do, surely, is to pledge at first, along with repeating the watchword of Islam, that they have accepted tawhid and prophethood.

There are other opinions too about what is meant here by **عُهۡدُ ٱللَّهِ** (Allah's order). For example:

(1) Allah has stated in Su. Al-A'raf: 172 that, after creating the Prophet Adam (a), his descendants acknowledged (in the spiritual world) that Allah is their Rabb, and bore witness to it. (More explanation can be seen in Al-A'raf (**إن شاء الله** (if Allah wills)). That is what is meant here.

(2) Allah has stated in Al-Baqarah: 38 and in Taha 123 that, when Adam (a) was coming from Paradise to the earthly world, Allah instructed that, if any guidance from Allah comes to you, you must follow it. That is what is meant. In reality, of these, the first opinion is included in the first kind of the two kinds of orders we stated at first, and the second in the second kind of them.

Among the bonds that Allah commanded to be joined are included all just bonds, such as the bond of blood, the bond of family, the bond of affection, the bond of neighbourliness, the bond of religion and the social bond. It is well known, surely, that there is no limit or count to the outrages that the disbelievers of the time of the Prophet ﷺ committed in utter violation of all these kinds of bonds. In the very same way, there is no count to the destructions and the turmoils that they brought about in the earth – in the land. The Munafiqs, the mushriks and the Jews, all of them, were ones who took part in those trades, each as far as they could. According to the part each group took, loss indeed befell them – in this world and in the next. Yes, 'it is they themselves who became losers' (**أُوْلَـٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلۡخَٰسِرُونَ**). As for the Prophet ﷺ and the true believers, victory too, in this world and in the next. It need not be thought that, because the statement in these verses is about those of that time, none of this applies to those who came later. If one looks with history and experience, it will be understood that the general state of the disbelievers and the hypocrites of all times is this very same.
