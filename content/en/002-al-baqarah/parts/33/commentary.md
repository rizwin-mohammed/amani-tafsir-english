*[Verses 200–202]*

*[p. 503]* Though the literal meaning of the word **مَنَاسِك** (Manasik) is ‘rites of self-sacrifice, rites of worship’ and such, it is for the rites connected with the Hajj that it is mostly wont to be used. When one has finished carrying out the Hajj rites, He is commanding to keep renewing the remembrance of Allah, carrying on extolment, meditation, supplication and the like. The reason for saying ‘as you remember your fathers’ is: when the Hajj was over, it was the habit of the Arabs that the people of each tribe, assembling at Mina and elsewhere, would keep enumerating, and singing the praises of, the worthy qualities and the greatnesses of their forefathers, and of their fathers. Indicating the impropriety of this, Allah is saying: What you need to do is not that; in its place, it is about Allah that you need to have memory and extolment and such. No, that is not enough; you need to give importance to the remembrance of Allah indeed over and above that – and so on. This command has been interpreted in another manner too. When you are children, you will keep calling, and saying, and asking ‘my father, my mother’ and such, surely; what is needed is that, like that too, and over and above that too, you remember about Allah and pray to Him – that indeed is the summary of that interpretation. Whichever of the two it be, it culminates indeed in one same idea – indeed in the necessity of keeping Allah in mind –. Continuing, Allah also says how the praying to Allah must be, and how it must not be:-

Though some of mankind will keep calling on Allah and praying, they will have only worldly matters as their aim. ‘Rabb, do give us such-and-such things in this world! **رَبَّنَا آتِنَافِيالدُّنْيَا** will be their supplication. About the matters of the Hereafter they will have nothing to pray. For those who are thus, there will be nothing to obtain in the Hereafter. The worldly needs, for their part, will also keep being obtained in the measure Allah has willed. There is another section of people: they, for good that is worldly and otherworldly, will also *[p. 504]* keep praying. ‘Do give us good in this world and in the next! ( **رَبَّنَا آتِنَافِيالدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةً وَفِي الآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً** )’ will be what they say. Yes, health, a life of peace, wealth for need, a dwelling, good household members, public approval, knowledge, wisdom, the convenience to do good deeds, safety from calamities and from difficulties – for all mundane good things beginning with these they will pray. But their aim does not end with that. Their main aim will be the obtaining of good things such as the pleasure of Allah, forgiveness of sins, heavenly favours. Therefore, they also pray: guard and save us, so that we do not, doing works that become the cause of the wrath and curse of Allah, become subject to the punishment of Hell ( **وَقِنَاعَذَابَالنَّارِ** ). Those who are thus too will obtain in this world, like the others, the things Allah has willed. In the Hereafter, for its part, they also obtain an exceedingly great good fortune in accordance with the gains they earned and laid by in this world as their supplications and deeds. Think over which among these two kinds of supplications is worthy as a model, and which one needs to adopt, and practise what is fitting – that is the gist. At the end He alerts that Allah will carry out the reckoning of all very quickly and give the recompense each one deserves, and that there is no delay or other obstacles at all to be faced in that.

In Su: Isra'il Allah says: ‘In case anyone intends this world, transiently, We will hasten for those whom We intend what We decide to want. Afterwards We will appoint Hell for him. He will burn in it as one who is reproached, and as one who is despised. In case anyone intends the Hereafter, and, being a true believer, carries on the striving proper to it for its sake, the striving of those who are thus will be accepted with thanks. To all – indeed to that group and to this group – We will send out the bestowal that is from your Rabb. The bestowal of your Rabb will not be one that is obstructed.’ (Isra', 18, 19, 20) This supplication that Allah has taught us as a model supplication, **رَبَّنَا آتِنَافِي الدُّنْيَاحَسَنَةًوَفِيالآخِرَةِ حَسَنَةً وَقِنَاعَذَابَ النَّارِ**, along with being very light to utter with the tongue, is a great supplication aiming at all kinds of good things, worldly and otherworldly. That it is indeed therefore that the Prophet ﷺ used to pray this supplication commonly, (Bu) and that the noble Prophet's supplication was mostly in these wordings, (A) and that in the midst of the Hajj rites too, such as doing tawaf of the Ka'bah, much place has been given to this supplication, is worth stating.

*[Verse 203]*

*[p. 505]* The intention here by **أَيَّام مَعْدُودَات** (some numbered days) is the three days following the day of the Great Festival. That is, the dates Dhul-Hijjah 11, 12, 13, which are called ‘Ayyam al-Tashriq ( **ايَّامُ التَّشريق** ). *[DOUBT: on the image the mark over the ت of التشريق looks like a shadda with fatha, like the one over the ي of ايام; the text layer encodes only a fatha there (التَشريق). Transcribed with the shadda.]* Allah reminds that in these days the dhikr (remembrance) of Allah is specially needed. It is on this basis indeed that, in those days, saying takbir after the prayers, and when throwing at the Jamrah (\*)s, and when slaughtering the sacrificial animals, and so on, has been made sunnah. ‘Ayyam al-Tashriq are days for eating, and for drinking, and for dhikr’, the Prophet ﷺ has also declared. (Mu) In these days the hajjis, staying at Mina, need to carry out the stoning at the Jamrahs. But, Allah also makes known that there is no objection both to completing it in two days and leaving that place and coming away on the second day, and to staying three days and coming away on the third day. Though coming away on the third day is good, both may be done – that is the gist. He alerts that it is to God-fearing reverence and to caution that importance needs to be given, more than to its being two or three days, and that both must not be with one's own selfish ends kept in front. At the end – as seen many times before – He again reminds that one must live guarding against Allah, and that one must keep in mind that all will at the end have to go back to His presence.

> **Note:** (\*) At Mina, at three special places, one needs to throw stones seven times each, surely. It is those places that are called Jamrah ( **جَمْرَة** )s.

*[Verses 204–207]*

*[p. 507]* After having stated about two kinds of people, who are those who pray only for worldly matters and those who pray for good things that are worldly and otherworldly, it is concerning two other kinds of people like that, and their characters, that it states in these verses. One group are hypocrites and of wicked mentality, the others are sincere ones and of excellent mentality.

It is about two kinds of mankind thus that it has stated in these verses. The gist of what was said about the first kind: talk that is attractive, and full of sweetness. Those who hear will come to feel delight and wonder. In order to establish their genuineness and purity, they will use the name of Allah now and then, and call Him to witness. In reality, what is inside them is intense enmity and rancour towards the truth and towards the party of the truth. They will also be experts in engaging in quibbling and wrangling. When they move away from the field of talk and come to the field of deeds, for their part, their effort and attention will be in the ways of disorder and of corruption in the land. They will also engage in actions of wrongdoing such as destroying produce, throwing the life of peace into disarray. When seeing all this, if anyone says to them that one must guard against Allah, that all this is what He does not like, and such, not only will they not give ear to that, but on the contrary, what will happen is that arrogance and rage seize them, and that their force and majesty urge them on to do more licentiousness and defiance *[p. 508]*. There is no result in admonishing, or in advising, those who are thus. To subdue them, Allah has got ready beforehand indeed – Hell! That is enough for them! When they reach there, this force and defiance of theirs will not be seen at all. Such is the gist of what was said about people of this kind. Just think over the seriousness of this warning made to them in very few words!

Though some of the commentators can be seen to have stated that this description is about the hypocrites of that time, or about some among them, and that its sending down was in their matter – as Qatadah, Mujahid, Rabi' (r) and others have stated – that this is something concerning all who are of this character, be it among the true believers, be it among the hypocrites, is indeed the right opinion. People of this kind are comparatively plentiful at all times, surely. In this time of ours especially. To the word **وَإِذَاتَوَلَّى** the meaning may come both ‘if he turns away’ and ‘if he obtains authority’. The intention will be, according to the first meaning, if they move away from the field of talk, and, according to the second meaning, if some authority or influence comes into their hands in the land. The description seen above is based on the first meaning. When one looks at the structure of the wording, it is to that that preponderance is seen. It is the second meaning that a section of the Quran commentators have accepted. According to this, the idea is also clearer. That description which Allah gave about the doings that come about if those in whose mind true belief and God-fearing reverence concerning Allah have not touched get authority and influence – we are, surely, keeping on seeing and experiencing it in the literal sense.

About the second kind, Allah has not explained much. Moreover, one can understand about them even without explanation indeed. The pleasure and the approval of Allah are their sole aim. For that they are ready to embrace any self-sacrifice. Not only wealth, majesty and the like, they are prepared to offer in sacrifice even their own soul for it. This being so, there is no need to say, surely, that their word and work will be in accordance with that. As an example of this, the Quran commentators bring out and show the event of Suhaib (r) (\*). Some have also stated that it was in his matter that this verse came down. Whatever the cause of the sending down, that the content in it is not one applicable to him alone, it is applicable generally - about th*[p. 509]*at there is no doubt. When Suhaib (r) got ready for the Hijrah, the Quraish stopped him. They insisted that we will not consent to your leaving the land with the properties you earned here, having come to this land as a poor man. If you want, you may go with your own body. He, who was skilled in archery and other things, without getting ready for a clash with them, leaving all his properties in Makkah, set out on the journey to Madinah. When he came to know this news, the Prophet ﷺ said: ‘Suhaib has gained profit! Suhaib's trade has turned to profit!’ This is that event. If what Allah said about the first kind is ‘Jahannam is enough for them’, what was said about the gain that those of this section obtain is indeed **وَاللَّهُرَءُوفٌبِالْعِبَادِ** (Allah is gracious concerning the servants). For these, who have set aside the whole of life for the pleasure of Allah, is there need to expect another promise bigger than this?!

> **Note:** (\*) Suhaib ( **صُحَيْب** ) was by origin a man of 'Iraq. In his young days, in a war, the Romans seized him and carried him off, making him a slave. In course of time he came to Makkah and settled, and earned quite some through trade. Since he, who was one of the early believers who believed first in Islam, had no close ones in the land, he had had to suffer very much more oppression from the Quraishi mushriks.

*[Verses 208–209]*

*[p. 510]* Allah, calling the true believers, commands that it is not allowed to acknowledge some of the legal procedures in Islam and not to acknowledge some others, and that entering into Islam must be by accepting all the procedural orders in it. It is said that it was following some of the People of the Scripture being reluctant, even after coming into Islam, to give up some old customs of theirs that this command came about. If that is right, what it does is to strengthen more the principle stated above. This command is applicable to all who practise Islamic laws in a few sides of religion, and along with that, keeping selfish interests and profits in affairs in front, keep adopting un-Islamic laws and principles in other sides. Allah also gives warning that doing so is following the footsteps of Satan, and that he is the supreme enemy of the true believers. He will not pay attention, surely, except to making those who side with Satan into rightful claimants to Hell. **إِنَّمَا يَدْعُو حِزْبَهُ لِيَكُونُوا مِنْ أَصْحَابِ السَّعِيرِ** (He invites his party only for them to be among the people of Hell.)

What is seen in the second verse is a warning a second time too: that those who, after having got clear knowledge that the truth is such-and-such, that the legal procedure in Islam is such-and-such, and so on, still leave the straight path and slip with their foot, will get a just punishment, and that it is not possible for anyone either to escape from it, or to rescue. The literal meaning of the word **سِلْم** (Silm) is ‘submission, peace, obedience’ and such. Whichever meaning among these one takes, what its interpretation ends up in will be indeed in Islam. It is therefore too that many are seen to have assigned it the meaning Islam.

*[Verse 210]*

*[p. 511]* To Allah's coming in shades of cloud, interpretation can be seen to have been given in more than one manner. The reason for this is two different positions that have come to be acknowledged among the scholars in interpreting the decisions *[DOUBT: the print has തീരുമാനങ്ങളെയും “the decisions”, but the author's Arabic in brackets is “the names of Allah and His attributes”; തിരുനാമങ്ങളെയും “the holy names” may have been meant. Translated as printed.]* and qualities and attributes of Allah that have come in the Quran and in the Prophet's sayings ( **اسمَاء اللّه وصفاتة** ). Its summary is this:

(1) Without giving them interpretations or conditions, to be content with what is understood from their outward meaning, and to leave them, holding that Allah knows the actual intention and interpretation. And also to remain without handling them, using them, wresting them out of the contexts. *[DOUBT: the print has ഉപയോഗിച്ച് “using (them)”; if ഉപയോഗിച്ച was meant, the sense is “without handling them by wresting them out of the contexts in which they were used”.]* This is the policy that the great men who were the predecessors have been adopting.

(2) To give them interpretations of meaning in accordance with the context, in a manner that does not become contrary to the greatness and holiness of Allah. This is the way that the ‘Mutakallim’s (the scholars of the science of belief) and the majority among the successors have been adopting. Though, as far as ordinary people are concerned, it is this that will seem more fit to be done, what is safer and more befitting to the exalted greatness of Allah is indeed the policy of the predecessors. Concerning this subject, quite some more explanation will come in the commentary of Al 'Imran, 7th verse. **إن شاء الله**

That, while all, for a long time, are sunk in utmost perplexity and fear in that ‘Mahshar’ great assembly in which all the creatures are gathered together on the Day of Qiyamah, they will approach many who are chief among the prophets, requesting them to intercede with Allah in order that some decision be taken in their matter, and that all will excuse themselves from it, each giving some reason, and that at last the noble Prophet Muhammad ﷺ will request Allah for that, and that, accepting that intercession, Allah will carry out the reckoning of the creatures – what has come thus in many hadiths is famous. Among them, in a hadith that many, such as Ibn Jarir (r), have quoted from Abu Hurairah (r), the summary of what was stated about the coming of Allah and of the angels is this: ‘.....Then each heaven will burst and split, and all the angels in it will come down. Thus, Allah and the angels will come in shades of cloud. The angels will keep chanting many kinds of tasbih (singing of praise)s. Allah will say: ‘O company of the jinn, and of mankind! Though from the time I created you until now I did not speak to you, I kept hearing and seeing all that you say and do. Here are the pages in which your deeds are recorded. Let those who find good in it praise Allah. Let those who see anything else not blame other than their own selves’. Thus, Allah will make decision among the creatures.’

*[p. 512]* What is understood from this hadith is that it is indicating the occasion when Allah comes for the reckoning of the creatures on the Day of Qiyamah that it has been stated here. But, what is it that was called shade of cloud? How is it? Why does He come in shade of cloud? We do not know any of this. Allah knows. Without adding or reducing anything in what He has said, we may believe it. Then, the import of this verse will be thus: In case they – those who, not entering into Islam completely as stated in the previous verses, followed the footsteps of Satan and went astray – do not understand the truth from the clear proofs and walk on the straight path, what else is there for them to wait for, except that occasion when Allah, along with the angels, comes and carries out their reckoning and makes decision?! Yes, there is nothing else to wait for. Once that occasion has come, then there is not any safety at all for them either.

The opinion of those who adopted the second way stated above is that the intention of ‘Allah comes’ is that the command of Allah – or the punishment – comes, and so on. As the forerunner of the punishment that came down in some communities before, what they first saw was a thing in the form of cloud. It was while they were rejoicing with the good hope that rain would fall that the angels rained a great punishment upon them. Is it waiting for such a huge punishment to befall these too that these are doing?! Such will be, according to this opinion, roughly the gist of the ayah. Whichever of the two it be, this is a strong warning that, after having wasted the juncture entirely, one will have to regret at a juncture when there is no result in regretting afterwards.

The import of having said **وَقُضِيَالأمْرُ** (And the matter has also been decided) is that it has been ordained and decided beforehand that such-and-such a matter is in such-and-such a manner, and that the time of belief and of action is such-and-such, and that the occasion of recompense is such-and-such, and so on. This was said on the basis that the verb **قُضِيَ** is in the past-tense verb form. It is not that its meaning cannot come in the future-tense form too, as ‘and will be decided’. It is thus that some commentators too assign it a meaning. Then the intention is also clearer, surely. Describing in the past-tense form a matter that is certain to happen is both usual in the language, and often seen in the Quran. It is indeed to Allah, who has ordained all, surely, that all matters also finally go and end. ( **والى اللّهترجع الامور** )
