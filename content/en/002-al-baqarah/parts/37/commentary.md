### Section – 29

*[Verses 229–230]*

*[p. 547]* In the past verses 224, 225, Allah stated some things concerning the oath. In the two verses that follow, He stated about ‘ila'’ too, which is a particular kind of oath. Incidentally, connecting with it, He touched upon talaq (divorce) too. Thus, in verse 228, in the course of explaining the laws relating to talaq, He spoke both about taking back the wife after talaq, and about the 'iddah. Following that, here He explains which is the talaq that may be taken back, and which is the talaq that must not be taken back, and how carrying out talaq ought to be, and so on. Many important laws relating to talaq are contained in these verses. Intermittently, four or five times, He has both qualified them as **حُدُودَاللَّهِ** (the legal boundaries that Allah has determined), and given a strict warning about violating them *[p. 548]*. It is the gravity that Allah has assigned to the said laws that all this points out. But, it has come to be necessary to say, with boundless sorrow, that, even with all this, the sight that is seen is the Muslim community both violating and getting past most of these strict legal boundaries!(**فالى اللّه المشتكى**)

Many rules of procedure that were in force among the Arabs – bringing about some reforms, or controls, in them – Islam has accepted. Belonging among them are both divorce and the taking back after it ( **الطَلَاقُ وَالرجعة** ). A person then had the freedom both to carry out divorce of his wife and to take back, as many times as he wanted. On account of this, women also used to have to undergo very many sufferings. Without either getting the concessions that ought to be got on account of marriage, or getting the freedom that ought to be got through divorce – in such a way that they were neither married nor divorced – so many women having to push on through their lifetime was also habitual. When some quarrel or wrangle arises between them, some may even say openly ‘I will never at any time either take you on, or let you go’. Both that, at the beginning of Islam, an incident like this took place among the Ansaris, and that that woman came to the Prophet ﷺ and made known a grievance, riwayahs have come. It is both putting an end to this vile practice, and bringing control to it through laws that are equitable and useful and practicable, and thus rescuing the community of women from falling victim to such wrongs, that Allah has done by means of these verses.

From what was stated above, it has been understood, surely, that in the period of Jahiliyyah, among the Arabs, in the matter of the divorce of women, what existed was a kind of anarchy. An anarchy that is like that, or beyond that, is running rampant in this time too, which is said to be the civilised age – even in European lands in which Christians have a vast majority. At the same time, if one is speaking concerning Christians, the ruling of the Bible is also directly to the contrary. If a man marries a woman, afterwards, for any reason at all, it is not allowed for him to send her away giving divorce. Even if, not agreeing with each other, it becomes necessary to live separated, marrying is not allowed for both parties. In case one marries – be it the man, be it the woman – that is also deemed adultery. (See Matthew: 31, 32 in 5; Luke: 18:1 in 16. Corinthians: 10- 12 in 7) *[DOUBT: the reference is printed “ലൂക്കോസ്: 16ൽ 18:1. കൊരിന്ത്യർ: 7ൽ 10- 12”; it is not clear whether “18:1.” is one reference, or verse 18 of Luke 16 followed by “1.” as the number of the book of Corinthians (First Corinthians). Rendered as printed.]* Though slight differences will be seen, more or less among the followers of the Hindu religion too, it is rules of procedure like this that exist. That Islam, for its part, has adopted, in between both these two mutually contradictory policies – a stance that is both suited to human nature and fitting for practical life, can be understood by anyone who thinks impartially. Now let us give attention to the main subjects contained in these verses. **ومن الله التوفيق**

*[p. 549]* (One:) **الطَّلاقُمَرَّتَانِ** (Talaq – divorce – is two times.) That the talaq that may be taken back in the 'iddah period is two times – that is the gist. The meaning is not, otherwise, that a person may carry out talaq of his wife only two times in all. From the sentences that follow, this is evident. That is to say, if one has carried out talaq the first time, before its 'iddah ends the husband may take the wife back into the marriage. He may also, without taking back until the 'iddah ends, keep the divorce established. In case he takes back, if need is faced, he may carry out talaq a second time too. After this talaq, the husband has become obliged to adopt one of two: **فَإِمْسَاكٌبِمَعْرُوفٍأَوْ تَسْرِيحٌبِإِحْسَانٍ** (Keeping her on according to good-custom propriety, or releasing her while doing good – in a good way.) That is, taking her back into the marriage, living on, dealing with her both in a good form and in accordance with propriety. The taking back must not be either with some evil intention or other in mind, or behaving towards her with behaviour that is not pleasant. In case of not being ready – not being able – for this, send her off in a good way, in a decent manner. The husband is compelled to adopt one of these two.

It will be fitting to the occasion to know also some facts and explanations connected with the laws stated above. For example:-

(1). From its having been said, not as ‘Talaq is two in number’ or something, but as ‘Talaq is two times ( **الطَّلاقُمَرَّتَانِ** ), it can be understood both that the practice of carrying out two or three talaqs at one and the same time indeed, in one stroke, is not right, and that each talaq needs to be on a separate time. About the variances of opinion in this matter we may speak below, **إن شاء الله**

(2) It is at an occasion that does not give rise to the period of the 'iddah becoming lengthened that the talaq must take place. Or, it must be in a period of purity in which union has not taken place. It must not be either in the menstrual period, or in a period of purity in which union has taken place. Besides what has been indicated concerning this in the first verse of Su: Talaq, the Prophet ﷺ has also stated it clearly in hadith. For detailed information, look at the interpretation of Su: Talaq.

(3) In case of not taking back until the 'iddah following the first-time talaq ends, she may afterwards contract another marriage. If it is liked by those of both sections, the first husband indeed who carried out the divorce may also marry her. The ruling if one carries out talaq the second time is also like this indeed. If it is either because of not taking back in the 'iddah period, or on account of its having been a talaq taking a recompense ( **خلع** ), as will be said below, that wife and husband become separated from each other, it is **بَيْنُونَة صغرى** (small separation) that it will be called in technical language. Because, if wanted, even after this separation they may return, by means of a new marriage, *[p. 550]* to the old bond. After the third-time talaq, taking back is not allowed. Unless, as said below, she both contracts another marriage, and he carries out divorce and the 'iddah ends, marrying again is also not allowed. Therefore this separation is also called **بَيْنُونَة كبرى** (big separation).

(4) Carrying out talaq either in the menstrual ( **حَيْض** ) period, or in a period of purity in which union has taken place, has been disallowed. It is called talaq that is an improper custom ( **طَلَاق بدعة** ). Though there is variance of opinion among scholars as to whether this talaq will be valid, there is no doubt in that it is disallowed.

(Two:) **وَلا يَحِلُّلَكُمْأَنْتَأْخُذُوا**..... *[DOUBT: the dots are printed touching the left end of the Arabic, that is, after its last word; whether they belong after the Arabic or before it cannot be told from the page.]* (It is not allowed for you to get back anything from what you have given them.) Wealth that has been given as mahr (as marriage-price) in the marriage, or a part of it, may not be taken back – that is the intended meaning. If anything has been given as a donation, that too is not to be taken back. What the Prophet ﷺ has declared is indeed that taking back afterwards what has once been given as a gift is the disposition of a dog that eats again a thing it has vomited. It is, surely, when there ceases to be harmony between wife and husband that talaq will take place. Then, his wishing to get back something that he himself had given her before will be natural. It is therefore that, when He made a statement about talaq, He specially mentions this matter too here. Even if a person had given his wives a very mighty sum indeed *[DOUBT: printed കൊടുത്തിരുന്നാവും; it may be a slip for കൊടുത്തിരുന്നാലും (“even if he had given”), which the English follows, or be read “a person may have given”. The sense of the sentence is the same either way.]*, that taking back anything from it at the time of divorce is culpable, and is not in keeping with decency, can be seen at Su: Nisa' 20, 21. But, in case she gladly gives up something, there is no objection to accepting it, moreover (Nisa': 4). But, if one sees that the two of them will not live together in unity, both agreeing with each other and fulfilling mutual liabilities, this prohibition is not applicable. That is the gist of having said **إِلَّاأَنْ يَخَافَا-الخ** (Except if they fear that both of them will not establish the legal boundaries of Allah.). What the way out is on this occasion can be understood from the next sentence:-

(Three:) **فَإِنْخِفْتُمْأَلا يُقِيمَا-الخ** (Now, if you fear that both of them will not establish the legal boundaries of Allah, then, there is no wrong for both of them in whatever she frees herself by giving.) In this sentence, the word ‘if you fear’ is specially noteworthy. It is those responsible on both sides, administrators, mediators and so on, whom this addresses. In case it both seems to them that the two of them will not get along together, and there comes to be a need for her that liberation must be attained from him, there is no objection to her both determining to gi*[p. 551]*ve a recompense for the divorce, and his accepting that and giving divorce – that is the import. It is to divorce with a recompense determined in this way that ‘khul'’ ( **خلع** ) is technically said. (\*) Without a fitting reason, a woman may not ask her husband – either determining a recompense, or otherwise – for divorce. In a hadith the Prophet ﷺ has said thus: ‘If any woman, without any trouble, asks her husband for her talaq, the scent of Paradise will become haram upon her.’ (Ibn Jarir; Ti; A; Da.) Basing on this hadith, many great men have expressed the opinion that, when there is no factional quarrel between them, ‘khul'’ is not allowed.

> **Note:** (\*) ‘To strip off, to take off and lay down’ and so on are the meaning in the language of **خلع**.

The wife of Thabit ibn Qais (r) came to the Prophet ﷺ and made known, concerning her husband, both that she herself had no aversion at all in the matter of religion or in disposition, but that (on account of some physical defects of his) it was not possible to live on together with him. The Prophet ﷺ asked her whether she could give back, over to him, the garden that he had given her (as mahr). She replied that she could give. And the Prophet ﷺ asked Thabit (r) to give her talaq, accepting that garden. This incident is one that Bukhari (r) and others have quoted. From this hadith it is understood that it is good that the sum determined as recompense in ‘khul'’ does not exceed the mahr that was given at the time of marriage. Asking for more is not justice and propriety, moreover. Therefore a section of scholars have expressed the opinion that the recompense determined in ‘khul'’ is not allowed to exceed the ‘mahr’. But, there is no wrong for both of them in whatever she frees herself by giving. ( **فَلاجُنَاحَعَلَيْهِمَا فِيمَا افْتَدَتْبِهِ** ) – that, surely, is the word that Allah said. What is suggested from this is that any recompense that she herself gives, so that she may attain liberation from the husband, may be accepted. Nor has it been made clear in the hadith that nothing more may be taken. Therefore, the majority opinion is indeed that there is no objection to determining, as recompense in ‘khul'’, any amount that both parties decide between them. It is said that some Companions too are of this opinion. In any case, asking, from the husband's side, for a sum that cannot be borne, without arriving at an understanding mutually, is injustice and wrongdoing. If ‘khul'’ has taken place, then, the husband's right to take back in the 'iddah following it will be lost.

(Four:) **فَإِنْطَلَّقَهَافَلاتَحِلُّلَهُ -الخ** (Now, if he has carried out divorce of her, until she marries a husband other than him, she will not now be permissible to him.) That is, if, after talaq has passed two rounds before, one carries out talaq a third time too, then there is no question *[p. 552]* of taking back. It is needed both that another husband marries her, and that he carries out divorce of her, and that the 'iddah according to law ends. Only then is it allowed for him afterwards to marry her again. Its having been said, not as ‘until another person marries her’ or something, but as ‘until she marries another husband ( **حَتَّىتَنْكِحَ زَوْجًاغَيْرَهُ** )’, is noteworthy. That that marriage must be a marriage in the ordinary course – must not be either by the compulsion or by the request of the first husband or someone – is what it shows. Though Allah has said only ‘until marrying’, the Prophet ﷺ has made clear that in that marriage union must also have taken place between them. (Bu; Mu) From the sentence **فَإِنْطَلَّقَهَافَلا جُنَاحَ -الخ** (Then, in case he carries out divorce of her, there is no wrong in both of them coming back to each other), that, just like that nikah (marriage) indeed, that talaq too must be one that took place in the ordinary course – must not be yielding to some other compulsion or request – can also be understood. There is no need, surely, to specially mention both that, once talaq is over, then the 'iddah following it too needs to end, and that that husband needs to not take her back.

If all this has become right, there is still one condition. Both must have the expectation that that wife and husband, to whose bond, after the marriage bond took place between them, disruption came about three times, will henceforth live on with each other in amity, keeping within the legal limit of Allah. In case either both, or one of the two, have no such expectation, it is not right for them to enter into the marriage bond again. From the sentence **إِنْظَنَّاأَنْ يُقِيمَاحُدُودَاللَّهِ** (If both consider that they themselves will keep on establishing the boundaries of Allah) this is evident. They, who have fallen out and parted three times, need, surely, to heed this matter specially the fourth time. Just reflect on the guidance that the Creator, who knows the psychology of mankind more than mankind, gives in each sentence for the good of man! It will become clear to anyone that it is not to the outward state – it is to the real state of mind – that there is consideration here. If it is with what is lodged in the mind being something else that the two of them set up the marriage bond between them again – though the marriage may be valid in the eye of the law – they will be guilty before Allah. In this world, for its part, that bond will serve only to produce unease for them more than before.

There is in vogue among Muslims today a habit of those who, after divorcing three times, intend to marry those women again, carrying out some tricks for getting past the conditions seen above that Allah has clearly stated. Some sections of scholars also keep on both abetting and giving backing to it. Influencing some fools or other by giving a recompense or something, conducting a nominal marriage outwardly, at the very next opportunity having them carry out divorce too. There is also the getting ready for it of some *[p. 553]* fools of themselves indeed, yearning for the recompense. It is by the name ‘standing for the ceremony’ that this marriage is known. This name indeed proclaims both that it is disregarding the law of Allah and that it is turning it into merely a ceremony. Both without fitting reason, and without second thought about the future, one will just carry out two and three talaqs and so on at a single stroke. On the very next day, one will also be in regret about it. The next deliberation will be about this accursed ceremony. In this way, from licentiousness to licentiousness, they infringe all the legal limits of Allah. If it were as Allah commanded that talaq took place from the very beginning, need would not have been faced for any licentious acts such as these. In any case, from what has been explained above it can be clearly understood that this standing for the ceremony, and making stand for the ceremony, and the marriage that takes place on its basis, both are not right, and are violating the laws of Allah. Not only that, a Muslim who has in memory the sentence that the Prophet ﷺ said about this ceremony – if true belief has taken a place in his heart – will certainly indeed not favour this ceremony. Asking ‘Shall I make known to you about the bull taken on loan?’, the Prophet ﷺ is saying to the Companions: ‘It is the one who stands for the ceremony. May Allah curse both the one who stands for the ceremony, and the one for whom one stands for the ceremony!’ (Ja; Ba; Ha) Some other riwayahs too like this can be seen.

Allah has strongly warned both that all the rulings contained in these verses are strict rulings, in such a way that there is no loophole for letting go, and that violating or getting past them will become a cause for the punishment and the wrath and curse of Allah. As indicated before, He said, repeating four or five times, that they are the legal boundaries of Allah. Both that it is those who violate that boundary who are the wrongdoers, at the end of the first verse, and that it is for the people who know that He explains all this, at the end of the second verse, He has reminded.

Now – as indicated above – we may speak two words more about the practice of carrying out all three talaqs at a single stroke at the very same time. In both that, as seen in the ayah, each talaq being on a separate time is the actual form of talaq, and that it is so that one needs to act, and that acting against it is not right, no Muslim whatever who believes in the Quran has a right to have dispute. Even so, if one carries out all three talaqs together like that at one stroke – be doing so haram in the eye of the law, or be it not – how may it be brought into effect? In this matter, what there is among scholars is differing opinions. Nor are those opinions ones that came about today and yesterday. They are ones existing from more or less the time of the Companions indeed. So many factional quarrels, and accusations and counter-accusations, have taken place concerning this in the past. Even now they take place. *[p. 554]* In case one explains, even if only in general, the opinions among scholars, and the explanations based on the words used for talaq, and the justifications of each opinion, and the proofs, and the replies from the opposite side, that will become a separate book indeed. Some have also composed separate books like that. As far as we are concerned, there is neither place nor occasion for it here. Nor is explanation going out of the circle of ideas of the Quran verses fitting for us. Along with that, those who have not heard and come to know, even if only in general, about the arguments and counter-arguments in this matter will also be very few in this time. Therefore there is no intention to venture upon any explanation here. Incidentally, we may make do with quoting only a précis of the main opinions that exist:-

If one says all three talaqs together in one and the same statement, what there is among scholars about its validity is mainly four opinions:

(1) Not even one will be valid. Because it is a talaq that is bid'ah (an improper custom). On the matter too of whether this falls within Zahiri ( **الظاهرى** ) talaq, and, even if it does fall, whether it will be valid, scholars have no unanimous opinion. *[DOUBT: the sentence is printed so (ള്വാഹിരീ ( الظاهرى )ത്വലാക്വിൽ ഇത് പെടുമോ …); its construction is unclear. It may speak of a kind of talaq called “Zahiri”, or ള്വാഹിരീ ( الظاهرى ) may stand apart as the name of those who hold opinion (1), with the rest asking whether this falls within talaq at all. Rendered literally.]*

(2) Only one will be valid. Both the remaining two will be void. Therefore, it will not be an obstruction to taking back in the 'iddah period. What is generally understood both from the Quran and from the hadiths, and what agrees with the basic principles of talaq, is this opinion. Some belonging both among the Companions and among the great men who were predecessors are of this opinion. (\*) It is this opinion that scholars such as Shaikhul Islam Ibn Taimiyyah, Ibn Qayyim (r) too have endorsed. It is for this, too, that the support of proof is seen more.

> **Note:** (\*) **راجع فتح الباري وغيره** *[DOUBT: the footnote is in Arabic only, with no Malayalam rendering. It most likely means: “See Fath al-Bari and others.” Transcribed as printed.]*

(3) All three will be valid. Therefore it is not allowed either to take back afterwards, or to marry again except after another person has married, and talaq and 'iddah have ended. The opinion of the majority of scholars both in former times and in later times is this. In the main madhhabs too it is this opinion that has been adopted.

(4) If union has taken place between husband and wife, all three will be valid. In case it has not, only one will be valid. It is seen that the opinion of both many who were disciples of Ibn 'Abbas (r), and of Ishaq ibn Rahawaihi (r), is this.

This is the summary of those opinions. Those who wish for more explanation need to inquire and come to know from the proper places. There is a wish to quote here the gist of a statement that, after explaining about the variances of opinion seen above, 'Allamah Muhammad ibn Isma'il San'ani (r), the interpreter of the famous hadith book ‘Bulughul Maram’, made. He says: *[p. 555]* ‘Those who hold discussions on subsidiary matters have spoken at length on this matter. Basing on 'Umar (r) having put all three talaqs (\*) into effect (\*\*), the people of the four madhhabs have all agreed in it. Their protest about those who disagree with it is very severe. For the reason of having given fatwa (pronounced a ruling) that only one will be valid, both Shaikhul Islam Ibn Taimiyyah (r) suffering beating, and the mounting of his disciple Hafiz Ibnul Qayyim (r) on a camel's back and taking him round the land, have come about. There is no need to say that this is only an intense partisan state of mind in a subsidiary matter. This is a matter in which both the predecessors and the successors of the community have variance of opinion. That being so, the fact that, in matters in which there is difference of opinion, there is nothing to reproach about those who favour some one or other is famous. It is in occasions like these that the just ones, who are thinkers and God-fearing, and those who are not, become distinguished from each other.’

> **Note:** (\*) What is intended is three talaqs carried out on one and the same occasion.

> **Note:** (\*\*) That it was to teach them a lesson, because the people had begun to make haste in the matter of talaq, that he put into effect like that, can be seen in that same riwayah indeed.
