*[Verse 144]*

*[p. 399]* This is the command that changed the temporary rule which had accepted Bait al-Muqaddas as qiblah and, in its place, appointed the Ka'bah as the permanent qiblah. Because of an intense yearning that it would be good if the qiblah of the Prophet Ibrahim (a) were received as the qiblah of the Muslims, and because of the disquiet from the Jews, saying that it is without accepting their religion that their qiblah has been accepted, the Prophet ﷺ, expecting some wahy from Allah, used to look upward eagerly From the word **تَقَلُّبَ** (to keep turning and overturning) having been used, it is understood that the Prophet ﷺ often did so, and that, except for keeping the wish suppressed in his mind, he used not to speak of it outwardly. We do see all that. We will fulfil that wish for you; the qiblah that you long for indeed, We will appoint for you – it is with a preface of this kind, signalling gladness, that Allah makes this command known to the Prophet ﷺ.

It was in the second year of the Hijrah that this command came down. That what the Prophet ﷺ first prayed after this, turning toward the Ka'bah, was the 'Asr prayer – it has come in the hadith that Bukhari and Muslim (r) have quoted. Because the mosque at Quba, which the Prophet ﷺ established first of all, is situated two or three nazhika away from Madinah, the information was received there while the jama'ah of the Subh prayer was taking place. That is – as said before – the reason for their turning toward the Ka'bah in the Subh prayer. The prayer being done while turned toward the qiblah is obligatory, surely. There are some occasions when, outside the prayer too, turning toward the qiblah is good. That it is needed while making supplication and while laying the dead in the grave can be understood from hadiths.

Because it has been said that, wherever one may be, one must turn the face toward it (**وَحَيْثُمَا كُنْتُمْ فَوَلُّوا** .....), *[DOUBT: on p. 399 the printed "....." may belong before or after this Arabic phrase (it stands at the left end of the Arabic, just inside the bracket); placed after, as on p. 388.]* it becomes clear that, between those who are near and those who are far, *[p. 400]* in this matter, there is no difference. But, as regards travellers, a relaxation in this rule in the sunnah prayers can be seen in hadiths. Must the one who is praying turn so precisely that, if he went straight forward, he would come and strike against the building of the Ka'bah? Or else. Is it enough if it is roughly such as to be toward the side of the Ka'bah? This is a subject on which there has been difference of opinion among scholars from early on. There is no doubt that what is needed is to be as precise as possible. But, as some scholars say and practise, it is not, moreover, that each of the prayers will be right only if exactness is made certain through scientific observations and instruments. However, there is no doubt either that those who have precise information and those who are in places near the Ka'bah must turn to it exactly indeed. That is why, when praying in jama'ah in al-Masjid al-Haram, the people, instead of standing behind the imam in straight rows, stand in circling rows, putting the Ka'bah and the imam in the middle. Otherwise, the prayer of only a few people would be toward the Ka'bah, and that of the others not so, surely. When praying inside the Ka'bah, one may also turn to any side.

Allah says that the People of the Scripture certainly know that this command is the real from Allah. What is meant by this may be the knowledge that they have received, from their scriptures and from prophets, concerning the characteristics of the noble Prophet ﷺ and of those who believe in the noble Prophet. Besides, it is not unknown to the People of the Scripture that both the house of worship first established for worshipping Allah (\*) and the qiblah of the Prophet Ibrahim (a) were the Ka'bah, and that it is the way of the Prophet Ibrahim (a) that the Prophet ﷺ has to follow. In any case – as seen next in the 146th verse – it is while having knowledge, and still covering it up, that they venture into accusations and trouble. The warning that it is not good for them, and that they will have to experience its fruit, is the sentence at the end, ‘Allah is not unmindful at all’ about what they do. Allah, continuing, says:

> **Note:** (\*) It was more or less 4000 years before the Quran came down that the founding of the Ka'bah took place. It was only roughly more than 1000 years before that Bait al-Muqaddas was founded.

*[Verses 145–147]*

*[p. 402]* If it were because of not understanding the truth and the reality that the People of the Scripture do not favour the matter of the qiblah, it would have been possible to make them understand the matter by convincing them with sign and proof. But what they are doing is deliberately covering it up while knowing the real properly. That being so, there is no fruit, surely, in showing them proofs. Then, in that case, can the Prophet ﷺ keep on following their qiblah indeed? No. The qiblah of the Jews is Bait al-Muqaddas, and that of the Christians the east side. Even if it is supposed that one may follow, it is not possible to accept other than one of the two. Now, will the People of the Scripture agree among themselves and one group become ready to accept the qiblah of the other group? That too is not going to happen. In brief, in the matter of the qiblah, there is no question of the Prophet and the People of the Scripture agreeing in any way. They will not agree among themselves either.

That it was said, addressing the Prophet ﷺ, that if you follow their desires you will be among the wrongdoers, and that you must not become one in the company of those who doubt, is not on the footing that some wavering may come about in the Prophet ﷺ. What is meant by it is to make known to them that they need not keep holding the delusive hope that on some occasion the Prophet ﷺ may give in to their liking and yearning, or that, just as Bait al-Muqaddas was accepted as qiblah for a little time, some change may happen in this command once again hereafter, or some such thing. It also serves for the true believers to understand the seriousness of this command.

For the pronoun (called **هُ**) in the word **يَعۡرِفُونَهُۥ**, the meaning ‘him’ and the meaning ‘it’ can both come. Therefore, meaning is commonly assigned to that sentence in two ways

(1) Him – the Prophet ﷺ – they will know just as they know their own children – thus. That is, the noble Prophet is Allah's prophet. Everything that he brings is real – such things they know without doubt – that is the import. It is this meaning that most commentators have adopted. Also what we have given in the translation. It is in the sense of knowing without a trace of doubt that it is said that they know as they know their children. Just as in Arabic, this usage is not lacking in other languages too. Given that nothing has been stated about the Prophet ﷺ before this, how the Prophet ﷺ is meant by that pronoun – it may be asked. *[p. 403]* That it can be understood by the context, and that it is that which is understood from the simile, as they know their children, is the reply to this.

(2) It – the matter of the qiblah – they know well, as they know their children – thus. That is. That the Ka'bah is the qiblah of Ibrahim (a), and that it is that which has to be the qiblah of the Prophet ﷺ too, they know properly – that is the gist. There are other opinions too, roughly like this. All are ones that culminate in one same principle indeed. Whether it is about the Prophet ﷺ or about the qiblah, it is, surely, the scholars among the People of the Scripture who would have that knowledge. The common people would have only some hearsay from them. Therefore, it is pointing to the scholars among them that it was said, ‘what a section of people from among them are doing is hiding the real while knowing’.

### Section – 18

*[Verse 148]*

*[p. 404]* For all the people of religions and people of sections, such as the Jewish people and the Christians, there will be, for each, a place of aim – a qiblah – for turning the face in their respective acts of worship. Each section will turn toward that aim of their own. Therefore, without looking at the favour or disfavour of others, in the matter of the qiblah and in all other matters indeed, in order to gain what is more excellent and beneficial – as if with zeal against one another – strive to advance hastily. Whoever, wherever, however it may be, all right, Allah will not fail to gather everyone together on the Day of Qiyamah and take fitting action upon everyone too. There is not one matter that He is not able to do.

*[Verses 149–150]*

*[p. 405]* With how much seriousness Allah has taken the matter of the qiblah into account can be understood from these verses. Think over, with the mind applied, the sentences said again and again, and the principles pointed out in between them. The place that the qiblah has among the emblems of religion, and the fierceness of the opposition that showed itself from the enemy camps following the change of qiblah, can be grasped from it. First, in the 144th verse, with the information that the Prophet ﷺ's wish would be fulfilled, the noble Prophet was commanded to turn toward al-Masjid al-Haram. Then, after many matters connected with it were explained, in these verses, addressing the Prophet ﷺ, that command has been repeated again twice. Along with that, addressing the true believers as well, it is repeated a fourth time too.

Repeating words or sentences in order to remind the hearers of the importance of the subject being stated is a custom commonly accepted in languages. In the Arabic language especially, this custom can be seen. Many reasons for reaffirming this subject by repetition in so strong a form Allah has brought out and shown in these verses. The people of all religions have a qiblah each. Likewise, for the Muslims too a qiblah is needed. It must be more excellent *[p. 406]* and advantageous than all – such things were indicated in the 148th verse. That this is a needful real thing that you receive from Allah was pointed out in 149 too. That it is in order that the opposing parties may not bring any argument against the Muslims, in order that Allah's favour may be completed for them, and in order that they may attain right guidance – this has been pointed out in 150 too. If one examines with a little application of the mind, many other indications too can be found in these verses. For this subject to deserve so much seriousness, there was another reason too in the surroundings of that time. This change of qiblah was the first ‘naskh’ (changing an existing ruling and putting another ruling into effect in its place) that took place in the religious laws of observance. Concerning the fierce oppositions that arose from the enemies of Islam in the name of naskh, and the confusions it brought about among some Muslims who were guileless, we have seen in the 106th verse and in its commentary, surely.

The gist of saying that from all places gone out to one must turn toward al-Masjid al-Haram (**وَمِنۡ حَيۡثُ خَرَجۡتَ** ...) *[DOUBT: on p. 406 the printed "..." may belong before or after this Arabic phrase (it stands at the left end of the Arabic, just inside the bracket); placed after, as on p. 388.]* is that, wherever one goes on a journey, from all those places one must turn toward the Ka'bah in the prayer; and the gist of saying that one must turn from all places where you may be (**وَحَيْثُمَا كُنْتُمْ**) is that, whether you are near the Ka'bah or far, whether in a place where the Ka'bah can be seen or where it cannot be seen, one must do so. If Bait al-Muqaddas is appointed as the permanent qiblah, occasion will arise for the People of the Scripture to say such things as: why is it that these imitate our qiblah, and are these not ones who should make the Ka'bah the qiblah? That, while claiming that Muhammad's way is the way of Ibrahim (a), along with that he abandons the qiblah of Ibrahim (a) – this and so on the mushriks too may say. It is indicating all this that it has been said, in order that mankind may not have a justification against you( **لِئَلَا يَكُونَ لِلنَّاسِ** ). But, as far as people who are obstinate and who have set themselves to denial with evil intent are concerned, truth and justification have no place. Whether the qiblah is changed or not, whether it is the Ka'bah or not, it is natural that they – if not one thing, another – keep raising accusations. It is about people of this kind that it was said, except the wrongdoers from among them(**إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ظَلَمُواْ** ......) *[DOUBT: on p. 406 the printed "......" may belong before or after this Arabic phrase (it stands at the left end of the Arabic, just inside the bracket); placed after, as on p. 388.]*.

There is no need to give ear to any of such reproaches and accusations; they need only be ignored and dismissed. Nothing is going to happen on account of them; none of them need be feared ; it is Allah alone who must be feared. The regulating and appointing of your religious laws for you is a favour that He graciously bestows on you; through it you can attain the straight way and gain success – thus and so on, alerting the true believers, Allah continues:

*[Verses 151–152]*

*[p. 407]* In the course of stating the principles contained in having made the Ka'bah the qiblah, it was stated in the last verse, surely, that it is in order that I may complete My favour for you and that you may attain the straight way. Not only that; I have done for you another mighty favour too. This is only one side of that favour. To show you right guidance, I have sent you a Rasul. That is a greater favour than this – thus and so on, in this verse, Allah alerts the true believers. Continuing, He points out what the Rasul's *[p. 408]* circumstances and functions are. After that, He reminds that, since I have done for you so mighty a favour, you must always be ones who have remembrance of Me, and must never show ingratitude toward Me, and that its fruit is indeed for you.

Because about the matters stated as the duties of the Prophet ﷺ – reciting to you the ayahs of Allah, refining, teaching the scripture and wisdom: about these – it has been explained before, in the account of the supplication of Ibrahim (a) (at the 129th verse), it is not repeated here. That it was as the answer to his supplication that the Rasul ﷺ was commissioned – this too we saw there, surely. What is meant by saying, he will teach you what you did not use to know, too (**وَيُعَلِّمُكُمْ مَا لَمْ تَكُونُوا تَعْلَمُونَ**), is indeed that many matters which cannot be known otherwise than through wahy and by means of a prophet – by intellect or otherwise – you are able to know by means of him.

The gist of a hadith that Imam Ahmad (r) has quoted is like this : Allah says: ‘Son of Adam, if you keep Me in mind in your own self (in the mind), I will keep you too in mind in My own self. If you keep Me in mind in a company (if you state), I too will keep you in mind in a company more excellent than that (in the company of the angels). If you draw near to Me by a span, I will draw near to you by a cubit. If you draw near to Me by a cubit, I will draw near to you by a fathom. If you come to Me (slowly) walking, I will come to you (quickly) running.’ Another hadith of this meaning can be seen in both Bukhari and Muslim.
