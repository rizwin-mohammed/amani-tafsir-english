*\[Verse 126\]*

*\[p. 368\]* It is requests for two matters that are contained in this supplication. One, to make the country of Makkah a secure country, and the other, to give provision by kinds of fruit to its inhabitants. About the first, it was described, surely, in the interpretation of the previous verse. About the second, it may be described further on. In Surah Ibrahim too it can be seen stated that he prayed for these two matters. But – as Ibn Kathir (r) and others have pointed out – from the differences in the words used there and here, it is understood that these two supplications were both made by him on two occasions. It can be understood from the sentences in that surah that it was after settling his wife Hajar and son Isma'il (a) in Makkah and building the Ka'bah that he made that supplication. What is quoted here may perhaps also be the supplication made immediately after first taking them to Makkah and placing them there. That at that time there was no habitation of people or watering place or anything there, and that it was later that all that came about, is also known from the Quran and from hadith. It may be because of that that he said, here in the supplication, Make this a secure country ( **ٱجۡعَلۡ هَٰذَا بَلَدًا ءَامِنٗا** ) and, there in that supplication, Make this country a secure country ( **ٱجۡعَلۡ هَٰذَا ٱلۡبَلَدَ ءَامِنٗا** ). That is, it has to be considered that at the time of this supplication it was not a country with habitation of people, and that the second supplication will have come about after habitation of people had begun there and it had become such that it could be called a country. (Allah knows)

The second request in the supplication is: Grant the people of that country the means of provision by kinds of fruit ( **وَٱرۡزُقۡ أَهۡلَهُۥ مِنَ ٱلثَّمَرَٰتِ** ). When he prayed that leadership should be maintained in his progeny too, Allah, surely, alerted him that leadership will not be given to those who are wrongdoers. Therefore, perhaps, he may have thought that praying for provision to be given to everyone would become an impropriety. It may be for that reason that he specially singled out: to those of the people of that country who believed in Allah and the Last Day ( **مَنۡ ءَامَنَ مِنۡهُم بِٱللَّهِ وَٱلۡيَوۡمِ ٱلۡأٓخِرِ** ). (Allah knows) But Allah made him understand that the matter of provision is not like that of leadership, and that in the matter of giving provision there is no difference of believer–unbeliever, just one–wrongdoer. That is what the sentence ....(provision may be given to those who disbelieved too) indicates. But, that it need not be considered that, because all are alike in the matter of provision, they will be equals in other matters too, *\[p. 369\]* Allah also points out along with that ..... **فَأُمَتِّعُهُۥ قَلِيلٗا ثُمَّ** (But I will make him enjoy comfort a little, and afterwards compel him to come to Hell) Yes, as far as the true believers are concerned, this ill fortune will not come about. The more or less that is received in provision and in comforts and conveniences is not on the basis of a person's good and evil; it is what Allah determines – on the basis of some secrets known only to Him – that is the import.

It was, surely, Give provision from kinds of fruit – that he prayed. Although the word **ثَّمَرَات** is sometimes said intending all kinds of fruit-produce, it is ordinarily used for the fruits from trees. In the sentence in Su. Ibrahim, it is by saying, Our Rabb, I have settled some of my progeny in a valley that has no cultivated land, near Your house ( **رَّبَّنَآ إِنِّيٓ أَسۡكَنتُ مِن ذُرِّيَّتِي بِوَادٍ غَيۡرِ ذِي زَرۡعٍ عِندَ بَيۡتِكَ ٱلۡمُحَرَّمِ** ), that he has prayed, Give them provision from kinds of fruit. Arabia, surely, is a country that has no water facilities and cultivation facilities. (\*) That a watering place first came about in Makkah with the appearing of Zamzam ( **زمزم** ) can be understood from hadiths and elsewhere. That is why he prayed in that way. In a hadith that Bukhari (r) quoted, ‘At that time the people of that country there had no grain. If they had had it, he would have prayed for them for that too.’ ( **وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ حَبٌّ وَلَوْ كَانَ لَهُمْ دَعَا لَهُمْ فِيهِ** ) – thus the Prophet ﷺ has stated. It need not be said that Allah has accepted this supplication of the Prophet Ibrahim (a) too. The country of Ta'if ( **طَائِف** ), situated about fifty nazhika away from Makkah, is a very fruit-abundant region. From there, and from the other regions in the surroundings, and from distant lands such as Palestine, Sham, all kinds of fruit have been imported into Makkah at all times from long before. As for today, all kinds of food resources keep flowing into Makkah even from very distant regions. In Su. Qasas Allah says: **أَوَلَمۡ نُمَكِّن لَّهُمۡ حَرَمًا ءَامِنٗا يُجۡبَىٰٓ إِلَيۡهِ ثَمَرَٰتُ كُلِّ شَيۡءٖ رِّزۡقٗا مِّن لَّدُنَّا** -**القصص: ٥٧** (Have We not made a secure Haram convenient for them? The fruits of all things are collected and brought there as provision from Our presence) More details can be seen in Surah Ibrahim. **إن شاء الله**

> **Note:** (\*) Although now some watering places have come about here and there, and cultivated places on a small scale have come into existence making use of them, in general even today the Arabian peninsula is not a farming country.

*\[Verses 127–129\]*

*\[p. 371\]* These are the supplications that the Prophet Ibrahim (a) and the Prophet Isma'il (a) made when building up the Ka'bah edifice. Not only the foundation of the Ka'bah – they also built the wall on top of it and completed it. What is understood from these verses is that the supplication too was taking place indeed when building the foundation. That, surely, is the most fitting occasion to pray, Accept this deed of ours, and so on. That it was to the Prophet Ibrahim (a) that the command to build the Ka'bah was received, and that, as he asked, his son Isma'il (a) cooperated in it, and that what was being done was that Isma'il (a) would gather stones and bring and give them, and Ibrahim (a) would take them and lay the wall – has been stated in hadiths. In this supplication, it is the six requests seen below that they have requested of Allah. From each one there are many things for us to understand. It was indeed for that, surely, that Allah quoted all that, too. These are they :

(1) **رَبَّنَا تَقَبَّلۡ مِنَّآ**....... (Rabb, accept this deed from us) – that is it. Even if the thing being done is commanded and is a meritorious deed, only if Allah accepts it will the reward for it be received. For many reasons, whether such as can be seen outwardly or otherwise, there is a likelihood of it not becoming acceptable with Allah. That Allah, *\[p. 372\]* in the midst of describing the qualities of the righteous, has stated: when giving any charity, they will give it with their respective hearts trembling at the thought that they are ones who return to their Rabb ( **وَٱلَّذِينَ يُؤۡتُونَ مَآ ءَاتَواْ**...... ) (23:60) – is indeed worth remembering.

(2) **وَمِن ذُرِّيَّتِنَآ أُمَّةٗ مُّسۡلِمَةٗ لَّكَ** *\[TN: The Arabic printed here is the same as that of request (3) below. The Malayalam rendering beside it, “make the two of us submissive ones to You – Muslims –”, is that of رَبَّنَا وَاجْعَلْنَا مُسْلِمَيْنِ لَكَ (2:128), evidently the words intended.\]* (Make the two of us submissive ones to You – Muslims –) – it is that. Make sincere ones who do not yield to anyone's command contrary to Your injunctions and prohibitions, who worship You alone – that is the gist. When those great men, who were not only Muslims but prophets too, pray in this way, consider how mighty the need for this supplication is as far as people who, like us, are sinners and immersed in faults and offences are concerned ! It is not enough for a person to be outwardly a Muslim from the legal point of view; he needs to be indeed a real Muslim, wholly subdued to Allah. It is not enough to have once become a Muslim; that Islam needs to continue until death. Allah says **وَلَا تَمُوتُنَّ إِلَّا وَأَنتُم مُّسۡلِمُونَ** (Do not die except while being Muslims)

(3) **وَمِن ذُرِّيَّتِنَآ أُمَّةٗ مُّسۡلِمَةٗ لَّكَ** (Make from our progeny too a community subdued to You – a community that is Muslims –) – thus. It is not enough to pray only for oneself; one needs to pray also for one's own children and family, and for the future generation. Each one must have the yearning and the interest that they too should become good. That in supplication it is for oneself that one must pray first, and that it is afterwards that one must pray for others, and so on, can be understood from this. In the course of stating many of the special qualities of the righteous, Allah says in one place: **وَٱلَّذِينَ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا هَبۡ لَنَا مِنۡ أَزۡوَٰجِنَا**....... (Our Rabb, grant us coolness of eyes from our wives and from our progeny, and make us foremost ones for the God-fearing – they will also be ones who say so. (25:74) When Allah made known, I will make you a leader for the people, we saw, surely, also that the Prophet Ibrahim (a) prayed that leadership be maintained in his progeny too. Although in the word our progeny ( **ذُرِّيَّتِنَآ** ) the Israelites, who are the line of descendants of Ishaq (a), and the Arabs, who are the line of descendants of Isma'il (a), will be included – looking at the context – it should be considered that this supplication is realised chiefly in the case of the Arabs. That the Muslim community has continued, too, is chiefly indeed through them, surely.

(4) **وَأَرِنَا مَنَاسِكَنَا** (Show us our rites of worship) – that is it. Although the word meaning of **مَنَاسِكَ** is rites of worship, it is used also specially intending the rites of worship relating to Hajj. When the context is considered, here too *\[p. 373\]* the greater likelihood seen is that this is the meaning. (Allah knows) That knowledge and permission need to be received indeed from Him as to how the form and the natures of the rites of worship done for Allah must be, and that it is not enough for man to give them a design himself – can be understood from this supplication. **لا يعبد الله الا بما شرع** (Worship must not be done to Him except in the manner Allah has ordained) – this is an accepted principle in Islam. That is also why all forms of worship that Allah or His Rasul has not shown and approved for us become improper practices.

(5) **وَتُبۡ عَلَيۡنَآ** (Accept our repentance) – that is it. When even prophets are accustomed to seek forgiveness of sins and to keep turning back to Allah in repentance, consider how much attention we need to pay in that matter! The Prophet ﷺ has said thus: ‘O mankind, turn back to Allah in repentance. I indeed keep repenting a hundred times daily.’ (Mu)

(6) **رَبَّنَا وَٱبۡعَثۡ فِيهِمۡ رَسُولٗا**.... (Send for them a Rasul from among them) – it is the request that begins thus. This supplication arose from the fervour of those great men that there should be an excellent community in their future progeny, living subdued to Allah. It is, surely, the divine messenger who is appointed to give counsel of truth to a people being a person familiar to that same people that will be good in many respects. Allah has fulfilled this supplication of the Prophet Ibrahim (a) through the sending of the noble Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. **انا دعوة ابي ابراهيم** (I am the supplication of my father Ibrahim) – that the Prophet ﷺ stated this has come in hadiths. On occasions that remind of the favour of having sent the noble Prophet ﷺ, it is by describing the Prophet ﷺ with the very same qualities that he singled out as the distinctive qualities of the Rasul in this supplication of Ibrahim (a) that Allah usually reminds of it. See verse 151 below, and Surah Jumu'ah verse 2)

At the time when Ibrahim (a) made that supplication, neither a people nor a community had yet come into existence in the Makkah surroundings. It was only going to come in the future. Allah, surely, is the One who knows best what the aim of each supplication is and which is the occasion on which it should be fulfilled. Thus after that supplication – after roughly four thousand years – Allah, as the fruit of that supplication, sent the noble Prophet ﷺ in Arabia as a Rasul. From this one thing needs to be specially understood. It is not necessary that the answer to supplications be received indeed then and there. It is at the proper time that Allah will carry out each one. That is also why the Prophet ﷺ has advised the true believers that one must not make haste in supplications, and that a supplication in which one does not make haste will receive an answer. (Bu.Mu)

The distinctive qualities that Ibrahim (a) singled out in the supplication as the special features of the Rasul, and with which Allah, in the Quran, in more than one place, has described the noble Rasul *\[p. 374\]* ﷺ – those distinctive qualities are these:

(1) Recite in their hearing the ayahs of Allah – the evidences *\[DOUBT: ലക്ഷ്യങ്ങൾ, here and in ലക്ഷ്യദൃഷ്ടാന്തങ്ങൾ just after it and in the word table of verse 129: "evidences" or "aims"; the same open question as parts 01–02, 07 and 17\]* and signs – ( **يَتۡلُواْ عَلَيۡهِمۡ ءَايَٰتِكَ** ). To explain to them the needed evidences and signs concerning the oneness of Allah, the life after death and the like – that is the gist.

(2) Teach them the scripture ( **وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ** ) It is through the Holy Quran that this description is fulfilled.

(3) Teach them wisdom too ( **وَٱلۡحِكۡمَةَ** ) ‘Sagacity,’ sagacious principle, wisdom – it is such as these that are usually assigned as the word meaning of **حِكْمَةَ** (hikmah). What is meant by it is to know and to act on each matter in its own respective place and in its true form. The Prophet ﷺ teaching ‘hikmah’ to the people is fulfilled through the practice which is his sunnah. It is, surely, through his word and through his works that the noble Prophet has explained to the community the practical form of the messages and the legal directions received from Allah, and the order of life in accordance with them. That is his sunnah, too. As some lovers of the material think, it is not the material sciences or scientific principles that are meant by it.

(4) Refine them ( **وَيُزَكِّيهِمۡ** ) The import of this is not that the Prophet ﷺ brought about merely some few worldly reforms of life. Not only in beliefs and practices, or in character, or in conduct, or in dealings – the refinement that the noble Prophet brought about was one that was pervasive in all spheres of human life – without distinction as important or unimportant. This is a historical truth that even the enemies of Islam cannot deny. If examples of this are lined up, it will go on very long. Merely one or two small examples only may be pointed out here:

Salman (r) is saying : ‘Some among the mushriks said thus mockingly : Your man (the noble Prophet) is seen teaching you even the voiding of excrement and urine, surely.’ I said: ‘Right, he has commanded us that we must not face the qiblah (at the time of voiding), and that we must not do the cleansing with the right hand, and that we must not make do with fewer than three stones – there must not be dung or bone among them –, (A.Mu) The gist of a hadith that 'Urwat ibn Zubair (r) quoted from 'A'ishah (r) is thus: ‘Marriage in the Jahiliyyah used to take place in four ways. One of them is the marriage that people adopt today. That is, a person proposes marriage to another for his daughter or for one who is under his authority, gives mahr and marries. Another : a man, when his wife becomes clean from menstruation, saying to her, Go to such-and-such a man and lead married life, sends her, and afterwards he does not approach her. Thus, after she has conceived from that man, if he likes, he will *\[p. 375\]* accept her. It is so that the child may come to have ability and worthiness that this is done. (\*) Another marriage is this : fewer than ten men go to a woman and have intercourse with her. When she has conceived and the delivery is over, she calls them and has them come, and determines that the child is that of such-and-such a one among them. Thus the child is reckoned as his. He cannot deny it. The fourth marriage is like this: many approach a woman; whoever goes, she will accept. These are prostitutes. They will also plant a flag at their door to make known that there is entry for anyone. If she conceives and gives birth to a child, calling all together, whoever's child among them the observer's opinion is that it is, it is determined accordingly that it is his. He may not refuse it (\*\*) Thus, when Muhammad ﷺ was sent with the truth, he demolished all the Jahiliyyah marriages other than this (first) marriage that is current among the people today.’ (Bukhari)

> **Note:** (\*) It is only to those who are leaders and worthy men that they usually send their own wife in this way their reckoning is that the child born from them will have ability and intelligence and the like **فتح البارى**

> **Note:** (\*\*) The last two are in existence, though rarely, even today among some uncivilised groups. Only, there will be some slight differences.

Observe the last sentence of each one of these three verses containing the supplications of the Prophet Ibrahim (a). In the first, praying that their deed be accepted, it is said, ‘O Allah, You are the One who hears all and knows’. In the second, praying that we be made Muslims, and that there be a community that is Muslims in our offspring too, and so on, it is also said, ‘O Allah, You are the One who greatly accepts repentance and the Ever-Merciful’. In the third, requesting that all that is needed be done to make them an excellent community by way of sending a Rasul among them in the future, it is also said, ‘O Allah, You are the Majestic and the Profoundly Knowing’. It can be understood from this, too, that at the time of supplication it is good to praise Him, as befits the occasion, by way of the excellent attributes of Allah through which the matters prayed for are usually accomplished, and that it is the propriety of supplication. This matter is comprehensible from other Quran verses too and from sayings of the Prophet.

### Section – 16

*\[Verses 130–132\]*

*\[p. 377\]* When the particle lying next to the verb form **يَرْغَبُ** (yarghab) is **فِي**, its meaning will be to wish, to show contentment, and when it is **عَنْ**, to hate, to show discontent. Here it is in the second form. The gist of these verses can be summarised thus:

‘The beliefs and ideals and the procedures that Ibrahim (a) adopted were as stated above. Yes, his way was the way of Islam. Then, those who abandon that way of his and adopt another way are making themselves fools by themselves. As for Ibrahim (a), he is a leader whom Allah chose and sent in this world to explain the straight way and tawhid to the people. In the Hereafter, he is also one of the good servants of Allah. In short, with the awareness that, Allah being the Lord of all the worlds, living submissive to His commands is man's duty, he was a Muslim fully submissive to all His commands. That is the reason for it. Moreover, he has also made wasiyyah to his children and progeny to live in that same state. So, the Israelites and the Arabs, who are his lines of descendants, are all bound to follow his way. The Israelites, surely, are the progeny of Ya'qub (a), his grandson. Ya'qub (a) indeed had made wasiyyah thus to his children too. Islam is the religion that Allah has appointed and graciously given to you. It is the sole support for all your good: therefore you may die only as true Muslims, without slipping away into any other way; – such and the like was the wasiyyah of both.

Life and death are in Allah's hand; man has no freedom in any of that. That being so, what is the purpose of commanding men, You may die only in such-and-such a state? – it may be asked. As Ibn Jarir (r) has pointed out, the answer to it is this: Never in your lifetime should you let go of Islam. You do not know, surely, when death will arrive. Therefore, whenever death comes, at that time there must be no occasion for you to be not Muslims. If you give occasion, you will become objects of Allah's punishment and wrath.
