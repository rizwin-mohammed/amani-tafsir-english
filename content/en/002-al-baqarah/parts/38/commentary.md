*[Verse 231]*

*[p. 556]* When the 'iddah period that women need to observe following talaq is about to be over, the husband has to do one of two: either, taking her back into the marriage bond, keeping her on. Or, without taking back, releasing her. That taking back may be done at any time before the 'iddah period is over has been understood, surely, from the previous verses. Here, it is on two matters that the subject is leaning: It is when the time-limit of the 'iddah is about to end that one of the two becomes unavoidable. If one wastes the opportunity of the 'iddah, the question of ta*[p. 557]*king back will cease to exist. This is one. The other: no matter which of the two one accepts, it must be in accordance with both justice and propriety. It is that it must not be either with some evil intention or other in mind, or of a kind in which both harm and difficulty come about. It is this matter that is the most important subject in this verse. It can be seen that, if one leaves out the first one or two sentences, the other sentences are all ones centred on this subject.

Sometimes it is in taking back that the goodness of both will lie. Sometimes, as far as the wife is concerned, it is that which will be good. On some occasions it will also be directly the contrary. It is after deliberating, taking into account both the circumstances and the surroundings, both in which the goodness lies and which is what ought to be done, that one must decide on one of the two. Carrying out talaq of the wife as soon as some slight quarrel or other arises, then taking back by the time the 'iddah is over, without much delay, when another reason is got, carrying out talaq again, taking back yet again, thus making her suffer in a state that is neither of the two, or, after taking back, not behaving in a good way, causing lapse in the liabilities that ought to be done for her, in the end her becoming compelled into divorce determining a recompense (into ‘khul'’) – it is a warning about evil practices such as these that the sentence **وَلاتُمْسِكُوهُنَّضِرَارًا-الخ** (Do not hold them back so as to do injury such as exceeds the bound) is.

Allah does not bring the subject to an end with disallowing that one must not take back women for the sake of doing harms of this kind. Doing so is a wrong done to oneself indeed. Or, He also warns that, both in this world and in the next, each one themselves indeed will have to experience its consequence. Not only that, He also indicates both that it is disregarding and mocking the ayahs of Allah, and that it is ingratitude shown both to His favours and to the good counsels that He has given through the Quran or by means of the Prophet ﷺ. At the end He alerts both that one must guard against Allah, and that one must keep in memory that He is One who knows all things secret and open. Then, just deliberate on with how much seriousness Allah has taken this subject into account! When one bears in mind the practice that, even with all this, most of today's Muslims keep on resorting to in this subject, intense sorrow is felt. If the enemies of Islam today carry on propaganda against the matrimonial personal laws in Islam, the real ones responsible for it are none other than the Muslims indeed who throw aside commandments of Allah of this kind into the category of the negligible. It is certain that, not only in married life, but in any other sphere too, in whatever time and in whatever land Muslims live, both observing exactly as they are the laws that Allah and His Rasul have taught and advised and given, and shunning the proceedings that are not in keeping with the principles and aims contained in them, its enemies will have to be at a loss, without getting a weapon for carrying on propaganda against the legal procedures of Islam.

The Prophet ﷺ has declared thus: ‘There are three subjects: in them, earnest too is indeed earnest, jest too is indeed earnest. That is, nikah, and talaq *[p. 558]* (marriage, and divorce), and taking back’ (Da; Ti; Ja.) That is, whether these three subjects take place in earnest, or in jest, legally it will be reckoned as valid indeed – it is not possible to get out of it saying that it took place as a jest – that is the gist. Other hadiths too that make this idea clear can be seen.

### Section – 30

*[Verse 232]*

*[p. 559]* This verse is one that addresses in general both the husbands who carried out divorce, and the managers of the affairs of the women who were divorced, and other responsible persons. Even so, it is to the relatives who carry out the management of the woman's affairs that there is more address. Secondly, to the husbands too who carried out talaq, both that one must not create an obstruction to a woman who has not been taken back in the 'iddah period accepting husbands mutually pleased, in a way that does not infringe the good-custom proprieties, and that one must not hinder them such that obstruction is faced to it – Allah commands both parties. In having said **اَزْوَاجَهُنَّ** (their husbands), both the first husbands who carried out divorce, and those who come forward to take on husbandhood newly, are included. What is important is whether, from both sides, mutual pleasure that is in a good way has come about.

From an incident that Bukhari (r) and others have quoted, the idea in this verse can be understood more clearly. It is like this: Her husband carried out talaq of the sister of Ma'qal ibn Yasar (r). Until the 'iddah was over, without taking back, he let her go. Then, love was felt both by him towards that woman, and by the woman towards him. Afterwards, among those who made a marriage proposal for that woman, he too made a marriage proposal. Then Ma'qal (r) said: ‘O fool! I both honoured you with her, and gave her to you as a mate. Then what you did was carry out divorce of her. It is by Allah indeed! Now, she will never at any time come back to you!’ Ma'qal (r) is saying: ‘Then, Allah came to know the need both of him towards her, and of her towards him. He sent down the (this) verse **وَإِذَاطَلَّقْتُمْ-الخ**. It is in this subject of mine that this came down.’ (Bu; Ti; Ja; Da.)

Even if the cause of the sending down was a particular incident, the ruling in the ayah is indeed a general one. Then, what Allah commands by means of this verse is that, not allowing women to marry husbands for whom mutual pleasure has been felt in accordance with good-custom proprieties, neither their managers (walis) nor anyone else must put them in a fix. For reasons such as greatness of lineage, former enmity, false pride, evil obstinacy, a vengeful state of mind, selfish interest, incidents in which the managers of the women's affairs, and sometimes those among the local people who have influence, and sometimes the husbands who carried out divorce before, and their partisans and all, distress women like this are common. As in the previous verse, in this verse too, look at both the importance that Allah assigns to this subject, and the warning that He gives to those who disregard it! ‘It is to those among you who keep on believing in Allah and in the Day of the End that good counsel is given by means of this’, He first alerts. Its meaning, surely, is that not accepting this advice is because of not having belief properly in Allah and in the Last Day. If one says it in another *[p. 560]* way, those who have belief in Allah and in the Last Day will not fail to observe this advice – that is the gist. Continuing, ‘That is what is both the most thoroughly pure and tidy for you’, He also says. If one does not value this advice, it will become a cause of both abasement and disgrace for you, both in this world and in the next. If one is to get along without that, you need to observe this advice – that is the gist. At the end: Allah knows all; you will not be able to know even concerning your own good and ill. Or, it is therefore that He advises all this much, and also explains all in this way – this too He has alerted. ( **وَاللَّهُيَعْلَمُوَأَنْتُمْلاتَعْلَمُونَ** ) How great is Allah's interest in the good of His creatures!

*[Verse 233]*

*[p. 562]* In some past verses He stated about both divorce and the matters connected with it. In this verse He explains some matters relating to the suckling of infants. The matter of bringing up infants feeding them milk is, surely, one side of married life. When wife and husband part from each other by way of divorce, that turns into a big question. It is therefore that Allah, the Most Compassionate, explains this matter too along with the matters relating to divorce. The legal directions contained in this verse may be summarised like this:-

(1) It is the mothers of the children – or, those who gave birth to the children – who need to give suck to them. ‘Mothers must give suck to their offspring ( **وَالْوَالِدَاتُيُرْضِعْنَأَوْلادَهُنَّ** )’ is the word that Allah said. An indication can be seen in that sentence that it is they, surely, who are both bound and entitled to give suck to the children they gave birth to. Then it can be understood from this that it is only in a situation in which mothers cannot fulfil it that other ways need to be sought. Some are wont to consider as a refinement keeping away from that task, both bringing up infants giving them other milks that are not breast milk, or artificial provisions, and mums preserving their youth and beauty. But, the fact that this refinement will cause damage to both the health and the goodness of infants – to the goodness of mothers indeed too – is a reality accepted among experts in medical science.

(2) It is two complete years that is the period of suckling. From this it can be understood that, though, when three or four months are over, the child will also eat other foods little by little besides breast milk, in that period the need for breast milk does not cease for the child. It is Allah, surely, who knows the science of health more than anyone else.

(3) What has been said as a period of two years is the fully complete period of suckling. Or, when the conditions stated below exist, it is not that one must complete two years. There is no need either for the mother to give suck for a period of more than two years, or for the father to bear the expense needed for it. Before two years are fulfilled, without a special reason, it is not, moreover, possible for the two of them to withdraw on their own from their respective liability.

From its having been determined that the period of suckling is two years, one can grasp another matter too. The ruling in Islam, surely, is both that, if the one who gives suck is some other woman who is not the real mother of the child, a mother-bond will come into being, on account of that suckling, between the child that sucked and her, and that in marriage matters and so on this suckling-bond will be regarded just like a family bond. Both that this suckling-bond becoming established will be only when it sucks before, for the child, two years of age *[p. 563]* are fulfilled, and that by sucking afterwards this bond will not be established, can be understood from this – from its having been said that the complete period of suckling is two years. The opinion of the majority of scholars too is that. It is for that, too, that the support of hadith exists.

(4) One needs to give mothers who give suck the wherewithal for food and garments. It is he to whom the child was born – it is the father – who is bound to give it. When both are in the marriage bond, even if there is no child to suck, that is the duty of the husband. When the marriage bond is not in existence, that is a concession and a right given to her both in the name of her taking the trouble to keep on bringing up the child giving it suck, and in the name of her not having enough time and facility, in that period, for entering other means of livelihood. Otherwise than that, it is not in the capacity of a set recompense, or a wage, for giving suck to her own child. This can be understood from the words indeed that Allah said. Without saying that these are the liabilities of the father, its having said that it is the liability of him to whom the child was born ( **وَعَلَى الْمَوْلُودِلَهُ-الخ** ) is also meaningful. It is that it is he, naturally, who needs to make the expenses needed for bringing up, giving suck, the child born to him, that this indicates.

(5) The amount or the measure that needs to be given for food and garment has not been fixed. There is only: in the measure that, in the ordinary way, is considered as propriety ( **بِالْمَعْرُوفِ** ). Or, by means of this she must obtain the matter of her food and garment, both without suffering and without depending on others. The capacity of both parties, means of living. way of living, family standing, the circumstances of the land and of the time – in accordance with all this, the measure and the rate of this will vary. In case dispute is faced between them, the sum and the rate may be fixed either by means of mediators or by means of those in authority. Moreover, it is only in accordance with each one's capacity and liability that he needs to give.

(6) It is also not allowed to compel either the father to give beyond capacity, or the mother to make do with less than the urgent need. This matter needs to be specially paid attention to in this time. Approaching the courts with artificial documents – both those on the mother's side, for getting as big a sum as possible decreed, without the father's capacity and straitness being regarded, and, directly the contrary, those on the father's side, for getting exempted as far as possible from the sum that needs to be given to the mother – is, surely, the habit of many in this time.

(7) Not only in giving and receiving the amount for food and garment, but in other matters too indeed, one must not cause harm to the mother or to the father through the suckling child. One must not bring upon the mother, through the child, any difficulties such as: without the child being released to her, or without the wherewithal for expenses being given, or without its being pos*[p. 564]*sible for her to enter into marriage. One must not bring upon the father too any difficulties such as: without receiving the child, demanding a sum exceeding capacity, without allowing even to see the child. Even when both are in the marriage bond, all matters of this kind are indeed ones to be guarded. Even so, when the marriage bond is not in existence, there is, surely, need to guard specially.

(8) The inheritors too have a liability like that said above. ( **وَعَلَىالْوَارِثِمِثْلُذَلِكَ** ) In the interpretations of meaning of this sentence, more than one variance of opinion can be seen. What was said as inheritor ( **الوَارث** ) having the possibility of being both intending the heirs of the father, and intending, among the heirs indeed, close heir ( **العصبة** )s such as parents, is the main reason for this division of opinion. In having said ‘what is like that ( **مِثْلُذَلِكَ** )’, there is the possibility both of all the liabilities said before being included, and of only the matter said just before it – that injury must not come about to the mother and the father through the child – having been intended. This is another reason for it. Basing on each of these liabilities, many matters, in branches and sub-branches, can be seen explained in the laws of jurisprudence from that one single short sentence. What we too have to say here concerning this is what Sayyid Rashid Rida (r) stated in ‘Tafsirul Manar’. That is, the secret of Allah's having used here this statement that has such extensive possibility of meaning will perhaps be indeed that all of them should be comprised in it. (Allah knows.) One matter we may say boldly: if the father or the mother is not alive, or if, by illness or otherwise, it is in effect as not being alive, those connected with both parties need to do what is needed in the matter of the child.

(9) It was said, surely, that the period of suckling is two years. But, on account of some circumstance or other, there is no objection to the parents, consulting together between themselves, with mutual pleasure, deciding that the child's suckling must be stopped before that indeed. But, the decision must not be one-sided. Because, it will happen sometimes that both the mother, for the sake of avoiding her difficulties, and the father, for obtaining release from his liabilities, have made haste. As far as the child is concerned, that will also be detrimental. When it is with full consent from both sides – if it is parents whom the intoxication of a bestial refinement that, as today, feels no mercy even towards one's own little ones has not affected – that decision may come about only taking into account the goodness of their child too.

(10) Regarding the goodness of the child, or the trouble of the mother, or the circumstances of the father, there is no offence for the parents in entrusting some other women who are not the mother to bring up the child giving it suck. In this matter Allah is not seen to have stated any special conditions here. Even so, that the father without the permission of the mother, or the mother without the consent of the father, to do so is *[p. 565]* not allowed, can be understood from the facts explained above. Its having been stated in Su: Talaq 6, **وَإِنتَعَاسَرْتُمْفَسَتُرْضِعُ لَهُ أُخْرَى** (In case you show straitness to each other, another woman may give suck for him- for the husband-), is also noteworthy. It is addressing fathers that Allah has spoken of this subject both here and in Su: Talaq. What is understood from this is that, in the liability of needing to heed the goodness of the child, it is fathers who have precedence.

(11) If it is another woman who gives suck to the child, to her too one needs to give, without stinting, pay in accordance with ordinary customary propriety. One must not either be miserly or cause delay in it. Besides this turning out as an ill to one's own child indeed, it will also be, surely, an injustice done to the mother who brings up that child.

(12) Lastly- just as Allah is often wont to alert after explaining legal advices like these, He reminds both that one must guard against Allah, observing properly all the matters said above, and that Allah is keeping on watching the carrying out of lapse, or artifice, in it.
