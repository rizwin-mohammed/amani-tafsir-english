*[Verse 234]*

*[p. 566]* **تَوَفي** (tawaffa): the literal meaning is ‘to fulfil, to complete, to take in full’ and so on. A person's dying is, surely, when Allah completes his lifetime. Therefore, words from that root being used in the intention ‘to die’ is common. This is a usage in Arabic like the way ‘time is over, attained the course of time’ and so on are wont to be said in Malayalam in the meaning ‘to die’. Though the word **عَشر** ('ashr) has only the literal meaning ‘ten’, the intention is ten days. In contexts where it can be understood that it is days that are intended, making do with saying the number is habitual in Arabic. (In 20:103 too it can be seen used like this.)

When husbands attain the course of time, their wives need to observe 'iddah for four months and ten days. When that is over, they can decide their future matter in a way that is not contrary to the good-custom proprieties. One must not do hindrance to them in that. They may, as is fitting, either accept husbands whom they like, or decide that marriage is not wanted for the present – that is the gist. In 'iddah periods both marriage and marriage proposal have been disallowed. In this 'iddah following the husband's death, using ornaments of beauty too has been forbidden. The bans all end along with the 'iddah.

What existed in the Jahiliyyah time was the practice of women, if the husband died, carrying on observance of mourning in an extremely pitiable state for up to one year. Until then, wearing garments both contemptible and soiled, without contact with kith and kin being allowed, they had to live on like domestic animals in some hut or other. Along with that, they also had to observe quite some superstitions and improper customs. Islam, for its part, both shortened the period of the 'iddah, making it one third of that, and did away with all the meaningless conventions in the observance of mourning. It also determined that it is enough to keep away both from ornaments of beauty and from marriage proposals. The Prophet ﷺ has said thus: ‘For no woman indeed who believes in Allah and in the Last Day, other than four months and ten days in the name of the husband- more than three days in the name of anyone else having died- will carrying on observance of mourning be permissible.’ (Bu; Mu.)

What comes from the ayah is that, in that the 'iddah following the husband's death is four months and ten days*[p. 567]*, there is no difference at all such as the youth or maturity of the women, or whether union has taken place or not, or the like. Moreover, in the details there is difference of opinion among scholars. But it is clear from the 4th verse of Su: Talaq both that pregnant women are exempt from the ruling, and that their 'iddah is only until they deliver the pregnancy. Both that, when Sa'd ibn Khawlah (r) died, his wife Subai'iyyah ( **سُبيعِية** ) was pregnant, and that, without much delay, she gave birth, and the Prophet ﷺ gave the ruling that with that her 'iddah had ended, Bukhari, and Muslim have also recorded. One main intention contained in having laid down for women to observe 'iddah after both divorce and the husband's death is to know whether they have pregnancy. That, for its part, will also be known in the ordinary course in three or four months. That being so, we do not know what the secret is of having laid down here the 'iddah period exactly as four months and ten days. It will be possible for us to say only that Allah knows. Like this, there exists, as something we do not know, the wise secret contained in the fixing of many things, such as the number of the prayer, the rate of Zakat, the number of some dhikrs. Other than that we will be able to conjecture that some principles are contained in some, it is not possible to decide about them objectively. Therefore, to matters of that kind it is said **تَعبدى** (‘ta'abbudi’ - a matter pertaining to worship). In the next verse, Allah continues and says some matters too connected with this 'iddah:-

*[Verse 235]*

*[p. 568]* By **كِتَابُ** (law) what is meant here is the 'iddah. Allah commands by means of this verse that, before the set period of the women who are in 'iddah following their husbands' death ends, one must not either make a marriage solicitation to them, or make any promises secretly concerning marriage, or make a determination that one may contract marriage when the 'iddah is over. But, He also states that there is no objection to either saying some word of indication, without saying the thing openly, or keeping on holding in the mind that one may contract marriage when the 'iddah is over. In this matter, both those who observe 'iddah following divorce of a kind in which taking back is not possible, and those who are in 'iddah after the husband's death, are alike. But, to women who are in the 'iddah of divorce in which there is possibility of taking back, even an indication of the kind said above is not allowed. Because, since the husband always has the right to take her back, that will be an encroachment against that right. It is warnings concerning watching over and guarding the legal boundaries of Allah without violating *[p. 569]* that are seen in the last part of the ayah.

### Section – 31

*[Verse 236]*

Though carrying out divorce of wives has acceptance from the legal point of view, by its nature it is, surely, not a good thing. ‘Among permissible matters, what is most anger-provoking to Allah is talaq (is divorce)’ is what has come in hadith. (Da; Ja; Ha.) That being so, one may, surely, doubt whether there is objection to carrying out divorce of the woman on an occasion when, after the marriage deed is over, contact - union- has not taken place between the woman and the man, and how much the sum (mahr) given to her as marriage-price is *[p. 570]* has not been fixed between both parties. Allah makes known both that, in case the need for divorce is faced, one may carry it out on that occasion too- there is no wrong in it- it is indeed lawful, and that there is no need to give mahr to her. Talaq taking place on this occasion is, surely, naturally a matter that deals a great wound to their minds. Therefore- in the capacity of a peace of mind for them- He commands both that one must give some resource or other as a gift, and that it must be in accordance with the capacity and rank of each one. This resource is known by the name **مُتَعة** (mut'ah).

What the ‘mut'ah’ must be, how much is needed – nothing of this has been made clear either in the Quran or in hadith. It has only been commanded that one must give something in accordance both with the husband's capacity and with good-custom propriety. But, Allah has also alerted that it is a duty indispensable for people who are well-doers. The intention of this is not that everyone need not give it. It is giving an encouragement, in strong language, both that it is a matter of well-doing and decent, and that no one must show miserliness in it. For a ‘mut'ah’ of a moderate rate, many great men can be seen to have stated many examples. Examples of it can be seen stated, both thus: a set of garments, a manservant, a shirt and a headscarf and a cloth; and thus: half of the mahr that the respective women are wont to get in the ordinary course, ten or thirty panams *[DOUBT: പണം is printed; it may be the old coin “panam” or simply “money”; rendered “panams”.]*, and so on. That some great men gave sums by the thousands as mut'ah is also narrated. To put it briefly, it may be said in general that what is meant by ‘mut'ah’ is giving some resource, in accordance with capacity, and the propriety of honour, and the differences of time and place, in such a way that those women get peace of mind and happiness.

When marriage is contracted, the husband giving mahr to the wife is indeed an indispensable obligation. But, when the marriage matter (nikah) takes place, fixing the sum of the mahr is not obligatory. It will be enough even if it is determined later. Nor is it that one must give the whole mahr along with the marriage deed indeed. It will be enough even if one gives it later, according to the determination that takes place between the two parties. Such things can be grasped from both this verse and the next verse. Besides, one can understand from the hadiths too. But, though they are wont to say, when the marriage deed takes place, that the mahr is this much, a practice of not paying it off is current among some. Only later, following divorce, or following death, when they settle with each other the financial accounts between both parties, are they wont to deliberate about the sum of the mahr. As a result of not grasping as needed the meaning of many who are lovers of the community being wont to raise their voice both that this is not right, and that the determined mahr must be paid off in due time, some can be seen to have misunderstood, too, that giving the whole mahr is obligatory when the marriage deed takes place indeed. The fact, moreover, is as said above.

*[Verse 237]*

*[p. 572]* Before contact- union- with the wife takes place, the husband divorced her. One has already fixed beforehand how much her mahr is. If so, giving half of that determined mahr is obligatory. The obligation of the ‘mut'ah’ is not here. Now, in case the woman, of her own mind, remits some part of it or the whole, the husband then need not pay it off. If the husband, in the capacity of his generosity, just gives more than half or the whole part, there is no objection for her too to accepting it. It is such things that Allah makes known in this verse. He has also specially alerted both that, from whichever side of the two it is, showing obstinacy and miserliness without letting go is not good, and that it is a state of mind of letting go that is suited to both caution and God-fearing reverence. Along with that, saying also that one must not forget the superiority-generous state of mind - that is among you, He also feeds fervour for it. There is no need to say that, both for observing superiority and decency, and for showing generosity and letting go, it is the man, compared with the woman, who is in many ways the more entitled. Sometimes it will come about that the circumstances have become the reverse. Then, it is on the woman's side that there will be preponderance for it.

What was said as ( **الَّذِيبِيَدِهِعُقْدَةُالنِّكَاحِ** one who has the marriage bond in possession) is intending the husband. Both setting up the marriage bond and releasing it come, surely, into force by his hand. The meaning of having said that the husband pardons- lets go- is indeed that he gives more than what he is duty-bound to give. If the husband had already finished giving, before the talaq indeed, the whole sum of the mahr according to the determination, then he will, surely, have the right to get back half of it. His not receiving that back too will be a generosity that he does. That Allah is keeping on seeing and knowing and watching who all are observing both law and propriety, who all are those having both caution and God-fearing reverence, and so on- as in the previous verses- at the end here too Allah has alerted again. Allah's intention is not only to explain laws of observance. It is that mankind, bringing them all into observance, become people of right guidance. It is therefore, too, that Allah repeats advices and warnings like these, adjoined to having explained legal directions for us.

*[Verses 238–239]*

*[p. 573]* In the past few verses, the subject of talk was some matters both matrimonial and communal. In the next verses, matters connected with them also continue again. In between, in these two verses He is stating about both the prayer, the most important by the deeds of daily observance in Islam *[DOUBT: printed “കർമങ്ങളാൽ” (by the deeds); the sense would suit “കർമങ്ങളിൽ” (among the deeds). Translated as printed.]*, and practising it exactly. This may be reckoned as an indication that, being absorbed in wife and children, household matters, family matters and the like, the attention to both the remembrance of Allah and the prayer must not slip away.

The usage **إقَامَةُ الصَّلاةَ** (establishing the prayer) is common in the Quran. The usage, as seen here, **المحافظةعلى الصلاة** keeping on guarding about the prayer) will be seen only on few occasions. What is meant by it in general is indeed keeping on practising with regularity, both habitually and at the exact time. The importance that Islam has assigned to the prayer is something that more or less everyone knows. What the Prophet ﷺ has stated in a hadith is indeed that it is the prayer that is the boundary line in between of man and of shirk and kufr *[DOUBT: printed “മനുഷ്യന്റെ ശിർക്ക് കുഫ്റുകളുടെയും ഇടക്കുള്ള”: മനുഷ്യന്റെ (of man) has no -ഉം, while കുഫ്റുകളുടെയും has one, so it can also be read “the boundary line in between of man's shirk and kufr”. Rendered as the pair the -ഉം points to; please confirm.]* (Mu). When Ibn Mas'ud *[p. 574]* (r) asked which is the most superior among deeds, ‘It is doing the prayer at its time’ was what the Prophet ﷺ said in reply. It was when he asked which then, that the noble Prophet said about both doing good to parents, and fighting in the way of Allah. (Bu; Mu.) Both the strictness that the noble Prophet ﷺ had in performing each prayer at its respective first time indeed, and the eagerness that the Companions had in it, are famous.

After saying as a whole that one must keep on guarding about the prayers, He has specially singled out and mentioned about the middle prayer ( **وَالصَّلاةِالْوُسْطَى** ). Though the actual meaning of the word **الْوُسْطَى** is what is in the middle, it has, surely, been understood before from the 143rd verse that this word is wont to be used in the intentions ‘the pre-eminent, ‘the excellent’ too. Here too that is the intention. There are differences of opinion on which the ‘middle prayer’ is. But, Imam Muslim (r) and others have narrated more than one hadith from which it can be understood, and which make clear, that it is the 'Asr prayer. (Imam Ibn Kathir can be seen to have quoted them.) Therefore, it is that which the greater part of the scholars have strengthened. The reason for specially mentioning the 'Asr prayer- as some commentators have pointed out- may be because, since its time is after the midday rest, more attention needs to be applied if one is to keep on performing it at the exact time. Looking from another angle, it is also in the middle of both Subh and Zuhr, the two prayers of the day, and Maghrib and 'Isha', the night prayers, that the 'Asr prayer is situated. **والله اعلم**

The gist of having said ‘And stand for the sake of Allah, being ones who have devotion’ is indeed that standing to pray before Allah must be both with devotion, and observing both discipline and composure. Then, it can be understood from this that matters such as talk, turning this way and that and looking round giving attention to other matters, getting through in haste, not keeping the hands and feet and so on still, all need to be shunned in the prayer. In the hadiths, for their part, about all these it has also been specially explained and said.

Zaid ibn Arqam (r) says: ‘We used to talk in the prayer. A man used to talk to his brother about his need. So, (this verse) that says **وَقُومُوالِلَّهِقَانِتِينَ** came down. Then, we were commanded to keep still.’ (Bu.) To a man, on one occasion, the Prophet ﷺ said thus: ‘In this prayer no talk of the people will do, it is tasbih, and takbir and Quran recitation.’ (Mu.) To a man who was levelling the soil (of the ground) while doing sujud, the Prophet ﷺ said: ‘If you are doing so, let it be one time.’ (Bu; Mu.) That doing more is not in keeping with discipline – that is the indication. In a hadith the Prophet ﷺ says: *[p. 575]* ‘If one of you yawns in the prayer, let him hold it in check as far as he can. He must not (opening the mouth) say ‘Ha’. Because, it is from the devil. He will laugh (rejoicing) on account of it.’ (Bu.) That the Prophet ﷺ has disallowed standing in the prayer with the hands pressed on the hips, Abu Hurairah (r) has stated. (Bu; Mu.) To what 'A'ishah (r) asked about looking round in the prayer, what the Prophet ﷺ said in reply is thus: ‘That is a snatching that the devil snatches from the servant's prayer.’ (Bu; Mu.)

When He commands that one must keep on practising the prayer both at the exact time and with devotion, one may, surely, doubt what one will do at difficult stages when that becomes not possible: it is the answer to that which is the second verse. That is, if one both fears, either about enemies, or about anything such as fire, wild beast, storm, flood, wrongdoer, and it comes about that one is unable to perform the prayer in its proper form, one must perform it as is possible, either walking, or mounted on a conveyance. Even so, one must not leave off the prayer – that is the gist. On this occasion it is not that one must turn to the qiblah. Just like that indeed, it will be enough if one does sujud, ruku' and so on too as well as is possible. It is only that one must do all as far as there is convenience. If the cause of fright is removed and one has become at peace, one must also perform it in the correct form indeed, just as Allah has taught.
