### Section – 35

*[Verse 258]*

*[p. 612]* It was stated, surely, in the previous verse both that the kinsman and the custodian of the true believers who believe in Allah will be Allah, and that He will lead them from the darknesses to the light, and, on the contrary, that the kinsmen and the managers of the affairs of those who disbelieve in Allah will be the Taghuts, and that it is from the light to the darknesses that they will push them. This verse is an example of that. What is seen in that event is both Ibrahim (a) having enjoyed the light of truth and of justice *[DOUBT: ന്യായത്തിന്റെയും is printed; ന്യായം is rendered “justice” beside “truth”; it may mean “right, reason” (part 40 rendered ന്യായമുണ്ട് “there is justification”).]* and won, and, the one who carried on dispute with him, *[p. 613]* that king, being unable to enjoy that light, his having been immersed in darkness and met with defeat. The name of the said king is known to be Namrud ( **نَمْرُود** ). It was customary in former times both for the kings who rule the land to pose as gods, and for the subjects to worship them making them gods. The time of the Prophet Musa (a) is, surely, after some centuries had passed after the Prophet Ibrahim (a). Fir'aun, who was the king in Egypt at that time, said things such as both that it is I who am your most exalted Rabb ( **أَنَا رَبُّكُمُ الْأَعْلَى** ), and that I have no knowledge of there being another Ilah for you except me ( **مَا عَلِمْتُلَكُمْ مِنْإِلَهٍغَيْرِي** ), surely. The practice of regarding kings as gods existed until recent times in countries such as Japan, Siam. It may be said that even today that belief has not entirely gone away from a few people. Although references about Fir'aun can be seen abundantly in the Quran, no reference about Namrud is seen in the Quran except in this verse. That name too has not been mentioned in the Quran. What can be understood from the conversation that took place between the Prophet Ibrahim (a) and him is that he too used to claim that he was a god – that is all.

In any case, none at all of the claimants to godhood *[DOUBT: ദൈവവാദികളായ is printed; it is rendered “claimants to godhood” from the context (kings who claimed to be gods); in earlier term tables ദൈവവാദി is “theist”, which would read “none at all of the theists”.]* is known to have claimed that it is they who created the heavens and the earth, and who keep on controlling the great matters in them. Even if anyone claims so, it does not seem either that there will be people at any time to accept it. Their claim will be both that, together with there indeed being a great power that is the Creator of the world and the Controller of the world, the authorities and the managements within the boundary of their authority are all theirs, and that, for that reason, they have the right to the worship and veneration of everyone. Along with that, all the welfare and prosperity in the land may also be made out to be favours on their part, and the calamities and untoward events to be their curses and wrath. That Allah alone is the Rabb and the Ilah (the Lord of the worlds and the object of worship) is, surely, the fundamentally chief principle of preaching of all the prophets. With this principle becoming accepted, both those claims and all the great fortresses that were built up making them the foundation collapse. Along with that, the haughtiness and authority *[DOUBT: ഔദ്ധത്യവും അധികാരവും is printed; അധികാരം “authority” repeats the word just before it (അധികാരത്തിന്റെയും); it may be a slip for അഹങ്കാരവും “arrogance”, as in “അഹങ്കാരവും ഔദ്ധത്യവും” on p. 614. Rendered as printed.]* that are innate to authority and to loftiness also become manifest in them. That is why here, when speaking about the dispute of Namrud, Allah specially singles out and says **أَنْ آتَاهُ اللَّهُالْمُلْكَ** (because Allah gave him kingship).

This controversy is not seen referred to in the Bible. Nevertheless it is said that this event has been described in the Talmud (*). What is said in it is indeed that it happened following Ibrahim (a) having reproached about the idols and having hewn and cut them up. (Allah knows.) In any *[p. 614]* case, just as, when the Prophet Musa (a) invited Fir'aun to tawhid, he asked things like **فَمَنْ رَبُّكُمَايَا مُوسَى** (Musa, then of you two persons- of both Musa (a) and Harun (a)- who is the Rabb? and, **وَمَا رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ** (What is this Lord of the worlds?), Namrud too may have asked Ibrahim (a) some questions. It may have been following that that he replied **رَبِّيَالَّذِي يُحْيِيوَيُمِيتُ** (My Rabb is the one who both gives life and causes to die). **اللّه اعلم**

> **Note:** (*) Very important among the books of the Jewish religion composed by eminent Jewish scholars in the early times of Christianity is the Talmud (Talmud) *[TN: the second “Talmud” is printed in English (Latin letters); the first renders the Malayalam തൽമൂദ്.]*. In the Bible Dictionary it says: ‘If one is to know evidently about the Judaic traditions, the Talmud that the rabbis composed and its earlier books must be examined…. The Talmud, which has the meaning ‘learning’, the Judean rabbis made. It is an ocean of arts containing crores upon crores of traditions, interpretations, sciences of argument and so on… The Judaic scribes opined that the Talmud and the Law of Moses are alike ones that have authority….’

Although scientists have not been able up to now to give a universally acceptable scientific definition to life and death, they are two matters clear in a way that everyone is able to recognise them both. In just the same way, although scientists may venture to state some external causes or other for both these matters, there is no doubt either that the real cause behind those causes is Allah. Only for the godless who, with closed eyes, deny all that the external senses cannot find will there be a contrary opinion in it. That whichever actual agent gives life and death to living creatures, that is my Rabb- it is to that Rabb that I am inviting- is the gist of what Ibrahim (a) said.

Does the egotism of Namrud agree to concede this?! What he tried was to get past that reality by giving a new interpretation to life and death. He said: ‘I both give life and put to death, surely.’ Yes, then is it not I who ought to be Rabb – that is the meaning. He would have both killed how many people before that, and let off many without killing even though he had the capability to kill them, surely. Or else- as some Quran commentators say- he may have both brought two persons and given the death penalty to one of them, and spared the other. Or else- as Ibn Kathir (r) has pointed out- it may also be that his arrogance and haughtiness and obstinacy made him say that both of those are indeed my own deed. Or, it may have been a word of arrogance like the one in which Fir'aun arrogantly said ‘I do not know of there being any other Ilah for you except me; (Allah knows.) In any case, that none of that becomes in any way a refutation of what Ibrahim (a) said *[p. 615]* is plain indeed.

Nevertheless, Ibrahim (a) did not venture either to analyse the word of Namrud, or to elucidate the words that he himself had said. What he did was to change the course of the talk, including a challenge as well, though it is indeed, in principle, another form of the first argument. He said: **فَإِنَّ اللَّهَيَأْتِي بِالشَّمْسِ.....** *[DOUBT: the printed “.....” stands at the left end of this Arabic phrase, touching it, so which side of it the dots belong to cannot be told from the page; they are placed after it here, where the rest of the verse is left out.]* (Then, Allah brings the sun from the east. You bring it from the west!) All right. If it is you who give life and death- or else you too give- then let that be so, there is a great power that makes this sun rise daily from the east and set on the western side. That is not you, surely. It is about that power that I say Rabb. If you are Rabb, you must be able to bring it from the west to the east too, surely. Let that just be seen *[DOUBT: അതൊന്ന് കാണട്ടെ is printed, with no subject; it may be “let me (us) just see that”.]* – that is the gist. Namrud, at a loss for an answer, was stunned. Allah has not gone on to say what happened afterwards. For the authors of claims that originated- not from doubt or from lack of knowledge- only from egotism and from haughtiness, withdrawing the claim is difficult, surely, even if they are at a loss for an answer. When Fir'aun was at a loss for an answer to Musa (a), what he did was both to be satisfied telling the people that this Rasul who has been sent to you is a madman indeed (26:27), and to threaten Musa (a) that in case you take an Ilah other than me I will put you in prison (26:29). There is justification for conjecturing that Namrud too would have done something like that. About that Allah has made nothing clear. In any case, that he did not either accept the preaching of the Prophet Ibrahim (a), or turn back from his claim, is certain indeed. That can be understood from the very saying **وَاللَّهُلايَهْدِيالْقَوْمَ الظَّالِمِينَ** (Allah will not put on the straight way the people who are wrongdoers.). It is well known, surely, both that he was thrown into the fire-pit, and that in the end he renounced the country and went on Hijrah.

*[Verse 259]*

*[p. 617]* In the previous verse, the story of Namrud, who both carried on dispute arrogantly with the Prophet Ibrahim (a), and, being unable to receive the light of truth, stood firm in the darkness, was described. It is while drawing the attention of those addressed, saying have you not seen ( **أَلَمْتَرَ** ), that that story was described there. On the contrary, what is described in this verse is an event in which Allah blessed a person who was both a true believer and a seeker of truth by giving more light. Saying **أَوْكَالَّذِي** (or like that of one who) in the meaning: or else here is another example- just pay attention to this-, Allah draws attention to this event too. Even among those who have the belief that it is Allah who gives life and death, there are both those who deny the life after death altogether, and those who, though they do not deny, deny the real state by giving some misinterpretations. Not only that, there are also those who suppose both that to happen once is impossible *[DOUBT: ഒരിക്കൽ സംഭവിക്കുക അസംഭവ്യമാണെന്നും is printed; rendered word for word; the sense is unclear. It may mean that (something such as coming back to life) happening even once is impossible, or ഒരിക്കൽ may stand for “once more”.]*, and that nothing can happen in this world beyond the external relations of cause and effect. For all such as these, this event is a sufficient sign.

Allah has not made clear either about whom it is that ‘the man who walked through a country’ was said, or which that country was. It is not seen in hadith either. For grasping the nature of the event and the lesson from it, there is no need to know that, either. Although some opinions can be seen both that it was the prophet Armiya (Jeremiah), and that it was the prophet Hasqil (Ezekiel), the more acceptable opinion is that it was the prophet 'Uzair ( **عزير** Ezra). That is also what the majority of Quran commentators are seen to have accepted. Whoever that person was, it can be inferred from the account of the Quran that he was a prophet. The majority opinion is indeed that the said country *[p. 618]* was Bait al-Muqaddas, or Yerushalem. Whoever he was, it is plain from the ayah both that that great man had along with him his donkey, and food and drink, and that it is the sight that he saw there as he travelled through that country lying destroyed and desolate that became the cause of the event. It is about the sight of houses and buildings lying collapsed and fallen that **وَهِيَخَاوِيَةٌعَلَىعُرُوشِهَا** (it is lying fallen with its respective roofs) was said. When vineyards fall and perish, what happens, surely, is both that first their trellises fall, and that the vines fall *[DOUBT: വീണമ്പുകയുമാണല്ലോ is printed (the site has the same); വീണ് “falling” is clear, but the word വീണമ്പുക is not known; only “fall” is rendered. It may be a slip for a word such as വീണമരുക “fall and settle”.]* over it in a way that it cannot be set up straight and put right a second time. It is from this that this usage has originated for it. The event in which Bukhtunassar ( **بختنصر** Nebuchadnezzar), who was the Babylonian king, both attacked and destroyed Bait al-Muqaddas, and carried out a mass killing of the Israelites, and, making the survivors slaves, carried them off to Babylonia, is historically famous. Thus, that country was lying both lifeless and depopulated for a very long time. What most Quran commentators also say is that it is that time that this refers to. This event has not been described in the Bible. Some events that seem to resemble some aspects of this, or that seem connected with this, can also be seen in it.

That great man's saying ‘after this country has died and become lifeless, how will Allah give life to this’ was not merely in the form of denial, or in the form of doubt. That it was a display of a wonder arising from belief in the boundless power of Allah can be understood from the statements that follow. Allah determined to show that matter to him through experience indeed. However firm the belief through evidences *[DOUBT: ലക്ഷ്യങ്ങൾ usually means “aims”; beside ദൃഷ്ടാന്തങ്ങൾ (“signs”) it is taken in its older sense “evidences, indications”. The option is “aims and signs”.]* and signs may be, seeing in experience will strengthen it more, surely. This fact can be grasped more from the event of the Prophet Ibrahim (a) that is told in the next verse. Allah both caused that great man to die for a hundred years, and afterwards, giving life, raised him up. Where was it that he lay dead for this much time? Did any human beings see it? When a hundred years passed, did he come alive suddenly? Or else, did he get life back step by step? Allah knows. About events like this, we have no means of saying anything going beyond the limit of the words that Allah used. There are no other trustworthy proofs either. We may make do with what Allah has told us. And we may believe in it completely.

After he came alive, Allah asked him how much time he would have remained in the state of death. Not because Allah did not know, only in order for him to understand the real. He would not know, surely, anything of a hundred years having passed. He said: I would have remained like that a day or a small part of a day. That the cave-dwellers ( **اصحاب الكهف** ), who, having fallen into a sleep that was a half-death *[p. 619]*, remained over three hundred years, also expressed the same kind of opinion among themselves when they awoke from their sleep can be seen in Surat al-Kahf 19. What is understood from this reply is that, when he came alive, no changes very different from the state at the time he died were evident in his frame *[DOUBT: ശരീരം is rendered “frame”, as in part 31 (open doubt there), to keep it apart from ദേഹം “self”/“body”; the plain English would be “body”.]*. That Allah asked him the said question, and that he gave the reply to it, may have been by means of wahy, or through an angel. What this indicates is that he would be a prophet. (Allah knows the fact) Allah informed him that the matter is not as he thought -that he had lain dead a hundred years-. It is then that he understands the real.

Here a doubt may be raised. There are two lives, surely: thus, a worldly life, and a life after death. As far as this man is concerned, the lives will become three in number, surely. Not only that, it is established by the Quran and by the Prophet's sayings that, after having died, in the intervening period until the resurrection ( **عَالمُ البرزخ** in *[DOUBT: the Arabic is printed with the Malayalam case ending ൽവെച്ച് (“in, at”) joined after it; it likely means “the world of the barzakh”, glossing “the intervening period” before it.]*), spiritual experiences, of happiness or of sorrow, according to the circumstances of each one, and some unearthly sights will be experienced. Then, should not this man too, who lay dead a hundred years, have experienced so? This is that doubt. The reply to be said altogether for doubts of this sort is this: Allah knows the real. It is not right to misinterpret according to our reasoning, basing on suppositions and doubts such as these, the matters that Allah has described in plain language. What we ought to do is both to accept them at their face value, and, when we do not find a fitting reply from other proofs for the doubts that arise from it, to be content, leaving it that Allah knows the fact. Along with that, some facts such as are said below also need to be remembered:-

(1) That material life comes into experience for a person only once is indeed a general rule. Not only mankind, other living creatures too are included in this rule. But, in the course of some events beyond the general laws of nature customarily occurring, it is not that some things too may not happen differently from this general rule. In this very verse, the information of giving life to that great man's dead donkey comes in the sentences that follow. In the next verse too the event of giving life to birds and showing it to the Prophet Ibrahim (a) is described. One of the special signs given to the Prophet 'Isa (a) was giving life to the dead. (3: 49; 5: 113). Then what ought to be thought, surely, is that it would have happened so at least once. In case of venturing to misinterpret here and there saying that they are figurative usages and so on, its result will be opening a way to misinterpret most statements in the Quran using that same criterion.

*[p. 620]* (2) If it is said as far as that great man in this event is concerned, there is also a possibility that no spiritual experiences at all occurred to him in that hundred-year period. Even if they had been experienced, there is also a possibility that, with the second material life, it was removed from his path of memory. Especially in events that are shown as a sign like this. Allah has capability for all things, surely.

(3) It may happen that Allah reveals some knowledges of the unseen to the prophets (72:27). Some matters that the people understand and believe by means of signs and through proofs, they will understand and believe on the basis of experience indeed. (Some examples of this can be seen in the explanation of the next verse.) Here is an experience that occurred to the Prophet ﷺ. In a speech he said: ‘Paradise and Hell were displayed to me. In good and in evil I have not seen like that day (any other day). If you knew what I know, you would laugh little (only), and you would weep much’ (Bu;Mu.)

Even after a hundred-year period had passed, no change had happened to the food and drink that were with that great man. At the same time, as for the donkey, it has both died and decayed, and only the bones have remained! Although it has not been said clearly that the donkey had died and decayed away, that too becomes plain from the sentences that follow. Thus, Allah showed him in experience through this event both that Allah has no difficulty at all in restoring that country, making increase of population and abundant prosperity in it, and giving life to it again, and that matters that seem difficult in human sight, such as the life after death, are mere trifles as far as Allah is concerned. Not only that, Allah has also intended by means of this to make that event a sign to others as well ( **ولنجعلك اية للناس** ).

The very remaining of the food and drink without any change through all this time is one sign contained in it. Therefore nothing in particular need be said about it. As for the donkey dying and rotting, it is only natural in the ordinary course therefore only the sign shown through the donkey needs to be described. Allah commanded him, who saw both that it had died and decayed and that only bones were remaining, both to look at those bones, and to observe the giving of life to it again, reorganising them and wrapping them with flesh. That it happened indeed as he looked, there is no need, then, to single out and say. When he saw all this, that great man said out: That Allah is capable of every matter- not one matter is difficult as far as He is concerned- I here know by seeing through experience, thus! *[p. 621]* ( **فَلَمَّاتَبَيَّنَلَهُ قَالَأَعْلَمُأَنَّاللَّهَعَلَىكُلِّ شَيْءٍقَدِيرٌ** ) That he has known by seeing in sight the real that he had been knowing and believing from before – that is the gist. Here, in the place of the present-tense verb **أَعْلَمُ** (a'lamu), it has also been read in the imperative verb form as **إعْلَم** (i'lam). The meaning then will be ‘Allah is capable of every matter you take knowledge, Allah said to him’. Either way, what is intended by knowledge ( **عِلم** ) is indeed knowledge by experience.

Some words that Sayyid Qutb (r) said after finishing describing this event become worthy of mention here. It may be quoted like this: ‘How did this extraordinary event happen? Just like all other extraordinary events! Yes, just like the extraordinariness in the first life! We do not know, surely, except that it happened through a way that Allah willed- how (why) it happened-. Darwin, who is reckoned as a great person among biologists, there, through his theory concerning life, tries to go down step by step into the depth of life and carry out examination. And then in the end, there, going to the first step, he touches ground! At the first step- or in the cell (Cell) *[TN: “Cell” is printed in English (Latin letters); “cell” renders the Malayalam കോശത്തിൽ before it.]*- how life came, he does not know. But he was not ready to concede that there is a power that he should have conceded to be one that man cannot understand. He is not ready either to concede that at least at this first step there is a Creator who gave life!….’

Some commentators are seen to have said both that, when that great man who is the character in this event afterwards went among the people, some who had known him in the early time and who had been seized by extreme old age were living, and that, when he said that I am such-and-such a person, they did not believe him, saying that he had died long ago. That when he went riding on his donkey and said I am 'Uzair, the people made him out a liar; that then he recited the Tawrat by heart without missing a single letter and made them hear; that it was then that they believed him; that it was from then that the Israelites began to say that he is the son of Allah: there was no one else who knew the Tawrat by heart. Some or other have also stated in this way. We cannot decide whether all this is right or not.
