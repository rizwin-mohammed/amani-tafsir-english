*\[Verse 89\]*

*\[p. 311\]* Concerning the coming of the last prophet and his qualities, the Jews have a traditional knowledge, through the Torah and through the prophets. The Jews of Madinah were also waiting impatiently for the coming of that prophet. Through them the Arabs too knew about this. When some clash or confrontation occurs between the Arabs and them, they used to say proudly things such as: let the prophet who is soon to appear come, we will show you; then Allah will be giving us strength and glory through that prophet. They also used to pray: ‘Send that prophet quickly and help us’. When that prophet whom they had been expecting and praying for came, and a scripture – the Quran – came down that affirms the scripture in their possession – the Torah – and establishes its truth, there! their whole manner changed ! The Prophet ﷺ was not a person belonging to the race of Isra'il; he turned out to be a person belonging to the Arabs; he also does not permit all the superstitions and evil practices that they practise as religion. It is all this that is the reason for the denial. So, what they did was to change into bitter enemies and complete deniers of that prophet, and to become objects of Allah's curse and wrath. This is what Allah says in this verse.

*\[Verse 90\]*

*\[p. 312\]* It is in order to preserve temporary desires of the self and selfish interests that they, without believing in the Quran, deny it. That, for its part, is also something that loses their future for ever. Then, what they are doing is, taking a price that is disbelief, selling and destroying themselves. In buying and selling, what is dearer than the thing that is sold will, surely, be the price that is obtained in exchange for it. Here it is the very reverse. What they sold and destroyed is everlasting future success, and what they got in exchange is everlasting punishment. Then what other deal is more senseless than this ?! The reason for their denial is, surely, the jealousy at prophethood having been obtained by one who does not belong to their race – by the Prophet ﷺ –. But prophethood, for its part, is not the monopoly right of any section, or something attained through some qualification. It is only a favour that He gives to those whom Allah wills. So they, who had already become subject to Allah's wrath in many ways, have, because of their disbelief in the Quran and in the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, again become objects of Allah's wrath. This is the gain they got through that buying and selling.

It is to the verb ٱشۡتَرَوۡاْ that the translation 'they sold' has been given. The translation 'they bought' too can be given to it. Most of the use of that word, too, is in this meaning. In buying and selling, when goods are exchanged from both sides, it is only that, as far as the one who receives the goods that are counted as the price in it is concerned, that deal is called sale, and, as far as the one who receives the other goods is concerned, it is called buying. Therefore, in the sense of exchanging one thing in place of another thing, it is wont to be used in both selling and buying. Even if the meaning 'buy' itself is assigned to it here – and such a meaning has also been assigned – the gist will end up being roughly just as described above.

*\[Verses 91–92\]*

*\[p. 313\]* Believing in all that Allah has sent down is, surely, obligatory. Then, if the Jews are told, should you not believe in the Quran, their answer will be: we do believe in the book that was sent down to us. That itself is enough for us. The Quran, for its part, is not denying that book sent down to them – it is establishing its truth –. Even so, what they do is disbelieve in it. In that position they are people who ought to come forward to belief more than the Arabs, who are people who do not know about scripture. But is it right, their having said that they believe in that book in their hands? No. If they properly believe in it, they would not have killed the many prophets who were among them before. Then what they said is not right. Is it only that? The Prophet Musa (a), surely, is the chief among their prophets. They are people who, even after he had brought many very clear signs for Allah's oneness, made the calf a god and worshipped it. Then, not only do they not believe *\[p. 314\]* in the Quran and in the Prophet ﷺ; they are people who have no proper belief even in the Torah, their scripture, and in the Prophet Musa (a), their prophet.

*\[Verse 93\]*

What has been done here, having said that the assurance was taken from the Israelites and that the mountain was raised, is to repeat once more and remind of the very same events stated before in the 63rd verse, and, along with that, to describe *\[p. 315\]* some related matters as well. When they were told things such as that they must accept the Torah very earnestly and strictly, and that they must hear and understand all the commandments in it, the reply they gave was سَمِعۡنَا وَعَصَيۡنَا (we heard, and we also show disobedience). The gist is: we will hear with the ear, but we are not ready to bring it into practice. Showing defiance and insolence in words and in works is, surely, the habit of the Israelites. As far as they are concerned, this too need be regarded only as a word like their having said to the Prophet Musa (a) that you and your Rabb go and fight, we are sitting here, and that without seeing Allah face to face we will not believe you, and so on. Perhaps the intention of that word may be that they obeyed with the mouth, and that what happened was that they showed disobedience by attitude and by works. Or else, it may be that, when the mountain was raised and the assurance taken, they said that they would hear and obey, and that afterwards, when some time had passed, they said that they were not ready to obey. (Allah knows)

The sentence وَأُشۡرِبُواْ فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ - الخ (because of their disbelief the calf has been made to be drunk into their hearts for them) points out the excess of the passion they have for that calf which they accepted as an object of worship. This sentence is in a lofty, figurative literary style. In this, the calf, the passion for it, has been likened to a drink, and the hearts to bellies. Theirs, in the love for that calf, is a feeling such as arises when one has drunk a very tasty and pleasing drink up to the nose and the belly is full – therefore no counsels at all have entry into their heart – such is the summary. The Jews' claim, surely, is that we are believers from before, we need not believe in anything new any more. Belief, for its part, leads to good deeds and to right guidance. Their position, on the other hand, is also the very reverse. It is about this that it has been said to them بِئۡسَمَا يَأۡمُرُكُم بِهِۦٓ إِيمَٰنُكُمۡ (What your belief commands you is very bad!). The indication is that your saying that you have believed is not right; if it were right, your state of affairs would not have been like this.

*\[Verses 94–96\]*

*\[p. 317\]* What is meant by the abode of the Hereafter ( ٱلدَّارُ ٱلۡأٓخِرَةُ ) is indeed Paradise. The Jews claim that Paradise is a monopoly right, that no one else will enter Paradise, that they are Allah's favourites and children, and so on.

Among the Christians too there are these claims (see 2:80, 2:111, 5:20) Allah refutes this claim from its root and establishes that it is a lie. Allah does not stop at saying that, if it is truly and with sincerity that they put forward this claim, let them just long to die. He has also said firmly that they will not become ready for it at any time. Not only that; He has also made clear that what they have is the wish to live more than all men – even more than the mushriks, who do not believe in the Hereafter –, and that, even if perhaps a life of a thousand years were obtained by any of them, he is not going to obtain through it any exemption at all from punishment.

The purport of having said فَتَمَنَّوُاْ ٱلۡمَوۡتَ (long to die) cannot possibly be that they yearn with their minds. Because, if they say ‘we yearn’ indeed without any yearning at all in the mind, it is, surely, difficult to establish that it is a lie. Therefore the Quran commentators have explained in two ways the gist of having said **تَمَنَّوُا** (long).

(1) What is intended is that they say with the mouth: it would be good if we died. What Imam Razi (r) says is that it is this that agrees more with the word of the Quran, and that it is to this that preference must be given.

(2) The gist is that they become ready to pray: of the two parties, the Muslims and the Jews, whichever party is the one that maintains untruth, let that party meet death. This is the opinion of Ibn 'Abbas (r). According to this, this will be a challenge to the Jews for a mubahalah (making a prayer of curse against one another) (\*). What Ibn Kathir (r) has approved is this opinion. He has criticised the first opinion. The summary of the criticism is this:-

> **Note:** (\*) It is both groups praying for Allah's curse and punishment upon whichever of the two parties is the party that maintains falsehood that is called mubahalah ( **مُبَاهَلَة** ). The account of the Christians having been called to a mubahalah can be seen in Al 'Imran 61.

That interpretation cannot be justified against the Jews. Because they believe that they are truthful in their *\[p. 318\]* claim, it does not follow that they must long for death. There is no inseparable connection between the yearning for death and goodness. There are ever so many good men who are ones who do not yearn to die. Their yearning will be to obtain more ranks. That the best among you is the one whose life-span was increased and whose deeds were good has also come in hadith. There is also room for the Jews to say thus: ‘Muslims, you, surely, say that you are the rightful heirs of Paradise; even so, you do not long to die when you are in health. That being so, how is it that you make applicable to us that matter which is not applicable to you?’ This is the defect of the said interpretation. According to the interpretation of Ibn 'Abbas (r) there is none of this fault; if you properly believe that you are the rightful heirs of Paradise and Allah's favourites and so on, become ready for a mubahalah. That is, the purport of that interpretation is: make the prayer that, whichever of the two groups is the party that maintains falsehood, let them meet death. Because the Jews knew that the mubahalah would surely destroy those who maintain falsehood, they did not become ready for it. The reason for calling this mubahalah **تَمَنّى** (to long) is that the party of truth will have a longing that it would be good if Allah destroyed the party of untruth; especially when there is clear proof.

In any case, the Jews did not dare to face this challenge. Allah has also, surely, said decisively that they will not dare. Then, this challenge contains not one – indeed two proofs for the truthfulness of the noble Prophet ﷺ. This challenge to the Jews can be seen repeated in Surat al-Jumu'ah too **فلله الحجة البالغة**

### Section – 12

*\[Verses 97–98\]*

*\[p. 319\]* There is no difference of opinion among the commentators that it was following the Jews' saying that Jibril (a) is their enemy, and that therefore the Quran that he brought is not acceptable to them, that these verses came down. If anyone counts Jibril as an enemy, let them count; they can see its result. But let them know one reality: it is by Allah's order that he has sent down the Quran to the Prophet ﷺ. Otherwise, it is not anything he sent down according to his own liking. He is only a messenger of Allah. The Quran, for its part, does not deny the former scriptures either. What it does is only establish their truth. Along with that, moreover, it is a giver of right guidance to the true believers and one that announces glad tidings about mighty gains. That being so, those who bear a grudge against Jibril are bearing a grudge against Allah, against the angels who are His messengers, such as Jibril and Mika'il, and against the prophets, who are human messengers. This, for its part, is sheer kufr and denial. Allah, too, is their enemy. It is things such as these that are the gist of what Allah said to them.

Two kinds of riwayahs can be seen, showing that it was in a conversation that took place between the noble Prophet ﷺ and the Jews, in an argument concerning his prophethood, that they said that Jibril (a) is their enemy, and, or else, that it happened in a conversation that took place between 'Umar (r) and them. Even so, it is the riwayahs that it was in the conversation that took place with the Prophet ﷺ that have preference. Whichever of the two it was, their gist in brief is this: the Jews raised some questions. They also had to agree to the reply. When it was asked, then why do you not believe in the Prophet ﷺ? the reply they gave was *\[p. 320\]* thus: it is because it is Jibril who brings wahy to the Prophet ﷺ that we do not believe. He is our enemy. He is the one who comes with calamities and punishments. If it were Mika'il who brings it, we would believe. He is our friend.

The summary of what has come in a hadith that Bukhari (r) and others have quoted as stated by Anas (r) is as follows: Learning the news that ‘the noble Rasul’ had come to Madinah, 'Abdullah ibn Salam (r) came to the noble Prophet. Saying that I am asking about some matters to which none but prophets can give a reply, he asked the Prophet ﷺ some questions. Saying that a little before Jibril (a) has given me information about them, the Prophet ﷺ gave a reply to it. When mention was made of Jibril (a), he said: Jibril? He is the enemy of the Jews. Then the noble Prophet recited these verses, and, hearing the noble Prophet's reply, 'Abdullah ibn Salam (r) believed at that very moment. As Imam 'Asqalani (r) has pointed out in the commentary on Bukhari, what becomes clear from this hadith is that these verses will have come down before 'Abdullah ibn Salam (r)'s conversation **الله أعلم** 'Abdullah ibn Salam (r) was a great scholar and leader in the community of the Jews.
