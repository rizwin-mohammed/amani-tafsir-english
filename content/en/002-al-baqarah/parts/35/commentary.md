*[Verses 219–220]*

*[p. 525]* For the word **خَمْر** ‘(khamr)’, meanings such as liquor, alcohol are customarily given. When its root meaning is looked at, the meaning in the language is to cover or to conceal. Because alcohol covers and conceals the intellect, that name came to it. The head-veil (scarf) that women put on the head to conceal the hair being called **خِمَارٌ** (khimar) is also indeed like this. The place of the liquor that is made from the sap of the palm species in our land – among the Arabs at that time, it was liquor that is made from grape juice, and from date juice, that was more in currency. But that name will be said of everything belonging to the varieties of liquor. Everywhere, it is indeed all kinds of liquors that are intended. In a way that leaves no room for doubt about the meaning, the Prophet ﷺ has also made it clear. He said: **كُلُّ مُسْكر خَمر** (Everything that produces inebriation is liquor. Bu; Mu.) Then, no matter by which name it may be known, such as liquor, alcohol, arrack, whisky, brandy, cannabis, there is no doubt both that all kinds of *[p. 526]* intoxicating substances will be included in this, and that all are forbidden. In a hadith the Prophet ﷺ also says thus: ‘Surely, some men from my community will drink liquor. They will give it other names.’ (Da; Ja.) The world is also seeing the fulfilment of this prophecy of the noble Prophet.

It is to the word **مَيْسِر** (maisir) that the meaning ‘gambling’ is customarily assigned. When looked at linguistically, its root meaning is indeed taking wealth into one's hands through some way or other that is easy and quick (**اخذالمال بيسر وسهولة**). It is a word that includes all kinds of games of dice and wagers. A single word fully suited to it is not seen in Malayalam. Just as some special kinds of gambling, and raffles, and lotteries, and wagers – all – are in existence in our lands and in our time too, at the time of the Quran being sent down, among the Arabs too, some special kinds of ‘maisir’s were in currency. On that account, there is no way to say that it is only those that are intended here. Gambling, and wagers, and raffles – all indeed are forbidden things. Either because some new names that are attractive, or beautiful, have been given, or because they have been justified by stating some rational arguments or other, a Muslim may not take part in any of them.

(Like some other laws in Islam, the commandment prohibiting drinking alcohol, and gambling too was put into effect step by step. It is as its first step that this verse stands. In both there are some ills and good points. But it is the ill that stands out. Therefore, what is needed is to shun those two – that is the gist of what has been said here concerning it. After this verse was sent down, though many of the true believers desisted from them, some kept on continuing again. Following their having drunk alcohol, a few untoward incidents also came about from them. Thereafter, the commandment came (in Su: Nisa' 43) that one must not approach the prayer as ones affected by intoxication. It was later, at the last, that the commandment strictly prohibiting (in Ma'idah : 93, 94) was sent down. More information can be seen at the respective places.) (**إن شاء الله**)

The nuisances and wrongdoings that are experienced from drinkers of alcohol by their own families, and neighbours, and the people of the locality need not be told to anyone. Not only quarrelling, and fist-fights – drinking alcohol leads to all vile acts such as law-breaking, theft, wayward acts, murders. Hardships and losses related to health and finance in addition too. The story of gambling too is more or less indeed this. If through liquor-drinking one gets into the habit of extravagance, through gambling the aim will be taking the wealth of others into one's hands unjustly. When the aim is not attained, bankruptcy too will be faced. All this indeed is culpable and sinful, surely. It is pointing to ill aspects like these that it was said **فِيهِمَا إِثْمٌكَبِيرٌ** (In both there is a big wrong). At the same time some temporary *[p. 527]* good points too are obtained through them. Delight, vigour. courage, digestive power, gaining wealth, employment opportunity, and so on. Though all are only temporary, they are benefits obtained through it, surely. It is intending good points of this kind that it was said **وَمَنَافِعُ لِلنَّاسِ** (There are some benefits for mankind). That it is the ills that exceed the good points, even those who assess them only through a mere material outlook can know. Yes, if there is conscience, the ills that are yet to be faced in the material life, and in the otherworldly life, for their part, are indeed more terrible and more serious. That it is not looking at the number of the good points and ills, as some people are wont to suppose – it is looking at the seriousness – that Allah has said that it is the offence that is big in excess compared with their benefit (**وَإِثْمُهُمَاأَكْبَرُ مِنْنَفْعِهِمَا**), is evident from what has been explained above, and elsewhere.

What is in these verses is questions and replies concerning three subjects. What is understood from several riwayahs is that these questions were in reality some inquiries that came from the side of the true believers. Perhaps, even if the question has not occurred by word of mouth, when the occasion and the circumstances are looked at, it may also be that it has been stated in question-and-answer form, keeping in front their wish to know about those subjects. (Allah knows.) The summary of a riwayah that Ahmad, Ibn Abi Shaibah, Ibn Humaid, Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi, Nasa'i, Ibn Jarir (r) quoted is as follows: ‘O Allah! Grant us an explanation about liquor that gives peace of mind! Because, it destroys wealth, and the intellect, – 'Umar (r) said so. Then, the verse **يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنْ الخَمْر والمَيْسَر** (They ask you about liquor and gambling) was sent down. 'Umar (r), when he heard this recited, said so again. Then the 43rd verse of Suratun Nisa' was sent down. Then too he said indeed so. And so, verse 93, 94 of Suratul Ma'idah was sent down. When it reached the place in it **فَهَل اَنْتُمْ مُنتَهُون** (Are you desisting?!), he; We have desisted! We have desisted! – said.’ It has also been reported in riwayah that some others too asked the Prophet ﷺ that you must tell us what the ruling in the matter of liquor and gambling is.

The gist of a report that Ibn Ishaq, and Ibn Abi Hatim (r) quoted from Ibn 'Abbas (r) is as follows: ‘A group belonging to the Companions were commanded to spend in the way of Allah. They went to the Prophet ﷺ and said: We do not know what it is that needs to be spent from our wealth. Then the verse **وَيَسْأَلُونَكَمَاذَايُنْفِقُونَ** (They ask you what they must spend) was sent down. Before that, a man, spending his wealth (entirely), used to spe*[p. 528]*nd in such a way as to be with nothing remaining, and, in case he did not get charity, with no means for food.’ There are also some other riwayahs like this. From this the intention of this second question has been understood, surely. The reply to this is **ٱلۡعَفۡوَ** (what can be let go – what is convenient). That the intended sense by this is what does not cause straitness and difficulty, and what is surplus after deducting the dire needs, can be understood from hadiths like those quoted below. It is that, surely, which everyone is able to bring into practice, and that is practicable. In case a person, being more ready for self-sacrifice, and giving preference to the need of others over his own need, spends more, that is indeed good and excellent. Allah's having said, praising the Companions who were Ansaris, that even if there was dire need for them, they give importance to others over their bodies (**وَيُؤْثِرُونَ عَلَىأَنْفُسِهِمْ وَلَوْكَانَبِهِمْ خَصَاصَةٌ**) is worth stating. (59: 9)

Jabir (r) quotes that the Rasul ﷺ said thus to a man: ‘Begin with your body. And so, give charity to it. Then if anything is surplus, it is for your household. If anything becomes surplus from the household, it is for your family members, and if there is surplus from your families too, here, do like this and like this’. (Muslim.) That is, after that give to whom you like – that is the gist. In another hadith he says: ‘O son of Adam (O man), spending what is surplus is surely a good thing for you. Holding it back is also an ill for you. You will not be reproached about what is only for need. Begin with your family burden (your wife and children for whom you are bound to spend). The hand that stands raised (that gives) is more excellent than the hand that stands lowered (that receives).’ (Muslim)

The third question is on the subject of orphan children. When it was sent down, both ‘Do not approach the property of orphans except through the best way’ (6: 152; 17: 34.) and ‘Those who eat the property of orphans wrongfully – what they fill their bellies with is only fire’. (4: 10) and so on, those who had been carrying on the management of orphans felt great hardship. They adopted strictness themselves, so as to take care not to mix together with them even in food and drink. That what is intended by this question is some inquiries that came from them when they became unable to bear this stance is understood from several riwayahs that Abu Dawud, Nasa'i, Ibn Jarir (r) and others quoted. The gist of the reply given to this can be understood like this:-

Be it in properties, be it in other matters, doing any matter by which goodness and prosperity come about for orphans is very good both for you and for them. Through it there is great benefit to be attained, for them in this world, and for you in the next. This is a general principle. Now, if you and they are adopting a joint life, there is no objection at all to that. They are your brothers in religion – sometimes in family too – surely. As if they were your *[p. 529]* own brothers, you must both deal with them in love and in harmony, and strive for their good and goodness, and guard against corruption or loss coming to them. Just as brothers use their properties jointly with one another in daily needs like food and drink, you too may do so. But it is only that it must not lead to bringing harm and loss to them, or to exploiting their wealth. What you are doing, what your intention is, and so on – Allah knows well. You must keep this in memory. Allah does not intend to put you to difficulty by imposing laws and compulsions that you are unable to put into practice. Such things are the import of what Allah said in that reply. **والله اعلم**

*[Verse 221]*

*[p. 530]* In a few verses from this on, what Allah teaches is many rules of procedure and laws that the true believers need to observe in the marital and domestic spheres. Commanding that it is not permitted both for men who are true believers – yes, who are Muslims – to marry women who are mushriks, and to give Muslim women in marriage to men who are mushriks, Allah has entirely made forbidden the making of marriage ties between Muslims and non-Muslims. Allah has also made clear both that, however much indeed a woman or a man belonging to them may attract you for any reason such as beauty, wealth, nobility of birth, attractive behaviour, merit, and so on, marriage ties must not be made with them, and that better than that is making marriage ties with slaves who are true believers. But, it is only concerning the mushriks (polytheists) that Allah said, surely – He has not stated anything about disbeliever (kafir)s in general, surely – that being the case, may marriage ties not be made with disbelievers other than mushriks? – it may be asked. The reply to this:-

(1) After commanding that if women who are true believers come in hijrah, they must not be sent back to the disbeliever (ka*[p. 531]*fir)s, Allah says in Suratul Mumtahinah: **لاهُنَّحِلٌّ لَهُمْ وَلا هُمْ يَحِلُّونَ لَهُنَّ –الممتحنة: ١٠** (Those women are not permissible for them – for the disbelievers –; they are not permissible for them too – for those women too –.) Then, it becomes clear that all kafir (disbeliever)s too are indeed like the mushriks.

(2) Even if, perhaps, open shirks like idol-worship are not seen – disbelievers who are pure of shirk, if not in one way then in another, will not be seen, moreover.

(3) The principle contained in having decided that there must be no marriage tie between the two parties, that Allah indeed explained. It is ‘It is to Hell that they invite.’ (**أُولَئِكَ يَدْعُونَ إِلَى النَّارِ**), surely. All the beliefs and customs of the disbelievers indeed are ones that clear the road to Hell, and that block the way to Paradise, surely. To put it briefly, Allah has made clear in the 6th verse of Suratul Ma'idah that Muslims may marry women from among the People of the Scripture with a few conditions. From the general law that marriage ties must not be established between Muslims and non-Muslims, only this has been exempted.

Because of the impact of material interests, some of modern mentality, when stating about this prohibition, can be seen trying to show the rigour of this law as somewhat lightened, saying both that in case wife and husband have no compatibility of ideals their marriage tie will not be successful, and that that is the gist of having said that they invite to Hell, and Allah to Paradise, and so on. In fact, though the marriage tie becoming successful is indeed the intention of Islam, it is not its success or failure that is the real background that has been considered in this prohibition. What has been considered in it is unity and disunity in true faith. Anyone can understand that from the words in this verse indeed. Above the unity of merely some ideal or other is agreement in religion and in belief. It is that which has been intended here.

The Prophet ﷺ has declared thus: ‘A woman is married for four things: for her wealth, and for nobility of lineage, and for beauty, and for religion. But, gain good fortune by the one who has religion (marrying her). (Otherwise) may your hands be smeared with dust.’ (Bu; Mu.) Or, it will be ill fortune for you – that is the gist. In another hadith the noble Prophet says: ‘This world is indeed a provision. Of the provisions of this world, the most excellent is a woman of good conduct (wife).’ (Mu.)

### Section – 28

*[Verses 222–223]*

*[p. 533]* The summary of a hadith that Imam Ahmad, and Muslim (r) quoted is like this: ‘When it became the menstrual period for women, the Jews used neither to eat food together with them, nor to move about together in the house. The Companions asked the Prophet ﷺ about this. It was then that this verse was sent down. And so, the Prophet ﷺ said that everything except coitus may be done. On hearing this, the Jews said that this man wants only to act contrary to us. Protesting at that, some Companions came and asked the Prophet ﷺ: Then, what if we carry out coitus too? Then the facial expression of the Prophet ﷺ changed (became angry)’. Among the Hindus too, like the Jews, the usage of observing untouchability with women in the menstrual period exists. As in every matter, in this subject too, what Islam has adopted is an objective approach.

Menstruation is a harm. Or, it is filth and unclean. Therefore Allah commands the true believers that in the menstrual period they must keep away from the wives without approaching. The import of having said that one must not approach is not that one must not go anywhere near them, or that they may not defile anyone by touch. It has become clear from the hadiths of the noble Prophet ﷺ both that it is that the approach connected with the said place of filth – coitus – is not permitted, and that there is no objection to other approaches and relations. Other than that menstrual blood is a filthy substance, Allah has not stated that when there is menstruation women will be filthy. What is intended by having said ‘become pure’ is, after the menstrual blood has stopped, to bathe and become pure *[p. 534]*. This bath is obligatory, moreover. The import of having said ‘go through the manner Allah commanded’ is indeed to approach in a manner that does not violate human and natural proprieties, and religious directions. This is a commandment that affects in general the married life between wife and husband. Or, it is not a commandment intending only the approach after purity from menstruation. It is clear, surely, both that menstruation is an impurity that is external and physical, and that the purification from it is the bath. But, Allah has also alerted both that, likewise, sins are some filths that are internal and spiritual, and that the purification from them is repentance, and that Allah will like both kinds of purification. Surely, Allah likes those who repent. He likes those who attain purity too.’

The sentence **نِسَاؤُكُمْحَرْثٌلَكُمْ** (Your women – wives – are your tillage-ground) points out a very great principle. Tillage is, surely, preparing the land and sowing seed, and the seed sprouting and the produce ripening. Then, in one sense, wives too are indeed a tillage-ground. Though the goodness of the tillage and favourable conditions will generally produce delight and gladness for the farmer, it is the yield from it that is his aim, surely. Likewise, offspring is the actual aim of this tillage. It is through this tillage that the human race both continues to exist in this world, and grows and increases. That is indeed a natural law that Allah, who is the Creator and Sustainer, has fixed. The meaning of this is that it is not merely as only a means for fulfilling romantic feelings, or physical yearnings – though that too is indeed worthy of consideration – it is aiming at that aim which is above that, that Allah has reckoned the marriage tie. In the sentences that follow too, an indication towards that can be seen. From many other Quran verses too this reality is also understood very clearly indeed.

After saying that wives are a tillage-ground, Allah has also stated, ‘Therefore go to your tillage-ground as you have intended’. Yes, that in going to them you may adopt every manner you like – that is the gist. But, it need not be specially said, surely, both that entering into the tillage-grounds must be through the ways suited for production, and that entries contrary to that are not right. Look at the lessons of married life that Allah teaches to mankind, who are His creation, and the words used for it! A few words that are very simple, and decent, and heartwarming! A style full of love and tenderness! Other than from Allah, where is a style of teaching of this kind to be seen?! . **اللهُ اكبر** What the Prophet ﷺ stated on one occasion – that the grace that Allah has towards His servants is more than what a mother has towards her suckling darling infant – is worth remembering here.

*[p. 535]* Pay attention to the next sentences: ‘You must make advance preparation for yourselves indeed, you must guard against Allah, you must be aware that there will be need to meet Him’ – Allah advises such things. Paying attention to married life and other things, one must not forget one's own future. Doing good deeds, and observing the injunctions and prohibitions of Allah, one must keep preparing ahead of time the ways for future success; there must always be remembrance that after death one will have to appear before Allah and face His reckoning – that is the import.

For the sentence **وَقَدِّمُوالأنْفُسِكُمْ** (Do and lay by beforehand for yourselves indeed), what most commentators have given is an interpretation in the meaning seen above. As Ibn Kathir (r) indicated, both the sentences after it – the sentences that one must guard against Allah, and that one must be aware that one will meet Him – also give support to this interpretation. Your wish must be both for good offspring who are doers of good deeds to be born from the approach with the wives, and for the line of descent to continue through them; you must also make supplication for that. That such things are the import of this sentence is indeed the opinion of some other commentators. It is basing on this being an occasion that speaks about the wife- husband relationship that this opinion is. As can be seen in the hadith quoted below – good offspring is, surely, a good deed that is not cut off even after death. That being so, there is no doubt that both wishing for it, and supplicating, are indeed a good deed. But, it must not be thought that it is only that which has been intended by this sentence.

Abu Hurairah (r) quotes that the Rasul ﷺ said: ‘When a man dies, his deeds that do not concern three things are (all) cut off. That is, almsgiving that keeps going on, and learning that is useful, and offspring of good conduct who make supplication for him.’ (Mu.) Ibn 'Abbas (r) quotes that the Rasul ﷺ said: ‘In case one of you, when going to his household (wife) (at the time of union), says **بِسْمِ اللَّهِ اللَّهُمَّ جَنِّبْنَا الشَّيْطَانَ وَجَنِّبْ الشَّيْطَانَ مَا رَزَقْتَنَا**, and offspring is decreed between the two, Satan will never bring harm to that offspring’ (Bu; Mu.) The gist of this supplication is this: ‘In the name of Allah. O Allah! Keep Satan away from us! Keep Satan away from what You give us (from the offspring) too!’

A very strange interpretation of meaning, which is neither of the two mentioned above, is seen to have been given to this sentence by a scholar now. It is like this: ‘And do and lay by beforehand (the preparations needed when going to the tillage-place).’ The interpretation that that scholar himself gave to this sentence is also as fol*[p. 536]*lows: ‘When going to the women, one must think about the future. That is, one must make and lay by the preparations needed for their upbringing and training when offspring are born in it. It is not enough to think about it only when offspring come about – that is the meaning’. This is indeed one of the interpretations of meaning that are carried out trying to portray the statements of the Quran, as far as possible, as an asset towards the success of material life. Anyone who has conscience and an impartial intellect can understand the motive behind this. Be that as it may, examine this interpretation of meaning a little, keeping some facts in front:--

What Allah said is that you must do and lay by beforehand for your own selves – for yourselves indeed – ( **وَقَدِّمُوالأنْفُسِكُمْ** ). What these say, for their part, is also that one must make and lay by the preparations needed for the upbringing and training of the offspring who are going to be born in the future. The making and laying by of these preparations, for its part, – according indeed to what these said – is when going to the women, moreover. We do not understand the meaning of this. If what is said were that this preparation must be made and laid by before marriage indeed, it could have been agreed that there is at least a little meaning in it. Now, have Allah and His Rasul commanded or advised either that only after setting aside beforehand the means for rearing and for training the offspring and so on may one marry, or may one approach the wives? That too there is not. On the contrary, Allah says both that those who are unable to marry women who are free may marry slave women (4:25), and that you must give in marriage to the unmarried and to the slaves who are good, and that if they are poor Allah will in due course give them sufficiency (24:32). The Prophet ﷺ too has commanded both that young men who have the ability to conduct marriage must marry, and that those who have not the ability must keep fasts. (Bu; Mu.) Other than that if there comes to be ability to marry one may do it, it has not been stated in the Quran or in hadith that it must be after setting aside beforehand the means that will be needed for the needs of the offspring who may come about in it. From what has been said this far indeed, it has been understood, surely, that this new interpretation of meaning of these is contrary to the truth.
