### Section – 36

*[Verse 261]*

*[p. 643]* This verse and the few verses that follow are ones describing both the importance of spending wealth in the way of Allah, and many matters that need to be known in spending wealth. By the way of Allah ( **سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ** ) what is meant is all the good subjects that He is pleased with. Standing in the forefront among them is ‘Jihad’ -or the righteous struggle in Islam. Allah has made clear (in Su: An'am, 160) both that for goodnesses at least tenfold recompense will be obtained, and that for evils recompense will be given only in that same measure. He informs through this verse both that for the goodness that is spending wealth in the way of Allah He will give recompense up to seven-hundredfold, and that, not only that, to those whom Allah wills even more than that will also be obtained. He also reminds both that He is one who has no stinginess, or reluctance, or hardship in giving however much recompense, and that He knows to whom all it is that more must be given, for what all it is that much must be given, and so on.

That, without saying that He will give seven-hundredfold recompense, it was said like grain that produces seven ears with a hundred grains each is noteworthy. The indication that is in this is that that increase will be both according to the purity of the wealth that is spent, the importance of the subject, the intention and aim of the one who spends and the like, and according to the rate of the goodnesses that come about as the fruit of what was spent. It is, surely, according to the seed's *[p. 644]* quality, the soil's quality, and the rate at which water and manure are obtained, that both the growth and the yield of the plant come about. Not only spending wealth: the state of all good deeds is this indeed. This fact can be seen made more clear in the sayings of the Prophet.

The Prophet ﷺ has declared in this manner: ‘If a person gives in charity anything equal to a dried date from pure earnings- Allah will not accept other than what is pure, moreover- Allah will accept it with His right hand (gladly). Then, just as one of you rears his foal (fondling it), He will go on rearing it for its person. Thus, it will become like a mountain.’ (Bu; Mu.) A person came with a camel with a nose-rope on and said to the Prophet ﷺ: ‘O Rasul, this is for the way of Allah.’ Then, the noble Prophet said: ‘In return for it you will have seven hundred camels on the Day of Qiyamah.’ (A; Mu; Na.) The gist of another saying of the Prophet is this: ‘For all the (good) deeds of man, for one goodness it will be as much as ten times it. Thus, it will be up to seven-hundredfold. Allah says: Except fasting -that is for Me -it is I indeed who will give recompense for it. He gives up his desire of the self and his food on account of Me....’ (Bu; Mu.)

*[Verses 262–264]*

*[p. 646]* Allah counsels the true believers about many matters that those who spend wealth in the way of Allah - on good causes- necessarily need to understand. Pointing out three matters that render almsgiving void of fruit, He also commands to give them up.

(1) Bringing up and enumerating the benefaction done ( **الْمَنّ** ). It is false pride and the craving to earn a good name that will prompt to this. As for those to whom the benefaction has been done, this will also inflict damage on their dignity and esteem.

(2) Bringing harm and disturbance, by word or by work, to the person to whom the benefaction has been done ( **الاذى** ) The origin of this is from the yearning to see thanks and loyalty kept up, or to obtain a return benefaction, on account of the benefaction done, and from the evil thought that he is indebted to him.

(3) Acting in order to show others, and with the intention that it would be fine if they see ( **الرياء** ), the aim of this is gaining name and fame among the people.

Allah informs both that all these three matters will make almsgiving fruitless, and that they will merit recompense only if they are secure from them, and are in the way of Allah, and that Allah has prepared and kept ready mighty fruit for such outlays of wealth. He has also made the promise that there is no need for fear that the good deeds done in that manner will go to waste, either without fruit being obtained for them, or by meeting with some detriment, and that there will never in future come the having to grieve about what was spent either.

That, in case any harm or disturbance comes about following upon doing almsgiving- whether it be affecting individuals or institutions- better than that with Allah is not doing that almsgiving; that what is more excellent than that is consoling by saying a good word, and behaving with accommodation, and so on, are the ideas in the second verse. That Allah has no need of any of the almsgiving of the people; that the good of it is for themselves indeed; that therefore they should guard against the matters by which it becomes fruitless; that, against those who do not guard, for the time being any action not being ta*[p. 647]*ken is because of Allah's forbearance, and so on, are the indications contained in the sentence ‘Allah is the Self-Sufficient *[DOUBT: ധന്യൻ has no earlier rendering in this translation; “Self-Sufficient” follows part 32 (സർവ്വധന്യൻ “the wholly Self-Sufficient One”) and the author's own bracket “(പരാശ്രയം വേണ്ടാത്ത)” in the verse 263 panel. Literally “one who is rich” or “one who is blessed”; “the Rich” is the literal option.]* and the Forbearing ( **واللّه غنى حليم** )’. He has pointed out in the third verse too, with an example, both that almsgiving must not be made fruitless by bringing up the benefaction done, and by causing harm and disturbance, and that that is equal to spending wealth for name and glory among the people without belief in Allah and in the Last Day, and that that is not befitting for true believers. When there is a little soil on a rock that has hardness and smoothness, if a strong rain strikes it, that soil will not at all remain there afterwards, surely. Like that are the deeds of those who are such. That through them there is no benefit at all for them to obtain in the Hereafter – that indeed is the gist of the parable.

Allah's having described at length in Suratud-Dahr (in Su: Insan) about the great favours that the righteous obtain, who, without intending any recompense or thanks, both yearning for the pleasure of Allah and intending to escape from His punishment, give food to the needy, can be seen. Abu Dharr (r) states that the Prophet ﷺ said in this manner. There are three parties: on the Day of Qiyamah Allah will not speak to them (on account of anger); nor will He look towards them; nor will He refine them. For them there will also be a painful punishment. That is, the one who keeps on bringing up what he gave, and the one who drags his garment on the ground (for dignity), and the one who sells off merchandise by means of a false oath’ (Mu.) He also goes on to describe what the aim of those who spend wealth should be:-

*[Verse 265]*

*[p. 648]* In this verse Allah describes a parable of those who spend wealth on good causes, both aiming at the pleasure and approval of Allah, and as a firming of the belief in His messages and promises. A garden situated in an upland region that stands raised quite some height from the ordinary level ground. Since the air and the soil there are pure from filth, the fruits and produce in it will, surely, have special quality and taste. The manures that are given will also become more beneficial. If good rain too is obtained along with that, twice as much fruit as what is obtained from other gardens will be obtained from it. Even if enough rain is not obtained, even if light rain is obtained, that garden will not fail to give quite some fruit. Just like this indeed is their almsgiving too. It is not for name and glory that they incur expense. Keeping on bringing up the benefaction done, or creating any disturbance on account of it, is also not something that comes about from them. Therefore their almsgiving will not fail to obtain fruit. However much more their purity of intention and firmness of belief become, according to that that fruit will also go on increasing – that is the gist. At the end He has also reminded that Allah is minutely seeing and knowing the actions and stand and state of mind of each one.

*[Verse 266]*

*[p. 649]* Date-palm gardens and grape gardens were, as far as the Arabs are concerned, the riches from which the very best income is obtained. In those gardens indeed there will also be, besides, plants and trees that give many other kinds of fruits. If enough water facility for watering and water-channels too come together, there is no need to tell the advantage of that garden. It is, surely, when old age takes hold of the garden-owner, and along with that he also has quite some children who have not reached the capability or the age to strive for a means of living, that the necessity and the usefulness of such a large garden, from which income keeps being obtained daily, become greatest. In this condition, if a mighty whirlwind and fire suddenly befall such a garden, and the garden just burns and perishes away, what will the story be then?! Consider and look! Like this is the state of those who both do many good deeds and do not give up the matters that make them fruitless. The Day of Qiyamah is, surely, an occasion on which there is no other way out at all except enjoying the fruit of the good deeds that were done in the worldly life. When arriving there, once all those deeds turn out to be fruitless, what other calamity and loss is there greater than that?! May Allah protect us. Amin.

The gist of a hadith that Imam Bukhari (r) quoted as though an interpretation of this verse *[p. 650]* is in this manner: Concerning whose case it is your opinion that this verse was revealed, 'Umar (r) asked in a gathering of the Companions. They replied that Allah knows. This reply did not please him. It came to: either that it is known, or that it is not known, one of the two must be said. On this occasion, Ibn 'Abbas (r) said that in my mind there is an opinion concerning it. He emboldened (the young man) Ibn 'Abbas (r) to say it openly without reluctance. He said: ‘A rich person does good things, being obedient to Allah. Then, through the prompting of Satan, they act in disobedience to Allah. It is about people of this kind.’ This is the hadith. As Ibn Kathir (r) pointed out, not only spending wealth: this is a parable that is applicable to those who do all deeds too.

### Section – 37

*[Verses 267–268]*

*[p. 651]* The ways by which wealth comes in are mainly two in number. What is earned by toiling and by doing work, and what is produced from the earth. Allah counsels the true believers both that, by whichever way of these two it may have been obtained, they must spend from it on good causes, and that the wealth that is spent must be good and pure. As was seen in the saying of the Prophet quoted before, Allah will not accept other than what is pure, moreover. That the thing that is spent must not be of an inferior kind, or having damage, or forbidden, is indeed the import of having said that spending must be from good things ( **طَيِبَات** ). The method of using bad and inferior-kind things for almsgiving can be seen in many. Allah disallows this. When anyone gives such things hither- other than by making accommodation and closing the eyes- anyone will be reluctant to accept it, surely. He also reminds that, like that, the things that are given thither too must be good and choice. Commanding and counselling like this is for the good of man himself indeed. Allah has no need or gain at all in any of that. He needs no dependence of any kind on anyone. That in His qualities and works and all He is wholly worthy of praises and extolments, and so on, are what the sentence **واللَّهَغَنِيٌّ حَمِيدٌ** (Allah is Self-Sufficient and Praiseworthy) indicates.

If what is in hand is spent, one will get trapped. That one will suffer without a way to em*[p. 652]*ploy for one's own urgent needs, thought and fear such as this are what make man a miser. At the same time, the very things which he feels stingy to spend in a good way, it may also come about that he sometimes spends on evil conduct and licentiousness. This is the fruit of the threats and the evil promptings that arise from Satan. Leading into error by carrying on evil mantra in the human mind is the job of Satan. It is he who thrusts those thoughts into human minds. On the contrary, Allah too does make some promises to human beings. That is not that, if anything is spent, he will immediately indeed obtain more than that in return. His promise is indeed greater than that. It is that He will forgive sins, and that He will give favour and kindness. That, moreover, is mightier than any gain that can be obtained in this life.

Here what is meant by ‘Satan ( **الشَّيْطَانُ** )’ is the actual Satan indeed, who is an unseen being. It is because some who deny the actual Satan are seen writing that what is meant here is the human devil who frightens that if wealth is spent poverty will take hold, that this is specially alerted. That there are some human devils who carry on this satanic evil mantra is right indeed. But, in this subject it will be the actual Satan who exerts more influence. Who is it that can deny the reality that there are so many people who, even without another person's threat or evil mantra indeed, live the whole lifetime as severe misers?! However, it is only ‘intellectuals’ such as is stated in the next verse who will be able to rightly discern both the actual Satan and the human devil.
