### Section – 13

*\[Verses 104–105\]*

*\[p. 334\]* The meaning of the word **رَٰعِنَا** (Ra'ina) is ‘heed us’, that is, attend to us, telling us in a way that we understand. That very same word was also used in their language as a word of abuse, such as idiot, fool. When the Prophet ﷺ says something, the Jews will say this word to the Prophet ﷺ in such a way that it seems that they are observing respect and propriety befitting the occasion. Their real intent, for its part, was also to mock the noble Prophet, calling him an idiot. (That they used some other contrived words too like this can be seen in Surah Nisa' 46). Because the true believers too used to say this word to the Prophet ﷺ, there is also no way to blame them on the face of it. Not only that, the Jews will also take delight in the true believers too using that word like themselves. Therefore Allah is advising the true believers that the true believers must not use such words, and that they must use words that give no room for doubt about the meaning and that are free from taint. He also commands to say ‘Unzurna’ ( **ٱنظُرۡنَا** ) in place of ‘Ra'ina’, and to hear properly what the Prophet ﷺ says. and to understand. It is indeed ‘look at us – attend to us –’ that is the meaning of this word. But in this there is no loophole for taint and mockery as there is in ‘Ra'ina’. In the warning at the end of the ayah, ‘for the disbelievers there is a painful punishment’, there is an indication that using such words of mockery to the Prophet ﷺ is because of disbelief, and that it *\[p. 335\]* does not suit true believers.

The disbelievers, who are the People of the Scripture and the mushriks, will not like you, who are true believers, obtaining any goodness at all from Allah's side. That is, their enmity and evil mentality are very severe. That such things as the favour that is prophethood, and the good fortune to believe in it and gain success, are obtained only by some, without being obtained by everyone, is not because Allah's favour and mercy are narrow. It is indeed very great and mighty. But it is to those whom He wills, in accordance with some secrets of wisdom known only to Him, that He will give it. Such things as these are the idea in the second verse. He knows well, surely, who those are who leave Allah's way and go astray, and who those are who attain the straight way (6:117)

*\[Verses 106–107\]*

*\[p. 336\]* **نَسخْ** (naskh): its linguistic meaning is to remove, to copy, and the like. What is meant by it is: when the principle that is the basis of a religious ruling comes to an end, annulling that ruling and bringing another ruling in its place. **آيَة** (ayah): its word meaning is mark sign, symbol and the like. Scriptural sentences and Quran verses are called ayahs. They are, surely, signs of Allah's injunctions and prohibitions and of His messages. The import of having said **نُنسِهَا** (causing the ayahs to be forgotten) is indeed removing them from reading and from hearts and doing away with the remembrance of them.

It is as a reply to one of the reproaches that the Jews used to raise against Islam that this verse has been brought down. It is said, surely, that Muhammad is a prophet and that the Quran is the word of God. It was Allah who brought down all the scriptures. That being so, the Quran annuls some laws of the Torah and brings new laws. Naskh is seen to be present even in the Quran. How does all this happen? What Allah once brought down, does He Himself later change? Is it because a mistake befell Him in the first ruling? Such was the nature of their reproach. As can be seen in the 142nd verse and in some verses following it, it was on the occasion when the qiblah, which had been Bait al-Muqaddas, was changed and the Ka'bah was made the qiblah and established, that this accusation became intense. They were not doubts arising from ignorance or from eagerness to inquire. Their aim was to oppose the Quran, making use of whatever loophole they got, and to create confusion among the true believers.

In reality, naskh is not something that began to be seen only in the Quran. There was naskh in the earlier scriptures too. Naskh can be seen in the very Torah that is in their hands, too. In the time of the Prophet Adam (a), marriage could be conducted between his sons and daughters. There were no other women then. Later that was prohibited. That the Israelites, in the early period, could marry two sisters together was annulled by means of the Torah. It was first commanded to the Prophet Ibrahim (a) to slaughter his son. Later that command was annulled and it was decided that it was enough to slaughter a sheep. It was first commanded to kill the Israelites who worshipped the calf. When a number of people had been killed, stopping the killing was commanded; many things thus. *\[p. 337\]* All this cannot possibly be denied by the Jews. The reply that Allah gives to this accusation is not only a reply that merely silences the Jews; it is also a reality that everyone in general must know and understand. Contained in this reply is plenty of material to think about for everyone who argues that there cannot be naskh in religious rulings and that it does not accord with wisdom. The reply that Allah gave to the Quraish too arguing this same argument, and accusing on that account that the Quran is the Prophet's ﷺ own composition, can be seen in Surat al-Nahl 101. These must heed that reply too.

The import of the reply stated here is this: ‘The heavens and the earth and all that is in them are Allah's. Ownership, rule and lordship are indeed His. There is only He to determine all that is needed for those in them. It is He who has the right and the competence to implement and to abolish the legal measures that He wills, and to decide such things as when – where – for whom – which laws are needed. No one has the authority to question it. He is the One who knows such things as in what their goodness and good lie, and what is more suitable for them. He is also the One who is able to do every matter, all these, surely, are well-accepted realities. Such a One annulled some ruling in the scriptures. Or He caused it to be entirely forgotten in such a way that it does not remain in the hearts of the people and in recitations – let it be so; then He also brings another ruling, more excellent and more beneficial than it, in its place. At the least He will bring something equal to it. The fact being this, questioning it and criticising it is defiance and wrongdoing.

In the intellectual, mental, cultural and social fields, mankind, like the other living species, is not at the same level from its earliest time until today. As time goes on, it keeps advancing and growing. Surroundings and circumstances also keep changing. In this change, means of communication, facilities of transport and so on also have a share. It is in accordance with that that the prophets who came in earlier times, and their missions and preachings, were only for some time and place. Thus, after the childhood stage of man's growth was passed, Muhammad ﷺ has been appointed as the Rasul for all times and places and as the final prophet, and the Quran has come down as the scripture for the whole of the world's people. In that situation, it is only natural that some legal measures that had been prescribed for one time, or for one people, in accordance with the circumstances of that day should become not practicable and appropriate for all times. In their place, settled legal measures practicable among all became necessary. Then, making naskh, through the Quran, of some laws and modes of customary practice and observance in the earlier scriptures *\[p. 338\]* is not merely an appropriate measure; it is also an inevitable need. It is not a shortcoming in Allah's law-making; it is its perfection that it shows.

Allah has made naskh of some laws of the Torah in the Injil, and of some laws of both in the Quran. Some divine books – leaves ( **الصحف** ) – that had been given to some prophets such as Ibrahim (a) and Musa (a), afterwards, without being preserved, have also been taken away even from the people's memory. That being so, there is no sense in the Jews or others putting forth reproach only about having made naskh in the Quran of some laws of the Torah.

Among Muslims there is no difference of opinion that there can be naskh in religious rulings and that there has been. The opinion of the majority of scholars is that even in the Quran there are a few ayahs that have undergone naskh (whose ruling has been annulled). When looked at factually, this indeed is the correct opinion. This can be understood from a statement of Imam Ibn Kathir (r). He says : “The Muslims are agreed that there is no objection to naskh occurring in Allah's hukm (religious ruling)s. Because, strong wisdom is contained in it. They all say that it has occurred: Abu Muslim Isfahani (r), the Quran commentator, says : that no naskh at all has occurred in the Quran. This word of his is weak and a mistake. He has strained to reply about the naskhs that have occurred. That the 'iddah period (of a woman whose husband has died), which was one year, was made four months and ten days is one of them. To this he has not said any acceptable reply. That, after Bait al-Muqaddas had been the qiblah, it was changed to the Ka'bah is also among them. *\[Verse 108\]*

*\[p. 340\]* We saw, surely, in the earlier verses many examples of the questions that the Israelites had been shooting at Musa (a). If some were superfluous, some were defiance and folly, and some others were mockery. The questions that arose when commanded to slaughter the cow, and demanding to be shown Allah, are some of them. The Jews too used to put unnecessary questions and inquiries to the Prophet ﷺ. They also kept urging the Muslims to it. Therefore, along with the protest against the Jews, Allah, through this verse, also commands the Muslims that the true believers must observe moderation and propriety in asking questions of the Prophet ﷺ. He also warns that going beyond bounds in questioning will lead the way to disbelief and that it must be guarded against seriously.

What has been said here does not mean that one must not ask the Prophet ﷺ about doubts and learn, or inquire about necessary matters. What Allah said is that questions of the kind that his people asked Musa (a) must not be asked of the Prophet ﷺ. *\[Verses 109–110\]*

*\[p. 342\]* You must not take part in any of the matters that the disbelievers who are People of the Scripture have been adopting contrary to Islam. They are your enemies. The yearning of many of them is that they must somehow lead you astray from true faith and make you disbelievers like them. It is for that, too, that they strive. It is not because they do not understand the real – it is indeed while understanding clearly – that they do it. It is only out of the jealousy that has become firmly rooted in their minds. Therefore you must be on guard, without falling into their trap. Let them do what they do; Allah is going to make the remedy for it later on. (He will indeed bring it into effect, giving you victory and glory, and giving them defeat and disgrace.) There is nothing that He cannot do; until then, you wait, and adopt patience and disregard. Keep observing duly the prayer and Zakat, which are your principal duties. In short, try also to earn by doing whatever is goodness and virtue. For any goodness at all that you do before death, nothing at all will go to waste without obtaining a mighty reward from Allah's presence. Nothing will be left out, either, without being included in His knowledge and account. Allah is giving the true believers many pieces of advice and direction such as these.

*\[Verses 111–112\]*

*\[p. 343\]* One of the claims in which the People of the Scripture, deceived by themselves, have fallen into error is their claim that they alone are the heirs of Paradise. Although the Jews and the Christians agree in this claim, neither are the Jews ready to grant that right to the Christians, nor the Christians to grant it to the Jews, moreover. That will become clear from the next verse. Allah says, affirming and driving it home, that there is not even a particle of proof or justification in it, other than its being merely a delusion of theirs. He also challenges them, if there is any proof, to present it. Along with that He has also pointed out the real position of the matter. Whoever they are who surrender completely to Allah's injunctions and prohibitions, doing acts of well-doing, it is for them that there are deliverance and Paradise and the like. Moreover, they have nothing to fear or to grieve. For those who are not so, nothing at all, whether Paradise or refuge, is to be obtained from Allah. In this, race, community or nobility of birth have no place at all. The gist is that this is the real.

The word meaning of the word **مُحۡسِنٞ** (muhsin) is one who does well, a man of good qualities, one who does goodness, and the like. What is meant is: one who does good things sincerely and with belief and good intention – one who does virtuous things that are approved from the religious point of view, aiming at Allah's pleasure –. That is why the good deeds that disbelievers do are of no value with Allah. Allah says : **وَقَدِمۡنَآ إِلَىٰ مَا عَمِلُواْ....... مَّنثُورًا** (Proceeding to the deeds that they have done, We will make it scattered dust. (Su. Furqan 23)
