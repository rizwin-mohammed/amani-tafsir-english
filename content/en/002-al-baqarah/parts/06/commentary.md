*\[Verses 31–33\]*

*\[p. 228\]* When He made known the news that He was going to appoint a khalifah in the earth, the hint of the reply they gave was, surely, that human beings, being ones who cause corruption and shed blood in the earth, are not fit to be made khalifah, and that they themselves, being ones who keep doing acts of worship such as 'tasbih, taqdis', are fit for it. Allah showed them in experience that this notion is wrong. He taught the Prophet Adam (a) the names of all things. After that, showing them to the angels, He asked them to tell their names. They were not able to do it. What they did was to admit their incapacity and to extol the greatness of Allah. When the Prophet Adam (a) was asked to tell their names, he explained all of it. In this way, through experience, Allah made them realise their mistake. They saw in experience the fulfilment of what Allah had said to them before: 'I know the things that you do not know.'

Most of the Quran commentators say that the purport of saying 'all the names' (**ٱلۡأَسۡمَآءَ كُلَّهَا**) is the names of all kinds of things. Some riwayahs from Ibn Abbas (r) and others also show that. Imam Bukhari (r) has given a separate heading itself to this subject. What he has quoted in it is the famous hadith known as **حديث الشفاعة** (the hadith of intercession) – what it contains is the account of human beings, on the Day of Qiyamah, in the 'Mahshar', *\[p. 229\]* requesting the leading prophets to intercede with Allah in order that a decision be taken in their matter. The part in it concerning the Prophet Adam (a) is like this: 'They will go to Adam and say: You are the father of mankind. Allah created you with His noble hand. He has made the angels do sujud to you, too. He has taught you the names of all things, too. Therefore it would be good if you would intercede with the Rabb for us, to give us relief from this place!...' (This hadith has been quoted by many, such as Muslim.) In this, the Prophet ﷺ has stated 'the names of all things', surely. But those commentators have also pointed out that, in the sentences that follow, joining with reference to those things **هُمْ** (they) – the pronoun (**ضَمِير**) used with reference to rational beings – is one style among literary styles (\*). This usage can also be seen in the Quran elsewhere too, in places such as (24: 45). Even so, some among the commentators have held the opinion that, on the basis of these following usages, what is meant here is only the names of rational beings. **أَللهُ أَعْلَم**

> **Note:** (\*) Usages which, on the face of it, seem to say 'rational beings' as a whole about things that include rational beings and those that are not, and 'men' about those that include male and female, are customary in the literatures of languages. Saying sometimes 'he' about a neuter thing, 'they' about a crowd of people that includes woman and man, and 'the sun and moon' meaning the sun and the moon, and so on, are examples of this. *\[TN: in the Malayalam these three words are അവൻ ('he', the masculine pronoun), അവൻമാർ ('they', the plural of അവൻ) and സൂര്യചന്ദ്രന്മാർ ('the sun and moon', with the plural ending -ന്മാർ that is used for persons). The English words cannot show this.\]*

It is said that the purport of saying 'taught the names' is that He taught that each one's name is such-and-such, and also that it is that He taught the details concerning all things, including their names. In any case, it need not be said that in knowing and in teaching about things their names have a chief place, and that it is through names that things are introduced. What all, and in what manner, it was that was taught to the Prophet Adam (a) – none of that is something we can know. Since all of that, as far as we are concerned, is unseen matters, there is no way for us even to infer anything beyond what Allah or His Messenger has said. Likewise, the explanation of what was said – that afterwards He showed those things to the angels – is also something we cannot know. In short: that it is the human race – not the angels – that is fit for the khilafah that Allah was going to appoint in the earth; that Allah has placed in human beings some natural *\[p. 230\]* characteristics that the angels could not conjecture; that, although the angels are indeed very pure and excellent creatures, these special characteristics are not in them; that Allah is the All-Knowing who knows everything and the Profoundly Knowing who does everything wisely; that what they themselves conjectured and hinted at in their first reply turned out to be a mistake; that great wise secrets are contained in making man khalifah – all this became clear to the angels through this event.

*\[Verse 34\]*

Showing respect and humility, displaying submission, bowing the head, bending – such are the meanings that **سُجُودْ** (sujud) has in the language. While including all these senses, it is in the meaning of the full prostration done by placing the face on the ground, by way of worship, that it is known in the technical language of religion. It is also the one given the most importance in religion among bodily acts of worship. That this sujud may be done to Allah alone is also something known to everyone in general. Therefore it can be seen that scholars have adopted different positions in explaining the sujud that the angels did to the Prophet Adam (a). If each opinion, its arguments and the criticisms concerning them were described, even briefly, it would go on quite long. Allah has stated in the 100th verse of Surah Yusuf that when the parents and brothers of the Prophet Yusuf (a) came from Palestine to Egypt, they did sujud to him. Paying respect by bowing the head and by bending with the body was customary in that time. It was a respect they paid in accordance with that. That is, it was *\[p. 231\]* not a sujud of worship. It was a sujud in the linguistic sense. Likewise, what is to be said here, in brief, is that what the angels did to the Prophet Adam (a) too was a kind of sujud in the linguistic sense.

Whichever explanation given here for sujud one looks at, it is certain that this sujud was not worship to the Prophet Adam (a). If anyone among the Muslims says so, – unless it is their ignorance – all there is to say is that some evil motive will be lying hidden behind it. A modern commentator is seen to have written 'that the purport of commanding to do sujud to Adam is that He commanded to submit to man's will'. Other than as a new interpretation arising from some ideas and ideals of his own, it has no basis beyond that. One matter should be remembered here. This command is to the angels. Their nature and form and character are all very different from human beings. That being so, it is natural that there is a difference in the nature of their sujud too. Therefore all that we can think about the form of their sujud is that it would be in a form suited to them. **والله أعلم**.

All the angels did sujud to Adam in accordance with Allah's command. Only Iblis did not do it. What he did was to show pride and to be conceited about his noble descent. 'Am I, created of fire, to do sujud to this one created of earth?!' – that was his argument. (7:12; 15:33; 17:61; 38:76) Then, how bad arrogance is! It is for that very reason that the Prophet ﷺ said too: 'One who has in his heart arrogance as much as a mustard seed will not enter Paradise' (Mu.) What was said about Iblis, **وَكَانَ مِنَ ٱلۡكَٰفِرِينَ**, has the possibility of meaning in two ways: 'he became one of the disbelievers', and 'he was one of the disbelievers'. It is explained that according to the 1st, he became a disbeliever because of having defied the command of sujud, and according to the 2nd, that although he was outwardly good, Allah knew that in reality his end would be as a disbeliever. These two meanings are not opposed to each other, either.

Iblis is one of the race of the jinn. (Su: Al-Kahf, 50) Even so, the command of sujud was binding on him too. That may be because he had been living among the angels like one of them, or because there was a special command to him. Allah knows. Whichever it may have been, among the reasons he gave for not following the command, he did not argue, surely, that that command was not binding on himself. Then, it becomes clear from that that until then Iblis had been in a good position such as to be counted in the company of the angels. It is pride and defiance that became the cause of the curse for all time and of punishment. As Imam Qurtubi (r) said, however high a rank a person may be seen to have reached in outward view, *\[p. 232\]* it is not possible for us to determine by that what that person's real end will be. Laith (r) is said to have spoken thus: 'Even if you see a man walking on water and flying through the air, you must not be deceived in him without checking his matter against the Kitab and the Sunnah'.

*\[Verses 35–36\]*

*\[p. 233\]* After the event of the sujud, Allah settled Adam, and his wife Hawwa' (a), in Paradise. He also permitted them to eat anything from anywhere as they wished. But He specially forbade them from eating from one particular tree. He also specially reminded them that eating from it is wrongdoing. This was a test. Allah knows rightly what its outcome would be. It is not that He did not know – but Allah has ordained it as a cause for taking the measures that follow it. Although his own noble descent, conceit and envy were the cause, it was, surely, because of Adam that it came about that Iblis was cursed as one driven away for all time. Therefore he was not able to bear Adam (a) succeeding in that test. Giving evil counsels and evil promptings, he made them leap into error. Yes, Adam and Hawwa' (a) ate from that forbidden tree. In this way he became the cause of their losing their life in Paradise and of their leading an earthly life.

When sending them to the earth, Allah sent them – Adam, Hawwa' (a) and Iblis – to the earth, specially reminding them that you will be enemies among yourselves, and that there will be there the facility to dwell until a fixed time and the resources needed for living. This was its consequence. It is with the warning that they must always be vigilant concerning the lifelong enmity between man and Satan, the enmity that arises among human beings, and the consequences that may be faced because of enmity, and with the promise that there are in this earth, for the human race, the facility of a dwelling-place to stay until the term that Allah has fixed is over, and the means of livelihood, that Allah sends them to the earthly world. Then, if man is suffering hardship in the earth for want of enough place for dwelling or for want of sufficient resources for livelihood – in reality there is no hardship at all. It is certain that there is only artificial hardship – it will be something that arises because of the entanglements that man has made, unjust laws and wrong approaches.

**جَنَّةٌ** (jannat) is a word from the root **جَنٌّ** (jann), which has the meaning 'to cover'. All gardens with plenty of trees are called 'jannat'. *\[p. 234\]* Trees cover their ground, surely. Although there is only a nominal connection between heavenly things and earthly things, that word is used in Arabic for Paradise and for the gardens of Paradise. There are two opinions on whether what is meant by that 'jannat' in which Allah first settled the Prophet Adam (a) was the Paradise situated in the heavenly world, or a special garden on the earth itself. 'What the parties named Mu'tazilah and Qadariyyah, and some other scholars, have held is that it was a garden on the earth. *\[TN: an opening quotation mark is printed before "Mu'tazilah"; no closing mark follows.\]* There are also alternative views that this was in Aden, or in Palestine, or in Iraq. In the Bible it is spoken of as 'the Garden of Eden'. It can be seen stated in the 'Bible Dictionary' *\[TN: printed 'വേദപുസ്തക നിഘണ്ടു' (literally 'Bible dictionary'); the page does not say which dictionary is meant.\]* that 'the efforts to know precisely which the place of the Garden of Eden was have not borne fruit at all', that 'the Jews who wrote books before Christ and some of the early Christians described the Garden of Eden as they pleased', and that 'what is called 'Parudisa' in the New Testament has come from the idea about the Garden of Eden'. But the opinion of the majority of scholars is that it is Paradise itself. What can be understood from the Quran and from hadith is also this very thing. When there are no special contexts, it is usually meaning Paradise that 'al-jannatu' (**الْجَنَّة**) is said.

When He commanded them to go down after Adam and Hawwa' ate from the tree, what Allah said to them was, surely, 'Until a time there is for you in the earth a dwelling-place and resources' (**وَلَكُمۡ فِي ٱلۡأَرۡضِ مُسۡتَقَرّٞ وَمَتَٰعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٖ**). Then, what becomes clear from this is that it is to the earth that they are going down, and that until then they were not in the earth. In Surah Ta-Ha 118, 119 He has described this same 'jannat' thus: **إِنَّ لَكَ أَلَّا تَجُوعَ فِيهَا** . . . (There you can go without hunger and without being naked. There you can go without thirst and without being struck by the sun.-) This description is more suited to the garden of Paradise than to a garden on the earth. That along with eating fruit from the forbidden tree, the shame of Adam and Hawwa' – nakedness – became exposed to them, and that they began to cover it, taking leaves, also comes in Surah Al-A'raf 22 (**بَدَتۡ لَهُمَا سَوۡءَٰتُهُمَا وَطَفِقَا يَخۡصِفَانِ**). This too shows that it is not a garden on the earth. Moreover, there is no doubt that the word 'Eden' in the Old Testament of the Bible and the word 'Parudisa' in the New Testament are a variant of the words 'Adn (**عَدْنْ**) and 'Firdaus' **فِرْدَسْ** in the Quran. *\[TN: the Arabic of the second word is printed فِرْدَسْ, without the و of فِرْدَوْس, although the Malayalam beside it reads ഫിർദൗസ്. Transcribed as printed.\]* In the Quran, for its part, it is about Paradise that these words have been used (18: 107; 23: 11; 13: 23; 18: 31;) see these and others) *\[TN: the closing bracket after "see these and others" has no opening bracket.\]*

There are no proofs to determine which the tree was that was forbidden to the Prophet Adam (a). There is no particular benefit *\[p. 235\]* in knowing its name, either. It is also worth stating that great men such as Ibn Kathir and Razi (r) have pointed out that there is no fit proof for determining which that tree is, that it may be some one among the trees that some people are seen naming, or some other one, and that no harm at all is going to come from not knowing it.

*\[Verse 37\]*

The Prophet Adam (a) grieved over the mistake that had happened on his part. The manner in which he should repent and turn back, and the words for it, he received from Allah Himself. His, surely, is the first mistake and the first repentance in human history. In this way, the gist is that through those words he sought release from sin, and Allah accepted it and forgave him. Which those sentences were can be understood from the 23rd verse of Surah Al-A'raf. Describing there the event of Adam and Hawwa' (a) eating from that forbidden tree, it has been said thus: (**قَالَا رَبَّنَا ظَلَمۡنَآ أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمۡ تَغۡفِرۡ لَنَا وَتَرۡحَمۡنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ ٱلۡخَٰسِرِينَ** – **الأَعْرَاف** They both said: Our Rabb, we have done wrong to ourselves. If You do not forgive us and show mercy to us, surely we will become among the losers! (7: 23) *\[TN: a single round bracket is printed with this Arabic quotation; no matching bracket is printed.\]* It has also been reported that many great men such as Mujahid, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and Abul 'Aliyah (r) stated that these are the words that Adam (a) learned from Allah. As can be seen in some other riwayahs, it is not that they cannot have said some other prayer-sentences too besides these sentences; but there are no proofs fit to be accepted. *\[p. 236\]* A fabricated hadith can be seen in some books, that it was by the 'haqq' of the noble Prophet ﷺ, Ali, Fatimah, Hasan and Husain (r) that they prayed. There is no doubt that this is a fabrication made by the **رَافِضَة** (Rafidah), a section in the Shi'a party, one that has gone beyond bounds. Many great men have stated that its narrator, 'Amr ibn Thabit, is one who narrates fabricated hadiths, that he is a Rafidi, and so on.(\*)

**تَابَ** (taba) means 'turned back' in the literal sense. When **تَابَ إِلَى الله** (he turned back to Allah) is said, what is meant will be that he grieved over what he had done and turned back in repentance. When **تَابَ الله عَلَيْهِ** (Allah turned back upon him) is said, what is meant is that Allah accepted his repentance – forgave the sin. It is on this basis that the meaning 'to repent' is assigned to that verb and to the other words from it when the agent of the turning back is creatures, and the meaning 'to accept repentance' when it is Allah.
