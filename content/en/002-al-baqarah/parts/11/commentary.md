*\[Verses 63–64\]*

*\[p. 278\]* What is seen in this verse is another example of the Israelites' having kept immersed in disobedience itself even after seeing mighty signs directly and giving assurance that they would obey the injunctions and prohibitions. It may be because this event was known among the Israelites of that time that it was not explained much

To the word **مِيثَاقَ** (mithaq) the meanings assurance, covenant, pact and so on can be given. A pact made firm by swearing or by way of a vow – that is the meaning Imam Raghib has assigned to it **طُّورَ** (tur) means mountain. In Su. A'raf 171, when speaking about this event, it is the word **الجَبَل** (the mountain) that has been used. When it is said **ٱلطُّورَ** with the particle al joined, it is also possible that it means Mount Sinai. Therefore some are of the view that by having said **رَفَعْنَا فَوْقَكُمُ الطُّورَ** (We raised Tur above you) what is meant is that He raised Mount Sinai, and some others that what is meant is that He raised some mountain or other. Whichever of the two it is, there is, surely, no particular benefit in knowing the name of the mountain.

As seen in the translation, the commentators can be seen to have assigned meanings to the sentences **أَخَذۡنَا مِيثَٰقَكُمۡ** (We took your assurance) and **وَرَفَعۡنَا فَوۡقَكُمُ ٱلطُّورَ** (and We raised the mountain above you) both as two separate sentences and as the two joined together into a single sentence. When it is a single sentence, the meaning will be: remember the occasion when We took your assurance, raising the mountain above you (\*) When the words of this verse and of the other verses that state about this event, and the statements of the Quran commentators, are examined, the purport of this verse can be understood thus: Because the Israelites have believed in Allah and in the Prophet Musa (a), they are bound to accept the injunctions and prohibitions in the Torah. But they made lapses in it and disregarded it. Then the raising of the mountain is a sign that Allah showed them. They feared whether it might *\[p. 279\]* fall upon them. At this juncture Allah commanded them that they must hold the Torah firmly, and that they must observe its injunctions and prohibitions properly. They also gave assurance that they would do so. And even then, what they did was to disregard all of it just as before. Nevertheless, because of Allah's favour and mercy, He did not make them all into ones who have lost.

That is, it is here too after describing many matters, including their demanding that they must see Allah and the thunderbolt's striking them, that this event has been quoted. Then it is clear, leaving no room for doubt, that the event of the thunderbolt's striking the Israelites and the event of raising the mountain above them are not one, and that the two are two. It is in that very way that all the commentators also speak. But some people with crooked interests are seen to have stated that the two are one event. That it is natural that they should fear whether that mountain, standing raised very high near them, might fall upon them because of the rumbling of a terrible thunder or because of an earthquake, and that the raising of the mountain above the head did not happen – that is their contention. It is clear that their aim is to make out that it was not any extraordinary event.

But as far as those are concerned who believe that Allah is wont to bring about some events beyond the laws of nature that man can know, that any matter that is not impossible (**محال**) when looked at rationally has the possibility of happening, that any matter that is rationally possible is within Allah's power, that, if Allah states a matter in clear words, creatures have no right to interpret it turning it upside down, and so on – such misinterpretations are not to be given even a grain of value. Here and in Su. Nisa', in two places, that We raised the mountain above the Israelites (**رَفَعۡنَا فَوۡقَكُمُ ٱلطُّورَ - رَفَعْنَا فَوْقَهُمُ الطور**), and in Su. A'raf that We uprooted the mountain above them (**نَتَقۡنَا ٱلۡجَبَلَ فَوۡقَهُمۡ**) – Allah said. In A'raf it has further been said: like a shade (**كَأَنَّهُۥ ظُلَّةٞ**). The literal meaning of **نتق** is explained below. It is a thing that gives shade, such as a tree or an umbrella, that is called **ظَلَة** *\[TN: printed ظَلَة, without the shadda and with a fatha on the ظ and on the ل; in the phrase quoted just before it the word is كَأَنَّهُۥ ظُلَّةٞ.\]*. Besides all this, that they thought that it *\[p. 280\]* would fall down upon them (**وَظَنُّوٓاْ أَنَّهُۥ وَاقِعُۢ بِهِمۡ**) is also said. It is astonishing that, after Allah has stated this much, one who prides himself on being a believer in the Quran dares to say that the raising of the mountain did not happen – that it did not rise leaving the ground, other than standing raised in the very place where it stands!

It is because of that that Allah kept manifesting, at the hand of the prophets of former times, wondrous signs beyond the rules of nature, to make people understand the matter. Compared with the former communities, it is, surely, Isra'il's and that of the Prophet Musa (a), who was appointed to rescue them, that is a community having the greatest history. *\[Verses 65–66\]*

*\[p. 282\]* It points out a disobedience that has passed among the Israelites and the punishment they received through it. Just as Friday is for the Muslims, Saturday was the main day that had been appointed for the Israelites. It had been appointed that on that day they must be free from all work, and that they must observe some special acts of ritual observance. This observance is called sabt – or shabbat (**سَبَت**). Saturday is also called the day of sabt (**يَوْمُ السبت**). Therefore the meaning 'on the Saturday day' and also 'in the observance of the shabbat' can come to the word **فِي السبت**. In substance there is no difference between the two, either. What the Jews observe as the shabbat even today is the very Saturday, the last day of the week. What the Christians observe it on is Sunday, as being the first day of the week. In a hadith the noble Prophet ﷺ has said thus about Friday. 'This was the day that had been set for them (the People of the Scripture). And then they differed about it. Allah has given us guidance to it. People are behind us in it. That is, the Jews the next day and the Christians the day after the next'. (Bu.Mu)

The event of the shabbat has been quoted a little more clearly in A'raf 163–166. The summary of the event is in this manner : 'The people of a country who lived on the seashore were people who carried on fishing. Because working on the day of the shabbat was not permitted, *\[p. 283\]* on that day they were not allowed to catch fish. The fish, for their part, would keep streaming in shoals on that day. That was a trial from Allah. They found a stratagem. On the Saturday day, making pits in places near the sea, letting water flow into them through channels. On the Saturday day, when fish come and fill them, closing the channels. After the time of the shabbat has passed, they would catch those fish and collect them. Although the good men who were among them tried advising them about this, they did not heed it. Allah saved those good people, and Allah punished those who transgressed by making them apes. It is said that that country was Ailath (**ايلة** Elath), which is now known by the name al-'Aqabah (**العَقَبَة**), and that the event took place in Madyan, near it. (\*) In all those parts the Israelites had been settled and living for an unlimited time.

That stratagem of theirs to circumvent the religious law is, surely, defiance of the law and deceit. They did not value the advice of the counsellors either. Then Allah gave them a punishment indeed fit for their deed. He changed their form, making them apes. Just as, in bodily shape, man and ape have some resemblance, their deeds too were, surely, in a manner such that it could be said that they observed the law outwardly. All who make it appear that they follow the injunctions and prohibitions of the religion outwardly, and in reality act contrary to the principles contained in them – whether they be of that time or be of later times – are in reality imitating those fishermen. That punishment is a warning that gives a lesson to all such people, and that the God-fearing, who keep guarding Allah's injunctions and prohibitions like those good men who kept advising those fishermen, are able to take advice from this event – Allah has pointed out in the 66th verse.

The moment Allah said to them **كُونُواْ قِرَدَةً** (become apes), it is certain indeed that they had become apes. About whatever matter it be, if He says that it must come into being, it will indeed come into being. (36:82) That the purpose of having said that He made them apes is that He did not change the form of their bodies, and that it is that He made their hearts like the hearts of apes – made advice and warning ineffective – is what Mujahid (r) has *\[p. 284\]* stated. No one else is known to have held this opinion. Ibn Kathir (r) has pointed out that this opinion of Mujahid (r) is only an isolated opinion that does not agree with the clear meaning of what Allah has stated here and in Ma'idah 63 and in A'raf 166. After quoting the statements and opinions of many great men, he has said thus: “The reason for our quoting these sentences here from all these Imams is to make clear that what all those great men have said is against what Mujahid – may Allah grant him mercy – said, that the bodily shape of those people was not changed and that only a mental change of nature had occurred. What is right is that they were transformed in shape and in nature too.”

It is, surely, natural that some people to whom no extraordinary events at all are digestible give priority to the isolated opinion of Mujahid (r), and disregard the other opinions and the proofs. While Allah has stated that punishment as a punishment given specially to a people who committed transgression on the day of the shabbat, and has said that it is a punishment that gives a lesson to those before and behind that event, no justification is seen for this opinion. Allah has stated in more than one place that when sin and defiance increase, the hearts are sealed in such a way that nothing that is needed enters into them. According to this opinion, this event is to be counted only as an event belonging to that category. In any case, none of those who were changed in form lived long afterwards, or had offspring born to them. Imam Muslim (r) quotes thus as stated by Ibn Mas'ud (r): 'To those who asked the Prophet ﷺ whether apes and pigs had come from those who were transformed in form, the Prophet ﷺ said: When Allah destroys or punishes a group, He has not given them offspring. Apes and pigs had existed even before that.'

*\[Verses 67–71\]*

*\[p. 287\]* This is the event of the cow that became the reason for this surah's being named al-Baqarah (**البَقَرة**), which has the meaning 'cow'. What the Quran commentators generally state is that the reason for its being commanded to the Israelites to slaughter a cow in sacrifice, as stated in these verses, is a murder event that is described in the following verses. Although differing opinions can be seen in the details, the nature of the event is in brief in this manner: An heir of a rich man killed him. He took the dead body and placed it on a highway or somewhere. Afterwards that same murderer himself laid the charge of murder upon the others. Thus there arose dispute and quarrel. The case reached Musa (a). It was then that he commanded them to slaughter a cow, as stated in the 67th verse. After it was slaughtered, as said in the 73rd verse, a portion of that cow was taken and the dead body was struck with it, and then life came to it, and it said that the murderer was such-and-such a person. The dead body again became lifeless as before.

According to this, it was the murder in question that happened first. The command to slaughter the cow is after that. When describing events, the Quran often does not have the practice of quoting them in proper order, the first part first and what is later afterwards. Sometimes it will begin the story from the middle and from the end. In describing in this way, some wise secrets are contained too, moreover. We have spoken about this in the Introduction under the heading 'The Quran's Method of Presentation'. Describing as an independent event the part containing the command to slaughter the cow, the Israelites' having raised many questions following it, and its after-effect, is, surely, sufficient to draw more attention to their traits such as weakness of belief, lack of discipline, their wish to escape from observing the law, mockery and quibbling questions. *\[p. 288\]* Describing afterwards the murder and the matters following it as though another independent event helps one, too, to think specially about Allah's great power, life after death, the truthfulness of the Prophet Musa (a) and so on. When it is described continuously from beginning to end as a story, it will not have this freshness and seriousness.

What some commentators have expressed as their opinion is that what happened first was the event of slaughtering the cow, and that it was afterwards that the murder and the dispute occurred, and that then the command came to strike the dead body with a portion of that cow. Perhaps – as some great men have pointed out – even if the event of the murder had taken place first, it can be that the command to strike the dead body with a portion of it came after the slaughter of the cow had been done. (The reality Allah knows) Whichever it be, there is no difference of opinion among the Quran commentators in this: that the event of slaughtering the cow and the event of the murder stated in the following verses are not two independent events unconnected with each other – both are two aspects of one and the same event. It is only from among some modern scholars who are interested parties that the argument that both are two separate events is seen to have come forth. About this we can examine further on **إن شاء الله**.

Whatever the occasion be, Musa (a) informed the Israelites that Allah was commanding them to slaughter a cow. While he was the Rasul of Allah and their guide and leader, they ought to have obeyed that command at once. But that command did not go down with them. A doubt too, whether Musa (a) was mocking them! No, they did indeed ask it openly. He is, surely, a Rasul who had studied and become acquainted with them sufficiently. He gave a reply to it with gentleness and tact itself. Am I to join the company of fools? May Allah protect me! This was the reply. Without saying who the fools are, he also pointed out by implication who they are. When it was settled that that command is not play or mockery – that it is a real matter – at least then they ought to have obeyed it. In the situation where nothing else in the way of conditions was said except that they must slaughter a cow, it would have been enough if they had slaughtered any one at all. But do they give up the traditional habit of seeking excuses to escape somehow from carrying out a command?! What cow must it be, of what kind must it be? – in this way, one after another, they went on raising questions.

Examine the words in each question. The weakness of their belief, the lack of discipline and the attitude of disregard can be seen clearly through those words. Unlike the way the Companions of the noble Prophet ﷺ used to address the noble Prophet, it is not 'O Prophet' or 'O Rasul' – it is 'Musa' – that they are wont to call their prophet. 'You must pray to your Rabb *\[p. 289\]* for us' – that is their demand, repeating it. An expression like 'to Allah' – 'to our Rabb' – is not seen on their tongue. If one hears that word of theirs, it may seem that Allah is the Rabb of the Prophet Musa (a) alone. Musa (a) does not argue with them about any of it. In a calm tone full of courtesy and goodwill, he gives a reply to each question. When each reply was received, had they ended the questioning right there, that would have been good for themselves indeed. As the questioning increases, what happens through it is, surely, that they themselves bear hardship and display lack of obedience. It is because of that that the Quran has also forbidden such questions. Allah says : 'O you who have believed, do not ask about things which, if made clear to you, would cause you displeasure. If you ask about them at the time when the Quran is being sent down, they will also be made clear to you.........'(Ma'idah 101) It has also come in hadith that the Prophet ﷺ used to dislike multiplying questions.

As each question comes, Musa (a) will pray to Allah. For the first time, the reply was received that it is enough if it is a cow in a middle state, neither very far advanced in age nor very young. Such a cow is, surely, not rare at all. He also advised them to carry out that command without questioning again. They did not yield. It became: what must the colour be. The reply was that it must be of a sheer yellow colour and pleasing to the sight. To get such a cow there would be, surely, a little more difficulty. But the questioning did not end with that. It became: because there are many cows of this kind, the matter has not become clear to us; it must be made clear once more. But, by the time it had come to this much, a little awakening of understanding has come to them. That they are asking because they cannot distinguish the cow that is to be slaughtered, and that if Allah wills we will attain the straight way – these words, surely, show that. So, with the last answer, that it must also be one not used in work such as ploughing the land and drawing water, without any mixture or spot of other colours, and without shortcomings such as maiming and lameness, the scene of enquiry ended. They also agreed that they had received sufficient description about the cow.

Now, a cow combining the said conditions and qualities had, surely, to be found. Searching and searching, at last they found it. It is said that it was by paying a huge price that they were able to obtain such a one. That is natural, too. When the difficulty of getting the cow, their mental attitudes and so on are taken into account, carrying out that command was, as far as they were concerned, rather remote indeed. Even so, in the end they did indeed bring it into practice. Now, what the secret is in having commanded thus to slaughter a cow can be seen in the following verses. Allah says:
