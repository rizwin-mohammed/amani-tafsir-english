*[Verse 58]*

*[p. 1033]* What are in this verse are two commands that deserve very great importance in Islamic life. Yes, that amanahs must be given back to their rightful owners, and that, in case of passing judgement in some matter between people, it must be according to justice. If peace, and amity, and justice, and propriety and the like are to prevail in human society, both these matters are absolutely indispensable. ‘Amanah ( **أَمَانَةَ** )’ means ‘trustworthiness’ as word-meaning. All the matters, and things, that one person has entrusted to another with trust, and that that person has taken up, are called ‘amanah’. In this same sense, ‘amanath’ and ‘anamath’ are wont to be said in Malayalam too. Amanahs can be divided into three.

(1) The amanah between man and his Rabb. All the duties that are known as to be kept by people towards Allah, by means of the prophets and by means of the scriptures, are included in this.

(2) The amanah among people mutually. The properties, and the secrets, that one person has entrusted to another with trust, and the proprieties to be kept in dealings, are all included in this.

(3) The amanah to be kept towards one's own selves. It is the matters indispensable for one's own good and welfare that are in this kind. Of these, it is intending the second - the one among people mutually - kind that, in the ordinary course, we are wont to say amanah.

If they are things, giving them back at the time when they are to be given back, without making delay and without committing fraud; if they are matters, carrying them out as *[p. 1034]* settled, without making a lapse; if they are secrets, keeping them without disclosing them outside; if they are dealings, not committing cheating and deceit in them - such is the import of having said that amanahs must be given back to their people’. In any subject whatever, worldly or religious, in case of taking a decision or pronouncing judgement among people, to carry out judgement according to fairness and truth, without selfishness and partiality, is the gist of having said ‘that among people one must pass judgement according to justice. When it is a decision between two parties, this will be possible only if one hears and understands the claims, and the arguments, and the evidences of both parties, and afterwards reflects about the decision to be taken. Only those who have the knowledge and capability fit for it may enter into this matter too.

Although it is rulers, judges, leaders, scholars and the like whom both these commands affect more strongly, others too are not exempt from it. Occasions when one has to keep amanah, and pass judgement, according to each one's situation and standing, may arise for everyone. At the least, in household matters at any rate it will become extremely necessary. One matter needs to be specially noted. Other than having said in general that amanahs must be given back to their rightful owners, Allah has not specified ‘to rightful owners of such and such a kind’. Likewise, other than that justice must be kept in judgement when passing judgement among people, it has not been determined either that justice must be kept towards those belonging to such and such a kind. What this shows is that there is no need to look whether the rightful owners of the amanah, and the people concerning whom judgement is passed, are good ones, or not, Muslims, or not. That deceit, and injustice, are not allowed towards anyone is a fundamental principle in Islam. It is for that reason too that, at the time of interpreting this verse, many great men have specially pointed out this matter. We must specially remember that Allah has reminded us, along with this command, that it is the all-knowing Allah, who keeps seeing and hearing and watching all matters, who commands these commands to you, and that this is a very beneficial and excellent good counsel for you. **والله الموفق والمعين**

The Prophet ﷺ says: ‘Fulfil the amanah to the one who trusted you. Do not cheat the one who cheated you.’ (Da; Ti; Ha; Ba.) In the very famous hadith that describes the chief signs of the hypocrite, it is ‘when trusted, to cheat’ that the Prophet ﷺ has counted as one sign. It has been narrated that Ibn 'Abbas (r) said thus: ‘If deceit appears in a people, Allah will not fail to put fear in their hearts. If adultery spreads in a people, death will not fail to increase among them. If a people reduce the measure and the weight, prosperity in food will not fail to be withheld from them. If a people carry out judgement other than according to fairness,
 *[p. 1035]* blood (shedding) will not fail to become widespread among them. If a people violate a covenant, the enemy will not fail to gain influence against them.’ (Malik)

There is no need to describe anything much about committing deceit and cheating without fulfilling amanahs, and about not keeping justice in judgements and decisions. They are matters that are very much mentioned in the Quran and in the Hadith. Both of them are also matters that everyone at large knows. But, let it be said that it is unfortunate and lamentable, today the world's people on the whole, including the Muslims who ought to take care more than anyone else in both these matters, are in a very disordered state in those two subjects. Other than on speech platforms, and in written documents - in the field of action - both have come to have no great place. Wherever one looks, it is injustice, and deceit, and a partial frame of mind, and selfishness, indeed. Each vile practice that he said in the quotation seen above from Ibn 'Abbas (r), and its respective consequence, the world is seeing today in the literal sense. Even so, the sight that is seen everywhere is of the human world moving on and on towards more ruin. **معاذ الله**

In a somewhat long hadith that Bukhari and Muslim (r) have narrated from Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman (r), it can be seen that he, quoting from the Prophet ﷺ, has described about amanah (trustworthiness) perishing from people's hearts. In it he is saying: ‘While people keep carrying on buying and selling with one another, there may come to be no one who fulfils amanah. Finally, it will come to be said (in wonder) that in such and such a family there is a trustworthy man, it seems.’ (Bu; Mu.) It is felt that today our state has almost reached this stage! As has been pointed out in that hadith, there will be many great, great intelligent men, and capable men, and big men and the like among people. But, it is ones with faith and trustworthiness that are hard to see. May Allah guard and protect the human community. Amin.

*[Verse 59]*

*[p. 1036]* The content of this verse is the foundation of the Islamic shari'ah. Its laws and principles are all acceptable only on this basis. All the personal, and social, and economic and political, and cultural aspects of life ought to remain confined within the four fundamental principles contained in this verse. In case any one aspect of life goes outside their bounds, that aspect will become un-Islamic. Calling the true believers, Allah says:-

(1) **أَطِيعُواْ ٱللَّهَ** (obey Allah). Obedience to Allah is fulfilled by way of obeying His Quran.

(2) **وَأَطِيعُواْ ٱلرَّسُولَ** (obey the Rasul too). The real authority of law-making belongs to Allah indeed. There is no doubt. But, it is the noble Rasul ﷺ, surely, who conveys all the messages of Allah including the Quran, and gives explanation and interpretation for them, and shows their practical form - all of it. Of these, it is all the rest combined, except the Quran, that is known by the name ‘Sunnah’ of the noble one.

After the lifetime of the noble Rasul ﷺ, obedience to him is fulfilled only by means of obeying his Sunnah. There is not even a single hint anywhere in the Quran or in the Sunnah that the noble one need be obeyed only in the lifetime of the noble one. No one among the Muslims has held so either. In case anyone, wearing a Muslim name, argues so - some
 *[p. 1037]* do not fail to argue so - that is not binding on Islam or on the Muslims either. (\*) Obeying the Rasul in subjects that have not been made clear in the Quran is in reality obeying Allah indeed. It is not another thing foreign to it. That is what Allah says below (in the 80th verse): **مَّن يُطِعِ ٱلرَّسُولَ فَقَدۡ أَطَاعَ ٱللَّهَ** (Whoever obeys the Rasul, he has obeyed Allah.) The reason Allah Himself has also made clear in another place. (53 : 3) **وَمَا يَنطِقُ عَنِ ٱلۡهَوَىٰٓ** (He does not speak according to his own desire.) The reason why it is the unanimous opinion of the Muslims that the basic authorities of Islam are the Quran and the Sunnah has, surely, been understood from what has just been said.

> **Note:** (\*) A unique book that has dealt in full detail, in an objective and scholarly manner, with the place that the Sunnah has in Islamic law-making, and the relation between the Quran and it, is **السنة ومكانتها فى الشريع الاسلامى للمرحوم الدكتور مصطفى السباعى** *[TN: the print has الشريع; the title of Dr Mustafa al-Siba'i's book is السنة ومكانتها في التشريع الإسلامي, so التشريع ("law-making") is evidently meant.]* Those who are interested, let them look at that book. It has also been translated into Malayalam and some other languages.

(3) **وَأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَمۡرِ مِنكُمۡ** (you must obey those in authority from among you too.) It is clear that what is intended by having said ‘from among you ( **مِنكُمۡ** )’ is from among the true believers. But, who are ‘those in authority ( **أولوالأَمْرِ** )’? In its definition the words of the scholars can be seen as differing. Rulers, chief officials, judges community leaders, scholars, religious scholars - such is their quintessence. If one reflects going a little inward - as some great men (\*\*) point out - it can be seen that it is a word that includes all of these. It can be said on the whole: those who have the right and the competence to take decisions authoritatively in their respective fields. For example: in matters of government the heads of government, and in public matters the community leaders, in matters of war the army commanders, in litigation disputes the judges, in religious rulings the religious scholars, in organisational subjects the heads of organisations, will be those in authority. It need not be specially said that the rulers, and their officials, will be included in any case.

> **Note:** (\*\*) **كما فى فتح الباري**

One more reality needs to be remembered. According to the Islamic system, it is not that the scholars are one thing apart and those in authority are another thing apart. Only those who have religious scholarship and knowledge according to the respective authority have the right to it respectively. The rulers must be religious scholars as well. If those who come under the jurisdiction of a group do not obey them, in the religious and worldly fields of the community thereafter peace and security
 *[p. 1038]* will not, surely, be there. It is for that reason that, besides the obedience of Allah and of the Rasul, it commands that those in authority too must be obeyed. The talk is, surely, about those in authority who are true believers. For that very reason, it follows that they may not command against the Quran of Allah and the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ, and that, if they command, that command will not be valid, and that then they need not be obeyed. More information can be grasped from the hadiths quoted below. **إن شاء الله**

(4) **فَإِن تَنَٰزَعۡتُمۡ فِي شَيۡءٖ فَرُدُّوهُ إِلَى ٱللَّهِ وَٱلرَّسُولِ** (Now, in case you fall out with one another in any matter, return it to Allah, and to the Rasul.) In matters that are religious, or connected, the divisions that arise between those in authority and others, or among people mutually. and the differences of opinion are all included in this. That is, purely worldly matters are not intended by it. Because, it is, surely, to return the subject of division to Allah and to the Rasul that Allah has commanded. That is to say, it means that whatever ruling the Quran and the Sunnah have commanded concerning the subject on which division arises, that must be accepted. How the form of building a house should be, how sowing crops in the field should be - nothing of that is, surely, wont to be described in the Quran and in the Sunnah. That such, worldly matters are exempt from this is clearer from the hadiths quoted below.

It needs to be specially noted that, although, when commanding to obey, He mentioned those in authority too along with Allah and the Rasul, in subjects of division He told to return only to Allah and to the Rasul. That only Allah and the Rasul have the right to handle law in religious matters, that no one else has a share in it, that even those in authority may only submit to what Allah and the Rasul command - such are the principles contained in it. That, when speaking about Allah, and about the Rasul, the verb **أَطِيعُواْ** (obey) was said repeatedly, and that, when speaking about those in authority, without repeating it, only **وَأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَمۡرِ** (those in authority too) was said, is noteworthy.

If one examines truthfully, and sincerely, and with good intention, problems for which a ruling cannot be found from the Quran and from the Sunnah - clearly or by hint, explicitly or implicitly - will be very rare only. There is variation, though, according to the fortune and qualification and truth-seeking of the examiner. It is mostly the details of some problems that keep changing according to circumstances and ways of thinking that will remain without getting a solution quickly from those two. It is there, too, that the right to seek opinion based on the teachings of both, and to make observation, *[p. 1039]* **الاجتهادوالاستنباط** has been given to qualified scholars. Whatever it may be, an opinion against the clear directions of the Quran and the Sunnah has no acceptance in Islam. Nor is there consideration for the abundance of those holding the opinion. As far as directions that are not clear are concerned, difference of opinion is natural. That is, it is there that the place for opinion is, too. All this is only some hints connected with the content of this verse. For detailed information one ought to rely on the books of hadith commentary, and the books of usul (the fundamentals of jurisprudence).

That, joining with having commanded to return to Allah and to the Rasul when division arises, it has said **إِن كُنتُمۡ تُؤۡمِنُونَ** (if you believe in Allah and the Last Day) needs to be heeded very seriously. In case of not doing so, you will not in reality be true believers. There is no meaning either in your saying that we are true believers. On the Day of Qiyamah you will also be subjected to the severe punishment of Allah - such is what that word hints. Not stopping with even this warning, Allah points out the positive good too of this command: **ذَٰلِكَ خَيۡرٞ وَأَحۡسَنُ تَأۡوِيلًا** (That is most excellent and has a better final outcome.) The gist is that in this world and in the next it will give you an auspicious final outcome, and that you will become a vessel for Allah's gracious glances. May Allah give the Muslim community inspiration and tawfiq to live according to the Quran and the Sunnah; Amin

There are very many ayahs and hadiths to be quoted that give light on the idea of this verse, and on its interpretations of meaning, and that help to understand more about them. It will be fitting to the occasion to recall some of the said hadiths here:-

(1) The Prophet ﷺ said: ‘Whoever obeyed me, he obeyed Allah. Whoever disobeyed me, he disobeyed Allah too. Whoever obeys the amir (the one in authority), he obeyed me. Whoever disobeys the amir, he disobeyed me. Surely, the imam (the leader of government) is a shield indeed. Fighting is carried on from behind him, and by means of him protection is kept (from the enemies). If he commands to guard against Allah, and acts with justice, he will have the reward of it. If he says something else, its liability will be upon him.’ (Bu; Mu.)

(2) When the Rasul ﷺ preached, like one who gives warning about the attack of an army, the eyes reddening, and the voice rising, and anger
 *[p. 1040]* becoming intense, Jabir (r) has quoted that he used to say thus: “......Now then, the best among speech is the Book of Allah. The best among conduct is the conduct of Muhammad. The worst among matters are what have been newly invented in them every new custom (evil custom as well) is misguidance too.’ *[DOUBT: “എല്ലാ പുത്തനാചാരവും (അനാചാരവും) ദുർമാർഗവുമാകുന്നു” can be read two ways: the bracket as a second thing every new custom is, “every new custom is (an evil custom, and) misguidance” (compare hadith 9 below, “every newly-invented thing is an evil custom”), or as standing beside new custom, “every new custom (and evil custom) is misguidance”. The rendering keeps the order of the words.]* (Mu.)

(3) The Prophet ﷺ said: ‘You must hear and obey - even if a Negro slave having a (black small) head like a grape is appointed as an official over you.’ (Bu; Mu.)

(4) 'Ubadah ibn al-Samit (r) says: ‘We have made bay'ah (pledge) to the noble Rasul ﷺ to hear, and to obey, in hardship and in ease, in gladness, and in dislike (in all of them indeed), and not to fall out with those in authority over the authority, and to speak the truth without fearing the reproach of any reproacher, wherever we may be.’ In the wording of one riwayah of this hadith this too can be seen: ‘Except if you see some clear ‘kufr’ for which there is a correct evidence with you from Allah, (at all other times) do not fall out with those in authority over the authority.’ (Bu; Mu.)

(5) Ibn 'Umar (r) says: ‘When we gave bay'ah to the noble Rasul ﷺ to hear and to obey, the noble one used to say to us ‘in the matter that is possible for you’.’(Bu; Mu.)

(6) Ibn Mas'ud (r) says: ‘The Rasul ﷺ said to us: ‘After me, you will see many selfish interests, and some matters that are not familiar to you.’ Then the Companions asked: ‘Then what is it that you command that we should do? ’ The noble one said: ‘Grant them their right, and ask Allah for your right.’ (Bu; Mu.)

(7) 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Samurah (r) says: The Rasul ﷺ said to me: ‘Do not ask for a position of authority. Because, if you get it having asked and obtained it, it will be entrusted to you yourself. (The help of Allah will not be in it.) If you get it without asking, you will receive help in it.’ (Bu; Mu.)

(8) Abu Hurairah (r) quotes that the Rasul ﷺ said: ‘In the last age, some false claimants who are conjurers of tricks will come. They will bring you some reports that neither you nor your fathers have heard. You must beware of them. Let them not lead you astray.’ (Mu.)

(9) 'Irbad ( **عِرباض** ) ibn Sariyah (r) says: One day the Rasul ﷺ gave us a very profound speech. Because of it eyes flowed, and hearts trembled. Then a man said: ‘O Rasul of Allah, this is like the
 *[p. 1041]* advice of one who goes away taking leave (saying farewell), surely. Therefore, may you give us a wasiyyah!’ Then the noble one said: ‘I give you wasiyyah to guard against Allah, and to hear and to obey, even if it be a Negro slave (in authority). Because, in case any of you lives after me, he will see very much division. Then, you must take up my conduct, and the conduct of the Khalifahs (my successors) who, having attained right guidance, acted with sound judgement. You must not let go of it, and you must bite on to it with the molar teeth. You must beware of newly-invented matters. Every newly-invented thing is an evil custom. (A; Da; Ti; Ja.)

(10) Rafi' ( **رافِع** ) ibn Khadij (r), who was an Ansari Companion and a farmer, says: When the Rasul ﷺ came to Madinah, those who were there had the custom of doing ‘ta'bir’ (\*) to the date-palm. The noble one said that it would be fine even if it was not done so. (Afterwards the people decided to give up that custom.) Then the fruit became less. They told the information to the noble one. Then the noble one said: ‘I am only a human being. If I command you anything in the matter of your religion, you must accept it. If I command you anything that belongs to my opinion (that is not religious), then I will be only a human being. (Mu.)
