*[Verses 86–87]*

The real meaning of the word **تَحِيَّة** (tahiyyah), to which the meaning ‘greeting’ has been assigned, is to pray for long life. It is in the sense of offering a greeting - or else saying salam - that it is usually *[p. 1070]* used. That is what is meant here too. If a person says salam to one, what is needed is to say it in return too in a form better than that. Allah commands that, at the least, one should return it at least in the very same form in which it was said to one. There is an indication in this as well that, if a greeting is made to one from the side of the enemies, one should deal in return more honourably and generously.

The wording of the salam that has been accepted among the Muslims is ‘Assalamu alaikum ( **السَّلَامُ عَلَيكُمْ**)’, is it not. It means: May there be peace and safety for you. It has been made clear in hadiths that, if a person says salam saying **السَّلَامُ عَلَيكُمْ**, one should return **وَعَلَيْكُمْ السَّلام وَرَحْمَةَ الله** (May there be for you peace and safety and the mercy of Allah), thus, and if he says salam saying **السَّلَامُ علَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَةَ الله**, one should return, adding along with it **وَبَرَكَاتُهُ** (and may there be His blessings too) as well, **وعَلَيْكُمْ السَّلَام وَرَحْمَةَ الله وبَرَكَاتُهُ**, thus. Besides returning more words than the one who said salam first used, displaying a good facial expression and cheerfulness is also needed. It has been indicated in some hadiths that what is needed is that the one who says first should stop after saying **السَّلَامُ عَلَيكُمْ** or **السَّلَامُ علَيْكُمْ وَرَحْمَةَ الله**, and that only then will it become convenient for the one who returns it to increase by adding **وَبَرَكَاتُهُ** as well. It has come in a saying of the Prophet that saying **السَّلَامُ عَلَيكُمْ** is ten goods, when one adds **وَرَحْمَةَ الله** twenty goods, and when one adds **وَبَرَكَاتُهُ** as well, it will be thirty goods. (A; Da; Ti; Ba.)

Returning salam, at least in a form not less than the salam said to one, is obligatory. It cannot be said that saying salam to each other when a Muslim meets another Muslim is - when looked at from the legal viewpoint - simply an obligatory command. But, morally it is an important duty. As for returning salam, it is indeed the general opinion of the scholars that it is obligatory even legally. What is understood from this verse too is just so. Some great men have opined, taking this verse as the basis, that one should observe courtesy in this manner in the letters that one person sends to another, and in courtesies such as gifts and the like, as well. The justification that they give for it is that not only salam but all kinds of acts of courtesy are included in the meaning of the word **تَحِيَّة**. There are many hadiths in existence about the importance of salam in Islam, the etiquettes to be heeded in its practice, and the like. For more details one should approach the books of hadith, and other Islamic law books.

*[p. 1071]* Salam is a particular emblem of Islam. It is also a principal duty that Muslim brothers should practise mutually every day. Although this matter is generally known to everyone, that many among the Muslims of today - especially in our countries - are not wont to heed it as much as is needed in this matter is very lamentable indeed! Among the many consequences that have arisen on account of the excessive influx of un-Islamic cultures among the Muslims, some indeed are the aversion that has crept in among the Muslims to saying salam, and the use of un-Islamic phrases of courtesy.

It is the warning that, whatever stand the disbelievers and the hypocrites may adopt, all the same, the fact that Allah alone is the object of worship, and the fact that after death Allah will gather everyone together on the Day of Qiyamah, hold the reckoning and take action, remain indeed as real, that is contained in the second verse.

### Section – 12

*[Verse 88]*

*[p. 1072]* There were a number of people who, outwardly accepting Islam, acted as Muslims, and at the same time, not being ready to leave their own people who were mushriks and come away in hijrah, lived mingled with them. The view of one section of the Companions was that, since they posed outwardly as Muslims and displayed a bond of love towards them, they were in reality indeed Muslims, and that they too should be treated just as the Muslims are. The opinion of another party was that, since, even though they were Muslims outwardly, they were not prepared to forsake the mushriks, and more or less took part in all their activities, they were pure hypocrites, and that they too should be treated just as the disbelievers are. It becomes clear from this verse and from the next verses that it is these people of the second opinion whom Allah confirms. Although they have declared that they had accepted Islam, on account of their subsequent doings they have turned over headlong into the old shirk itself, and have deviated from the path of truth. That being so, what justification is there for reckoning them as rightly guided and good people? That indeed is what Allah asks.

Concerning the purport of such expressions as Allah led into error; Allah turned over to the former state; Allah set a seal on the heart, it has already been explained before, in Su: Fatihah in the explanation of ‘hidayah’, and in the interpretation of Al-Baqarah verse 7 and the like. It is in the sense of ‘They went astray, they returned to the former state, nothing that is needed came to enter into their heart’ and the like, that they culminate. Although the causes are from them, everything is the creation of Allah, is it not. Allah explains in the next verses the real state of the hypocrites mentioned above and what the policy to be adopted towards them is:-

*[Verses 89–90]*

*[p. 1074]* The matters pointed out in these verses concerning those hypocrites who were mentioned in the previous verse may be summarised thus:

(1) They are not true believers in reality. They are indeed disbelievers. What they desire too is that you also should become disbelievers like them. Therefore friendship and a bond of love should not be maintained with them.

(2) If they are Muslims, they should have come away in hijrah. Now, in case they come in hijrah from ‘Dar al-Harb’ (the enemy land) to Madinah, then they may also be regarded as Muslims.

*[p. 1075]* (3) In case they refuse to come away in hijrah, they should be regarded indeed as disbelievers and enemies. Battle measures should be adopted towards them too, just as towards the Makkan mushriks. Yes, the mushriks who had declared battle against the Muslims should be put in bonds, or killed, at the place where the opportunity was got. Likewise, the same policy has to be adopted towards these too. Any alliance with them, or accepting their help, is something not permissible.

It is good to remember one thing here. At that time the Muslims had obtained freedom of religion and a life of peace only in Madinah. All around - especially in Makkah - the enemies are watching for an opportunity to destroy Islam. Therefore, it was dangerous in many respects for those who embrace Islam to live among the enemy peoples. Therefore, in the circumstances of that day, going in hijrah to Madinah was obligatory for all the true believers who were capable of hijrah. (It comes in the 98th verse that those who, despite having the desire, are unable to do so are exempt from it.) Afterwards, with the conquest of Makkah coming about, and the Arabian peninsula turning almost into a Muslim country, the obligation of hijrah ceased to be. On the day of the conquest of Makkah the Prophet ﷺ said: ‘There is no hijrah after the conquest. But what there is, is ‘jihad, and niyyah’ (fighting and the intention of fighting). When you are asked to go out for battle, go out for battle.’ (Bu; Mu.)

(4) But, if they are those who belong to some people that stands in truce or in alliance with the Muslims, or those who have gone seeking refuge with them, or else, those who live in a moral dilemma, hesitating to fight with the Muslims and with the mushriks who are their own people, they must not be reckoned as enemies and treated as with the mushriks. It is enough to consider that their not having gathered strength against the true believers is a favour of Allah.

(5) Even though they did not come away in hijrah from the enemy land, if they are people who stand aloof without fighting the Muslims - together with the enemies or alone -, and who conduct themselves with the Muslims in peace and in amity, no action must be taken against them. Yes, it is not permissible to kill them, or to seize them and put them in bonds.

*[Verse 91]*

*[p. 1077]* The reference in this verse too is indeed concerning some kinds among the hypocrites explained before. What was said before was about those who, accepting Islam even if only in name, desire to live without conflict with the Muslims and with their own people who are mushriks. The state of these is different from that. Although they are wont to act as Muslims when needed, these keep cooperating and taking part in the disorder activities of their own people that are of the nature of shirk. As soon as they get an incitement to join in such disorders, they fall over into it of themselves. Thus, what these have will be a two-faced policy for getting refuge for themselves on both sides. Allah makes known that, as long as they do not change this state and stand in peace and in restraint without taking part in any of the undertakings against the Muslims, these too should be reckoned as open enemies, and that, when the opportunity is got, they should be seized and put in bonds, or killed, and that there is full justification and right for that.

Look in how clear, fitting and detailed a form Allah counsels the true believers, and teaches them the policy programmes and the methods of fighting to be followed towards the enemies! At the same time, look too how fittingly, and with justice, Allah explains the psychological classifications of the enemy sections, and the actions to be adopted towards each of those sections! **الله أكبر**

### Section – 13

After explaining many things about the actions to be adopted towards the enemies of Islam, and towards the hypocrites who live connected with them, Allah explains the laws concerning killing true believers, or those who have a bond of alliance with them:-

*[Verses 92–93]*

*[p. 1079]* It is with the preface that the event of a true believer killing another true believer, except when a mistake has befallen, must not happen at all, *[p. 1080]* that Allah begins the subject. Continuing, He has classified homicide into several categories and explained the rulings concerning each one. That may be summarised thus:-

(1) A true believer killing another true believer by mistake. On account of that, two things become obligatory upon the killer.

**One:** Setting free and releasing a slave who is a true believer. Since a true believer has been lost at his hand, releasing another true believer from slavery is very fitting, is it not. The word that Allah used is **رَقَبَة** (raqabah). Just as one is wont to say ‘one head’ in Malayalam in the sense of a person, in Arabic one is wont to use sometimes **رَأْسُ** (head), and sometimes **رَقَبَة** (nape). But, the use of **رَقَبَة** is mostly meaning slaves - without the distinction of whether they are male or female, young or old -. Even so, since there is the condition that that slave should be a true believer, some scholars have opined here that it will not do if they are small children who know nothing about Islam.

**Secondly:** A ransom *[DOUBT: തെണ്ടം rendered 'ransom'; see the doubt in verse 92. Here it stands for دِيَة (blood-money).]* must also be paid off to the heirs of the one killed, as their compensation. It is a hundred camels that have been fixed as the ransom for one person. When camels are not obtainable, it may be goats and cattle, or money, instead of it. (For detailed information, the books of hadith and the books of fiqh should be consulted.) Since this ransom belongs to the heirs of the one killed, they have the right, if they wish, to make a concession to the killer. In case they themselves exempt him from it, he need not give the ransom. Even so, there is no exemption from releasing the slave.

It has been stated before in Al-Baqarah : 178 that the penal law for deliberately killing a true believer is carrying out retaliation in return. The gravity of that crime is further explained here in the 93rd verse too.

(2) The one killed, though he is indeed a true believer, being a person who lives among them themselves, without going in hijrah from the midst of the people who live in fighting and in enmity with the Muslims. Then, what is needed is to release a slave who is a true believer as an atonement for the killing. Since the heirs are of the enemy section, and the one killed is one who did not come away separating from them, there is no need of the ransom here. In case the ransom is given, it would be giving help to the enemies, is it not. *[p. 1081]* Since, even though they have accepted Islam in reality, they are people who live among the enemies, mixing with them, it will be difficult to understand such people properly. An example of this is the story of 'Ayyash ( **عياش ـ رضـ**). That is, he, who was the brother of Abu Jahl by the same mother, became a Muslim and went in hijrah. When he later met a person who had been beating him and leaving him exhausted for the reason that he had become a Muslim, he slew him. In reality, that man had believed in Islam and set out for hijrah. Nor did 'Ayyash (r) know that information. It is for such events of mistake that this ruling is.

(3) If the one killed by mistake is a person belonging to a people that has a truce in force with the Muslims, the ransom must be given and the slave must be released as well. What becomes clear from the words of the Quran is that there is no difference whether the one killed is a Muslim or not. That is also the opinion of one section of scholars.

(4) In case it comes about that one is unable to set a slave free, either because of inability, or because of not obtaining one, it is obligatory for the killer to keep fast for two months continuously. Since there is the condition that it should be continuous, if one breaks the fast in between, one will have to fast two months again.

The ransom and the releasing of a slave are the worldly penal measures for homicide, are they not. Releasing another life from slavery in place of having destroyed a life, making the slaves existing among the Muslims free whenever an opportunity is got, giving an atonement for the wrong that one has done, even if by mistake - such are the principles contained in having fixed the releasing of a slave. This is also to be discharged from the killer's own property. That is why it was commanded to keep fast instead, in case he is unable to do it. The ransom is a compensation to be given to the families of the one killed. As for that, it is also simply a heavy liability. Therefore, it is known from the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ that the means for the ransom should be collected not from the killer's own property - but from his near heirs ( **عصبة**) -. If the heirs are unable, it should be from the public treasury (Bait al-Mal). Only when that too is not possible should it be given from the killer's property. (For detailed information, the books of hadith and the books of fiqh should be relied upon).

It is about killing true believers by mistake that has been *[p. 1082]* stated up to now. We have seen before in Suratul Baqarah that, if one kills a true believer deliberately, the punishment is killing in return, and that, in case the heirs of the one killed exempt him from the retaliatory killing, the ransom is enough. But, to what extent Allah has reckoned deliberately killing a true believer as terrible and cruel can be understood if one reads the 98th verse *[TN: printed "98-ാം വചനം" ("the 98th verse"); the verse described (the recompense of one who kills a true believer deliberately) is verse 93, which the author names correctly earlier in this commentary ("93-ാം വചനത്തിൽ", p. 1080).]* once. That his recompense is the everlasting punishment of Hell, that he will be the object of Allah's anger and curse, that there will also be a punishment of a great measure for him - such is what Allah has clearly stated in it. In reality, the gravity of this sin can be understood even by any one warning among those mentioned. That being so, just think about the state when all come together! It is taking into account these warnings and the like that Ibn 'Abbas (r) and others have opined that there is no tawbah (his repentance will not be accepted) for one who has killed a true believer deliberately. Even so, the majority opinion is indeed that, taking into consideration many other proofs too, it is not unlikely that Allah will forgive him if he turns back in remorse. The proofs and justifications that both sides have, and the reply to the other side, are not explained here. In any case, there is no difference of opinion for anyone in that, apart from shirk and kufr, the gravest sin among sins is a true believer deliberately killing another true believer without a just cause. It is also something known without doubt from the Quran and from the sayings of the Prophet.
