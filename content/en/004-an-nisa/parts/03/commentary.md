*[Verses 5–6]*

*[p. 944]* That the properties of the orphans must be given up to them was stated in the 2nd verse. It is some explanations connected with that which are in these verses.

(1) The property must not be handed over to those who are fools. Although in reality the property is indeed theirs, in case they do not have firmness of mind and sense of affairs, giving it up to them will, surely, become a cause for the ruin and the misuse of the property. Not only in the matter of orphans, this ruling is applicable also in the matter of the insane, children and the like. It is noteworthy that Allah has also hinted at the reason why it must not be given up to them on the ground that they are the owners of the property. Look at His having said ‘your properties (**أَمۡوَٰلَكُمُ**)’ without saying ‘their properties (**أَمۡوَٰلَهُمۡ**)’, and at His having described the properties as ‘that which Allah has made a mainstay for you (**ٱلَّتِي جَعَلَ ٱللَّهُ لَكُمۡ قِيَٰمٗا**)’. Whosesoever the ownership of the property may be, its ruin is a loss that affects others too; in its prosperity there will be benefit for others too; therefore those who are responsible have the duty to take care about its coming to ruin; wealth is one among the things indispensable for the life of man; those who handle it must not be ones without sense of affairs: it is such things that are the hints contained in this. In case the wealth that is in a person's ownership is handled through the right way, - if not in one manner, in another manner - besides its actual owner, many others too will obtain benefit by it. In case it perishes, - if not in one way, in another way - many others too will experience its harm. This is a fact that anyone will understand if he thinks a little. Then, handing over wealth to fools will, surely, become an injustice done not only to them but to others too.

(2) Although properties must not be handed over to fools, the people who carry on the handling of the property must, through it, fulfil for them the means for their livelihood such as food, and needs such as clothing. In this two *[p. 945]* hints can be seen. One: since they have property, the expenses that they need can be spent from that very property. Others need not bear it. The other: it should be tried to nourish and grow their properties in such a way that wear and decrease do not affect them, and to make it up from its income. This can be understood from His having said ‘Livelihood and clothing must be given through it - by way of it -’, without saying ‘Livelihood and clothing must be given from it.’ Allah has also commanded, surely, in places such as 2:220, that things from which good comes about for orphans must be done.

(3) A good (proper) word must be spoken to them. This command is one that contains a somewhat wide meaning. Not behaving towards them in a tone of authority and with sternness, speaking in a manner in which gentleness and love are evident, giving good counsels and directions suited to the occasion, saying words such that peace of mind and enthusiasm are felt, and the like: all these will be included in this. Although in reality they are fools, they are, surely, not capable of understanding their own deficiency. Therefore they may think and say, ‘Ought we not to be allowed to handle our property according to our liking?’ and such things. It will be good to convince them that they themselves are their real well-wishers, by means of soothing words such as ‘All this is yours, for its prosperity it is necessary to act in such and such a manner, it is for that that I am doing in such a manner’, and otherwise.

(4) It is when the orphans reach the age of marriage - majority - that their property must be given up. But they must keep being tested beforehand itself as to whether they have the capability and firmness of mind to handle it in the right form in case it is given up. The training needed for that must also keep being given. Thus, only if it is ascertained that by the time majority comes they have the capability and sense of affairs for that, must the wealth be given up. If what becomes clear from the test is that they do not, the handling of the property must also continue as before. Afterwards, when some time passes, if it is seen that the former state has changed and the necessary sense of affairs has come, it must then also be given up. It is the discernment to take on the supervision and the running of the property, without any of squandering, carrying out misuse, using it in evil deeds, not spending on what is required and where it is needed, and the like, that is meant by **رشد** (firmness of mind - sense of affairs). That intellectual maturity alone is not enough, and that religious firmness of mind is also needed, is the opinion of some great ones such as Imam Shafi'i (r). Or, their position is that that too is included in that word. That it is enough if there are discernment, intellect, and the ability to handle wealth, is indeed the general *[p. 946]* opinion. In the place of ‘majority coming’, the word Allah has used is ‘reaching marriage (**بلغ النكاح**). It is, surely, when majority comes that, naturally, the occasion of marriage and the need for wealth are faced. That completing fifteen years of age, the occurrence of seminal emission or menstruation, and the like are signs of majority coming can be understood from the Hadiths too.

(5) Other than that there is permission for the manager to use from that property in a moderate measure, in the manner described below, it is not permissible to take and appropriate or to use anything excessively, nor, thinking that it will, surely, have to be handed back when the orphan grows up and majority comes, to press anything into one's own grip and keep it beforehand itself. All these are evils that usually come about among those who bear the supervision of the properties of orphans and of the helpless. It is for that reason that Allah reminds of this matter in this much detail. But Allah has not stated anything precisely as to how much the one who carries on the handling may use from that property - or may not use -. He has contented Himself with saying that, if he is rich, he must keep honour and decency (**وَمَن كَانَ غَنِيّٗا فَلۡيَسۡتَعۡفِفۡ**), and that, if he is poor, let him eat - use - according to propriety (**وَمَن كَانَ فَقِيرٗا فَلۡيَأۡكُلۡ بِٱلۡمَعۡرُوفِ**). Because the rich man has with him the means for his needs, and because taking and appropriating anything from their property in the name of carrying on the handling of the orphans does not befit honour and propriety, what is required is that he does not appropriate anything from it. When he is poor, he will not have another way for his essential needs. He also has to spend his time and labour in the handling of that property. Therefore, in an ordinary state, he may take and use from it only in a measure that is very essential: such is indeed, on the whole, the gist of what has been said here. It need not be specially said that, according to the variation of each one's wealth, poverty, the measure of the orphan's property and so on, the amount that the managers may use will also differ.

That **اكل** (eat) is used in the meaning of ‘use’, and that **معروف** (what is proper) is used in the intent of ‘what is reckoned as proper in accordance with ordinary customs and the practices of good custom’, we have pointed out before in more than one place.

The purport of His having said that, if he is poor, he may use according to propriety, can be seen explained by many in various manners. He may take a loan from that property for his need; and when he has the ability, he must also pay it back: so some. That he may take what is needed to quell hunger and to cover nakedness: so some others. He may fulfil his own needs from the income of the property. *[p. 947]* It must not be taken from the capital: so some others. Some have the opinion, going as far as this, that the essential needs may be fulfilled even from the capital itself - there is no objection even if the property is gradually lost on account of it -. After quoting these opinions and some riwayahs that are the basis for them, it is to the opinion that, at an unavoidable stage, it may be taken as a loan from the orphan's property that Ibn Jarir (r) has given preference. He also specially reminds that it is, surely, undisputed matters that in the orphan's property one may handle only in accordance with its good; that the manager is not the owner of that property; and that, whether it be an orphan or not, no irregularities may be brought about in another's property. What Ibn Kathir (r) has done is only to point out that the opinion of the fuqaha' is that, when the proper wage of his labour and the measure of his need are looked at, whichever of them is less, that he may use; and that there are two opinions on whether, when he has the ability, he must give back what he took and used. What is understood from the riwayahs that he quoted there and otherwise is indeed that it is the opinion that it need not be given back that has preference with him. **الله أعلم** If all the opinions are examined, it can be understood that all of them stand resting on the principle that he may take and use, for essential needs, in as small a measure as possible, and that he must also try to the utmost so that ruin does not befall the property.

(6) When the property is given up, it must be witnessed. This is a precaution so that no accusation may come afterwards. Even if the property has been handled honestly, when some accusation comes, a record is needed, surely, to be saved from it. This direction of Allah is mainly intending the good of the manager. However the record and the evidence may be, Allah knows rightly how the matters were in reality dealt with, how much truth and exactness have been kept in it, and so on. He will also ask account concerning it. That must remain in memory: it is such things that Allah reminds at the end. (**وَكَفَىٰ بِٱللَّهِ حَسِيبٗا**)

Abu Dharr (r) was a Sahabi who was pure-hearted, without any love of wealth at all, and eminent in self-sacrifice. The Prophet ﷺ said to him: ‘Abu Dharr, I see you as a weak one. I like for you too what I like for myself. You must not take authority over two persons. Nor must you handle the property of an orphan.’ (Mu.) What the Prophet ﷺ teaches by means of this is that those who carry on the handling of the property of another - especially of orphans - must not only be honest, but must also be ones who have the capability and the ability to carry on the handling as required.

*[Verse 7]*

*[p. 948]* After stating some things concerning orphans and women, He is describing the laws of inheritance. Before entering the subject, Allah teaches by means of this verse some basic principles that have to be known in the subject of inheritance. In the three verses that follow, He also gives some other related directions. It is after that, from the 11th verse onwards, that the subject is explained.

Although some systems of succession were in existence among the Arabs at the time when the Quran was being sent down, they were mixed with wrongdoing and injustice. What the Quran does is to wipe away the said injustices and wrongs and to bring down a systematic and just code of inheritance law. No other aspects of law will be seen described in the Quran as much in detail as the laws of inheritance have been described at length. It is natural that there are differences of opinion among the scholars in the details concerning the laws of practice. But the reason why the chapter in which difference of opinion is least is that of inheritance is that very thing. That the community shows great laxity in rightly grasping and observing the counsels of principle which have to be specially borne in mind when handling inherited properties, and which Allah has stated in the verses up to the 11th verse, is a very regrettable fact. *[p. 949]* It is by exploiting this laxity as much as possible that some people of crooked mentality, who claim themselves to be ‘people of progressive ideas’, have in this recent time come to import some new confusions concerning the laws of inheritance in Islam. That most of the corruptions that used to take place in the handling of inherited properties in the Jahiliyyah period have today entered into the Muslim community too is also a fact. May Allah give the community firmness of mind. Amin.

The things that Allah has stated in this verse as the basic principles of the order of inheritance are these:-

(1) Man and woman - male and female - have a fixed right in property. Giving property rights to children and women was not customary in the Jahiliyyah. Their reasoning for it was that property is needed only by those who are able to fight and to resist on behalf of the land. It is refuting this system that it has been specially mentioned, ‘For men too there is a share, and for women too there is a share, (**لِّلرِّجَالِ نَصِيبٞ**, **وَلِلنِّسَآءِ نَصِيبٞ**). The custom in which the men divide and take all the rest, satisfying the women heirs by giving something trifling, without giving the share according to law, can be seen in many families in this time too. This is a new Jahiliyyah system, defying the law of Allah.

(2) It is only to the property that the owner of the property leaves behind (**مِّمَّا تَرَكَ**) - which remains as the remainder when he dies - that the law of inheritance applies. Before a person's death, the families or others have no fixed right at all in his property (other than zakat, debt and the like). When it comes to this, that there is a right only in the property that the owner of the property leaves behind on account of his death, it is clear that nobody who died before him - however close the relation they may have had - has a right to that property. On the contrary, in case any heir dies after the death of the owner of the property - even if it is immediately after he died - his right does not cease to exist. Only that his share will be given to his heirs.

(3) The law will apply to all the property left behind. There is no difference at all such as whether it is currency or otherwise, whether movable things or otherwise, or the like. The word Allah has said is, surely, ‘from whatever he left behind’ (**مِّمَّا تَرَكَ**). As is in practice among some communities and among some families, special *[p. 950]* laws, such as that some special implements such as the clothes and weapons that the deceased used to use belong to the elder children, and that the house in which he lived belongs to the younger children, are not at all in Islam. There is no objection to the heirs, being mutually satisfied, deciding that such and such a thing may be given up to such and such persons. On some occasions that will also be very good.

(4) It is from being the children or the near relatives *[DOUBT: അടുത്ത കുടുംബങ്ങൾ, literally "near families"; here and in verse 7 it renders al-aqrabun, so "near relatives" is used throughout this part (cf. the open doubt on കുടുംബങ്ങൾ in part 01). Options: "near relatives" / "near families".]* of the deceased person that the entitlement to the right of property arises. This can be understood from His having said ‘from the property that the parents and the near relatives left’ (**مِّمَّا تَرَكَ ٱلۡوَٰلِدَانِ وَٱلۡأَقۡرَبُونَ**). As regards wife and husband, it can be said that, according to the opinion of some, they too are included among the near kin. Even if not, it is clear, from Allah's having specially included wife and husband among the heirs, that the wife-husband relation also gives entitlement to the right of property. In any case - as is evident in the talk of some rationalists - it is not on account of poverty or of helplessness that a right in inherited property is obtained. The distinction whether one is poor or rich is not considered in it. It will be only on the basis of the relation with the deceased person. There are, in Islam, legal measures quite separate intending the poor and the needy. Some of them also come in the next verses themselves. The principle in those having a more distant relation losing the right when those having a closer relation are present can, surely, be understood from what has been said here. The grandson's having no right when there is a son, the brother's son's having no right when there is a brother, and so on, are all on this basis.

(5) The greater or lesser amount of the property is not to be considered. Whether it is only very little or is in plentiful measure, the measure of the right is just the same. What Allah has said is, surely, **مِمَّا قَلَّ مِنۡهُ أَوۡ كَثُرَ** (whether from what is little or from what is much). If it is so trifling that it cannot be divided, the heirs may, with give-and-take, together make some decision; other than that, nobody's right will be lost for that reason. But Allah Himself states clearly below that there is for the heirs only the remainder after deducting the debt of the deceased and the lawful wasiyyah.

(6) Allah has already fixed how much the share for each heir is. Nobody has the authority to make any amendment or alteration in it; it is compulsory to allot and give to each one the portion He has fixed: such is indeed the purport of the sentence **نَصِيبٗا مَّفۡرُوضٗا** (a fixed share). Neither individuals nor the government will at any time have the right to bring about a change in the ruling that Allah has fixed, in the name of the doctrine of the equality of man and woman, or in the name of poverty and hardship, or in the name of any other reasoning.

*[Verses 8–10]*

*[p. 952]* The first among these three verses is one addressing the heirs. The gist is that, on the occasion of dividing the inherited property, if there are families, orphans or the poor who do not obtain any fixed right, they must be sent away, made glad, by giving them a little each from it and also saying a good word. Before the division is over, the heirs, consulting among themselves, may fulfil this duty from the capital, or, after it has been divided and taken, from the shares that each one got. The opinion of one party among the scholars is that this is not a compulsory law, and that it is a meritorious optional law. No, in reality this is indeed a compulsory command - but, in course of time, what people do is that they keep showing indifference without observing it -: such is indeed the opinion of another party. It is for this opinion that reason is seen in many respects. (Allah knows) Those who criticise some laws of inheritance in Islam in the name of today's doctrine of equality of condition (socialism) ought to specially heed this command.

Whichever of these two opinions is accepted, when the division of property takes place, if any of those mentioned above are present, - considering the position of the property and the general state of the heirs - they must be made glad by giving them at least a little each, and be comforted by saying good words such as, ‘It is because there is not much to give that we are not able to give more, we too are indeed people in a hurry *[DOUBT: തിടുക്കക്കാർ, clearly printed; literally "people in a hurry" (തിടുക്കം, haste, urgency). In this sentence it seems to mean "people in straits / in need" ("hard-pressed ones"). Rendered literally. Options: "people in a hurry" / "hard-pressed ones".]*’. Inheritance is, surely, what the heirs obtain without their own effort. That being the state, they ought to show special generosity. On the occasions of harvesting from fields of cultivation, or from gardens and the like, likewise, when those related and the poor are present, they too must be satisfied by giving them something or other in this manner. This is indeed a very good usage. The story of the calamity that befell some garden-owners, who avoided this usage and planned, cunningly, to harvest without letting the poor know, evading their eyes, can be seen described by Allah in Surah Qalam. That is worth remembering here.

The purport of the second verse is usually interpreted in more than one manner. Whichever of them is accepted, it is an important principle that is contained in it.

(1) Those who have the apprehension that, if they die, their heirs such as their children may be left weak and helpless, must take precaution in that matter beforehand itself: such is indeed one among them. That is, in short, such persons must reserve for the heirs, without spending the whole property, or some considerable part of it, on other matters, or making a wasiyyah of it. The incident of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (r) is to be stated here. When he was in illness, he, to make a wasiyyah of a large part of his wealth, sought from the Prophet ﷺ *[p. 953]* permission. The Prophet ﷺ did not agree to it, and at last said that, at the most, one third is enough, that itself is plenty. Continuing, the noble Prophet ﷺ said: ‘Leaving your heirs as rich men is better for you than leaving them as poor men who hold out their hands to people.’ (The full form of this Hadith, which Bukhari and Muslim have recorded, has been quoted before in the interpretation of Al-Baqarah 180 - 182.) The custom of neglecting and deceiving the heirs, by setting aside the main part of the property for some charitable matters and by making a wasiyyah, when about to die, is usually seen in many. It must be understood that this is not right, and is moreover culpable.

(2) Those who have fear about their children becoming helpless and weak after them must likewise fear about others too: such is indeed another interpretation. That is; the gist is that they must turn attention to the good of their children, by advising those who are near death not to make wasiyyahs that become harmful to their children and others, and otherwise.

(3) The people who handle the wealth of orphans, just as they do not like their own children to be weak and to experience hardship after their lifetime, must keep great carefulness in handling the wealth of the orphans under them too: it is like this that another interpretation is. The wording and the context of the ayah, moreover, suit all the said interpretations. In any case, it is not that there are not other interpretations too like this.

The idea of the third verse is clear. In short, the one who takes and eats the wealth of orphans in a form that is not right, although for the time being he fills his belly with it, its result will be experiencing the punishment of having to enter Hell and fill the belly with fire. When it is remembered that, among the seven great sins that he described, one that the Prophet ﷺ has counted is eating the property of orphans, it can, surely, be guessed how terrible that crime is. Although this warning is applicable on every occasion, the reason for saying it connected with the laws of inheritance will be in order that, as was seen above, bringing about irregularities in giving them their deserved share when carrying out the division of property, and in taking up the supervision of their wealth, may be specially guarded against. **الله أعلم**
