*[Verse 33]*

*[p. 997]* Interpretation of meaning can be seen given to this in more than one form, on the basis of the words in this verse, the usages, the grammatical rules, and some related riwayahs. Only what is more relevant is quoted below. Before that, it will be good to know in a general way about some words in it.

The singular of the word **مَوَالِي** (mawali) is **مَوْلَي** (mawla). This will be used for all who have a close relationship with one another in some way or other. For example: the slave, the master, the person who set free from slavery, the one who has been set free from it, the neighbour, the owner, the one who has an alliance relationship, the ‘ādarbandhu’ *[DOUBT: printed "ആദർബന്ധു" (clear on a 300 dpi crop), which is not a known word. It may be a slip for ആദരബന്ധു ("one related by honour") or for ആദർശബന്ധു ("one related by ideals"); left untranslated.]*, the close friend, the one who has close kinship: that word is customarily used for all of these. There are two opinions: that what is meant by it here is the heirs of inherited property in general, and that what is meant is specially the ‘asabah’: (**الصَبَة**) *[TN: so printed (300 dpi), evidently for العَصَبَة ('asabah).]* people, the heirs who are closer in nearness by family relationship.

The plural of the word **يَمِين** (yamin) is **اَيمَان** (ayman). This is commonly used in the sense of oath - swearing - and in the sense of right hand. When important terms of agreement such as an alliance, a treaty, a covenant are made firm, that, surely, takes place by holding one another's hand (right hand), and by making it firm through words of oath. In that respect, whichever of the two literal meanings of ‘yamin’ is adopted, it may be said **عَقد اليَمِين** for establishing such agreements and terms. Accordingly, the opinion of most interpreters is indeed that what was said as **عَقَدَتۡ أَيۡمَٰنُكُمۡ** (your oaths - right hands - established) intends the alliance covenant. Some have the opinion that it intends the marriage transaction.

Besides, what most count is that the first sentence in this ayah ends at **وَٱلۡأَقۡرَبُونَ**, and that the part from **وَٱلَّذِينَ** up to **نَصِيبَهُمۡ** is a second sentence. It is according to this that ‘waqf’ (stopping in recitation) is made at **وَٱلۡأَقۡرَبُونَ**. What we have adopted in the translation is also thus. Some interpreters count all that is from the beginning up to **نَصِيبَهُمۡ** together as one single sentence. According to this, it may be given meaning thus: ‘For the property that parents and close relatives and *[p. 998]* those with whom your right hands - oaths - have established a relationship have left, We have appointed for everyone those connected - heirs -.’ Now let us understand the summary of the relevant interpretations hinted at above:-

Those of the opinion that what is from **وَٱلَّذِينَ** onward is one sentence and what is before it is another sentence give the ayah interpretation in two ways:

(1) The gist of the first sentence is that, when parents and close relatives die, Allah has appointed fixed heirs for the property they leave behind, for everyone; and the gist of the second sentence is that, of the obligations you must discharge for those with whom an alliance relationship exists with you, according to that alliance relationship, you must discharge for them all the obligations that are not the right of property; and the purport of this interpretation is indeed that what this verse does is to abolish their giving one another the right of property on the basis of the relationship of brotherhood which the Prophet ﷺ had established between the Muhajirs and the Ansaris following the Madinah Hijrah, and the alliance relationships that had been customary among the Arabs in the time of Jahiliyyah, granting one another the right of property, and to establish that henceforth only the heirs according to the inheritance law system in Islam have the right of property.

(2) The gist of the first sentence is that, for the property that parents and close relatives have left, He has fixed heirs - who have a close family relationship and are known as the ‘asabah’ people - for everyone; and the gist of the second sentence is that the fixed share of those for whom the marriage relationship has been established - of husbands and wives - must be given. That is, the purport is that what is needed is to give only the fixed shares of husbands and wives (if it is the husband, a half; and a quarter; if it is the wife, half of the husband's), and to give all the rest to those close heirs - to the ‘asabah’ people -.

According to the opinion that all that is from the beginning of the ayah up to **نَصِيبَهُمۡ** together is one single sentence, its gist will be thus: For the property that parents, and close relatives, and those for whom the marriage relationship has been established - or, husbands and wives - have left, Allah has appointed fixed heirs for everyone. Or, henceforth only those heirs have inheritance rights. The share that has been fixed for each one of those heirs must be given. Although some interpretations other than those seen above, and some related explanations, can be seen here, there is not seen any need to enter into that.

### Section – 6

*[Verses 34–35]*

*[p. 1000]* What Allah makes known in these verses are some important matters that must be observed between husbands and wives in married life. In case they are rightly accepted, quarrel and dispute *[p. 1001]* arising between husbands and wives will be rare. If it arises, it will also be resolved. The ideas contained in these verses may be summarised thus:-

(1) The overlordship and supervision of women is the men's. In any sphere in which more than one person has a share, in case there is no authoritative supervisor for it, confusion and disorder will hold sway there. In domestic life the need for this can be seen to be greater. It is because of that that there has even come to be a proverb, ‘like a house without a master’. Here Allah declares that that authoritative position is the man's. Allah also points out a reason each for it, which is by nature and which arises naturally. Each one of the two is indeed such as shows that the merit for that position is not the women's. The 1st is the excellence that Allah has given to one group over the other group - yes, to men over women - (**مَا فَضَّلَ ٱللَّهُ بَعۡضَهُمۡ عَلَىٰ بَعۡضٖ**). That is, the distinction the man has physically and mentally: although, when individuals are taken and examined, there may be some women who surpass some men in some particular matter, when looked at as two classes, it is a reality that anyone with an impartial mind knows that it is the man who stands ahead in this. It is because of this reason itself that Allah has not sent prophets from among women, and that the Prophet ﷺ has stated in a Hadith that Bukhari (r) has reported: ‘A people who entrusted rulership to a woman will not succeed.’ The 2nd is the fact that it is the men who spend wealth for the women (**بِمَآ أَنفَقُواْ مِنۡ أَمۡوَٰلِهِمۡۚ**). It is, surely, the men who bear all the expenses of the women, such as mahr, food, dwelling, clothing. This too naturally makes men deserving of leadership over women.

From its having been said that the leadership of women is the men's, no one need misunderstand - as those who have been seized by the madness of modernity are wont to think - that Islam has not allowed freedom to women, or that what Islam intends is that they be the slaves of men. Settling the account of the rights between women and men in a single sentence, Allah says: **وَلَهُنَّ مِثۡلُ ٱلَّذِي عَلَيۡهِنَّ بِٱلۡمَعۡرُوفِۚ وَلِلرِّجَالِ عَلَيۡهِنَّ دَرَجَةٞۗ** (البقرة) (Just as there is obligation upon them - upon the women -, there is obligation towards them also, according to the propriety of good conduct. And for men there is a degree above them, moreover. 2 : 228) Since more information has passed there, it is not described here. What is seen in this verse is only the explanation of that degree. It will be good to know also the occasion on which this verse was revealed.

A man, taking his daughter along, came to the Prophet ﷺ and made a complaint that her husband had beaten her. The Prophet ﷺ said that that is not allowed, and that retaliation may be taken for it. On this occasion *[p. 1002]* this was revealed: so Ibn Jarir (r) and some others have narrated through several channels. It has come in some riwayahs that it was because that woman had been estranged from her husband that he struck her, and in some other riwayahs that, when this verse was revealed, the Prophet ﷺ said: ‘I intended one thing; and Allah intended another.’

(2) To keep obeying Allah, and conducting oneself with the husband in reserve and restraint; in his absence, to guard his property, honour, children and so on, and the things that would bring disrepute to her or to him: these are the marks of women who are good and of good conduct. That is the purport of the sentence ‘Women of good conduct will be those who have obedience and who guard in the state of absence’. It is hinting at their having obtained, through marriage, the way to protect their chastity and to fulfil the needs of life, and at husbands' having been commanded to fulfil their rights and needs, that it has been said **بِمَا حَفِظَ ٱللَّهُۚ** (as Allah has guarded and protected). The gist is that, because Allah has done all this for their good, they are bound to keep the things said above, not only in the presence of the husbands - in their absence too -. Ibn Jarir, and Bayhaqi (r) and others, report from Abu Hurayrah (r): The Prophet ﷺ said: ‘The best of women is a woman (of this kind): if you look at her, she will make you glad; if you command her, she will obey you; if you go (away) out of sight from her, she will guard you in the matter of your property and in the matter of her own person.’ Afterwards the noble one ﷺ also recited this Quran verse (Surat Nisa' : 34, 35).

(3) In case the wife, not yielding to the husband's wishes and admonitions, shows disobedience and estrangement, he must first try giving her good counsel about its consequences and so on. If that does not take effect, he may keep her away, not allowing her to lie with him at the time of lying down. If even with that the estrangement does not end, he may also strike her as a discipline. It can be understood from the Hadith quoted below that only a striking of a kind that causes no injury is allowed. If, by any one of these, she ends her estrangement and quarrel and comes to obedience, then no measure at all may be taken against her. Or, at the very point where it ended, at that very point the measure against it must also be ended. Following that, abusing, threatening, making conduct narrow, and the like, none of these may be done: this and the like is the gist of what was said, and then, if they have obeyed you, do not seek any way against them.

After explaining and describing all this in detail. Allah warns men about bad practices such as behaving *[p. 1003]* improperly towards women, flaring up without reason, striking for a trivial matter, not giving them the concessions that must be given to them. ‘Surely Allah is Exalted and Great’. The hint in this sentence is that, in case the man misuses the authority of control he has over the woman, he must remember that Allah is ready to take fitting measures against him. All that has just been said are the ways that must be observed - when estrangement and disobedience arise from the woman's side - on the basis that the overlordship of the woman is the man's. Now, if it is from the man's side that estrangement occurs, what is the way for it? About this it is stated below in the 128th verse. It can be seen there, **إن شاء الله**. Or else, if a dispute between the parties has arisen from both sides? That is the subject in the second verse. That is:-

(4) If quarrel and discord arise between them from both sides, and not only from the side of the wife or of the husband, the way of remedy for it is to make a settlement through mediators from both sides. From each side, mediators who seem good to them must be appointed. They must examine the faults and failings on each side and make a decision that is more fitting and practicable. That is also their obligation. Both groups will also be bound to accept their decision. Only when no way is seen to reconcile and make peace in any manner may a decision to sever the marriage tie be made. Allah has promised that, in case the mediators sincerely intend that the two groups should come right with each other, Allah will open the way to bring about agreement between them. This is something the mediators must specially keep in mind.

It is by making aware that it is Allah's directions of law that will always be practicable and successful, that it is they that will end auspiciously, that it is Allah who knows best about the particular conditions of men and about the solutions suited to them, and so on, that Allah ends this subject. Yes, **إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيمًا خَبِيرٗا** (Surely, Allah is All-Knowing, and One of minute knowledge). There are many sayings of the Prophet worth quoting that serve to give more light on the subjects stated above. By way of example, let us recall some of them here:

The Prophet ﷺ says:- (1) ‘Let not anyone at all among you strike his wife in the way slaves are struck. Afterwards, at the end of (that) day, he may, surely, lie with her.’ (Bu; Mu.)

(2) ‘In case a man calls his wife to his bed and she refuses, the angels will curse her until the morning dawns.’ (Bu; Mu.)

(3) ‘Whichever woman dies while her husband is pleased with her, *[p. 1004]* she will enter Paradise.’ (Ti.)

(4) ‘(In reply to a person's having been asked about his duty towards his wife) If you eat food, give food to her too; if you put on clothing, give clothing to her too; refrain from striking on the face; refrain from disgracing (by word or otherwise); refrain from forsaking (her) (when it becomes needed) except inside the house - or, refrain from staying estranged.’ (A; Da; Ja)

(5) ‘The one most complete in faith among the true believers is the one among them who is best in character. The best among you are those who are best to their women.’ (Ti.)

(6) (In reply to some people's having asked: just as their subjects do sujud before the Persian kings, may we too not do sujud before the noble one?). ‘If I were to command anyone to do sujud to anyone, I would command women to do sujud to their husbands.’ (A; Da)

(7) (In the famous sermon at Hajjat al-Wada') ‘You must guard against Allah in the matter of women. For they are those in relationship with you. They have a duty towards you of not allowing those whom you do not like to tread on your bed. If they do that, strike them with a blow by which no injury (wound or bruise) is caused. It is due to give them their food and their clothing according to propriety.’ (Mu.)
