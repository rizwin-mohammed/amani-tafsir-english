*[Verse 25]*

*[p. 987]* The meanings of **مُحْصَنَات** (muhsanat), which is from the root **اِحْصَان** (ihsan), were described, surely, in the interpretation of the previous verse. In this verse, the first such word has been used in the sense of free women, and the second in the sense of women who are chaste wives. From His having stated the matter of marrying slave women following the first, *[p. 988]* and from His having described (them), as an explanation, as it were, of the second, as those who do not commit vile conduct and secret liaison, these different intended meanings are clear. The word **فَتَيَات** (fatayat) is the plural of **فتاة**, which comes in the meanings ‘young woman, maiden, maidservant, servant-girl, slave woman’ and so on. The intent of what was said as ‘your servant-girls (**فَتَيَٰتِكُمُ**)’ is indeed: the believing slave women who live among you.

When marrying free women, surely, more mahr will have to be given compared with slave women. The other obligations too are just so. Therefore Allah makes known that those who do not have the financial ability fit for marrying free women, and for whom marriage has become an urgent necessity, may marry believing slave women. What has been said following on, that Allah knows well about your faith, and that some of you are, surely, from some others, is noteworthy. It is enough if it is ascertained that they are, outwardly, people with true faith; the real state Allah alone will know. It is to true faith, rather than to nobility of birth and greatness of family, that value must be attached. Among slaves themselves there may be those who have more faith than the free. Except that, by the way things happened, some of you became free and some slaves, in humanity and in true faith all are equal, and all are brothers: such are the many hints that are in those words.

Allah makes known that marrying slave women must be with the consent of the people who are their guardians - of their masters -, and that the proper mahr must be given to them too, according to the circumstances and the customs of time and place. In addition, He also reminds that care must be taken that they are women who guard chastity and who do not engage in fornicating practices, secretly or openly. Although this matter must be considered in every marriage, it will be because, as regards slave women, there is a likelihood - compared with free women - of their being backward in keeping decency and chastity, that He has specially reminded of it. **الله اعلم**

Even when the marriage takes place heeding all the conditions seen above, it is, surely, not that some vile conducts may not at times arise from those wives. If anything like that happens, they must be punished according to the law. But Allah also commands that only half the punishment that has been ordained for free women need be given to them. Giving lightening in punishments to people who are high and honourable, and adding severity in them to people who are ordinary and of the lower ranks, is, surely, the custom generally among people. It can be understood from this that the outlook of Islam is the opposite. The opinion of the scholars is indeed that, in penal laws in which it is not possible for half of the punishment to be reckoned exactly, it may be done according to the decision of the rulers and the judges.

*[p. 989]* At the end, Allah has specially singled out and stated that only those who, besides being unable to marry free women, also fear that, if they remain without marrying, they may, being unable to keep chastity, become subject to evil conducts, may marry slaves. (**ذَٰلِكَ لِمَنۡ خَشِيَ ٱلۡعَنَتَ مِنكُمۡ**) Along with that itself, He also reminds that it is better to be patient as far as possible, and thus to avoid slave marriage. Perhaps, surely, after some time a circumstance may arise for marrying free women themselves. Marrying slaves who are in the ownership of others is also not fitting for decency and dignity. The children born in them will also be slaves. It is because of all this that Allah discourages this matter so strongly.

### Section – 5

*[Verses 26–28]*

*[p. 990]* The gist may be grasped thus:- To explain to you what the procedures are that you must keep practising in the moral way; to make you imitate the good conduct of the prophets and the righteous people who have passed before; to make you free from evil conducts and from sins; and thus to make you fit recipients of Allah's pardon and grace. It is all this that Allah intends by explaining such legal directions as these. It is Allah, surely, who knows best which is the way serviceable for your good and benefit. Allah intends that you should be free from sins. At the same time, what the people of Satan, who are slaves of the desires of the self, wish is that you should go wholly astray from the right path. Therefore you must not attach value to their evil promptings. and provocations. Man is indeed an excellent creation. At the same time he has many weaknesses as well. He will not be able to hold down and keep in check all his emotions. It is, taking this weakness into account, practicable and lightening procedures that Allah has fixed for you. One among them indeed is His having fixed that, in case one is unable to marry free women, one may marry slaves.

*[Verses 29–30]*

*[p. 991]* After describing many matters concerning orphans and women, and concerning inheritance and marriage, and so on, He is describing some matters that man must obey in the financial sphere. Allah forbids acquiring wealth through all ways that are not in accordance with the law, such as dealings in interest, fraudulent sales, gambling, betting, lottery, adulteration, and so on. He also permits all buying and selling that is done with mutual consent and that, along with that, has no objection in law. When a dealing that Allah has not permitted takes place, it will not become permissible on the ground of the consent of the parties to the dealing. In permissible taking and giving, the consent of both parties being present is necessary.

It is in order to be able to secure the realisation of the consent of the parties to the dealing that two kinds of **خِيَار** (freedom of opinion) in dealings have been permitted in Islam.

(1) The freedom to confirm the dealing, or to decide against it, before separating from the gathering in which the dealing takes place. This is known by the name **خِيَار المَجْلِس** (the freedom of the gathering).

*[p. 992]* (2) The freedom, fixing a time limit with one another, to do one of the two - confirming the dealing or rejecting it - even after separating from the gathering. This is known also by the name **خِيَار الشَّرط** (the freedom by making a stipulation). This **خيَار** is allowed only up to three days. The Prophet ﷺ says: ‘The one who sells and the one who buys will have freedom of opinion while they have not separated (leaving the place). Except if it was a dealing of freedom of opinion. It is not allowed for the other one, fearing that one will withdraw from the dealing, to separate from him (quickly).’ (A; Da; Ti; Na.) The detailed account must be known from the books of Hadith and of Fiqh.

What was said here as **لَا تَقۡتُلُوٓاْ أَنفُسَكُمۡ** (do not kill your own selves - yourselves -), its intent is usually interpreted in three ways.

(1) That you must not commit killing among one another. It is this that most interpreters have adopted. All the true believers are, surely, like the parts of one body. Then, in case one is killed, what it does is to affect one's own body itself. This is the hint in the word ‘do not kill yourselves’.

(2) You must not kill yourselves by yourselves by way of doing sins and wrongs. Or, you must not yourselves prepare the way for your ruin.

(3) You must not destroy yourselves by committing suicide or by doing things that become a cause of death. It is indeed a sentence that has the possibility of carrying all these three intended meanings.

On a journey, when 'Amr ibn al-'As (r) was affected by janabah (major impurity), he contented himself with ‘tayammum’ instead of bathing, for the reason that he could not bear the cold. Some Sahabah informed the Prophet ﷺ of this matter. When the noble one questioned 'Amr (r), his reply was: ‘O Rasul of Allah, I feared that the cold would kill me; and Allah has, surely, said **لَا تَقۡتُلُوٓاْ أَنفُسَكُمۡ** (do not kill yourselves)’. When he heard this, what the Prophet ﷺ did was to keep silent without saying any reproach. This incident is one that Ahmad, Abu Dawud (r) and others have reported. Many Hadiths stating that the one who commits suicide will not receive Paradise, and so on, are worth stating. What the last sentence (**إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ بِكُمۡ رَحِيمٗا**) points out is indeed that Allah's giving such prohibitions and admonitions, for the wealth and the bodies of men to be safe, is because of Allah's mercy and kindness towards you. At the end of the past 28th verse it was, surely, said that man has been created weak. Look at what Allah states in the next verse, a great example of the mercy that Allah shows him taking that weakness into account:-

*[Verse 31]*

*[p. 993]* This verse is a promise that gives very much hope to the true believers. Although man is, from one side, an excellent creation, from another side he is, surely, also weak. Allah has explained to him all the things that are harmful to his salvation and deliverance, and has also forbidden him from them. Because of his weakness, those who remain completely free from those forbidden things will be only very rare. Therefore what is seen in this verse is indeed an exceedingly great generosity that Allah, the Most Compassionate and the Lord who knows about men more than men, gives him. That is: if they do not avoid all the forbidden sins, in case they avoid at least the major sins, which are the great things among them, Allah will cover up and pardon the lesser kind of sins from them. Not only that, He also promises that He will make them enter an honourable place and honour them. And what is intended by this honourable place is nothing other than Paradise. It need not, surely, be said that this is a great favour for which we are bound to show Allah special thanks at all times.

But which are the major sins (**الكبَائِر**)? No clear proofs fit for giving a definite answer to this are seen in the Quran or in Hadith. It is only that a few definitions can be seen in the statements of some great men. When we examine them, we can understand roughly thus. Things about which there has been a warning of severe punishment, things that have been stated to become a cause of Allah's curse and wrath, things that have been forbidden in strict language, things that become subject to *[p. 994]* punitive measures in this world itself, and so on, and those of a kind that defy the greatness or the rights of authority of Allah, and those that cause a breach of peace in the midst of the community, and those that violate the bonds of kinship that must be kept with one another: all such sins are included among the great offences.

It is well known that in some strong Hadiths the Prophet ﷺ has singled out and stated seven major sins. When the Prophet ﷺ said, ‘Avoid the seven destructive sins (**السبع الموبقات**)’, some Sahabah asked, ‘Which are they?’ Then the noble one said: “Associating partners with Allah, and killing, otherwise than according to right, the soul (person) that Allah has forbidden, and sihr (black magic), and eating interest, and eating the property of the orphan, and turning back on the day of the thick of battle (when one is engaged in clashing with the enemies), and making an accusation of fornication about women who are believing women and who do not know evil conducts.” This saying of the Prophet is one that Hadith scholars such as Bukhari, Muslim (r) have recorded. In some other Hadiths that Bukhari and Muslim (r) and many others have narrated, some other things too can be seen counted as major sins. Displeasing parents, cursing them, giving false witness, swearing a false oath, and so on, are included in that. Then, it can, surely, be understood from this that the Prophet ﷺ too has not stated definitely that the major sins are only such and such, and that what he has done is to point out, as fitted the occasion, some important ones among them. Some scholars among the predecessors can be seen to have stated that, if examined in the light of the Quran and of the sayings of the Prophet, the major sins will be found to be seventy and more than that.

On this subject, among the many riwayahs that have come of Ibn 'Abbas (r) having stated it, one riwayah that Ibn Jarir (r), Ibn Abi Hatim (r) and others have reported is specially noteworthy. The gist of that riwayah is this: When it was asked, “Which are the major sins? Are they seven in number?”, Ibn 'Abbas (r) said: “It is closer to seven hundred than to seven. Even so, along with seeking forgiveness of sins there is no major sin. Along with making it a habit there is no trivial sin either.” What Ibn 'Abbas (r) points out is that, however great sins may be, in case one turns back from them regretting as one ought, and seeks forgiveness of sins from Allah, Allah may forgive it, and that, however trivial they may be, in case one keeps repeating them without turning back regretting, they will end up as great sins. It is, surely, clear from all this that it is not possible to determine and say that all such and such are great offences and all such and such are small offences. May Allah include us all among the righteous people who avoid the great sins and repent from the small sins, Amin.

*[Verse 32]*

*[p. 995]* After stating about attacking the life and the property of others, Allah advises that even the evil cravings that become a cause of such injustices must be given up. Allah will, surely, have given some excellence over others in many things, such as wealth, comforts and conveniences, positions and honours, natural distinctions, and qualities of character. One must not covet that those kinds of qualities should be one's own, nor be envious of them. Whether men or women, all will receive the gain and the reward according to the deeds and the labours that each one does and earns. Men and women will have some special features of their own. Except that both groups conduct themselves according to that, there is no use in coveting the gains of others. *[p. 996]* What is needed is to wish that the good things others have should come to be one's own too, and to strive for that, and along with that to pray to Him for Allah's grace and generosity to keep raining down on oneself. Allah knows rightly to whom all, what all and when all it is to be given, or not to be given: thus is the gist of this verse on the whole.

It has been reported by Bukhari, Muslim (r) and others that the noble Prophet ﷺ declared: ‘There is no envy except in two groups - that is, in one to whom Allah has given wealth and who spends it out on a needed matter, and in one to whom He has given knowledge and who judges according to it and teaches it (to others) -.’ It may seem outwardly that there is a contradiction between this saying of the Prophet and this verse of the Quran. In reality there is indeed no contradiction at all. The purport of what the Prophet ﷺ said is that one should have the wish for that good fortune, like that of the said two groups, to be one's own too, and that, when wealth and knowledge are received, one should have the readiness to act like them. The word ‘envy (**حَسَد**)’ here is merely a figurative usage only. What is meant by the envy stated in this Hadith is not envy in the sense of feeling dislike at the good that others receive and coveting that it is oneself who should have it - on the contrary, it is the wish that one too should have good qualities like that -.
