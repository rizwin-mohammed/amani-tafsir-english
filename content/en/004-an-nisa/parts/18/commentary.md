*[Verse 94]*

*[p. 1083]* For the verb **ضَرَبَ** (daraba), the meaning in the ordinary course is ‘struck, cut’ and the like. According to the difference of the particles and of the nouns that stand connected to it, many other meanings also come for it. An example of that is what is seen in this verse. It is in the sense of ‘travelled’, or else went on a journey, that it is here *[TN: no full stop is printed at the end of this sentence.]* In place of **السَلاَم** there is also a reading **السَلم** here. In either case, peace, submission, the phrase of greeting ‘salam’ and the like have been assigned as its meaning. What is meant here is displaying the sign of Islam, and the stance of peace, by means of salam, or by means of the phrase of tawhid, or otherwise. **مَغَانِمُ** is the plural of the word **مَغْنَم**; ‘good fortune, goods obtained by good fortune without labour, property obtained from the enemies in battle’ *[p. 1084]* and the like will be its meaning. Another word in which these same meanings come is **غنيمة**, and its plural **غنائم** too. It is for the wealth obtained in battle that these words are usually used.

Before the Arabs had finished ranging themselves under the banner of Islam, there were in the enemy countries, here and there, some isolated individuals who had believed in Islam. When the Muslims march against the enemy land, there would not be any outward signs for recognising such people. Therefore, when such individuals come into the sight of the Muslims, it is natural, is it not, that they reckon them as of the enemy section, and that it becomes a cause for them to fall prey to the sword. Even if they say some words showing that he is a Muslim, it may also be supposed that it is a device for momentary safety. A few such incidents also took place. Therefore Allah commands the true believers that they should take care so that such untoward incidents do not befall in future, and that they must not give occasion to regret having acted in error without the truth becoming clear.

Concerning the occasion of revelation of this verse, many riwayahs can be seen. One among them is thus: A Muslim army band met a man on their journey. With him there were also a few goats. He said salam to them. They thought that it was a momentary ruse. They killed him, and took possession of the goats. (Bu.) Another is this: When an army band in which Miqdad ibn al-Aswad (r) was included came near the enemies, the enemies scattered here and there. One man remained behind in the place. That man had a good deal of property too. He said that he bears witness ‘La ilaha illallah’: Miqdad (r) was not convinced of it. He killed him. When the Prophet ﷺ learned the information, he reproached Miqdad (r), saying ‘What will you do tomorrow with La ilaha illallah?’ (Bazzar, Tabarani) In short, it may be said generally that it is some such incidents that are the cause of revelation of this verse.

The gain that you obtain by killing a person without being satisfied with his outward state is merely a little perishable material goods. With Allah there are countless goods and possessions and favours lying in store. On another occasion He may make the opportunity for you to obtain from that. Therefore do not venture upon such transgression. Just think over your own former state. You too were formerly disbelievers like those individuals. Afterwards, when you accepted true faith, the state of not daring to make it public has passed over you too. It is only that, by the kindness and graciousness of Allah, your state has now improved. You need to remember that it is those who live in that same state *[p. 1085]* whom you assault. You should take good care that such untoward incidents do not occur in future.’ That, about a person who shows that he is a Muslim, one must not pass the judgement ‘not’ without clear proof - such is the purport of what Allah said. At the end a warning has been given that Allah is knowing all your actions properly, and minutely.

*[Verses 95–96]*

*[p. 1086]* The people who, even without any hardships such as disease, physical disability and the like, sit still at home without taking part in the righteous struggles for the din, and those who go out to struggle with person and with wealth, are not alike; Allah has promised that He will indeed give to everyone the very best recompenses according to their respective good deeds. In any case *[DOUBT: "ഏതെങ്കിലും സമരശാലികൾക്ക്": ഏതെങ്കിലും is read here as "in any case" (as ഏതായാലും); it can also be read with സമരശാലികൾക്ക് as "to some strivers" / "to strivers, whichever they may be".]*, that Allah will give to the strivers very many excellences and positions of rank that the others do not have - that indeed is the gist of these verses. What Allah does is giving the true believers zeal and enthusiasm to conduct righteous struggles such as battle. Those who have difficulties such as disease and the like are ones who have an excuse for not taking part in battle and the like. That is why He has qualified (them) as those who do not have such hardship ( **غَيۡرُ أُوْلِي ٱلضَّرَرِ** ).

This statement is about those who sat still without going to battle on an occasion when it was not obligatory that all the individuals who had no accepted excuses should take part in battle - when battle was a general obligation, and it would suffice if a number of people were ready for it -. It can be seen that Bukhari (r) has quoted Ibn 'Abbas (r) as saying that it is concerning those who took part in the battle of Badr, and those who did not take part, that this verse makes the statement. Conversely, Allah's having reproached in very strong language the individuals for whom taking part in battle is obligatory withdrawing without a proper reason, and making excuses, can be seen in Suratut Tawbah. This can be understood, is it not, from the very fact that, without saying any reproach about those who sat held back, Allah has promised a good recompense to both parties, and that He said, about those who took part in battle, that He will give them greater excellence.

For all the struggles conducted for the truth and for just rights, one says **جِهَاد** (jihad), and for the one who conducts the struggle, **مجاهد** *[p. 1087]* (mujahid). Among the struggles, the most principal is indeed battle with the enemies. What is mainly meant here too is that itself. Imam Ibn Taimiyyah (r) has given a definition of ‘jihad’ thus: ‘Jihad is indeed employing ability - all kinds of abilities within one's influence - in achieving the matter liked by Allah, and in preventing the things displeasing to Allah.’ (\*) Then it is clear that the sphere of jihad is very wide. The definition that Imam Raghib (r) gave to it is thus: ‘Using the whole of one's ability in resisting the enemy ( **استفراغ الوسع فى مدافعة العدو** )’ Continuing, he has also pointed out, quoting a verse of the Quran and a saying of the Prophet, that there are three kinds of jihad, thus: jihad against the visible enemy, jihad against Satan, jihad against one's own self, and that there will be jihad both with the hand and with the tongue. If examined minutely, the definitions of both these great men can be seen to culminate in the same idea.

> **Note:** (\*) **العبودية لابن تيمية رحمه الله — ص٤٦** – Al-'Ubudiyyah, by Ibn Taimiyyah, rahimahullah – p. 46.

### Section – 14

*[Verses 97–99]*

*[p. 1088]* After making a statement about those who sit still at home without taking part in the struggle, He is making a statement about the Muslims who, without going in hijrah from the land of shirk, stayed huddled among the enemies. There were in Makkah a number of people who, after accepting Islam, because of the reluctance to leave land and home, kept concealing their faith, and causing lapses in religious observances, so as not to earn the loathing and hatred of the mushriks. They would often have to yield to the wishes of the mushriks. They would also say that that policy is necessary because they are in the enemy land. After the hijrah to Madinah, with the facility for a peaceful Islamic life *[p. 1089]* having come about there, it was obligatory for all the Muslims to go in hijrah to Madinah. But those mentioned above were not ready to give up worldly interests for the safety of religious life and for the good of Islam. It is about them that the reference in this verse is.

Ibn Jarir, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and Baihaqi (r) quote Ibn 'Abbas (r) as having stated; ‘A good number of people among the inhabitants of Makkah had believed in Islam. They went on regarding Islam as trivial. Thus, the mushriks brought them too along in the battle of Badr. Some got wounded in the battle and some were killed. Then, the Muslims said: These brothers of ours were Muslims. The enemies brought them by compulsion. So, they sought forgiveness for them. Concerning these, the (this) verse **إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ تَوَفَّىٰهُمُ ٱلۡمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ**.... was revealed. They wrote this information, and that such people (those who did not come in hijrah) have no excuse, to the people remaining in Makkah. Thus they set out(on hijrah). The mushriks went after them and put them into trouble. Concerning them, the verse **وَمِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ مَن يَقُولُ ءَامَنَّا**....... There are among mankind some who say, we have believed (in Allah), and then, if some harm befalls in the matter of Allah. make the trouble of the people as though it were the punishment of Allah..... (Ankabut : 10) was revealed. (\*) The Muslims wrote this information too to them. Then they grieved, and despaired that there was no way for them to obtain good. Thereupon, concerning them, the verse **ثُمَّ إِنَّ رَبَّكَ لِلَّذِينَ هَاجَرُواْ**... (Then your Rabb - to those who went in hijrah after falling into trouble, and afterwards struggled and were patient - after that, is One who forgives and the Ever-Merciful. (Nahl : 110) was revealed. (\*\*) Then, the Muslims again wrote to them this information too, and that there is a way of escape for them - therefore set out -. Thus, they set out (to Madinah). Then, the mushriks seized them and clashed with them. In that some were killed.

> **Note:** (\*) Although Surah Ankabut is among the Makkan surahs, this riwayah gives strength to the opinion that the first eleven verses in it are Madinan.

> **Note:** (\*\*) There is also an opinion that this verse, and some other verses in Surah Nahl, are Madinan.

In a report that Bukhari (r) quoted from Ibn 'Abbas (r) by way of 'Ikrimah (r), this too can be seen: ‘Some people who were Muslims were with the mushriks. What they (the mushriks) intended was to make it seem that their band was large against the noble Rasul ﷺ. Thus, when an arrow is shot (in the battle), some would be struck by it and killed, *[p. 1090]* or some would be struck by a sword-cut and killed. Then, Allah sent down the (this) verse **إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ تَوَفَّىٰهُمُ ٱلۡمَلَـٰٓئِكَةُ**.... Whoever those who were the cause of the revelation may have been, the ruling in the ayah is applicable to all who are like those seen above. That is, this verse affects all those for whom freedom of religion is lacking, who have to disregard religious values and laws for the sake of the wishes of the enemies, and who, even when there was a way to lead an Islamic life in peace, without venturing upon it, give priority to worldly comforts and conveniences.

The purport of what is stated in this verse can be understood thus: In case those who keep doing wrong to their own persons themselves, by staying in ‘Dar al-Harb’, the enemy country, and by not being ready to come in hijrah to ‘Dar al-Islam’, the land of Islam, die in that same state, the angels who take their souls will reproach and rebuke them in such terms as: What state were you in - how did you handle your religious affairs - which party were you in -. Their reply will be such as: We were weak - we did not have strength and power enough to keep religious observance - we had to live yielding to the enemies -. Then the angels will refute them, saying such things as: Was not Allah's earth wide - could you not have gone to a land where you get freedom - why did you hang about among the enemies themselves? The excuses of such people are not acceptable with Allah. Allah will indeed give them the punishment of Hell. But, not all who do not go in hijrah will be included in this. Some will be absolutely compelled to it. That is, the poor ones who are unable to escape by using a device, or by cleverness, or by using strength, such as men who have weaknesses such as disease, old age and the like, women who are frail, children, will be exempt from this. Allah will give pardon to such people.

The verb **توفى** (tawaffa) can be both the past tense verb and the present - future tense verb ( **الماضى والمضارع** ). In either case there is no difference in the intended meaning here. It has been explained before more than once that its meaning is such as ‘take in full, fulfil completely’, and that it is used intending causing death too, in the sense of taking the souls completely. That what was said here as **توفيهم الملئكة** is intending the angels causing them to die is indeed what the commentators of the Quran have generally accepted. Some can be seen to have stated that it is intending the angels seizing them and taking them to the Mahshar. Allah says about the importance of hijrah:-

*[Verse 100]*

*[p. 1091]* The matter is not as those who are reluctant to go in hijrah from the land of shirk think. Those who go, sacrificing their homeland, aiming at the good of the din of Allah, will not have to suffer hardship without the facility to live. There is plenty of room and facility on the earth to live in comfort, escaping from the desires of the enemies. Not only that, even if anyone dies before reaching the intended place, after setting out leaving his homeland, keeping the commands of Allah, and longing for His recompense, and with an interest in the companionship and nearness of the Rasul, Allah will not refrain from giving them a great recompense. Because of not reaching the proper place, *[p. 1092]* no loss whatever is to come to them. Allah will forgive the fault that has passed, and will show mercy towards them. Such is the gist of what was said in this verse.

In a famous hadith that 'Umar (r) reported, the Prophet ﷺ says:- ‘Surely deeds will be only according to intentions. For each man, what there will be is indeed what he intended. That is to say, if a person's hijrah was to Allah and to His Rasul, his hijrah is indeed to Allah and to the Rasul. If a person's hijrah was to the dunya (for the sake of worldly affairs), he will get it. Or else, if it was to a woman, he will marry her. Then, his hijrah is indeed to that to which he goes in hijrah.’ (Bu; Mu.) The summary of a hadith that the Prophet ﷺ stated, quoting from Allah, and that Ibn 'Abbas (r) reported, is thus:- ‘Allah has recorded every good and evil (the good and the bad). A person intended to do a good, and then did not do it; then He will record it as one complete good. If one intends to do a good and does it as well, He will record it as ten goods, and as multiples up to seven hundred and more than that. A person intended to do an evil, and then did not do it. Then He will record it as one complete good. One intended to do an evil. And then did it as well. Then He will record it as one evil (only).’ (Bu; Mu.) By means of these sayings, the Prophet ﷺ teaches us that the fruit of all deeds, including hijrah, will depend on the intentions and aims held by them.

An incident that the Prophet quoted, which gives more light on the idea in this verse, is worth remembering here. The summary of the incident is thus: ‘A man who had killed ninety-nine people in former times went to a great priest, and asked, Is there tawbah for me (if I turn back in repentance, will it be accepted)? The priest's reply was ‘No’. Becoming enraged on hearing this, that man killed that priest too, and completed a hundred killings. Afterwards he went to a great scholar. He asked, Is there tawbah for me? He said: ‘There is. Who is it that will block tawbah?! You go to such-and-such a country. There are people there who worship Allah. Worship Allah along with them. Do not return again to your own country. It is an evil country.’ Thus, by the time that man had gone half the way, death overtook him. In his matter a dispute arose between the angels of rahmah (mercy) and the angels of punishment - that he is their man -. That is, the side of rahmah saying that he had turned back in remorse in his heart, and the side of punishment saying that he is a man who has not done any good! The angels were directed to measure the distance from there to the land that man had abandoned and to the land he was intending to go to. When they measured, he *[p. 1093]* was a span nearer to the land he was intending to go to. Thus, the angels of rahmah took him over.’ (Bu: Mu.)

Some have been heard saying that this hadith is mixed with a good deal of exaggeration, and that it is not reasonable. In reality, to those who take to heart this verse of the Quran before us and the occasion on which it was revealed and the like, and along with that the promises of encouragement that Allah gives for good deeds and good intentions, no exaggeration or lack of reason whatever will appear in incidents like this at all.
