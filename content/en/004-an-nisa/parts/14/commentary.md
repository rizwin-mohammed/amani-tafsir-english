*[Verses 66–68]*

*[p. 1050]* These too are indeed verses connected with the statements made in the previous verse concerning the hypocrites. Allah has not imposed upon human beings any unbearable commands, such as that they must destroy their own life by means of suicide, or by killing one another, or that they must abandon their own dwelling-place and leave the country. Allah has ordained only what, for them to do, *[p. 1051]* is possible, and what is necessary for their good and their safety. If at all He prescribes any such stringent laws, only very few people will become ready to obey it. If it comes to that, the real believers and the hypocrites will also be clearly separated. But, Allah has not prescribed any such harsh laws at all. Therefore, it is accepting duly the counsels that are given to them that is good for them in this world and in the hereafter. Their faith and truthfulness will also become firmer by that. Not only that, it will also become the cause of receiving a huge reward from Allah, and of receiving the help of Allah so as to stand firm steadfastly on the straight path without straying from it. This, in brief, is the import of these verses.

It has been narrated as a riwayah that, when these verses came down, many Companions showed willingness that, if Allah had commanded them to be killed, or to abandon the country and go, they were ready to carry it out, and that the Prophet ﷺ praised them. It has also come in some riwayahs that the Prophet ﷺ, pointing to some Companions, said that, if it had been so ordained, this man would have been one among those few people who obey it. Allah continues:-

*[Verses 69–70]*

*[p. 1052]* After stating about the necessity of obeying Allah and the Rasul, and the consequence of defying them, what is stated in these verses is about the final gain that is to be obtained by obeying them. The gain more fortunate than all the gains that are to be obtained in this world and in the hereafter by obedience is also that itself. That is, a heavenly life, maintaining friendship and contact with the favoured ones who have become vessels for the supremely great favours of Allah, up to the prophets - who are of the highest rank among all human beings -! What else is there more fortunate than this?! And that is something obtained only by the favour and generosity of Allah. Allah knows all matters, beginning with to whom, and what things, must be given. He Himself is sufficient to manage accordingly. The gist is that He has no need of anyone else's help or support.

Allah describes who those favoured ones are:

(1) Nabis(prophets).

(2) Siddiqs (the truthful).

(3) Shahids (true witnesses).

(4) Salihs (the righteous) it is these.

Prophethood is a high rank that Allah specially bestows on those whom He wills. It is not attained by man's merit or by effort. Those with the most excellent position among human beings are also the prophets indeed. Leaving them aside, then, in order, the siddiqs, and the shahids, and the salihs. It is not possible to give a precise definition to these three positions. It may be said in general that the truthful ones who stand firmly rooted in the truth in belief, and in word, and in deed, and who take care that no wavering or faltering comes in whatever crisis they face against the truth, are called siddiq ( **الصديق**)s, and those who have borne witness to the truth through sacrifices extending to offering up even life for the sake of the truth are called shahid(**الشهيد**)s. Though those with the higher position compared to the shahids are the siddiqs, both ranks are obtained from obeying Allah and the Rasuls itself. Allah says: ‘Those who have believed in Allah and in His Rasuls *[p. 1053]*, those people indeed are the siddiqs and the shahids with their Rabb.’ (Su: Hadid : 19) Salih( **الصالح**)s is the word that is said in general of those who, accepting true faith, remain on the right path and do good deeds. Allah says: ‘Those who believe, and do good deeds, We will surely include among the salihs.’ (Ankabut : 9) It can be seen that Allah, in some places in the Quran, has described even the prophets as salihs. Then, it can be understood that what is meant here by salihs is the righteous who have not attained the position of the prophets, and of the siddiqs, and of the shahids.

Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b al-Aslami (r) says: ‘I used to stay the night with the noble Rasul ﷺ. When I brought and gave him the needs such as water for wudu', he said: ‘Ask (for what you need)’. I said: ‘Companionship with you in Paradise is what I have to ask of you.’ The noble one said: ‘Is there anything else? ‘I said: ‘That alone is what there is.’ Then the noble one said: ‘Then help me in your own matter by way of increasing sujud (prayer).’ (Muslim) A hadith that has been reported from many Companions and through many routes is like this: ‘A man loves a group: he has not reached (in rank) together with them. The Prophet ﷺ was asked about such a person. Then he said: ‘A man will be together with those whom he loves.’ (This is what all the principal hadith scholars have reported.) Anas (r) is saying: Muslims have never at any other time rejoiced with as much joy as they rejoiced on account of this news (that the noble one told).’ It has come in many riwayahs that many Companions, who had been sharing with the noble Prophet ﷺ in all joys and sorrows, said to him: ‘While you will be in the most exalted rank in Paradise, even if we attain Paradise, we will not be able to see you and to keep company with you, surely’, and that by means of this verse peace of mind came to them.
