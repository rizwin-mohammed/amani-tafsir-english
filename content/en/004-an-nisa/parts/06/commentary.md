*[Verse 22]*

*[p. 975]* There was in the Jahiliyyah an evil custom of a man's son, born from another wife, marrying the wife of the man who had died. It is that which Allah has forbidden very strictly in this verse. He has also exempted from the prohibition what had passed before this prohibiting command came down. When this prohibition came down, some such marriages and the like being in existence - in case they were annulled - many problems would, surely, have to be faced concerning the children born in them, and so on. It is also because of that that husbands and wives who come to Islam from among the disbelievers are allowed to continue, just as they are, in Islam too, the marriage bonds they had contracted before according to their old customary practices.

It is not only making that vile custom forbidden that Allah has done. By describing it as **فَٰحِشَةٖ** (vile conduct), **مَقْت** (hated), **سَآءَ سَبِيلًا** (a very evil way), and so on, He has also made clear that it is an utterly wretched, despicable and condemnable custom. It is, surely, on rational, religious or moral foundations that the good and the evil of a thing are assessed. Imam Razi (r) has pointed out that what these descriptions indicate is that, looked at on whichever of these foundations, this custom is a very wretched arrangement. Even when describing the badness of fornication, Allah has stated (in 17:32) only the first and the last of these three descriptions. Here, besides that, He has added **مَقۡتٗا** (hated) too. It has come in a Hadith that, even after this prohibition was sent down, the Prophet ﷺ sent men to kill a man who, in the old way, had married his father's wife after the father's death, and to seize his property. (A; Ha; Ba) Besides this being a kind of fornicating intercourse, it is also an insolence shown towards fatherhood and motherhood. The father's wife, even if she is not directly the mother, is, surely, one who must be given the place of the mother. It is said that even in the time of Jahiliyyah, when this custom was in practice, this used to be called **نكاح مقيت** (hated marriage).

In the word fathers (**آبَاء**), grandfathers too are included. Scholars have understood that, in what was said as those whom they married (**مَا نَكَحَ**), wives with whom union has not taken place after the marriage are also included. What is understood outwardly too is that itself.

### Section – 4

*[Verse 23]*

*[p. 977]* Following His having forbidden and condemned, in the previous verse, sons marrying the wives of their fathers, Allah describes in this verse and in the next verse who they are to whom marriage is forbidden for the true believers, whether by family relationship, or by the relationship of suckling, or by the relationship of marriage. Those to whom marriage is haram (forbidden) by reason of family relationship are these:-

(1) **Mothers:** Besides one's own mothers, the mothers of the mother and of the father (grandmothers) also are included in this.

(2) **Daughters:** Besides one's own daughters, the daughters of one's own children also are included in this.

(3) **Sisters:** All sisters who have both mother and father in common, or else who have only the father or the mother in common, are included in this.

(4) **Aunts - that is, the sisters of the father:** Since paternal grandfathers and maternal grandfathers are included among fathers, the aunts who are their sisters also are included in this.

(5) **The younger aunts and elder aunts who are the sisters of the mother:** In this the sisters of grandmothers of every kind will be included.

(6) **The daughters of brothers.**

(7) **The daughters of sisters.** In both of these, the daughters of the brothers and sisters of the three kinds stated above are contained.

It is as a relationship just like blood relationship, in the matter of marriage, that Islam reckons the relationship of suckling too. In the impermissibility of marriage, in conduct mixing with one another, and the like, both relationships are *[p. 978]* alike. For a child who has sucked the breast of a woman - be it male, be it female - that woman becomes the mother, and that woman's husband at that time the father. If the child who sucked the breast is male, it will be reckoned as their son, and if female, as their daughter. With that, the brothers and sisters and the like of those parents also become relatives to whom marriage is forbidden for them. Those who are stated in this verse as those to whom marriage has been prohibited by reason of the suckling relationship are only:

(1) the mothers who gave the breast, and

(2) the sisters in that relationship.

Nothing has been stated clearly about the others. Even so, the principle described above can be understood from the expressions your mothers who have given you the breast (**أُمَّهَٰتُكُمُ ٱلَّـٰتِيٓ أَرۡضَعۡنَكُمۡ**) and your sisters by the suckling relationship (**وَأَخَوَٰتُكُم مِّنَ ٱلرَّضَٰعَةِ**). It is that itself that the Prophet ﷺ has made clear in a Hadith too: ‘Surely, whatever birth makes forbidden, suckling too will make that forbidden.’ (Bu; Mu) In a report of Muslim, what there is, is: what becomes forbidden by reason of family relationship will become forbidden by reason of suckling’.

After describing the women to whom marriage becomes forbidden by reason of the suckling relationship, a statement that Sayyid Rashid Rida (r) made in Tafsir al-Manar, saying ‘It is a matter that needs to be pointed out’, seems necessary to be recalled here. Its gist is thus: ‘In the matter of the suckling relationship, a careless attitude has become common among the people. They will have the child given the breast by one or more women. They do not take care to know properly the children and the sisters of the mother who gave the breast, the children of her husband and his sisters, and thus to consider the laws that arise between them and that child in connection with marriage and the like. Because of this, it often happens that a man unknowingly marries his sister, or aunt, or younger aunt - elder aunt, in the suckling relationship.’

### Those who have been made forbidden by reason of the marriage relationship are these:

(1) **The mothers of wives:** It was said before that maternal grandmothers and paternal grandmothers are included among mothers. The strong opinion indeed is that the mothers of the wife are forbidden all the same even if union with the wife has not taken place. Allah has not, surely, stated here the having taken place of union as a condition either.

(2) **Reared daughters:** That is, the daughters born to the wives in a previous marriage. But they will become forbidden only if union with the wife has taken place. That is, merely because a man has married a woman, *[p. 979]* her daughter born in her previous marriage will not become forbidden. If she is divorced before union, he may afterwards marry that daughter if he wishes. When a man marries a woman and brings her home, the usual custom, surely, is that her children from her previous marriage too are brought up under his protection. It is in that sense that ‘your reared daughters who are under your protection (**وَرَبَـٰٓئِبُكُمُ ٱلَّـٰتِي فِي حُجُورِكُم**)’ has been said. Not as a special stipulation. A hint too can be seen in it that it is, surely, not good that you should marry those whom you have been bringing up under your protection like your own children. It can be understood from this that, even if the care and protection of those children is carried on in the hands of some others, they may not be married. And in this there is no difference of opinion.

(3) **The wives of sons:** In the word sons, grandsons too are included: but adopted sons will not be included. Although in the Jahiliyyah there was the custom of adopted sons being given the place of real sons, Islam has forbidden the custom of adopting. (See Surat al-Ahzab.) It is also in order to exclude adopted sons that He has described the sons as ‘who are from your backbones (**مِنۡ أَصۡلَٰبِكُمۡ**)’. It means your own sons.

(4) **Bringing two sisters together in marriage:** That is, marrying two sisters together, or marrying the other one too while one is in the marriage. The correct opinion indeed is that this affects sisters in the family relationship, and sisters in the suckling relationship too. This too was an evil custom that was usual among the Arabs. That is why He said ‘except what passed before (**إِلَّا مَا قَدۡ سَلَفَ**)’. It has come in Hadiths that the Prophet ﷺ commanded some who came into Islam while two sisters were in their marriage to send away one of the two. (A; Ti; Ja) What this signifies is that, even though their marrying the two sisters together was before the prohibition, they may not keep them together any longer.

It is only about bringing two sisters together in marriage that it has been made clear here. Even so, the Prophet ﷺ has clearly declared that one may not bring together with a woman her aunt, younger aunt - elder aunt (the sisters of the father or of the mother), or the daughter of brothers and sisters either. Basing themselves on this Hadith, which all the Hadith scholars such as Bukhari and Muslim (r) have recorded, the great majority of scholars have also agreed on it. Only a few erring sectarian groups, and some modern individuals who pride themselves on being progressives, have a different opinion on this. The harmful aspects that arise because of two sisters living at the same time in the marriage of one man, *[p. 980]* arise also because of a woman and another woman who has a close kinship relationship with her coming together. It is also basing it on this very principle that the Prophet ﷺ has forbidden it. The limit that scholars usually fix for this prohibition is: if, in case one of the two women is imagined as male, marriage between them is not allowed, then both of them may not be brought together in the marriage of one man either. Allah continues:-

### Juz' – 5

*[Verse 24]*

*[p. 981]* The words **مُحْصَنَة** (muhsanah) and **مُحْصِنٌ** (muhsin) are descriptive nouns that originate from the root **اِحْصَان** (ihsan), which comes with the linguistic meaning ‘to guard, to ward off, to keep safe’ and so on. The first is the feminine in the passive form, and the second the masculine in the active form. The word **ٱلۡمُحۡصَنَٰتُ** seen in this verse is the plural of the first, and the word **مُّحۡصِنِينَ** the plural of the second. It is taking into account the said root meanings that a fortress is called **حِصْنٌ**, and a land protected by building a wall-rampart all around is called **بَلَدٌ حَصِينٌ**, and so on. It is in the sense of guarding against falling into evil conduct and disgrace - that is, protecting wifely chastity and purity of chastity - that those words are used on such occasions. While the linguistic meaning is taken into account, the intent of those words can, along with it, differ according to the context. As Imam Razi (r) and others have pointed out, **اِحْصَانٌ** can be seen used in the Quran in more than one sense.

For example:-

(1) **Meaning freedom:** What is intended by **ٱلۡمُحۡصَنَٰتِ**, seen in the next verse and in 24:4, is free women.

*[p. 982]* (2) **Meaning purity of chastity - that is, wifely chastity:** The **مُّحۡصِنِينَ** in this verse and the **مُحۡصَنَٰتٍ** in the next are intending men who guard chastity and women whose chastity is guarded.

(3) **Meaning being married:** What is intended by **وَٱلۡمُحۡصَنَٰتُ**, seen in this verse, is married women. It means that, besides those described in the previous verse, marrying women who are in a marriage is also forbidden to another man. Because it is, surely, clear that there is no objection at all to marrying those in the first two meanings - free women and those with purity of chastity -. It is from receiving protection - by freedom from the deficiencies of slavery, and by purity of chastity and by marriage from evil ways and from disgraces - that these intended meanings originate. It is in order to understand the reason and the background of the difference of meaning, according to the context, that comes to the word **مُحْصِنٌ**, as ‘free man. married man, man pure of chastity, and so on, that this much has been described. Not only here, but on some occasions too, it will be good to have this description in memory.

Now, let us pay attention to the matters stated in this verse. In the previous verse it was described who the women are to whom marriage has been forbidden for particular reasons. It is about those who, although they are not included in any of those, have been forbidden for the reason that they are in the marriage bond of others, that **وَٱلۡمُحۡصَنَٰتُ مِنَ ٱلنِّسَآءِ** (and those from among women who have attained wifely chastity) has been said. It was, surely, pointed out before that this word has been used here in the sense of married women. From what was said, that married women may not be married, one group has to be excepted. That is the women who have been seized as captives in war with the enemies of Islam. Even if they were married, the marriage bond between them and their husbands who are in the enemy land (‘Dar al-Harb’) becomes annulled with that. Nor will it be possible for the two parties to continue the marriage bond afterwards. Only after the captives have been officially distributed among the fighters, and it has been ascertained that they are not pregnant, may they be approached. If one wishes, one may marry them and take them as wives, or keep them as slaves, or give them in marriage to others. It is about these women that **إِلَّا مَا مَلَكَتۡ أَيۡمَٰنُكُمۡ** (except those whom your right hands have owned) was said.

It has come in Hadith through many routes that the Sahabah felt reluctance to enter into relations with those who had been married among the women made captive in the battle of Awtas (**اوطاس**) - while they had husbands -, that they asked the noble Rasul ﷺ about this, and that on that occasion this verse came down. (A; Mu; Ti; Na) The children born from the marriage of these women being free, and these women, by their *[p. 983]* master's or husband's death - if they have not been freed before that - becoming free of themselves, we have pointed out in the interpretation of the 4th verse. [See what has been described about the expression **ملك اليمين** - ownership of the right hand - in the supplementary note of interpretation to the 6th verse of Surat al-Mu'minun, about the Islamic position concerning slaves in the note of interpretation after Surat al-Ahzab, and in the interpretation of 47:4].

After describing the women whom it is not allowed to marry, by saying that this is the law Allah has laid down upon you (**كِتَٰبَ ٱللَّهِ عَلَيۡكُمۡ**), Allah has pointed out that no one has authority to bring an amendment to this, and, by saying that those besides what has been said have been permitted to you (**وَأُحِلَّ لَكُم مَّا وَرَآءَ ذَٰلِكُمۡ**), that you have freedom to marry those you like among the other women. Allah has also reminded for what purpose this freedom has been given, and what must be heeded in using it. That is what is seen in the sentence **أَن تَبۡتَغُواْ بِأَمۡوَٰلِكُم**… (so that you seek out and take women by your wealth, as those who guard chastity, not as those who practise harlotry). If one is to marry, one must give mahr according to the condition of the women. Afterwards one must also bear their expenses. Even when one adopts illicit ways without contracting a lawful marriage, one will have to spend wealth. Therefore you must not go and fall into evil conducts that are a disgrace and a danger to yourselves. You must use your wealth so that you maintain your purity of chastity and your moral honour and decency. It is good for you to seek out women of a kind that suits that, and wed them. It is for that that marriage has been permitted to you: such are the hints contained in it. It is, surely, the custom of the Quran, without stopping at stating the laws specifically, to point out also the wisdom-secrets and the intended aims contained in them. (It is described below about the intent of the word **مَّا وَرَآءَ ذَٰلِكُمۡ**, the women beyond that).

**فَمَا ٱسۡتَمۡتَعۡتُم بِهِۦ مِنۡهُنَّ** …… (Whoever from among them you have taken benefit by, you must give them their rewards as a fixed obligation). By saying so, Allah specially reminds of the matter of mahr. What is meant here by rewards (**اجور**) is the mahr. Mahr is, surely, a present that is given to them on account of the pleasures that are made use of from them. That is the reason for calling it a reward. Delaying in giving that present to those whom you make use of in order to guard your purity of chastity is not at all *[p. 984]* becoming. The gist is that paying it off is an indispensable duty of yours.

Although the matter of mahr is very important, after the mahr has been settled and fixed, in case both parties, mutually agreeing, make some concession in it, there is no wrong at all in it. If the wife gladly remits some part of it, or the whole of it itself, to the husband, he need not pay it afterwards. If he adds and gives something more than what was fixed, there is also no objection to the wife accepting it. Such things are the gist of the sentence ….. **وَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيۡكُمۡ** (there is no wrong for you in what you give, mutually agreeing, after the determination has been made). What was stated before in the 4th verse is that, in case something of the mahr is given up from the side of the wives, it may be accepted. Here, for its part, it has been pointed out that the concession may be from both sides. It has been stated about this matter in Al-Baqarah : 237 too. But Allah has there specially pointed out also that it is from the side of the husband that more generosity should show itself. Allah ends this verse by saying **إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ عَلِيمًا حَكِيمٗا** (Surely, Allah is All-Knowing and Profoundly Knowing). It is Allah who sends down these legal directions, He is All-Knowing and Profoundly Knowing; that being so, it is He who knows, better than you, which way of practice is serviceable and practicable for your good in this world and the Hereafter; in case you violate these bounds of law, it will bring harm on yourselves: such are some of the hints contained in this sentence.

After stating about many women to whom marriage has been forbidden, in the past verses 22, 23 and 24, Allah says: **وَأُحِلَّ لَكُم مَّا وَرَآءَ ذَٰلِكُمۡ** (Those beyond that - beyond what has been said above - have been made permissible to you.) Then, a doubt may occur to someone: will not what has come in the Hadiths - that all the women who become forbidden by reason of family relationship will become forbidden by reason of the suckling relationship too, and that, like bringing two sisters together, bringing together a woman and those like her aunt is also forbidden, and so on - go against this? Exploiting and holding up this possibility of doubt, some people who are interested parties usually make it out that those Hadiths are not in keeping with the statement of the Quran, and use it as a weapon for questioning even the authority of Hadith. The reply to the said doubt can be understood from what is described below:-

What Allah has done in these verses is not describing in detail all the women to whom marriage has been forbidden. Allah has made clear in Al-Baqarah 221 that one may not marry women who are mushriks, and in Al-Baqarah 230 that for one who has done talaq (divorce) of the wife three *[p. 985]* times, one may not marry her unless another man has married her. This matter can be understood from His having contented Himself there with what He stated about them, and not stating about them here. What has been done in these verses is to state clearly about many of the women whom it is not allowed to marry by reason of family relationship, suckling relationship and marriage relationship, and not to state clearly about the others - because they can be understood from the words or from the principles of wisdom -. It is also about those who have not been clearly stated that the Prophet ﷺ has described to us in the Hadiths.

Nobody has any dispute that, just like one's own mothers, the mothers of the parents (grandmothers) also may not be married, and that, just like one's own daughters, the daughters of the children also may not be married. Including the mothers of the parents in ‘mothers’, and the daughters of the children in ‘daughters’, He has contented Himself with saying, as a whole, mothers (**أُمَّهَاتِ**) and daughters (**بَنَاتِ**). In just the same way, there is also no doubt that among brothers and sisters are included those who have the mother and the father in common, and those who have the father or the mother in common. Here too He has contented Himself with saying generally ‘sisters, brother, sister’ (**اخوات، اخ، اخت**) and so on. All this can also be understood from the words the Quran has used. When stating about mothers and sisters in the suckling relationship, He indicates that, just as that relationship becomes firmly established between the child who sucks and them, it necessarily becomes established among the others too. That is by describing them as **أُمَّهَٰتُكُمُ ٱلَّـٰتِيٓ أَرۡضَعۡنَكُمۡ** (your mothers who gave you the breast) and **وَأَخَوَٰتُكُم مِّنَ ٱلرَّضَٰعَةِ** (your sisters by the suckling relationship). Otherwise it would, surely, have been enough to say the women who gave you the breast and their daughters. It is a matter anyone can understand that the principle contained in prohibiting bringing two sisters together in marriage will also arise when bringing together a woman and her mother, aunt and the like. But all, surely, will not be of the same level in understanding hints and principles of wisdom. Therefore the Prophet ﷺ himself has described such matters to the community. Allah has also, surely, said to the Prophet ﷺ: ‘We have sent down this authority to you in order that you may explain to them what has been sent down to them.’ (16 : 44) Some interpreters can be seen to have stated that it is as a pointing towards the facts described above that, without saying ‘those beyond those women (**مَّا وَرَاء هُنَّ**)’, He has used in that sentence ‘those beyond what has been said **مَّا وَرَآءَ ذَٰلِكُمۡ**. **والله اعلم**
