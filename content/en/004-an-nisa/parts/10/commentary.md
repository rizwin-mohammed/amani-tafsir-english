### Section – 7

*[Verse 43]*

*[p. 1013]* Addressing the believers, Allah is explaining to them certain rules relating to religious observance. We pointed out in Surah Baqarah that the drinking of alcohol was totally prohibited in Islam in two or three stages; that what was stated in Al-Baqarah : 219, that although there are some benefits and harms in it, it is the side of harm that is the greater, was the first stage; that the sentence in this verse, that one must not approach prayer as ones affected by intoxication, is the second stage; and that finally, by means of Ma'idah : 93, 94, it was strictly prohibited. With its being discouraged by means of the ayah in Surah Baqarah, some people stopped the drinking of alcohol. Yet, many kept on using it. Thus, some few untoward incidents came about, and when some Companions prayed having drunk alcohol and as ones seized by intoxication, they also recited the Quran verses wrongly. It can be seen stated in many riwayahs that it was following this that this verse was revealed.

‘Do not approach prayer as ones affected by intoxication’ is the word that Allah said, without saying ‘Do not pray as ones affected by intoxication’ or the like. *[p. 1014]* From this it can also be understood that entering the mosque while affected by intoxication, and going into where others are praying, must be avoided. Since prayer is an obligatory act whose time has been fixed, when it comes to this, that one may not pray while there is intoxication, it can be seen implied in this commandment also that toddy may be used only in such a way that the intoxication can pass away by the time of prayer. It will be for that very reason that it has come in some riwayahs that, after this verse was revealed, the believers used to use alcohol only at night. That in that period the custom was that prayer was held in congregation (jama'ah) in the mosque is also worth remembering. Since, without saying ‘as ones who have used alcohol - or toddy -’, it has been said ‘as ones affected by intoxication (**وَأَنتُمۡ سُكَٰرَىٰ**)’, it can be understood that even if intoxication affects one by means of something - other than by means of the drinking of alcohol -, one may not approach prayer. Likewise, from the words ‘until you come to know what you say’, the harms that arise if one prays with intoxication need not be stated, surely. Presence of mind and devout humility are lost. One will not be able to keep exactness and order in words and in actions. About one who dozes off in prayer the Prophet ﷺ has said thus: ‘When one of you is praying, if he dozes off, let him withdraw from the prayer. And let him sleep until he comes to know what it is he is saying.’ (A; Mu.) In one riwayah there is this as well: ‘Perhaps he may try to seek forgiveness of sins, and then blame his own self.’ Then is there any need to speak about the prayer of one affected by intoxication?

Opinions can be seen that the intent of having said ‘Do not approach prayer (**لَا تَقۡرَبُواْ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ**)’ is that one must not approach the act of prayer, and that it is that one must not approach the mosques, which are the place of prayer. Yet, the opinion that is seen as more fitting is that it is intending approaching both the act of prayer and the mosques, which are the place of prayer - the two -. That is also what agrees more with the sentence that follows, beginning **وَلَا جُنُبًا**. **الله اعلم**

There is no doubt that knowing the meaning of the Quran that is recited, and of the dhikr, du'a and the like that are said, in prayer, and keeping it in mind, is very necessary. But, what some are seen arguing, on the basis of the words until you know what you say (**حَتَّىٰ تَعۡلَمُواْ مَا تَقُولُونَ**), that prayer will be right only if the meaning of all the words that are recited and said in prayer has been grasped, is not right. That is, indeed, an imagined meaning that the sentence in reality does not have. Not only that, there is no evidence to say that only the prayer of those who know the meaning of the Quran, and of the dhikr - du'a and the like that have come in the Quran or in Hadith, will be valid; and it is not at all practicable either. It is about those affected by intoxication in such a way that they do not know what the words they say are, *[p. 1015]* or whether they are indeed in order, or the like, that it has been stated here. Not about those who do not know the meaning or the language.

Following having said that one must not approach prayer as ones affected by intoxication, it has also been said ‘Do not approach as people in janabah either’. The meaning of the word ‘janabah’ is ‘to stand away, to become distant’. What is usually intended by it in the technical language of Islam is the major impurity that arises because of intercourse and seminal emission. The person in janabah has to stand away from prayer, Quran recitation and the like, surely. The import of having said ‘except as those passing through a way’ is: there is no objection to walking through the mosque for some need. - One may not enter the mosque and stay on there -. That is what brings out the direct meaning of the word, and what the principal commentators and scholars have been accepting. The doors of some Ansari Companions who lived in the houses that stood adjoining the mosque of Madinah opened into the mosque. When they were in janabah, they had to pass through the mosque to bring water and so on. That it has come in some riwayahs that this statement is taking such situations into account is worth stating (**كما فى ابن جرير و غيره**) In the last period of the life of the noble Prophet ﷺ, he commanded that all such doors, except the door of Abu Bakr (r), be closed. (Bu.)

In short, if some need arises for the person in janabah before he bathes and becomes pure, he may pass through the mosque without staying on there. The ruling for women who have menstruation too is just like this. Some also hold the opinion that there is no objection for them when they do not fear that the mosque will become soiled. Some great men have also given the meaning ‘except as travellers’ to the words **إِلَّا عَابِرِي سَبِيلٍ**. As Ibn Jarir (r) and others have stated, since it states about travellers next, no relevance is seen for this opinion. What agrees more with the words of the ayah too is the meaning said first itself. **والله اعلم**

What to do in a situation in which one cannot purify with water is what is explained in the sentences beginning **وَإِن كُنتُم مَّرۡضَىٰٓ** (if you are sick). It is four situations that Allah has specifically mentioned here.

(1) **Being sick.** It need not specially be said that a nominal sickness is not enough; it must be a sickness by which, through using water, some significant harm may be caused. Sick people who, though there is no objection to using it, and though water is near, are unable to use it, and have no one to help, are also included in this.

(2) **Being on a journey.** Since it has not been said whether it is a long journey or not, *[p. 1016]* it can be understood that it is enough if it is a travelling that can ordinarily be called a journey (**سَفَر**). In any case, nothing such as going and coming to the market or to the farm will be counted as a journey.

(3) **Coming from the place of excretion of faeces and urine** (**أَوۡ جَآءَ أَحَدٞ مِّنكُم مِّنَ ٱلۡغَآئِطِ**). Or, urinating or defecating. The literal meaning of **غَآئِطِ** is a place that is sloping and low. For it is in such places, surely, that people usually sit for the purpose of excretion of faeces and urine, in order to be hidden from people. Therefore it is used in such meanings as the place where one sits screened - the place where one goes down to the water's edge - the place where one sits outside’. It is also used in the meaning ‘faeces - excrement’.

(4) **Making contact with women** (**لَٰمَسۡتُمُ ٱلنِّسَآءَ**). Or, having intercourse.

The opinion of some commentators, indeed, is that the import of having said **فَلَمۡ تَجِدُواْ مَآءٗ** (and then you did not get water) is that it becomes impossible to use it, either because of not getting water or for some other reason. According to this, this condition will apply to all four occasions stated above. The majority opinion, indeed, is that the intent is not being unable to use it - but is indeed not getting it -. But, there are differences of opinion: that this condition applies to all four occasions stated above, that it applies only to the last three, and that it is one that applies only to the last two. The opinion that it is one that applies to the last three is seen as more fitting. **والله اعلم** It is not intended here to lengthen this by quoting the said opinions and the reasons and evidences of each one. Yet it seems fitting to the occasion to point out here certain matters connected with the subjects in this verse:-

(1) Whether it be on a journey or not, there is no dispute that, even while water is at hand, if it becomes difficult to use it because of sickness, or because one has seen that, if the water there is is used, he himself or others will have to suffer from thirst, or for some other reason, one may do ‘tayammum’.

(2) Some are seen stating as a general ruling, putting forward some of the opinions indicated above about the condition not getting water (**فَلَمۡ تَجِدُواْ مَآءٗ**), that on a journey it is enough to do tayammum whether water is at hand or not. They also give as the reason for it that on a journey wudu' and bathing will be difficult; that especially in modern vehicles such as the train, the ship, the aeroplane and the like, both of them are hard; and so on. As said above, unless difficulty arises in using the water there is for some reason, no way is seen to accept this as a general ruling unconditionally with regard to travellers. Except some rational arguments. *[p. 1017]* No acceptable evidence whatever, or any significant scholarly opinions, are seen either. What is understood from the Hadith quoted below, too, is the opposite.

(3) Whichever opinion indicated above is accepted, there is no difference of opinion that the condition not getting water will apply to the last two situations - to the excretion of faeces and urine and to intercourse -, when there is a sickness for which water cannot be used, there is no need either to look whether there is water or not. What is to be considered here is whether the condition not getting water has a place when one is on a journey. In reality, the very rule of tayammum was revealed on an occasion when the Companions were in distress on a journey without getting water. See:-

The summary of that event, which Ahmad, Bukhari, Muslim (r) and others have quoted through many routes, is this: On a war journey the necklace of 'A'ishah (r) fell off. She had borrowed it from her elder sister Asma' (r). Caught up in searching for it, the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions had to stay on the way. There was no water with them. In that place too there was no water. The people spoke reproachfully to her father Abu Bakr (r) about having been stuck in a state without water because of the necklace of 'A'ishah (r). Thus, he went to his daughter, said many things, and also poked her on the waist. Since the Rasul ﷺ was then sleeping with his head on the lap of 'A'ishah (r), she only kept from moving. When the Rasul ﷺ got up in the morning, there was no water (for wudu' and so on). It was on this occasion that the ayah of tayammum was revealed. Then, the people came to praise 'A'ishah (r). Usayd ibn Hudayr (r) said: O family of Abu Bakr, this is not your first blessing. (Many other good things too have come about because of you). Thus, all did tayammum and prayed. The journey began. When 'A'ishah (r)'s camel was made to get up, the necklace was also found underneath it at the place where it had been lying. (A; Bu; Mu.) It has also come in a Hadith quoted by Ahmad (r) and some others that, however long water is not got, one may make do with tayammum all that while. Then no relevance at all is seen for the opinion that on a journey tayammum is enough for the reason that, though there is water to be got, using it is difficult.

(4) When there is no way of using water because of sickness, and when water is not got on a journey, if bathing becomes obligatory, one may do tayammum in place of bathing, and if the need for wudu' arises, in place of wudu'. It is clear that, since bathing is not obligatory because of the excretion of faeces and urine, tayammum has been laid down in place of wudu', and since bathing becomes obligatory because of intercourse, in place of bathing.

The real meaning of the word **لَٰمَسۡتُمُ** (lamastum) is to touch one another - or, to touch each other -. The word is used intending intercourse. The word **مَسَّ** (mass) *[p. 1018]*, which means to touch - or, to lay hands on -, has been used in the Quran itself (2 : 236, 237) intending intercourse. And it is, at that, a custom of the Quran to state by implication, and in figurative form, about matters (such as intercourse) of which speaking openly in plain words is considered not decent. It is because of all this that the import to have intercourse is given to this word. Some great men such as Imam Shafi'i (r) have given that word here its very linguistic meaning. On this basis they have also held the opinion that, just like the excretion of faeces and urine, wudu' becomes void if one touches strange women, and that one must take wudu' again for prayer. These people also take as evidence that, in the place of **لَٰمَسۡتُمُ**, it has been read **لَمَسۡتُمُ** (lamastum). Its meaning will then be indeed ‘to touch’. The difference of opinion among scholars as to whether wudu' becomes void if one touches women or not is one that has existed from the time of the Companions onwards. But, when the Hadiths are examined, it is to the opinion that the intent of that word here is intercourse that strength will be seen. As Ibn Jarir (r) and others state, it has come in many Hadiths that the Prophet ﷺ prayed after kissing his own wives, without doing wudu'. (The Hadiths can be seen quoted by Ibn Jarir, and Ibn Kathir (r) and others).

Now, let us attend to tayammum. The linguistic meaning of the word **تَيَمُّمۡ** (tayammum) is ‘to go resolving on, to intend, to seek out’ and so on. It is to the wiping of the hand and the face with soil in the manner stated below that ‘tayammum’ has come to be said technically in the religion. This name came to it from having said: when any occasion explained above arises, resort to the clean face of the earth (**فَتَيَمَّمُواْ صَعِيدٗا طَيِّبٗا**). The meaning seen in the lexicons for the word **صَعِيد** (sa'id) is ‘the upper surface of the earth, what is on the face of the earth, what is seen raised up on the earth, soil, grave, level place’ and so on. There is difference of opinion among the scholars, on the basis of the meaning of this word, as to which all substances may be made use of for tayammum. That is, at that, quite natural. Imam Shafi'i, Imam Ahmad (r) and others have understood that only soil may be used; Imam Abu Hanifah (r) and others, that soil and things connected with soil, such as sand, lime and the like, may be used; and Imam Malik (r) and others, that those and, besides them, any substances on the face of the earth, including plants and rocks, are permissible. If the Hadiths are examined, the very word **صَعِيد** can be seen used in the Hadiths at times. Yet at times it has also been made clear as **تُرَاب، تربة** (soil).

Muslim (r) has quoted thus from Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman (r): The Rasul ﷺ said: ‘By three things excellence over other people has been given to us: our rows (in prayer) have been made like the rows of the angels; for us the whole earth has been made a mosque (a place *[p. 1019]* for praying); its soil has also been made for us for doing purification if water is not got.’ (Mu.) In some riwayahs of this Hadith **تُرَاب** can be seen, and in some **تربة**. Either way, the meaning is indeed soil. Besides, this word can be seen in some other Hadiths too. The evidence of Shafi'i, Ahmad (r) and others is these Hadiths. In short, although it is not possible to say decisively that it will not be right if tayammum is done with substances that are not soil, there is no doubt that its being with soil is better. It is also understood from the Hadiths that it is good for the soil to be one that has dust. Nor is it known that the Prophet ﷺ, or anyone else with the knowledge of the Prophet ﷺ, did tayammum touching and wiping any of plants, metals, rocks and the like. It becomes clear from the attribute **طَيِّبٗا** (clean - good) that the soil must be clean and unmixed with filth (**نجس**).

It is the form of tayammum that the words **فَٱمۡسَحُواْ بِوُجُوهِكُمۡ وَأَيۡدِيكُمۡۗ** (and then wipe your faces and your hands) show us. There is no difference of opinion among the scholars that the whole face must be wiped. In the matter of the hands there is difference of opinion. When speaking about wudu', it has been made clear in Surat al-Ma'idah : 7 that one must wash up to the elbow. It has also come in some Hadiths that explain about tayammum that the Prophet ﷺ wiped up to the elbow. Therefore one opinion is that wiping up to the elbow is obligatory. The other opinion is that it is enough even if only the palm (the forehand) is wiped. That the word **ايدى** (hands) has been used in Ma'idah 41 intending the palms, and a Hadith that Bukhari and Muslim (r) have quoted, are pointed out as evidence for this. The Hadith is this: ‘On a journey janabah befell 'Umar (r) and 'Ammar (r). There was no water for bathing. 'Umar (r) remained without praying. And 'Ammar (r) rolled in the soil and prayed. (\*) When he came to know the matter, saying ‘It would have been enough if you had done like this’, the Prophet ﷺ struck the two palms on the ground and blew on them, and then wiped the face and the two palms with them. In some riwayahs of this Hadith, which Bukhari - Muslim have narrated through many routes, it has also been made clear that the Prophet ﷺ struck the ground one strike. On the basis of this it can be understood, surely, that it will be enough if one strike is struck for the face and the hand together, and that it is enough if only the palms are wiped - up to the elbow is not obligatory -. That is also the opinion of many great men. The opinion of another section of scholars is that both hands must be wiped up to the elbow, and that one must strike on the soil two times, separately for the face and for the hands. For more information one must rely on the books of Hadith and the books of fiqh.

> **Note:** (\*) As Imam Asqalani (r) has pointed out, just as the tayammum that is done in place of wudu' is roughly in the form of wudu', perhaps he may have thought that the tayammum in place of bathing must be over the whole body, like bathing. **الله اعلم**

*[Verses 44–46]*

*[p. 1021]* It is about the Jews that it is stated in these verses. It is intending their having deliberately abandoned right guidance and accepted misguidance for selfish gains that it is said that they bought misguidance. They are severe enemies of the believers, and ones who very much desire to see them go astray. Allah knows this reality well. Therefore the believers must never trust them and count them as friends and helpers. Allah Himself is amply enough to help and to save the believers. All this Allah is exhorting the believers in the first verse. Next, He points out some of their practices that show how full of stain the state of mind of the Jews is, and how corrupt their character is. That is, they distort words from their proper places. That is, they misinterpret the words of the Scripture. Or, they change some of its words and, thrusting in in their place some other words that suit their own desire, deceive the general public. The doers of this will be the scholars among them. (2: 75, 79) At times, altering and misinterpreting the words they hear from the Prophet ﷺ was also their custom. Another practice is this: words that, if heard outwardly, give the impression that they are good and desirable, and in reality *[p. 1022]* full of deceit and crookedness, they will use towards the Prophet ﷺ, Allah also points out three examples of this:-

(1) **سَمِعۡنَا وَعَصَيۡنَا** (sami'na, wa 'asayna) We have heard, and have shown disobedience - though we have heard with the ear, we are not ready to obey - is the meaning. It is in the place of what the believers are accustomed to say, **سَمِعۡنَا وَأَطَعۡنَا** (we have heard, have obeyed), when the Prophet ﷺ advises them anything, that they will say this. Either, they will say the word **سَمِعۡنَا** (have heard) aloud and the word **عَصَيۡنَا** (have acted against) softly. Or, it will be bending and twisting the tongue a little so as to give the impression that it is **أَطَعۡنَا** that they say **عَصَيۡنَا**. Perhaps - as some commentators say - it may also be that that word may be one that Allah has quoted in the sense that, though they show obedience with the tongue, they show the opposite by action. (Allah knows).

(2) **ٱسۡمَعۡ غَيۡرَ مُسۡمَعٖ** (isma' ghayra musma'in) This word can have two meanings, fit for praise and for mockery. That is, in the form of a prayer for good, thus: ‘Do hear, may it not come about that you hear anything that is not fit to be heard’, and in the form of a prayer for evil, thus: ‘Hear, may you not come to be able to hear’. It is in the second intent that those accursed ones used to say it to the Prophet ﷺ. In this, when only the word **ٱسۡمَعۡ** (do hear) is said, this double meaning will not come.

(3) **رَٰعِنَا** (ra'ina) This is a word that can mean ‘do regard us’, and ‘fool’. It is outwardly giving the impression of propriety, and in reality with evil intent, that they will say this. (About this it has been explained before at 2 : 104.)

What they do is to toss out all this, according to the occasion, cunningly bending and twisting, and keeping to the rhythm. Their aim is to get satisfaction for themselves by mocking the Prophet ﷺ and Islam. It is about that that it has been said **لَيَّۢا بِأَلۡسِنَتِهِمۡ وَطَعۡنٗا فِي ٱلدِّينِۚ** (twisting their tongues, and taunting in religion). What they ought to have done was to make it **أَطَعۡنَا** (have obeyed) in the place of **عَصَيۡنَا** (we have shown disobedience) in the first word, and in the second, without saying **غَيۡرَ مُسۡمَعٖ**, say only **ٱسۡمَعۡ** (do hear). and, in place of the third, make it **ٱنظُرۡنَا** (do regard us). If they had done so, it would have become good for themselves, and would have become a straightforward word. For that is, surely, the mark of true faith, and respect towards the Prophet ﷺ and the true religion. But, are those wicked ones going to venture on that?! They are, surely, ones who have become objects of the curse and wrath of Allah because of the severity of their rejection of the truth and *[p. 1023]* of disbelief. Therefore, about them, there is no right at all to the expectation that they will accept true faith. No one from among them is going to believe, beyond some very few individuals. Or, only outward shows of true faith may be seen from some, with policy. Allah also makes known that nothing, need be expected from them beyond that.
