### Section – 16

The next few verses are ones that refer to an incident that took place among some belonging to the Ansar and to the Jews, and that is described below. Even so, each sentence, and idea, contained in them are lessons that every Muslim should keep in mind. The nature of that incident, which many great men have quoted through many ways, both briefly and in detail, and which most commentators of the Quran count as the occasion of revelation of these verses, is in brief as follows:-

In an expedition at which the Prophet was together with them, Qatadah ibn Nu'man, belonging to the Ansar, and his paternal uncle Rifa'ah (r) had taken part. The coat of mail of one of them (of Rifa'ah) was stolen. Suspicion arose concerning a household known by the name of the sons of Ubairiq (أُبَيْرِق), a man of Banu Zafar (بَنُو ظَفَر), which is an Ansari tribe. Accordingly the owner of the coat went to the Prophet ﷺ and said that Tu'mah (طَعَمَة), the son of Ubairiq, has stolen my coat. In some riwayahs it has come that this man's name is Bashir, and that this man was a hypocrite and one who spread poems against the Muslims. In any case, when the real thief came to know of the matter, he took the coat and at night-time carried it off and put it in the house of a Jew named Zaid ibn al-Samin. (It is said that what he did was to entrust a few food provisions and that coat together to him for keeping, and that, while carrying it off, the mark of the food provision having leaked from the sack all along the way could be seen.) The thief made that matter known to some in his family. They went to the noble Rasul ﷺ and made known that their friend (Tu'mah or else Bashir) was innocent, and that in reality the one who stole the coat was that Jew, and that the stolen property had been found with him. Since their friend was a Muslim, and the one who stole was a treacherous Jew, they requested that the friend's innocence should be proclaimed publicly and that the noble one should argue on his behalf. The Jew described his innocence, and the reality of the incident, and *[p. 1103]* submitted the evidences that he had. On the basis of the stolen property having been found in his possession, and of the evidences submitted against him, it came about that the Prophet ﷺ rejected the Jew's plea, and proclaimed the innocence of the son of Ubairiq.

Before the stolen property was found from the Jew's house, they - Ibn Ubairiq's people - had submitted a complaint to the Prophet ﷺ concerning Qatadah, and his paternal uncle Rifa'ah. The complaint was that, without any proper evidence, they had laid a theft case against their household, who were Muslims. Qatadah (r) says: I went to the noble Rasul ﷺ and spoke. Then the noble one said to me that what you are doing is laying theft upon a Muslim family who are good people, without evidence and proof. Alas! It was in regret that I should not have said that, that I came back. My paternal uncle Rifa'ah (r) came and asked me the matter. When he heard the reply that the Prophet ﷺ had said, he said (with grief): **الله المستعان** (It is of Allah that help is to be sought.) Afterwards there was not much delay. .... The verses from **إِنَّآ أَنزَلۡنَآ إِلَيۡكَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبَ** to **فَسَوۡفَ نُؤۡتِيهِ أَجۡرًا عَظِيمٗا** ..... (from 105 to 114 inclusive) were revealed. When these verses were revealed, the noble one handed over that weapon (the coat) to Rifa'ah. Qatadah (r) says: When I went to my paternal uncle - he had grown old in years and had come to have no sight in the eyes - with the weapon, he said: ‘Brother's son! It is a contribution to the way of Allah’ What I had formerly supposed was that admixture had come into his Islam. Now it became clear to me: that his Islam was indeed true Islam. When these Quran verses were revealed, the son of Ubairiq (the real thief) joined the company of the mushriks. Then Allah .... revealed the verses from **وَمَن يُشَاقِقِ ٱلرَّسُولَ** to **فَقَدۡ ضَلَّ ضَلَٰلَۢا بَعِيدًا** ... (115, 116). This is the incident.

As Sayyid Qutb (r) pointed out, even if there are no other evidences for the Quran being the word of Allah, these verses are sufficient for unbiased minds that reflect with attention to be convinced of that reality by themselves. When evidences such as would disclose the real form of an incident are absent, and evidences against it are present, these verses lay open without reserve - even before the freshness of the incident fades - the hidden secrets that lie unknown in the folds of that incident. There is no way for this but to be from Allah, the Knower of the unseen. The Jews are a community that has become the target of the Quran's reproaches more than any other community. The Jews of Madinah especially. Even so, the innocence of that Jew, and along with it the hypocrisy, and treachery, of some individuals belonging to the company of the Sahabah who were Ansar, have, in these verses *[p. 1104]*, been publicly proclaimed. In language so full of force - taking in the nooks and corners of the circumstances *[DOUBT: "അണപ്പിച്ചുകൊണ്ടും" is printed clearly; it is taken as "taking in, covering (every nook and corner)"; it may also mean "bringing up close (the nooks and corners)".]*, and along with that including words in a tone of reproach even concerning the Prophet ﷺ - there is indeed no possibility of issuing a detailed statement except from Allah, who knows all minutely. Now, pay attention to the statements of Allah:-

*[Verses 105–108]*

*[p. 1105]* It is not a wrong from the point of view of the law that the Prophet ﷺ, on the basis of the outward evidences, proclaimed the son of Ubairiq (Tu'mah, or else Bashir), the real thief, as innocent, and justified the plea of his party. For human beings there is no way but to give judgement according to outward evidences. Hidden matters no one knows except Allah, do they not. The Prophet ﷺ too is not exempt from this. That is why on one occasion the Prophet ﷺ said thus: ‘Know well: I am indeed a human being. It is only according to what I hear that I give judgement. Some of you will have made his case clearer than some others. Thus, I may also give judgement in his favour. If I give to someone (else) by judgement the (real) right of a Muslim, (in reality) it will be a piece from the Fire. Let him take it up, or abandon it (as he pleases).’ (Bu; Mu.)

Nevertheless, it is the hint that, in comparison with the exalted position of the noble Prophet ﷺ, the noble one ought to have conducted somewhat more careful inquiry concerning the real state of the incident, that is seen in the sentences **وَلَا تَكُن الخ** (You must not be, on behalf of the treacherous, *[p. 1106]* one who argues) and **وَلَا تُجَٰدِلۡ عَنِ ٱلَّذِينَ**.... (You must not dispute on behalf of those who practise treachery to their very own selves). What many commentators say is that the command to seek forgiveness of sins was with reference to the Prophet ﷺ having said to Qatadah (r) that what you are doing is imputing theft to a Muslim family without evidence and proof. The opinion of some others is that the command to seek forgiveness of sins was with reference to the attempt to clear the son of Ubairiq from the guilt according to the outward state. There is also the possibility, too, that it was with reference to both. **الله اعلم**. In any case, what need be understood from all this is only that, in keeping with the exalted rank of the Prophet ﷺ, somewhat more careful inquiry ought to have been conducted. That is, this does not mean that the Prophet ﷺ committed a sin.

It must be understood that the Prophets' seeking forgiveness of sins, and Allah's commanding them to seek forgiveness of sins, and speaking in the form of reproach about some of their actions, are not because they committed sin, and that none of that is contrary to the accepted principle that the Prophets are ones protected from sins, and that it is with reference to some trivial lapses not befitting their great position. Besides, it is also a lesson, is it not, for the true believers who are their followers.

Although the qualifying as the treacherous, those who betrayed themselves, those who are great traitors and sinners, and so on, is, as far as the incident quoted above is concerned, with reference to the son of Ubairiq and his people, it is in a way that includes all who carry out such treacheries and artful tricks that Allah has used those words. That the ruling of the ayah is general, even if the occasion of revelation was particular, is an accepted principle. It is because of that, too, that on such occasions Allah, without disclosing the names of the chief characters of the story, stops at stating attributes that admonish them. The law of the Quran is for those of all times, is it not. It is because each one experiences the fruit of his own action himself that it has also been stated about the treacherous that they are those who practise treachery to themselves. It is about the plotting at night by secret conspiracy, to carry the stolen property and put it in the Jew's house, and, when the need came, to make him the culprit and slip away, that it was stated in the 108th verse that they made night-plans, and that they concealed from people - and did not conceal from Allah. It has come in one riwayah of this incident that, before the case reached the Prophet ﷺ, they made an attempt to lay that theft upon a man named Labid, and that, when Labid heard it and became enraged, they slipped away from it. From all this, the purport of what Allah said in qualifying them is clear, is it not.

*[Verses 109–112]*

*[p. 1108]* The meaning in these verses is clear even without any explanation. The reproach that you carried on dispute on their behalf points to the arguing on behalf of Tu'mah - or else Bashir -, the thief in the said incident, and what was said about having done a sin and then imputed it towards an innocent one points to the imputing of that theft upon that innocent Jew. As pointed out before, it need not be specially said that all these statements indeed apply to all who act likewise. Allah warns that when a person, having done a fault or a sin, afterwards imputes it upon another who is innocent, or argues in favour of the imputation, he will have to bear, besides the sin first done, the sin of having fabricated a lie about another person, and also the responsibility for all the harms that befall him because of having portrayed him as the culprit. It has come to be necessary to say that many among the general public of today, including Muslims, are deserving of this severe warning **والى الله المشتكى**

### Section – 17

*[Verse 113]*

*[p. 1109]* The attempt that the people of Tu'mah - or else Bashir - made, covering up the true state and fabricating false evidences, to establish his innocence through the Prophet ﷺ, Allah frustrated. They also made the true state clear to the Prophet ﷺ. *[DOUBT: "സത്യാവസ്ഥ അവർ നബിﷺക്ക് വെളിവാക്കിക്കൊടുക്കുകയും ചെയ്തു" is printed clearly with അവർ "they" (300 dpi); the sense of the passage suggests അവൻ "He" (Allah): "He also made the true state clear to the Prophet ﷺ". Translated as printed.]* Likewise, on many occasions when it was not possible *[p. 1110]* to understand the true state, Allah has laid open to the noble one their inner details through wahy. These are special favours that Allah did for the Prophet ﷺ. If that had not been, a kind of people, treacherous like them, would have made an attempt to lead the Prophet ﷺ astray, away from the right path. Allah has guarded and protected the Prophet ﷺ in such a way that none of that came about. On the contrary, the evil fruit of such stratagems their authors themselves will have to experience. The purport of what was stated in this verse is such things as that the favours and mercy that Allah did for the Prophet ﷺ are not this alone; He has given the Prophet ﷺ prophethood, which is the greatest favour, and the other matters connected with it.

What is meant by the Scripture ( **الكتاب** ) is the Quran, and what is meant by wisdom ( **الحكمة** ) is indeed the sunnah. How many messages of wisdom other than the Quran the Prophet ﷺ has received through wahy, has he not. That is why it is said that He sent down the Scripture, and wisdom.

*[Verses 114–115]*

*[p. 1111]* ‘Secret talk, confidential conversation’, and such, is the meaning of the word **نَجْوَى** (najwa). It does not matter whether the subject of talk is good or bad. Even so, its use will mostly be in bad matters. It is because the subject of talk is not fit for others to know that, most often, it has to be made secret, is it not. That is why the Quran is seen to reproach secret talks on some occasions, too. But if confidential conversation takes place on necessary subjects such as matters of charity, good deeds, efforts to settle and set right the quarrels and discord between people, then it is indeed a good thing. It must also be done with the intention of the pleasure of Allah. Then, it is promised in the first verse that Allah will give a great recompense for it. What is in the second verse is a forceful warning that those who, having clearly understood that the right way is such-and-such, still do not yield to it, and divide off into a camp in disagreement with the noble Rasul ﷺ, and, abandoning the way that the true believers adopt and ought to adopt, adopt another way, Allah will for the time being leave to their own course, and afterwards will also put them into the Fire and punish them severely.

It is plain indeed that these two verses do not affect only some particular individuals - they affect everyone generally. That is clear from the wordings, too. At the same time, in the theft case quoted above, *[p. 1112]* some individuals have also been specially hinted at in both verses. It is the secret conspiracy that the son of Ubairiq and his people together held at night in order to clear him from the theft crime, and to lay the crime upon the Jew, that is hinted at in the first verse. The conspiracy was put into effect, and for the time being it became fruitful, and very soon, without much delay, the Quran was revealed laying open all the secrets. Since they were known as a Muslim family, besides the sons of Ubairiq knowing more or less about the Prophet ﷺ, they also received through it a clear evidence of the truthfulness of the Prophet ﷺ and of the right guidance that he calls to. They know, do they not, that the one who disclosed those hidden secrets, which even the noble Rasul ﷺ was not able to know, is none other than Allah. In that situation, what Tu'mah - or else Bashir - ought to have done was to go before the Prophet ﷺ, disregarding the temporary disgrace, and turn back in repentance. Not only did he not do that, what he did was to commit more insolence, too. He went straight to Makkah and joined the company of the mushriks. It is concerning this that the hint in the second verse is. It has been reported in riwayah that in Makkah he had stolen even the belongings of those who had been his hosts, and that his end had culminated in a very tragic form. **الله اعلم**

It will be good to remember some matters here, as the occasion offers.

(1) What is meant by showing division and partisanship towards the noble Rasul ﷺ during his lifetime is plain indeed. After his time, its meaning is to adopt other ways contrary to the principles that he called to, and to the ways of practice that he showed.

(2) Those who disregard the truth even after understanding it through proofs and evidences, it is difficult for them to become good. Advising them will also be fruitless. Those who do not understand the truth - although they are at fault for not trying to understand it - can be hoped to become good when they understand the truth. Advising them will also be fruitful.

(3) The true believers are those who adopt the way of the noble Rasul ﷺ, are they not. Then, it need not be said that he who differed from the noble one is one who followed a way that is not that of the true believers, and that he is liable to the warning of Allah.

Differing from the Prophet ﷺ - as Ibn Kathir (r) pointed out - may be of two kinds.

(1) Differing by being contrary to the clear statements of the Quran and of the sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.

(2) Differing by being contrary to subjects which, even if in clear statements *[DOUBT: "വ്യക്തമായ പ്രസ്താവനകളിലെങ്കിലും" is printed so (300 dpi), literally "even if in clear statements"; the sense of the passage suggests "പ്രസ്താവനകളിലില്ലെങ്കിലും" "even if not in clear statements", i.e. matters of consensus.]*, without difference of opinion the true believers *[p. 1113]* have generally come to accept, and which have come to be established. It is certain that the Muslim community will generally agree only in matters based on the teachings of the Quran and of the sunnah. In a hadith that Ibn 'Umar and Ibn 'Abbas (r) reported, the Prophet ﷺ says: ‘Allah will never gather this community together in misguidance.’ (Ti; Ba.)

The gist is that the community as a whole will not unite in misguidance. Hadiths in this sense can be seen besides, too.

The sentence that was said about him who deliberately follows misguidance after the right guidance has been clearly understood, that Allah will turn him away in the course in which he turned ( **نُوَلِّهِۦ مَا تَوَلَّىٰ** ), is very grave and very pregnant with meaning. He is one who has insolently misused all of the intellect, knowledge, conscience, and freedom of will that Allah gave him. Therefore, he has no right to obtain from Allah the tawfiq, and help, to adopt the right path and attain salvation. And it is not possible for anyone to bring onto the right path him to whom Allah does not give the right path.
