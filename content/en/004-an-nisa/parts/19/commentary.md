### Section – 15

*[Verse 101]*

Many things were stated about battle and hijrah. For both purposes journeys have to be made, have they not. On such journeys, since it is difficult to perform properly the five obligatory prayers, which a Muslim can never omit, Allah explains the ways of remedy for that in this verse and in the next verse together.

**قَصْر** (qasr) means ‘to shorten’. What is meant here is praying by shortening the prayer. Qasr is of two kinds:

(1) What is done in the prayer with the number of rak'ahs reduced, and without turning to the qiblah, and without completing ruku', sujud and the like, and walking and running, and so on, with many reductions differing from the ordinary form being made *[p. 1094]*. This is explained in the verses that follow. It is commonly known by the name **صلوة الخوف** (the prayer performed in fear), or else the prayer on the occasion of fear.

(2) Praying a prayer that has four rak'ahs as two rak'ahs only. Although it may be said that this is indeed a particular kind included in the first, it is this that is known by the name **قَصْر** (praying shortened), and that is generally accepted on journeys. The first qasr, in the state of fear, has been established by the Quran, and the second qasr, in the state of peace, by the sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ. When Ibn 'Umar (r) was asked, ‘The qasr prayer on a journey is not seen in the Book of Allah, is it; it is only the prayer of fear that is seen in it, is it not?’, he replied thus: ‘Brother's son, at the time when Allah sent Muhammad the Prophet ﷺ as Rasul, we did not know anything. Now, it is only that just as we see the Rasul ﷺ doing, so we too keep doing. Making qasr on a journey is a sunnah that the Rasul ﷺ put into practice.” (Na; Ja; Ba; Ibn Hibban)

What Allah said is ‘There is no wrong in making the prayer qasr when travelling through the earth’. Then, it may be doubted how far in distance the journey must be, and also whether, in view of His having stopped at saying ‘there is no wrong’ without saying that one must make qasr, it is not better to omit it. As regards the distance of the journey, no evidence sufficient to determine that qasr on a journey is permitted only if there is such-and-such a distance is seen in the sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ. Since, when some riwayahs and the like are examined, it comes out that the distance of the journey needs to be taken into account, there are differing opinions among the scholars on this subject. And that is merely natural too. If all is examined together - as many great men state - what must be understood is that there is no fixed distance to be determined. But it is also certain, moreover, that making qasr is permitted only if there is a distance such as is usually described as a journey in the ordinary course, and that qasr is not permitted in the comings and goings to nearby places, and for daily needs. **والله اعلم**

Explaining all the opinions indicated above and their evidences, Imam Ibn al-Qayyim (r) says: ‘The Prophet ﷺ has not fixed for his community a definite distance of way for making the prayer qasr, and for omitting the fast. He has left it to them unconditionally. The matter of tayammum is also just so. But as for the riwayahs that fix a journey of one day and of two days, and of three days, and so on, none has been firmly established.’ (**زادالمعاد**)

From the word ‘There is no wrong in making qasr’ (**ليس عليكم جناح**), it comes out only that it may be *[p. 1095]* done, and may be not done. However, it is known from many hadiths that it was the practice of the Prophet ﷺ to make the prayer qasr on all journeys without exception. Nor has it been proved that the Prophet ﷺ completed four rak'ahs on journeys. Therefore many great men have even held the opinion that qasr is obligatory on journeys. The majority opinion is that it is not obligatory. In any case there is no doubt that making qasr is the best. But what might the hint contained in the sentence ‘There is no fault for you’ be? Even though the running between Safa - Marwah in hajj, and in 'umrah, was obligatory - because of some doubts that befell the Sahabah - when making a statement about it too, what Allah said (in Al-Baqarah : 158) was ‘there is no wrong’. Likewise, in the matter of making some reduction in the form of the prayer, which deserves very great importance, too, many have a right to feel doubt, do they not. It must be because of that that it has been used so here too. **الله اعلم**

Ya'la ibn Umayyah (r) says: I asked 'Umar (r) about the purport of the verse ‘There is no wrong for you in making the prayer qasr if you fear those who disbelieved putting you into trouble’, in view of the people having become secure (from the enemies). Then 'Umar (r) said: ‘I too have felt just as you felt wonder (doubt). So I asked the noble Rasul ﷺ about it. Then the noble one said: ‘It is a sadaqah (a gift) that Allah has given you. Accept His sadaqah.’ Ahmad, Muslim (r) and many others have quoted this hadith. As Allah Himself pointed out, although it was on an occasion when the enemies were lying in wait that qasr was permitted, the gist of what the Prophet ﷺ said is indeed that it is a generosity which Allah has given to the community, so that they may afterwards continue to make use of that permission on all journeys. That what has been said here is about the qasr of the second kind seen above - not about the first qasr - can be understood from the next verse.

The detailed particulars of qasr, and of the rules relating to it, and the differences of opinion in it, need not be touched upon here. Those who wish to know more should look at the commentaries of hadith and the books of fiqh.

*[Verses 102–103]*

*[p. 1098]* Many rules and counsels are contained in these verses. From what was said addressing the Prophet ﷺ, ‘If you are among them, and conduct the prayer for them, it can be understood that it is the army leaders who should give leadership to the army in the prayer (who should be imam), and that even at the time of battle it is in jama'ah that one should pray, and that, when it is seen to be necessary, it is the commanders of the forces who should make arrangements for the prayer of fear such as was said next. It is the principal aspects of the prayer of fear (**صَلاَةُ الخَوْف**) that Allah has explained here. It is from the sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ that its explanations can be understood. What is explained in these verses is not the form to be adopted on the occasion when encounters with the enemies are going on on the battlefield. When the two camps have clashed together and are carrying on armed conflict, there will not be the facility, will there, to divide the army into two or more parts and station them, and to pray one rak'ah each, joining each part. On such difficult occasions - whether it is in battle or not - it has been stated in verse 239 of Suratul Baqarah that it will suffice if each one prays as he is able, on foot or on the back of a mount, while walking too, and without completing ruku' - sujud and the like.

The gist of what Allah has explained here of the form of **صَلاَةُ الخَوْف** (the prayer performed in fear), which is to be performed at the time of battle, is this: Dividing the army into two, the Prophet ﷺ begins the prayer together with one section. When the sujud is over, that section moves back and stands facing the enemies. With that, the other section comes forward and they pray one rak'ah along with the Prophet ﷺ. But how many rak'ahs should each section pray together with the Prophet ﷺ? How should they complete the remaining rak'ahs? None of this has been explained. Nevertheless, what comes from this is that each section should pray one rak'ah each together with the Prophet ﷺ, and that the army may complete the remaining rak'ahs as convenient, singly, or together, or by taking turns with the Prophet ﷺ. **الله اعلم**

*[p. 1099]* When the hadiths are examined, it can be seen that the Prophet ﷺ has performed the prayer of fear in more than one form. Giving preference to some of them, some have also held the opinion that that form is the more correct. The fact is that it was whichever form was more suitable, taking into account the circumstances of the respective occasion, that the noble one adopted, and that it was not in one particular form alone at all times that the Prophet ﷺ put it into practice. When the comparison in number between the Muslim army and the enemy army, the particularity of the positions where both parties stand, the number of rak'ahs of the prayer being performed, the nature of the conflicts and the like, and also the fact whether the ranks on the battlefield have clashed with one another and begun the conflict or not, and so on, are taken into account, one same form will not be practicable at all times, will it. It can be seen that all the forms that the Prophet ﷺ adopted do not go outside the basic form that Allah pointed out in this verse, and that what he did was to adopt changes suited to the occasion in the details only. To know in detail, one should refer to the proper places.

As is usually seen in other books. When explaining rules, the Quran does not usually adopt the practice of stopping at explaining them alone. It is its practice to point out also the principles on which the rule is based, the rationales contained in them, and the things that must be known in carrying them out. Many examples of this can be seen in these verses. Look:-

(1) Along with saying that the first section should pray together with the Prophet ﷺ, they must take their weapons of war in hand. ( **وَلۡيَأۡخُذُوٓاْ أَسۡلِحَتَهُمۡۖ** ) So it has been counselled. It should not be thought that because one is in prayer one must not bear weapons. What this indicates is such things as: if need arises, one must be able to make an attack even in the prayer; if one stands for prayer without taking weapons, the enemies will try to use that opportunity.

(2) It is counselled again that the second section too, when entering the prayer, should take weapons in hand. There is mostly no room for the enemies to be anticipating in advance the Prophet ﷺ beginning the prayer together with the first section. Once the prayer has begun, it is certain that they will pay more attention to the movements of the Muslims. Therefore, without stopping at saying that they should bear weapons, it has been alerted too that they must also keep vigilance ( **وَلۡيَأۡخُذُواْ حِذۡرَهُمۡ** ). The reason for saying that vigilance is needed has also been made clear. That is, it is not bad if you let attention slip concerning your weapons and goods and materials - then they can make one great onslaught against you - such is what they desire. It is the love and mercy that Allah has towards the true believers that all this signifies.

*[p. 1100]* (3) But praying while bearing sword, spear, arrows, armour and the like is a somewhat difficult matter, is it not. When it is repeatedly said that weapons must be borne in the prayer, there is also occasion to feel that it is an obligatory command that cannot be omitted. Therefore it is stated that when there are difficulties such as rain, disease and the like, weapons need not be borne ( **وَلَا جُنَاحَ عَلَيۡكُمۡ إِن كَانَ بِكُمۡ أَذٗى**.... ). Even so, vigilance concerning the enemies must not be let go. The matter ( **وَلۡيَأۡخُذُواْ حِذۡرَهُمۡ** ) has been specially reminded. *[TN: the words printed here, وَلۡيَأۡخُذُواْ حِذۡرَهُمۡ, are from the earlier part of verse 102 (the second section); the words that follow the exemption for rain and sickness in the verse are وَخُذُواْ حِذۡرَكُمۡ.]*

(4) Allah has very great love and kindness towards the true believers. Towards the disbelievers He has extreme loathing and anger. That being so, why may Allah not defeat the disbelievers and subdue them? No one need have such a misgiving. Allah has prepared a humiliating punishment for them. ( **إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ أَعَدَّ لِلۡكَٰفِرِينَ**.... ) Yes, by saying that in this world they will gradually be humiliatingly defeated, and that in the hereafter they will have to suffer a disgraceful punishment, the true believers are consoled.

(5) Then, it has been alerted too that even after performing the prayer as explained above, the remembrance of Allah must not be let go, and that, according to the occasion, standing or sitting or lying, in whatever way it may be, one must keep remembering Allah ( **فَإِذَا قَضَيۡتُمُ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ** ...... ).

(6) It is stated too that if, the battle being over, the fear passes away, and the situation becomes calm, then afterwards the prayer must be observed in its proper order itself as usual ( **فَإِذَا ٱطۡمَأۡنَنتُمۡ فَأَقِيمُواْ**... ). That is, that it must be done in jama'ah, and performing all the components, conditions and proprieties in their proper form.

(7) It has also been specially pointed out ( **إِنَّ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ كَانَتۡ**..... ) that the reason for stating the matter of the prayer so strictly - in such a way that there is no exemption even in battle - is that it is an obligatory rule whose time has been fixed in such a way that the true believers may not omit it. Among the deeds to be adopted in practice once true faith has been accepted, the prayer is the most important, is it not. This is a sentence that every Muslim should remember very seriously. It should also be remembered that it was following His having said that even in battle the prayer must be performed without letting its time go wrong, and that, even though with some relaxations allowed from the ordinary state, it must be performed in jama'ah itself, that Allah said this sentence.

Ibn Mas'ud (r) says: I asked the noble Prophet ﷺ, ‘Which among deeds is the most beloved to Allah?’: The noble one said: ‘It is praying the prayer at its time.’ I *[p. 1101]* asked: ‘Then which?’ The noble one said: ‘It is doing good to parents.’ I asked: ‘Then which?’ He said: ‘It is struggling in the way of Allah.’ Ibn Mas'ud (r) says: ‘He told me these things. If I had asked more, the noble one would have told me more.’ (Bu; Mu.) Jabir (r) says: The Rasul ﷺ said: ‘What is between a servant and kufr (disbelief) is abandoning the prayer.’ (Mu). The prayer is the boundary line between Islam and kufr. That is, the gist is that abandoning it is kufr. Buraidah (r) says: ‘The Rasul ﷺ said: The covenant that is between us and them (the others) is the prayer. Whoever abandoned it has become a disbeliever.’ (A; Ti; Na; Ja.) 'Abdullah ibn Shaqiq (r) says: ‘The Sahabah of the Prophet ﷺ did not hold the opinion that abandoning anything at all among the deeds is kufr - except the prayer.’ (Ti.)

*[Verse 104]*

When the enemies, who are the mushriks that had girded their loins and come forth to annihilate the true din, sound the war-trumpet, it is not enough only to confront them; one must also try to seek them out and destroy them as far as possible. In that, cowardice or weakness *[p. 1102]* must not be in you. You must be intent on it with patience, and with zeal. Through confronting them you will probably be experiencing many hardships and losses and wounds and so on. On account of that you must not stand back. You should remember that just like you, they too are experiencing all that. But you have a special quality that they do not have. You are ones who hope that many favours from Allah will be obtained in this world and in the hereafter. They do not have that hope. The purport is that, in that respect, you should have patience, and briskness, and pluck more than they, should you not.
