### Section – 2

In a Hadith that has been quoted as stated by Jabir (r), it has come thus: The wife of Sa'd ibn Rubayyi' (**سعدبن ربيع رضى الله عنه**) went to the noble Rasul ﷺ and said: ‘O Rasul of Allah, here, these two are the daughters of Sa'd. Their father took part along with you in the battle of Uhud *[p. 954]* and was killed as a martyr. Their paternal uncle has taken all their property. He has left nothing for them. Nobody will marry them unless they have some property.’ Then the noble one said: ‘Allah will make a decision in it’. Thus, the ayah of inheritance was sent down. Then the Rasul ﷺ sent someone to the paternal uncle of those girls and said to him: ‘To the two girls of Sa'd two thirds must be given, and to their mother one eighth. What remains is for you.’ (A; Da; Ti; Ja)

*[Verse 11]*

*[p. 955]* After giving many general principles of guidance affecting inheritance in verses 7 - 10, the legal explanation of it begins from this verse. In the three verses, this verse, the next verse, and the last verse of the surah, Allah has described all the main laws concerning inheritance. *[p. 956]* The laws of inheritance in Islam, which are known by the name **علم الفرائض** (‘ilm al-fara'id’ - the knowledge of the determinations of shares), are indeed a priceless branch of knowledge codified on the basis of these three verses and the related sayings of the Prophet. This is a part that deserves very much importance among the sciences of jurisprudence in Islam. Some Hadiths too can be seen that indicate its special importance. Many great ones have written books specially on this subject. ‘Ilm al-fara'id’ is a code of law of which the Muslims can be proud before any other community in the world. Even so, the reality that, among our Muslim scholars of today, the persons who have obtained the needed knowledge in this subject are few, has to be admitted with exceeding sorrow. Since the place where the legal aspects are explained in detail is the books of jurisprudence, we need speak here only about the matters that have been clearly stated in these verses. **والله الموفق**

It is by awakening very strongly, in the form of good counsel, that ‘Allah makes ‘wasiyyah’ to you in the matter of your children’ (**يُوصِيكُمُ ٱللَّهُ فِيٓ أَوۡلَٰدِكُمۡ**), that Allah begins to describe the laws. What this indicates is the importance of the subject, and the strong encouragement to bring it into practice. Although the heirs are many, the children are, surely, those who have the closest relation with the person who dies. Therefore the matter of the children is stated first. Moreover, it is also in a setting in which the right of property was not given to daughters and to young sons that this law comes down. That being the state, a hint too may be contained in it that it is the matter of your children that you must keep in memory first in inheritance. (Allah knows)

The singular of the word **اولاد** (‘awlad’, that is, children) is (walad). That word is used for all, without the distinction among the children whether male or female, or young or grown-up. Allah's wasiyyah is indeed that for one male there is the share of two females (**لِلذَّكَرِ مِثۡلُ حَظِّ ٱلۡأُنثَيَيۡنِ**). Some secrets can be seen to be contained in His having reckoned the right of the male by making the share of the female the standard, without saying ‘What the female has is half of the male's’ or the like. What this sentence does is to put an end to the custom in which the right was not given to women and to give them stability of right, and along with that to concede to men the rank in comparison with women. That is, fixing the right of the man is not the only purpose. That may be the reason for Allah's saying it like this. **الله اعلم** It may be asked: although there is a difference of rank between woman and man, while the relation of both parties with the parents is alike, ought not the right to be given to both parties in the same measure? It is the man, more than the woman, who faces the need for wealth. *[p. 957]* The obligation to fulfil and provide all the essential expenses of women, such as food and dwelling, is also the man's. Besides, it is also the man who will face more the need for the expenditure of wealth in outside matters. These are some of the principles contained in fixing for the man double that of the woman.

Knowing some technical principles that have been accepted in the law of inheritance in Islam will be very helpful for rightly understanding the legal aspects in it. Therefore a general account about them too may be pointed out here. In the matters concerning inheritance, when it is said ‘father, mother, son, daughter, brother, sister, paternal uncle, husband’ and so on, the meaning is the father, mother, son.... of the deceased person. In just the same way. The shares beginning with ‘half, one sixth, one eighth’ will also be reckoned on the basis of the total wealth of the deceased person. For example, when it is said, half to the daughter and one eighth to the wife, its meaning is indeed: from the total property of the deceased, half of it to his daughter and one eighth of it to his wife.

Among the heirs there are two kinds:

(1) Those for whom a fixed share has been reckoned; they are called **اهل الفروض** (holders of fixed shares). No increase or decrease will come in their fixed share.

(2) Those for whom a definite share has not been fixed. They are called **العصبة** (‘asabah). What remains after giving to the holders of fixed shares will be for them. If there are no holders of fixed shares at all, the whole property will also be obtained by them. These will be those who are nearer in family relation with the deceased, such as son, grandson, father, brother, brother's son, paternal uncle, paternal uncle's son. On some occasions it may come about that, along with some of them obtaining a fixed share (**فرض**), they also obtain that too, if there is a remainder after deducting the rights of the other sharers present. That is, the gist is that an occasion may also come about in which it is possible for one person to have the right in both capacities. An example of this can be seen below. The explanation has to be known from its proper places.

It is, surely, about the right of the children and of the parents that this verse treats. If, in its light, it is examined keeping in front the technical principles stated above, the laws seen below can be seen to be contained in that verse:-

(1) When a person has only sons as heirs, the whole property will be theirs. If they are more than one, they must divide and take it equally. If there are other holders of fixed shares, what is for these will be the remainder after deducting theirs.

*[p. 958]* (2) When there are no sons, if there is only one daughter, she will obtain half, and if they are more than one, they will obtain two thirds (2/3). What Allah said is: if the daughters are more than two, for them two thirds as well (**فَإِن كُنَّ نِسَآءٗ فَوۡقَ ٱثۡنَتَيۡنِ** الخ). Even if they are only two, it is this same ruling indeed that applies. In the 186th verse *[TN: the print has "186-ാം വചനത്തിൽ" ("in the 186th verse"), clear on a 300 dpi crop. On p. 955 the author names the third verse on inheritance as "the last verse of the surah", and on p. 962 he refers to "the 176th verse" on 'kalalah', where the right of brothers and sisters is made like the right of daughters; 186 is evidently a slip for 176.]* it is stated that two sisters have two thirds. When two sisters obtain two thirds, two daughters must, surely, obtain that much in any case. This can also be understood from the incident of the wife of Sa'd ibn Rubayyi', quoted above. Moreover, the scholars have no difference of opinion on this subject.

(3) If the children are male and female, it must be divided in the measure of half the male's to the female. If there are other holders of fixed shares, they must so divide and take the remainder after deducting theirs; if not, the whole property.

(4) When there are children - whether they be male or female, one or more - the share of the mother and of the father will be one sixth (1/6) each. But, while there is no son, in case something remains after deducting that of the other sharers, the father will obtain that too. The mother will obtain only one sixth.

(5) In case there is no child, nor more than one person who are sisters *[DOUBT: printed സഹോദരികളായ ("who are sisters"), clear on the image; item (6) and the verse (സഹോദരങ്ങൾ, إِخۡوَةٞ) speak of brothers and sisters together, so സഹോദരങ്ങളായ ("who are siblings") may have been meant. Options: "sisters" as printed / "brothers or sisters".]*, the right of the mother will be one third (1/3).

(6) If there are more than one person as brothers and sisters, what the mother will have will be only one sixth (1/6). On both these occasions, in case something remains after deducting that of the holders of fixed shares, the father will also obtain it (as ‘asabah). Besides these - on the basis of this verse and the related Hadiths - the scholars have described many other rulings too in the chapter of inheritance.

Only the property that remains after deducting, if the deceased person has made some wasiyyah, that too, and, if there is any debt that he is bound to pay off, that too, may the heirs divide and take. The importance of wasiyyah, and many matters that have to be known concerning it, have gone by in Al-Baqarah 180 and in the interpretation. The Hadith in which the Prophet ﷺ declared that wasiyyah must not be made exceeding one third of the total property, we have also quoted there. The state of debt is not that. The debt must be paid in full from the property. In case nothing remains after deducting the debt, the heirs will also not obtain anything. Only in case someone else is ready to pay off the debt is there a way not to pay it from the property. It is also to debt indeed that priority must be given over wasiyyah. Dividing and taking the property without heeding the debt and the wasiyyah will be culpable, and a seizing, as wrongdoing, of the right of others.

*[p. 959]* After describing many laws, Allah says: ‘Among your fathers and children, you do not know who are nearer to you in benefit (**ءَابَآؤُكُمۡ وَأَبۡنَآؤُكُمۡ**... **لَكُمۡ نَفۡعٗا**). Yes, only Allah knows that. It is not possible for you to fix the right of each one on that basis. It is for that reason that He Himself has fixed for you the right of all. You will not be able to see and understand the secrets of wisdom contained in each one: such things are, on the whole, the gist of what has been said here. In what was said as fathers, besides the direct fathers, grandfathers too are included; and in what was said as children, the direct children and the children of the children are included. What is meant by benefit may be worldly benefit and benefit of the Hereafter. There is no need to describe the benefit obtained in this world. Some will be good, ones who spend wealth only on good matters, and ones who pray for the good of the deceased. It has been stated in 13:23 and 52:21 that Allah will gather together, along with the righteous who are dwellers of Paradise, the persons of good conduct among their fathers, offspring and wives. These are all examples of the good things of the Hereafter.

We saw before, in the explanation of Al-Baqarah 180, that, before the laws of inheritance came down, making some wasiyyah to parents was compulsory. Ibn 'Abbas (r) has stated thus: ‘What there was (before) was the wealth to the children and the wasiyyah to the parents. Then Allah did ‘naskh’ (abrogated) of it what He willed. Thus He made the share of two females for the male. He fixed for the parents, for each one, one sixth and one third; for the wife, one fourth and one eighth; for the husband, half and one fourth.’ (Bukhari)

It was said before, surely, that according to the Jahiliyyah system the right of property used to be given only to the grown-up sons. When all this is looked at together - as Ibn Kathir (r) and others have pointed out - the main hint contained in that sentence will be that it is because some secrets of wisdom are contained in it that firm and exact shares have now been fixed for all the fathers, children and the like, who had no exact rights of share at all in the right of property - none of that will you be able to grasp -. The two sentences that follow can also be seen strengthening this matter once more.

It is Allah who fixed that the share of such and such persons is in such and such a manner, more for such and such persons, less for others, and so on. Nobody has the authority and the right to bring about a change in it. Nor will it be proper. The reason is that very fact that He is the All-Knowing, who knows every matter, and the Profoundly Knowing, who knows all the secrets of wisdom. Yes .... **فَرِيضَةٗ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ** (A determination of shares from Allah! Surely, He is All-Knowing and Profoundly Knowing).

*[p. 960]* Some bearers of Muslim names can be seen in this time who argue that the order of inheritance in Islam is only some reforms that suited the circumstances of the Arabs of the time when the Quran was sent down, and that some changes that accord with reason and suit the changing times are needed in it. Although what is in reality behind this is the intoxication of materialism and lack of faith in the Quran and in Islam, it is in fact a planned excuse that they have found out, because they have a lack of courage to say that openly. Those two sentences that Allah said at the end are indeed sufficient for anyone who has real faith in the Quran and in Islam to see a fitting reply to this argument.

*[Verse 12]*

*[p. 961]* After describing the share of right of the parents and of the children, He is describing the rights of wife and husband and others. The rulings contained in this verse can be summarised thus:

*[p. 962]* (1) When a woman dies, if she has no children, the right of her husband will be half, and if there are children, one fourth.

(2) When a man dies, if he has no children, the right of the wife will be one fourth, and if there are children, one eighth - half of the husband's -. Even if a person has more than one wife, there is no change in this. That share must be divided equally among them. As said before, the distinction whether the children are one or more, male or female, will not be considered here either. It need only be looked at whether the deceased person has children or not. Even if those children are ones born in another marriage, it is the same. When there are no children of his own, even if there are children of the sons, the state is this same one.

(3) When a person dies, if his father or children are not alive, **كَلَٰلَةً** (kalalah) is said about him. What is meant is distant family. When it comes about like this, if he has a brother or a sister with the same mother, they have the right to one sixth. If they are more than one, all of them together will obtain one third. As far as they are concerned, the male and the female have the same position. Their share may be divided among them equally.

It is only in the form of ‘kalalah’ that the right has been fixed for brothers and sisters with only the mother in common. It has not been made clear here concerning these brothers and sisters that they must be ones with the same mother. But below, in the 176th verse, it is stated again about ‘kalalah’. It has been made clear there that the right of brothers and sisters is just like the right of daughters. Then, that can only be intending the brothers and sisters who come as ‘asabah - those with both mother and father in common, or those with the father in common -. Therefore it becomes clear that what is meant by the brothers and sisters mentioned here will be those with only the mother in common. In this matter there is no difference of opinion among the interpreters of the Quran.

One condition made clear once in the previous verse - the matter that the property may be divided only after deducting, if the deceased has made some wasiyyah, that too, and, if he has a debt, that too - Allah has reminded three times in this verse, repeating it along with each ruling. It is certain that it will be because there is a likelihood that, owing to the greed of men for wealth, that matter will often not be heeded, that Allah awakens to it again and again. Even so, the experience is that the people do not heed it as much as is needed. This is a subject that every heir who is a true believer must earnestly take to heart. Notice also that at the end it has further been said: in a manner in which mutual harm is not caused (**غَيۡرَ مُضَآرّٖ**). Its meaning is, surely, that in the matter of the wasiyyah or in the matter of the debt, no occasion must arise for anyone to do harm to anyone. Although this matter is applicable *[p. 963]* everywhere, there is a reason for specially mentioning it here. It is when there are no near relatives of the deceased that it becomes ‘kalalah’. When it comes about that among the heirs there are none having a close relation, there will often be property owners who do harm to the distant heirs, by making some wasiyyah, or by bringing a debt upon the property by way of some dealings of buying and selling, in order to prevent them from obtaining their property. On the contrary, it is also natural for the distant heirs, who have no close relation with the deceased person, to neglect, in the intoxication of the right that falls to them, the liabilities that have to be met from that property. These are, surely, all matters seen in experience daily. The distant relatives will not have as much eagerness in observing a person's wasiyyah and in paying off his debt as his fathers and children have. It will be taking all this into account that Allah, who knows best the psychology of man, has stated here one more condition like this, and has repeated the matter of the wasiyyah and the debt. **الله اعلم**

Abu Hurairah (r) quotes that the Rasul ﷺ declared: ‘A man, or a woman, keeps working in obedience to Allah for sixty years. Then death draws near to him. Then he causes harm in the wasiyyah. Thus, Hell becomes established for them.’ Continuing, Abu Hurairah (r) also recited these sentences beginning with **مِنۢ بَعۡدِ وَصِيَّةٖ**. (Da; Ti; Ja) In a riwayah that Imam Ahmad (r) has quoted, this idea can be seen explained in more detail;

Persons who have no near heirs, or who, even if there are, do not like them to obtain their property for some personal reason, must pay great attention to the matters stated above. Charities, wasiyyahs, waqfs, transfers: none of these that they carry out with the intention that the heirs must be prevented or deprived will be acceptable with Allah. They must remember that, even if outwardly - or in the view of the law - they are considered good deeds, with Allah all that will be evil deeds. This can be understood if the last part of the previous verse and of this verse is just calmly taken to heart once. Many Hadiths can be seen forbidding the doing of wrong in making a wasiyyah and in carrying it into effect, and the making of a wasiyyah too, besides that, for those who obtain inheritance. Some have also gone by in Surat al-Baqarah.

After giving all the laws and the counsels and directions seen above, Allah says again: All this is a wasiyyah from Allah (**وَصِيَّةٗ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ**); Allah is One who knows everything, He is very Forbearing (**وَٱللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَلِيمٞ**), and so on. Concerning the gravity contained in these sentences and *[p. 964]* their breadth of meaning, all who have good sense and true faith can more or less come to know by reflecting. All this is in order that the true believers may not transgress the bounds of Allah's laws. Look at what Allah says again, continuing, without stopping even with this much:-
