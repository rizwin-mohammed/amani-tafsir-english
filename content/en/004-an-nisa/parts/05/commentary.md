*[Verses 13–14]*

*[p. 965]* These verses are clear even without the help of an interpretation or an explanation. This is a heavy warning to those who violate and overstep the bounds of law stated above, and a great promise to those who observe them as is needed. Besides, Allah also makes known by means of this that accepting the interpretations that the noble Rasul ﷺ - by his word and by his deed - has given and shown for the words of Allah and for the laws is compulsory. Violating the laws of Allah is, surely, abasing them. That is why He has specially warned that for it there is an abasing punishment. This heavy warning affects in general those who do not rightly observe the order of property-right that Allah has fixed, and in particular those who come onto the stage with revisionism in it.

### Section – 3

*[Verses 15–16]*

*[p. 966]* Although more than one interpretation can be seen given to these verses, the majority opinion is that the first verse is about women who commit fornication, and that what is meant by vile conduct (**ٱلۡفَٰحِشَةَ**) is fornication. This opinion also has the support of Hadith. Many of the existing Islamic laws have been put into effect gradually, without bringing them into effect at one stroke. They say that this is one among them; that what is stated in this is a temporary punishment for fornication before the penal law of the crime of fornication was sent down in Surat al-Nur; and that with the punishment in Surat al-Nur coming into effect, the period of this punishment ended. According to this, the gist of this verse will be thus: If women among the true believers commit fornication, and it is proved by means of four witnesses, they must be kept in house detention, without allowing them to go outside, until their lifetime ends by means of death - or else until Allah establishes another way out that has to be adopted concerning them -. Later, with the permanent penal measure for fornication coming down, that law came into effect, and the law of house detention became obsolete. The Prophet ﷺ has also declared, saying ‘Allah has made a way for them’, that that is the penal measure of fornication. (Mu; Da; Ja; Ti) Since the words of the ayah themselves make known that this is not a permanent law and that it was a temporary law, there is not seen much relevance in this verse being interpreted as a permanent penal law for unnatural *[p. 967]* vile conducts that are not fornication. **الله اعلم**

The opinion of many is that the second verse is intending the women and men who adopt forbidden ways for the satisfaction of sexual passion. There are other opinions too, that it is about men who do unnatural vile conduct, and so on. (Allah knows) The purport of His having said to torment them both - the one who does the vile conduct and the one to whom it is done - is beating, rebuking, disgracing and the like. Besides keeping in house detention being a kind of punishment, it is, surely, also removing the opportunity for being caught in such evil conducts. Whether these verses are intending fornication, or intending other unnatural vile conducts that are not fornication, nobody has any dispute that their crime is great. Even so, Allah has made known that, in case they turn back, sincerely regretting *[DOUBT: മനഃപൂർവ്വം ഖേദിച്ച്: "sincerely (wholeheartedly) regretting" / "deliberately (intentionally) regretting"; മനഃപൂർവ്വം commonly means "deliberately".]*, and become good by doing good deeds, no measure need be taken against them afterwards, and that Allah will accept their regret. In the next verse, He describes what kind of repentance it is that Allah will accept:-

*[Verses 17–18]*

*[p. 968]* These two are verses that must be taken to heart very seriously *[TN: the print has ഗൗരപൂർവ്വം, evidently a slip for ഗൗരവപൂർവ്വം ("seriously").]*. To happen to do evil deeds because of thoughtlessness and foolishness is a nature innate to man. But Allah will not fail to accept the repentance of those who, after doing culpable things, turn back from it, regretting, without delay. On the contrary, the repentance of those who keep doing crimes and, when death comes, turn back expressing regret, and of those who die being disbelievers, will not be acceptable with Allah either: this indeed is the gist of what has been stated in these verses. We have stated before, surely, that the real meaning of the word **تَوْبَةً**, to which the meaning ‘repentance’ has been given, is ‘return, regret’ and the like, and that, as regards Allah, what is meant by return is accepting repentance.

What Allah said is not that only the repentance of those who do wrong owing to foolishness and repent immediately will be accepted - it is indeed that only the accepting of the repentance of such people is an obligation on Allah -. Then, of those who are not like that - those who deliberately do *[p. 969]* wrong and do not repent immediately itself - Allah may accept the repentance, or may not accept it. The purport indeed is that both will happen according to His will. Allah has, surely, promised that all sins other than associating partners with Him (**شرك**) are forgivable, and that He will accept the repentance from them from those whom Allah wills. (See verses 48 and 116, and verses such as 3 : 135). Ibn 'Umar (r) has also quoted thus that the Prophet ﷺ declared: ‘Surely, so long as death has not reached the throat-pit of the servant (man), Allah will accept his repentance.’ (A; Ti; Ja; Ha) But it need not be said that repentance as quickly as possible will become more acceptable. Allah has also clearly declared that regret at the time of death is of no benefit, and that He will not forgive the sins of those who die as disbelievers.

*[Verse 19]*

*[p. 970]* What was said as inheriting women by compulsion indicates a custom that there was in the Jahiliyyah. If a man died, they used to reckon his widowed wife as though she were an inherited property with which his heirs could do anything as they liked. Neither she nor her family has the power of control in her matter. This verse forbids such customs. Ibn 'Abbas (r) says: ‘When a man died, those who had more right concerning his wife were his managers. If they wished, some of them would marry her. If they wished, they would also give her in marriage (to someone else). If they wished, they would also remain without marrying *[DOUBT: വിവാഹം കഴിക്കാതെയുമിരിക്കും, literally "would also remain without marrying"; it may mean that they neither married her themselves nor gave her in marriage ("would leave her unmarried").]*. Thus, the right concerning her was theirs, more than her own people. It is on this subject that this verse came down.’ (Bu; Da; Na.) Other riwayahs too can be seen about this, with some slight differences.

Continuing, Allah forbids by means of this verse another evil custom too. That is, if a man comes to have hatred or dislike for his wife, to cause her hardship, without either treating her well or sending her away in a good manner. Thus, she comes to be in a state of being neither married nor unmarried. The need behind this, for its part, will be to get remitted the whole or some part of the mahr and the like that he has given her - that he is due to give -. When she is distressed, she will, surely, become compelled to remit or give up something in order to obtain release from the bond of marriage. This evil custom is a wrongdoing that is going on even today among some Muslims, just as it was in the time of Jahiliyyah. It is pointing out the aim behind this wrongdoing that Allah says ‘You must not keep them obstructed in order that you may take away some of what you have given them’ (**لَا تَعۡضُلُوهُنَّ لِتَذۡهَبُواْ** الخ).

But Allah has also pointed out that, in case they commit some clear vile conduct, there is no objection to keeping them obstructed so. The opinion of many is that what is meant here by vile conduct (**فَٰحِشَةٖ**) is fornication. Another opinion indeed is that not only fornication, but clear *[p. 971]* evil conducts such as bad character, disobedience and estrangement are also included. It is this opinion that Ibn Jarir (r) has approved. Ibn Kathir (r) too has said that this is the good opinion. Some have also opined that theft too is included in it. (See Tafsir al-Manar) In any case, the evil conduct must be so plain that it can be clearly known. It is not enough that it be an accusation based on conjecture or subject to emotion; many have pointed out here that, when some evil conduct seen above clearly appears from her - if there are no other ways - he may make his conduct towards her narrow so as to induce her to ‘khul'’ (divorce in return for a compensation). (Remember what has been described in 2 : 229 about ‘khul'’ - **خُلع** -).

After forbidding both the said evil customs, the sentence **وَعَاشِرُوهُنَّ بِٱلۡمَعۡرُوفِ** (And you must live with them according to the propriety of good conduct) is indeed a great piece of good counsel that becomes the foundation of the conjugal, domestic and family successes of wives and husbands. The gist is that one must behave well, as far as one is able, in speech, in deed and in conduct, in all of them indeed. The Prophet ﷺ says: ‘The best among you will be the one among you who is best to his household.’ (Ti; Ja.) Allah too has said, surely, (2:228): ‘Just as there is an obligation on them - on the wives - in this direction, so there is an obligation to them in that direction too, according to propriety’. For those who know about the domestic life of the noble Prophet ﷺ, much example can be seen in this matter.

It is usual for some bad traits innate to women to show themselves in women. If not one fault, another fault will be in most women. Sometimes, bad traits that surpass women's will be in men too. Thus, it is natural for husbands to come to have hatred and displeasure towards wives. What Allah points out in the last sentence is that the way of remedy for this is patience and forbearance, and that it is that which will end up beneficially in the future. As soon as dislike and hatred arise towards wives, one must keep them forbearingly, without taking a retaliatory measure for it, or hurrying to give divorce. If you do so, it may come about that you experience by it many benefits that you could not envisage beforehand: this indeed is the gist of what Allah said. In reality, we must keep in mind this counsel of Allah not only in the matter of wives, but in the matters of children, neighbours, relatives and friends, and the like, all. This is one that must be remembered especially concerning women. A woman, even if she is little in beauty, may be one of good character. Or she may be one capable of guarding the husband's house and property and honour and respectability, all. Even if one woman is less capable of managing household affairs, she may be one who does not depart from his bidding, and one very attentive in the moral spheres of him and of the *[p. 972]* children. Thus, even if there is some one fault, there may also be good in her more than it. It is that which the Prophet ﷺ points out in a Hadith: ‘A believing man must not keep resentment towards a believing woman. If one trait of hers has become hateful to him, another trait of hers will be pleasing to him.’ (Mu)

*[Verses 20–21]*

*[p. 973]* What is in these verses is a severe reproach about an evil custom that was in practice among the mushriks in the time of Jahiliyyah, and that has been seen among the Muslims too in later times. When a man feels a desire to change his existing wife - or else one among his wives - and to marry a new woman in her place, to show displeasure to the existing ones, and to make his conduct bad. If he wishes, he may also make some false accusation against them. Thus, he may express the demand to get back the mahr and the like that he had given them before, and they may become compelled to it. While in former times what was demanded on such occasions was to get back what had been given before, in this time there are also not lacking wrongdoers who demand that and, beyond it, a big sum as well. Allah forbids this custom very strictly. It need not be said that wives may be divorced only when there is a fitting reason and there are no other ways out. In any case, what Allah says is indeed: if one does decide to give the existing wife divorce and send her away, that must not be by imposing on her accusations that do not exist, and by extorting wealth from her unjustly. Allah makes known that that is not only a harsh wrongdoing and a great sin, but is also a breach of contract so harsh as is not befitting humans.

You were, surely, people who, before displeasure arose between you, kept up contact, mutually agreeing, and lived together as mates in conjugal and household matters. Besides, as for the marriage transaction, you have also given them by means of it a mighty pledge that you would either keep retaining them according to propriety, or let them go in a good manner (**إِمۡسَاكُۢ بِمَعۡرُوفٍ أَوۡ تَسۡرِيحُۢ بِإِحۡسَٰنٖ**). While the facts are like this, how will you take back *[TN: the print has വങ്ങും, evidently a slip for വാങ്ങും ("take").]* the wealth you gave them? What justice is there for you in it...? So asking, Allah also reminds in the second verse that it is an abominable trait unbecoming of humans. It is reminding of this contract made by means of marriage that, on the occasion of giving in marriage, some specially remind the bridegroom of this sentence (**إِمۡسَاكُۢ بِمَعۡرُوفٍ أَوۡ تَسۡرِيحُۢ بِإِحۡسَٰنٖ**) too. In the very famous sermon that the Prophet ﷺ gave in ‘Hajjat al-Wada'’, in the course of speaking about women, he said: ‘You have received them as an amanah of Allah. And you have taken their private parts as made lawful according to the word of Allah.’ (Mu.)

*[p. 974]* The meanings of **قِنطَارٍ** (qintar), to which the meaning ‘heap’ has been given, have been described in the interpretation of Ali 'Imran 14. It has also been pointed out there that its use is mostly in the sense of abundant wealth. The purport of what Allah said is indeed that, even if a man had given his wife very much wealth as mahr, he may not take back any of it at all. But it has also been made clear in the 4th verse that, if she gives up something gladly, of her own accord, there is no objection to taking it. It is a generally accepted matter that exceeding the bounds in mahr is not good, and that what is needed is to observe moderation and propriety. Some riwayahs supporting this have also been quoted from 'A'ishah (r) and Ibn 'Abbas (r). Even so, it can be understood from this verse that it has not been laid down that mahr is allowed only up to such and such a limit at the most, and that therefore there is no right to limit it to a particular sum and confine it.

An incident that took place in the time of the Khilafah of 'Umar (r) makes this clearer. The gist of that incident, which Ahmad, Abu Ya'la (r) and others have quoted through many routes, is thus; ‘Do not exceed the bounds in the mahr of women; the Rasul ﷺ did not give his wives mahr of more than four hundred silver coins; if increasing the mahr had been a good thing, he would have given more mahr.’ Thus 'Umar (r) once said to the people in a sermon. Then, a woman stood up and said: ‘You are not allowed to forbid so.’ When 'Umar (r) asked the reason, what that woman did was to recite this (20th) verse. 'Umar (r) again mounted the pulpit, approved what that woman had said, and said that everyone knows more than 'Umar, and, correcting what he had said first, left that matter to women, to their liking. That is the incident.
