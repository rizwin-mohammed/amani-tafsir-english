*[Verses 36–37]*

*[p. 1005]* The foremost among the duties of man is, surely, to worship only Allah, who is his Creator and Lord, and not to associate anything else with Him. Or, tawhid. After the duty towards Allah, it is to his parents that man is next most indebted. Therefore, immediately after speaking about the duty towards Allah, joining it to that, He commands to do good to parents. Reminding also of the matter of doing good to parents along with stating about tawhid *[p. 1006]* can be seen in the Quran not only here - in other places too -. (See 2:83; 17:23; 31:14). It is also generally known that Allah and His Rasul have laid very great emphasis, in many Quran verses and in sayings of the Prophet, on conducting oneself well with parents and on not displeasing them. It may be said in sum that giving them every kind of help, cooperation and service they need, fulfilling their wishes for them, not doing anything at all that is disagreeable to them, behaving towards them with respect and propriety, speaking only good words, and the like, are the duties that children must do towards parents. After speaking about parents, He then commands to do good also to the others to whom man is mainly indebted, and specifies who the important ones among them are. That is:

(1) **Families of close relationship:** Doing good to families is not merely a charity; it is also the keeping of the family bond. One's own brothers and sisters, the brothers and sisters of the parents, their children, and the like, are all included in this.

(2) **Orphans:** It is well known that emphasis has been laid about them in this surah and in many other places in the Quran, and in many Hadiths.

(3) **The poor:** Or, the needy who have no means to get through their bare necessities.

(4) **Neighbours of close relationship:** Or, those who, besides neighbourhood, are closer either by family relationship, religious relationship and the like, or by nearness of the house.

(5) **Distant neighbours:** Those who do not have the special relationship stated above, and neighbours who are not the people of the next house, are included in this.

Very many Hadiths can be seen about the proprieties of neighbourhood. ‘Whoever believes in Allah and in the Last Day, let him do good to the neighbour.’ is what the Prophet ﷺ has said in a Hadith. (Bu; Mu). From this one single Hadith itself, the importance Islam has attached to the proprieties of neighbourhood can be understood. When the son of a Jew who was a neighbour lay in sickness, the Prophet ﷺ went to visit him in his sickness. Ibn 'Umar (r) says that he heard the Prophet ﷺ declare: ‘Jibril (a) kept on making wasiyyah to me in the matter of the neighbour, so much that I feared he might even give (him) the right of property.’ (Bu; Mu). It is also reported in riwayahs that for this reason, when a goat was slaughtered in his house, he repeatedly asked the servants to send some of it to the Jew who was his neighbour. When the estrangement that existed between the Muslims and the Jews in that period is also taken into account, the seriousness that the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions attached to the relationship of neighbourhood can be understood. Muslim (r) has quoted a *[p. 1007]* Hadith that, when cooking a curry, one must take notice of the neighbours, at least by increasing the water in it; and Ahmad and Bukhari (r) a Hadith that, if there are two neighbours, preference must be given to whichever of them has the nearer door.

In the obligation towards neighbours there is no difference between Muslim and non-Muslim. It need not specially be said that when the neighbour is also a Muslim and a relative, that obligation will be stronger.

(6) **The companion at the side:** All companions and friends who live connected with one another in travel, occupation and the like are included in this.

(7) **The wayfarer:** Travellers who have left their land and become without support, those who have come as guests, travellers through the lands with a good intention: all these are included in this.

(8) **Those whom the right hand has owned:** Or, slaves. The noble Prophet ﷺ has declared thus about slaves in a Hadith: ‘They are your brothers, and your workers. Allah has placed them under your authority. Therefore, if anyone's brother is under his authority, let him give him food from what he eats, and give him to wear from what he wears.’ (Bu; Mu). Another saying of the Prophet is thus: ‘If his worker brings food to one of you, in case he does not seat him together with himself, he must give him a handful or two handfuls from it. For it is he, surely, who experienced its heat and toil (the trouble of cooking).’ (Bu; Mu.). Anas (r) has stated that even on the occasion when death was near, it was about prayer, and about slaves, that the Prophet ﷺ was mainly making wasiyyah. (A; Ba.)

Except that good must be done to all those stated above, Allah has not explained in detail in what ways all that good must be. There is no need for that either. It need not be said that what is intended is to help each group, according to their respective circumstances, needs and occasion, by word, by deed, by body and by wealth, as far as one is able. If it is examined what type those who stand most backward in this matter will be, it can be seen that they will be people in whom certain bad qualities have come together. Yes, boastfulness, arrogance and stinginess! It is from a generous state of mind, humility, sympathy, kindness to the poor and the like, that the motivation to do good to others arises. For the vain, the arrogant-minded and the habitually miserly, there is, surely, no likelihood of these qualities arising. These bad qualities lead them to another bad quality also. That is, their interest will be in others too, like themselves, not doing benefit to people. Thus, they criticise others' spending wealth on good matters, and *[p. 1008]* try to turn them back. Concealing, without letting it be known outside as far as possible, the favours such as wealth that Allah has given them will also be the habit of such people. This, for its part, is ingratitude towards Allah, ignoring His favour, and lack of faith in Him. Therefore the punishment that Allah is going to give them will also be one that humiliates and disgraces them to the utmost. (**وَأَعۡتَدۡنَا لِلۡكَٰفِرِينَ عَذَابٗا مُّهِينٗا**)

Ibn Mas'ud (r) says: The Rasul ﷺ said, ‘He in whose heart there is an atom's weight of conceit will not enter Paradise.’ Then a man asked: ‘A man likes his clothing to be good and his sandal to be good (will this be conceit?)’ Then the noble one said: ‘Surely Allah is Beautiful. He likes beauty. Conceit means defying the truth. and disregarding people.’ (Bu; Mu.) In a Hadith the Prophet ﷺ says: ‘Allah likes that the mark of the favour that He has done for His servant is seen appearing on him.’ (Ti.) Many Quran sentences and sayings of the Prophet that censure bad qualities such as stinginess, vanity, arrogance and ingratitude are well known.

*[Verse 38]*

After stating about those who are not ready to do good to others because of stinginess and the bad qualities connected with it, He states, on the contrary, about those who show no reluctance to spend wealth, and *[p. 1009]* whose aim in spending - instead of being the reward from Allah or salvation in the Hereafter - is that they should get name and fame among people. He also warns them that Allah will not like these either, that their companion is Satan, and that, if Satan has become the companion, its consequence will be extremely dangerous. It is, surely, because of the absence or the deficiency of faith in Allah and in the Last Day that the aim of spending becomes worldly. And the motivation for this is got from Satan, at that. Satan, for his part, misleads man and leads him to the punishment of Hell. (22:4)

Allah has declared unequivocally that the deeds of those who intend to win renown by doing good things in the sight of people - even if they are by nature virtuous deeds - are not acceptable with Allah, and that only deeds intending Allah's pleasure and reward will be of value with Him. In a Hadith it has come thus: The Prophet ﷺ said: ‘The most fearful of the things I fear about you is the small shirk.’ It was asked what the small shirk is. Then the noble one said: ‘It is acting in order to show people .’ (A.) It has also come in the riwayah of Bayhaqi (r) that on the Day of Qiyamah Allah will say to them: ‘Go to those whom you used to act to show in this world; and then see whether you get any reward or benefit with them.’

*[Verse 39]*

If they - those who spend wealth in order to show *[p. 1010]* people - had spent believing in Allah and in the Last Day, and intending the reward from Him on that Day, no harm whatever was going to befall them. Nothing of what they do will go unknown to Allah either. He, for His part, is not One who does any wrong or injustice at all. A good deed - however trivial it may be - He will give reward for it by doubling it and making it great. Besides, He will also give other immense rewards of His own, as His generosity. (Recall what has been explained at 2 : 245 and such places) Allah says:

*[Verses 40–42]*

*[p. 1011]* Gist: The prophets of each community will testify before Allah at the ‘Mahshar’ that they have preached, as was needed, to their respective communities the way and procedure that each community must practise and follow. The noble Prophet ﷺ will also come as witness over this community. Do these people not consider what the condition of these - of those who do not believe in Allah and in the Last Day, and spend wealth in order to convince the world, and show stinginess in spending in the way of Allah - will be on that occasion? These people, who did not give ear to the preachings of the Rasul, will be convinced there that there is no salvation for them. They will not see any way of salvation at all. ‘Alas! How good it would have been if the earth had been levelled including us too, so that none of this would have come about!’: thus they will long at that time. Yes, as has been said in Surat al-Naba', each disbeliever will say: ‘How good it would have been if I had become dust! ( **يَٰلَيۡتَنِي كُنتُ تُرَٰبَۢا** )’. On that Day, they will not be able to conceal before Allah anything at all of their evil doings and secrets, or to escape thus.

It comes in Surah An'am 23 that at the Mahshar the mushriks will say on oath, ‘We were not ones who did shirk’. To its having been asked that there is, surely, a contradiction between that and what has been stated here, ‘They will not hide any talk from Allah’, Ibn Jarir (r) and others have quoted that Ibn 'Abbas (r) gave reply thus: ‘Yes, when they see on the Day of Qiyamah that the offence that is shirk will not be forgiven at all, and that there is the likelihood that Allah will forgive the other offences, it is in the hope that they might get forgiveness that they swear that they were not mushriks. Then (as is seen at 36 : 65) Allah will set a seal on their mouths. And then their hands and feet will speak openly of all that they did. It is then that they wish, ‘How good it would have been if the earth had been levelled with them!’, and do not hide *[p. 1012]* any talk from Allah.’

It has been narrated thus that Ibn Mas'ud (r) said: The Prophet ﷺ said to me, ‘You must recite the Quran to me to hear’. I said: ‘O Rasul of Allah, shall I recite it to you while the Quran has been sent down to you?! The noble one said: ‘Yes, I like to hear it from someone other than me’. So, I recited Surat al-Nisa', and when I reached the ayah **فَكَيۡفَ إِذَا جِئۡنَا مِن كُلِّ أُمَّةِۭ بِشَهِيدٖ وَجِئۡنَا بِكَ عَلَىٰ هَـٰٓؤُلَآءِ شَهِيدٗا** (How will it be when We bring a witness from every community, and bring you as witness over these?!), he said: ‘Then, it is enough now!’ The two eyes of the noble one were shedding tears.’ (A; Bu; Ti; Na.) When the Prophet ﷺ, recalling the seriousness of that stage at which one has to give a statement in the court of Allah, the King of kings - even if it is merely in the position of a witness, not as an offender or an accused -, trembles this much in this world, what will be the condition there of the unfortunate ones against whom the charge-sheet is prepared according to his testimony, and who become subject to the punishment of Hell?! May Allah guard and save us, Amin.
