*[Verse 3]*

*[p. 933]* To grasp the purport of this verse, it is necessary to know the occasion in which it was sent down and its background. It is mainly three opinions that there are concerning this. One has been quoted from 'A'ishah (r), another from Ibn 'Abbas (r), and the third from Sa'id ibn Jubair (r) and others. The first two persons are among the prominent Sahabi scholars, and the third person is a prominent Tabi'i scholar. In that respect too, and because their statements are seen to agree very well with the wordings of the Quran, most Quran commentators can be seen quoting all these three opinions in their books. When looked at relatively, it is the opinion that 'A'ishah (r) stated that is seen to have more strength. It is for that very reason that the majority of scholars also accept it.

The first is the Hadith that many Hadith scholars such as Bukhari and Muslim (r) have recorded from 'Urwah ibn Zubair (r), the son of the sister of 'A'ishah (r). Its gist: ‘'Urwah (r), the son of her sister, asked 'A'ishah (r) about this Quran verse. She replied thus: ‘Son of my sister, an orphan girl will be under the control of her manager. She too will have a share in the wealth. Her wealth and beauty will attract him. So, he will intend to marry her. That will be without giving any mahr (marriage-price) such as is given to other women. Allah has forbidden (by means of this verse) to marry them except by keeping justice towards them and giving them mahr of a high standard.’ He has also commanded them - the managers - to marry those suited to them from the other women who are not orphans.” (Continuing, in the 127th verse, *[p. 934]* 'A'ishah (r) has also pointed out that it states about being reluctant to marry orphans because their wealth and beauty are less. We can speak about that there. (**إن شاء الله**) Other strong riwayahs of this meaning have also come.

It is intending the custom of marrying the orphans who were under their control and relation, aiming at their property or beauty, and of showing injustice to them in their rights such as mahr, that it has been said **وَإِنۡ خِفۡتُمۡ أَلَّا تُقۡسِطُواْ فِي ٱلۡيَتَٰمَىٰ** “If you fear that you will not keep due justice in the matter of the orphans”: so indeed 'A'ishah (r) points out. According to her statement, the gist of this verse will be thus: if it is seen that one may do injustice in that way, those orphans must not be married. From the free women who are not orphans, women who seem good can be married, two or three or four each - as one likes -. There are, surely, plenty of other women too. But, in case apprehension is felt that justice will not be kept there too, one woman only must be married. Otherwise, women who are in ownership - who are slaves - must be taken. That is the good way so that wrongdoing and injustice may not happen. Some references in the 127th verse, and the direction that comes in the next verse that reluctance and stinginess must not be shown in the matter of giving the mahr of women, can be seen to give support to this interpretation.

The gist of what is quoted from Ibn 'Abbas (r) is as follows: ‘(Before Islam) in the Jahiliyyah, a man used to marry ten or more women. And they used to take pride in it. So, when the wealth in hand ran out, they would use the property of the orphans under their control for the expenses of those wives. Therefore, what this verse does is to fix a limit to marriage.’ This is the gist of what Ibn Jarir (r) and others have recorded from him. According to the interpretation of 'A'ishah (r), what has been said as **مِّنَ ٱلنِّسَآءِ** ‘from the women’ is intending the women who are not orphans. According to this interpretation of Ibn 'Abbas (r), orphans and those who are not are both included in it.

The gist of the quotations from Sa'id ibn Jubair (r), Qatadah (r) and others is this: ‘Doing injustice in the wealth of orphans was a hated thing even in the Jahiliyyah period. But they did not have that hatred in showing injustice in the matter of other women. A man will marry ten or more women. And he will not keep justice towards them. Therefore, He has commanded that if you have fear of showing injustice to orphans, likewise you must not show injustice to the other women too, and that therefore only four persons may be married, and that if it is seen that even then justice cannot be kept, one must be content with one - or else one must be content with slave *[p. 935]* women -.’ This is what Ibn Jarir, Ibn Abi Hatim (r) and others have quoted. It is to this interpretation that Ibn Jarir (r) is seen giving preference. Nevertheless, it is the interpretation of 'A'ishah (r), quoted first, that in many respects seems reasonable and more acceptable, and that the majority of scholars are seen to have accepted.

In this recent time a scholar is seen to have said that there is a possibility for an interpretation like this too: ‘If you are not able to show due justice to the orphans, marry those women with whom those orphans are.’ This is his word. He is also not seen to have explained what the correct intent of what he said is. In any case, we do not understand the agreement between the sentence structure of the ayah and this statement.

A scholar who is fond of modernity has recently given this verse an interpretation that is very strange, and that no scholar up to this time is seen to have held: ‘This is a context that speaks about the care of orphans, not a context that speaks about matters of marriage; it is because of not looking at that and because of not remembering the importance that Islam assigns to the care of orphans that many have been caught in a blunder here; his own interpretation is the straightforward interpretation, all the rest are ones made by bending and twisting - the gist of that interpretation, which he presented establishing his own scholarship with such words, is like this: ‘If there is fear that you will not keep justice in the care of orphans, you must marry two or three or four women, as required, who seem good to you - who are capable of the care of orphans -, and make them live together with you. If it is seen that here too you will not keep justice, you must marry only one woman. If there is no ability for that, you must marry at least one slave woman for this need.’ This is the sum total of the statement he made, stretching it out very greatly. That is, it is as a means suitable for carrying out the care of orphans that it has been directed that up to four wives may be married, or that a slave woman may be married. The gist is that, other than for this purpose, more than one wife must not be taken. Before passing judgement about this new interpretation, some facts have to be remembered:-

(1) No way is seen to accept as it is the claim that this is a context that speaks about the care of orphans. In the first verse of the surah nothing at all has been stated specially about orphans. In the second verse there are some directions that its managers have to accept in the matter of managing their property. Moreover, managing property and care are not one. The third is this verse. In the two verses that follow too, what there is are not matters that affect only orphans. That being so, in saying that the context here is one that describes the care of orphans, *[p. 936]* there is not much substance. Nevertheless, if the interpretations seen above are examined, which have been quoted from the eminent Sahabis and from the Tabi'i leaders, who had more facility, knowledge and capability than these people for understanding the context and the idea of the Quran verses, and which the scholars who are experts in Quran interpretation have been accepting, - in one way or in another way - all of them indeed are, moreover, ones connected with the matter of orphans.

(2) His claim that it was because of not understanding that it is a context that speaks about the care of orphans, and supposing that this is a context that speaks about matters of marriage, that all those before met with a blunder, has gone somewhat excessive. It is clear from their statements that none of the great ones before supposed so, and that what they have said are only some interpretations that connect the matter of marriage and the matter of orphans with each other. To tell the truth, this new interpretation is one that has sprung from the eagerness to interpret some doctrines and laws of Islam in the name of the desire for progress and in a manner suited to modern interests. It is as a foundation for it that there is the claim that none of those before understood the context of this verse, and that it is he who has now been able to understand it. That monogamy is the accepted policy of Islam; that it is only that a concession has been permitted, that up to four wives may be married in unavoidable emergency situations; that, moreover, that too is with a condition - which, according to these people, is mostly not humanly possible - of keeping justice among the wives: it is such things that some modern-minded people of today are used to establishing. (We can criticise this later.) Going one step further forward in this, he has here added one more condition as his own contribution. That is, it is that, if more than one person is to be married, it must be for caring for orphans, and the women who are married must be capable of it. When all this is seen, it would seem that nobody before him had understood the importance of caring for orphans! Wonder!!

(3) Let those who have good sense pass whatever judgement they like about his boast that the interpretations that those great predecessors give to this verse - who know more than these people about all of, not only the importance of the care of orphans, but the literary language of the Quran, the occasions of sending down, and the interpretations of it obtained from the Prophet ﷺ or from his practice, and who surpass these people in carefulness and in truthfulness - are twisted, and that his own interpretation is the straightforward one. To those who have become acquainted with seeing many discoveries of this kind, ‘straightforward’ in their language, like this and beyond this, nothing in this will seem novel. Rather than explaining the ideas of the Quran in the proper way, *[p. 937]* for those who maintain an interest in propagating their own ideas in the name of the Quran, it is natural that they may come to have to say things like this.

It is about the first part of this verse that we have spoken till now. In the last part, what Allah says is that, if you fear that you will not keep justice, you must either marry one woman only, or take a slave woman (**فَإِنۡ خِفۡتُمۡ أَلَّا تَعۡدِلُواْ فَوَٰحِدَةً أَوۡ مَا مَلَكَتۡ أَيۡمَٰنُكُمۡ** *[TN: the printed Arabic here begins وان ("and if") in place of فَإِنۡ ("then if") of the verse; the Quran text is given here.]*). What is intended is the keeping of justice in fulfilling the needs such as food, clothing and dwelling, which the wife is entitled to receive from the husband, and the husband-wife relations. The gist is that, if it is seen that, owing to incapability or otherwise, one will not be able to keep that, marrying more than one woman is forbidden and punishable. If, after the marriage, it is seen that justice is not being kept in these matters, the wives can also approach the rulers and seek a remedy. We are used to seeing, surely, that those who took more than one wife without heeding this condition which Allah has fixed for more-than-one marriage to be valid, and those who do not discharge that duty after the marriage, keep experiencing its bitter fruits in this very life. The punishment of the Hereafter as well, besides that! But, by its having been said that justice must be kept among the wives, it is not compulsory that it must necessarily be in the same manner towards all the wives in mental attachment and in personal love. That, surely, is also not humanly possible. It is only that one must try to keep as much equality as possible in that too. And the difference that comes about in that must not become a cause to bring about increase or decrease in the other rights. Allah states about this in the 129th verse. This is clear from the Hadith. More explanation can be seen in the proper place. **إن شاء الله**

Some people in this age have come to firmly suppose that, even if this condition that Allah has fixed - the keeping of justice among the wives - is achieved, taking more than one wife is a contemptible and uncivilised, corrupt custom. It is an idea that originated from Christian customs, gained currency through the Indo-Western cultures, and thus obtained a permanent installation in the list of ‘modern civilised ideas’. Today even anyone's saying something against it has become old-fashioned and reactionary. Even those who have been speaking criticism without ceasing about the reforms of Western culture are ones who, in the field of practice, generally imitate them blindly. Many of today's people of progressive ideas also make efforts to give an Islamic colouring to the said idea. Owing to not grasping duly about the Quran and the Sunnah, and to unfamiliarity with the Islamic sciences, many who are common people also get intoxicated by their eloquent styles.

The gist of the statements that these people are used to making on this subject is this: *[p. 938]* ‘The policy of Islam is monogamy. Polygamy(\*) it utterly hates. But, when a circumstance with no other way out is faced, the Quran has given a concession that up to four at the maximum may be married. That, moreover, is with a condition that mostly cannot be attained - the stipulation of keeping justice among the wives -. At the same time, the Quran itself (4:129) also states that you will not be able to keep justice among women. Then, in effect, the policy of the Quran is monogamy.’ It is like this that the course of these people's argument goes. Some among these very people would also be seen saying thus; ‘Monogamy is the real doctrine of Islam. But, because unrestricted polygamy had been current among the Arabs, the Quran did not suddenly declare it forbidden. What it did, for the time being, by means of this verse, was to limit it to four with some conditions. It is only a permission in the special surroundings of that time; that ruling is not applicable to later times.’ Both these arguments are nothing other than an encroachment against the Quran and Islam. If one ventures upon a detailed criticism of them in the light of the sources, of history and of reason, because that would become very long, and because many scholars have issued statements and writings explaining the true state of things in this, it is not intended to speak much here. Only some general hints may be given:-

> **Note:** (\*) What is meant by ‘polygamy’ is taking more than one wife. It does not have the meaning of taking very many wives, as that word suggests.

(1) No evidence is obtained from religious sources, or from the practices of the prophets, to establish that marrying more than one wife is by its nature a bad practice. Not only that, not even one hint that praises monogamy or that censures polygamy will be seen in the Quran or in the Hadith. If there were anything, these people would, surely, search it out and bring it. Bukhari (r) records that Ibn 'Abbas (r) asked Sa'id ibn Jubair (r), ‘Have you married?’, and that, when he replied ‘No’, Ibn 'Abbas (r) said thus: ‘Then you must marry; because the best person of this community was a person who had more wives.’ It was intending the Prophet ﷺ that Ibn 'Abbas (r) said this. If having more than one wife were merely undesirable, or something belonging to a low character, Ibn 'Abbas (r) would never have said so. Allah Himself has made it clear in Suratul Ahzab that it is a permission that Allah gave specially to the noble Prophet ﷺ. Although each marriage of the Prophet ﷺ was on account of a particular reason each, if having more than one wife were in reality a bad custom, how would Allah permit that bad custom as a special feature of His Prophet?!

*[p. 939]* (2) Many things have to be considered, such as physical and domestic conditions, social and civic surroundings, and circumstances in which women exceed in the population. When looked at in that way, it is in every way good that at least some abandon the vow of one wife and take up polygamy. When one thinks with a moral, familial and ethical outlook and with practical sense, its necessity also increases still more.

(3) It is an undeniable truth that it is in the lands that most condemn polygamy that moral and ethical anarchy holds sway more. The sight of people being compelled, for the reason that there is no way to take a second wife, to carry on intercourse with other women, and of life gradually becoming like that of animals, with humanity perishing, is plentiful in those places today. But, in the circumstance of this age, in which only fornication that is committed by force is reckoned as an offence, in which recognition is given to man-woman relations that take place with mutual consent, and in which the licentiousness that holds sway under sweet names such as love - affection - art is counted as signs of civilisation, it may not be that many people are found to admit all these realities. But those who have reason and conscience know that, by the currency a vile practice gets among the people, it does not become something that is not a vile practice.

(4) In a surrounding in which there were no laws or rules that were against polygamy or that controlled it, and in which it was widely current, what the Quran does is only to control it, limiting it to four, because of the evil results that were happening among the community owing to it. What the Quran did was not to bring a new law of polygamy into a surrounding in which only monogamy was in practice, nor to uproot a custom that was in existence. The chief among those evil results is indeed the not keeping of justice among the wives. It also insists that, in case apprehension is felt that that may happen, only one wife is permitted.

(5) The condition that Allah set, that one must not be in fear that one will not keep justice, - as these people say - is not at all a ruse that Allah made in order to abolish it in effect, along with permitting polygamy outwardly. Nor is it an imaginary condition that man cannot put into practice. Although keeping justice completely in every way is not humanly possible, what Allah has fixed is only that justice must be kept in the matters in which one is able. (More information about this can be seen in the 129th verse.) **إن شاء الله**

One fact must not be forgotten here. Many among the Muslims have been marrying more than one woman without heeding this condition that Allah has fixed. Those who violate that condition after the marriage are also plentiful. This is *[p. 940]* not the fault of the Quran or of Islam. It is one among the many limits of Islam's law that many among the Muslims are today violating. For that they will surely have to answer before Allah.

(6) In the circumstance of the time when the Quran was sent down, the law was that; because that circumstance is not there today, polygamy is not desirable today: about babblings such as these, for the time being, there is only this much to say: it is only a desire that religious values and religious laws must keep being changed in accordance with the course of time and the will of the people. These people are used to saying some such things like this in many other matters too, such as interest, purdah and inheritance. No need is felt to speak anything more here concerning this.

(7) In case the polygamy that Islam has permitted - observing its rules, stipulations and proprieties duly - were accepted, and no obstacles were created to it legally or by policy, the very tyrannical reign of the moral and sexual anarchy that is holding sway in the world today would disappear.

What was said, that if you fear that you will not keep justice among women you should marry one woman, and what was said, that when that is not so two each or three each or four each may be, are concerning women who are free. It is for that reason that, after that, it has been said ‘or what your right hands have owned’. What is meant by this is slave women. Since nothing has been stated about the number of slave women, and since the word **مَا مَلَكَتۡ** “what have owned” can be used for one and for a greater number, what is understood is that there is no objection to the number of slave women exceeding four. There is no evidence for the claim, as some interested parties say, that a slave woman may be taken only when one becomes unable to marry even one free woman. The taking of slave women is in two ways. One, by marriage itself. The other, to take those who are in one's own ownership as consorts. The child will also be free. Afterwards she must not be sold. With his death - if she has not been freed before that - she also becomes free of herself. When looked at thus, this too is one way among the various ways that Islam has adopted for the slave woman to become free.

It is noteworthy that, after saying that, if fear is felt that you will not keep justice among the wives, you must take one wife or slave women, it has been said **ذَٰلِكَ أَدۡنَىٰٓ أَلَّا تَعُولُواْ** “That is more suited so that you may not deviate from order”. In either case, what Allah reminds by means of this is indeed that His commanding to be content with one free woman, or else with slave women, is for your own good.

*[Verse 4]*

The marriage-price which the man must give to the woman at the time of marriage, and which is known by the name mahr (**مهر**), is indeed what is also called **صدقة** (saduqah). Sometimes it is also called **صداق** (sadaq). It is in the meaning of a voluntary gift, which is usually given to children or others without expecting any recompense, that **نحلة** (nihlat) is commonly used. *[p. 942]* Taking into account the linguistic background of this word, the meaning ‘formal obligatory gift’ has also been assigned to it. It was stated, surely, in the last verse, that justice must be kept in the matter of orphans and of wives. Following that, it is about the mahr, which deserves a chief place among the rights of women, that this verse has described.

Allah commands that the mahr of women must be given to them as a voluntary gift that is given with joy - without any reluctance or stinginess at all -. This is a command that affects husbands who, even when the mahr has been fixed, show unwillingness to hand it over at the proper time and in the proper form, and the managers who hold it back without handing it over to the women. It is seen in some riwayahs that both these customs were usual before, in the Jahiliyyah period. It is also a custom that can be seen in many today. The custom of not paying off the mahr until one of the wife and husband dies and parts, and of telling its account only when considering the inheritance matters of the one who died, and the custom of heeding the mahr only when parting after falling out with each other, are in practice among many today. It can be understood from this verse that all this is culpable; that the mahr fixed at the time of marriage must be paid off to them with an open heart, even without its being demanded from the women's side; and that the husband or the managers (the wali) may not dispose of it without their complete consent. But - as some people are used to supposing - paying off the whole mahr even before husband-wife contact takes place between the woman and the man, or at the very time when the marriage rite takes place, is not an obligatory law. This can be understood from the verses 236, 237 of Sura: Al-Baqarah too.

Allah also makes known that, although paying off the mahr is a very strict obligation, if women, of their own mind, being satisfied, give up anything from it, or give it back, there is no objection at all in accepting it, and that no reluctance need be had in that.
