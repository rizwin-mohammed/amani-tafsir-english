*[Verse 1]*

**يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ ٱتَّقُواْ رَبَّكُمُ** (O mankind, guard against your Lord): this is the first of the two surahs that begin with this sentence. The other is Suratul Hajj. What is described next here is about the origin of the human race; there, for its part, it is about the Last Day. This indicates that, whether the origin and beginning of men is looked at, or their end and return is looked at, they have no Lord other than Allah, and that *[p. 927]* therefore guarding against Him is a duty that man has by nature.

The word **خَلقٌ** (creating) is customarily used in the meaning of giving existence from nothingness, and in the meaning of giving form to another thing from one thing. What has been said as **خَلَقَ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضَ** (He created the heavens and the earth) is in the first meaning. This is clear from its also having been said about this very thing in another place: **بَدِيعُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ** (the One who made the heavens and the earth newly - 2 : 117). What has been said here, **خَلَقَكُم مِّن نَّفۡسٖ وَٰحِدَةٖ** (they *[TN: the print has അവർ ("they"), evidently a slip for അവൻ ("He").]* created you from one and the same soul - person -), is intending creation in the second meaning. As Imam Razi (r) has stated. There is no difference of opinion among the Muslims that what is meant by this **نفس** (soul) is the Prophet Adam (a), the father of mankind. It is, surely, with the address **يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ** (O mankind), which includes the human community as a whole, that Allah reminds of this subject. It is for that reason too that, intending the whole human community, it is customarily said in the Quran and elsewhere in this way: **يَٰبَنِيٓ ءَادَمَ** (O children of Adam). That man is called **ادمى** (one belonging to the Adam kind) is also for that very reason. After saying that Allah created and formed you, who are men, from one and the same soul, He has also pointed out how He brought it into practice. Yes, **وَخَلَقَ مِنۡهَا زَوۡجَهَا وَبَثَّ مِنۡهُمَا** الخ (and He created its mate from it itself, and spread from the two of them many men and women). It need not be said, surely, that what is meant by the mate is Hawwa' (a), the mother of mankind, and that it is from the union of the mates who are men and women that man has been multiplying by giving birth.

Allah has made it clear (in 3:59 and elsewhere) that it was from earth that He created the Prophet Adam (a). Although nothing has been stated here about his origin, it has been made clear that it was from him that his mate was created. The meaning of the sentence **وَخَلَقَ مِنۡهَا زَوۡجَهَا** (and He created its mate from that soul) is, surely, that He created and formed the mate of that soul, which is Adam, from it itself. But there are no evidences fit to state definitely how the mate was created from him. What the Bible (Genesis : 2, 21 - 23) says is that, when Adam (a) was created, since there was no mate suited to him, Allah gave him a deep sleep, and that, while he was sleeping, He took one of his ribs and created that bone into a woman. It is not possible to establish this taking the Bible's statement as the basis. Because there is no means of discerning the truth and the untruth in it. Nevertheless, there exists a strong saying of the Prophet which makes it appear that there is truth in what has been said. Giving a wasiyyah to treat women in a good manner, the Prophet ﷺ says thus: *[p. 928]* **فانهن خلقن من ضلع** (رواه الشيخان) (“....Because they have been created with a rib.” (Bu: Mu.) Taking this Hadith as the basis, what the majority of scholars say is that it was from the rib of Adam (a) that Hawwa' (a) was created. What is understood when the wording of the Hadith is looked at directly is also like that. At the same time, there is also a possibility that the Prophet ﷺ stated it in the form of a simile. Look at the sentences that follow in the Hadith: **وان اعوج شيء من الضلع اعلاه**..... “In the rib, the most crooked is also what is uppermost in it. Therefore, in case you try to keep it standing straight, you will have to break it. In case you leave it to its own way, it will remain being crooked indeed.” To put it briefly, whether it be from the rib, or let it be otherwise; it can be stated definitely that it was from the person Adam (a) that Hawwa' was created. Although there is no evidence fit to state with certainty that it was from the rib, there are also no evidences at all to pass judgement that that opinion is wrong. **والله اعلم**

The one among the Quran commentators who has another opinion is Abu Muslim Isfahani (r). For the sentence **خَلَقَ مِنۡهَا زَوۡجَهَا**, what he takes as the meaning is **اى من جنسها** (that is, from its kind). Although he is one of the Quran commentators of earlier times, on the basis of some rational arguments of his own he has many isolated opinions. This is one among them. In this age, some modernity-lovers can be seen imitating this opinion of his and also presenting new arguments such as to support it. Leaving aside a few rationalist arguments that deserve criticism, the main argument they have to state, it seems, is that the word **نفس** (nafs) has been used in the Quran in the meaning **جنس** (kind). They also point out places such as 30:21, 16:72, 42:11, 9:128, 3:164 as examples for that. But, because there are defects such as are indicated below, no way is seen to accept this opinion.

(1) In all the places these people point out, what has been used is not the word **نفس** (nafs). It is its plural **انفس** (anfus). Because the meaning ‘kind’ comes when it is plural, it does not follow that the singular must have that meaning. Not only that, such a meaning is also not found for that word in the Arabic lexicons. Even if it is supposed that there is, when the singular word **نفس** means kind, the plural **انفس** ought to mean ‘kinds’, surely. It can be stated definitely that in those five examples that these people pointed out, there is no means of taking the meaning ‘kinds’. ‘Soul, life, body, individual, person, own, oneself, self, mind, blood’: these and the like are the meanings found for ‘nafs’ in the lexicons. Anyone who examines those examples can understand that none of these meanings will fit in the examples these people pointed out. *[p. 929]* In the first three examples, what Allah has said is either that He created for you mates from your ‘nafs’es themselves (**خَلَقَ لَكُم مِّنۡ أَنفُسِكُمۡ أَزۡوَٰجٗا**), or that He made for you mates from your ‘nafs’es themselves (**جَعَلَ لَكُم مِّنۡ أَنفُسِكُمۡ** الخ). What has been said in the fourth is that a Rasul has come to you from your nafses (**جَآءَكُمۡ رَسُولٞ مِّنۡ أَنفُسِكُمۡ**), and in the last that He has commissioned a Rasul from their ‘nafs’es (**بَعَثَ فِيهِمۡ رَسُولٗا مِّنۡ أَنفُسِهِمۡ**). It is clear, surely, that none of the meanings seen above will fit in these places, and that it is for that reason that ‘kind (**جنس**)’, which is the collective of ‘nafs’es, is adopted in these places as the intended meaning. No other example is seen, moreover, in any Quran sentences in which, other than in the plural form - in the singular form -, ‘nafs (**نفس**) has come, where the meaning ‘kind (**جنس**)’ has to be given, or where that meaning fits.

(2) At the same time, in another verse, **يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّاسُ إِنَّا خَلَقۡنَٰكُم مِّن ذَكَرٖ وَأُنثَىٰ وَجَعَلۡنَٰكُمۡ شُعُوبٗا وَقَبَآئِلَ** “O mankind, We have created you from one male and from one female, and We have made you many branches and tribes.” (49:13). thus Allah has also made it clear. The meaning of this, surely, is that although you, who are the human community, are many branches and tribes, it is from one male and from one female that you have all been created. To put it briefly, the idea contained in both these verses is indeed that it is from the Prophet Adam (a), the father of mankind, and from Hawwa' (a), his mate and the mother of mankind, that Allah has created and made the human race and increased it this much. The mates of every living kind are from that very kind. The import is that what both these sentences do is not to point out the fact that, likewise, the mates of men too are ones belonging to the human race.

Although those who try to interpret by assigning the meaning ‘kind’ to the word ‘nafs’ can be seen stating some other arguments as well, they are merely rational arguments. Therefore it is not intended here to lengthen this by giving them a reply of the same form. Although this subject is not one that is a matter of belief or a matter of practice, it is only because some people are seen making some statements such as make the Quran commentators in general into fools, making an isolated opinion of Abu Muslim Isfahani (r) a walking stick, that we have spoken this much.

After saying that you must guard against your Rabb, who began producing you, who are the human community, from one and the same soul- person-, and afterwards multiplied and increased you from it and from its mate, He has said again that you must guard against that Allah in whose name- putting Him forward- you are used to asking one another *[p. 930]* (**وَٱتَّقُواْ ٱللَّهَ ٱلَّذِي تَسَآءَلُونَ بِهِۦ**). You know that the Lord who has created you and been bringing you up is Allah; it is for that reason, surely, that you often demand and ask many things of one another, putting Him forward. The hint contained in this is that, on that account, you yourselves admit that guarding against Allah is a duty of yours by nature. ‘I request in the name of Allah; remembering Allah, you must do for me in such-and-such a manner; it is putting the One who created forward that I say this’: men's asking and saying to one another in this way is customary in this age as in former times, surely. It is not rare to hear such words, in situations of being stuck, even from those who are not believers in God.

The importance given in Islam to keeping family ties, and to treating the family in a good manner, will not be seen to have been given in any other religion. Along with commanding to worship Allah, it can be seen that it is often counselled in the Quran to do good to parents. In the Hadith also many things can be seen. In the verses that follow in this surah, moreover, there come very many statements concerning orphan children and many domestic and family matters. On that account, specially alerting about keeping family ties in this very first verse would be fitting, surely. Therefore, joining it to His having emphasised guarding against Allah, Allah has also reminded that you must guard family ties too (**وَٱلۡأَرۡحَامَ**).

The above translation and commentary have been adopted on the basis of the reading which, joining it (**عطف**) to the name **الله** (Allah), gives the vowel a (**الفتح**) to the last letter, as **وَٱلۡأَرۡحَامَ** (wal-arhama). The reading that is generally accepted is also like that indeed. Nevertheless, it has also been read here as ‘wal-arhami’, giving the vowel i (**الكسر**) to that letter. Then it will be joined (**معطوف**) to the pronoun in **بِهِۦ** (bihi). According to this reading, the meaning of that sentence will be: ‘Guard against that Allah in whose name and in the name of family ties you keep asking one another’. Even then there is nothing to change in the principle contained in reminding about family ties, joining them to the name of Allah.

From reminding of the history of the origin of the human race, it is clear that all men are ones born as the offspring of the same parents, and that in reality the human community is one huge family of all together, and that therefore they ought to live in mutual love and friendliness. But there is no need to say specially that guarding against Allah and guarding family ties do not end up in the same intent. Guarding against Allah is realised by living according to His legal directions, and *[p. 931]* guarding family ties by treating well those who have family ties. Allah finally reminds men that He is carrying out close observation whether you are fulfilling all the duties towards Allah and towards the families *[DOUBT: കുടുംബങ്ങൾ, literally "families"; in Malayalam usage it may also mean "relatives, kinsfolk" (those who have family ties, as in the sentence before). Options: "the families" / "the relatives".]*. (**إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ كَانَ عَلَيۡكُمۡ رَقِيبٗا**)

*[Verse 2]*

In the last verse, after commanding to guard against Allah and family ties, from this verse onward Allah describes many matters, as if by way of its elaboration. Among them, the matter of the orphans is, surely, one that deserves very great importance. Therefore priority has been given to the matters that have to be guarded in the subject of the orphans. The commands in this are ones addressed to all who manage the property of orphans. Whether they are of the family or not. The management of the orphans in each family would mostly take place, surely, at the hands of the members of that family. On that account it is only that this would affect the family heads of the orphans more. It is children who have lost their fathers who are called **يتيم** (yatim). Customarily that name is not said of those who have come of age. There are three commands in this verse concerning the yatim (orphan)s:-

(1) Their properties must be given up to them. That is; the gist is that, when they reach maturity of age and firmness of mind, those who had been carrying out the management until then must give up *[p. 932]* their properties to them without reluctance. That wealth need not be given up before coming of age, or to those in whom the firmness of mind to manage wealth has not come, is stated in verses 5, 6. Even after maturity of age and firmness of mind have come, even if, owing to some special reason, there is difficulty for the child to take over the property, it is not that it must necessarily be given up. But it is only that the subsequent managements must be according to the child's liking and with consent.

(2) Do not take the corrupt in exchange in place of the good. That is, do not accept, in the place of the good that is blameless and thoroughly pure, bad things that are culpable and impure. This may come about in many ways. For example, taking things of a superior kind belonging to the orphans this way, and putting that way, in its place, what is of lower grade than it or what is worse. Carrying out management in a manner suited to one's own interest, other than in a manner conducive to their good and prosperity; carrying out transactions of buying and selling that turn out as loss to them; and so on.

(3) Do not use their property and one's own properties mixing them together - without looking to carefulness and exactness -. This is squandering their wealth and spending it by diverting it. But Allah has stated in 2 : 220 that there is no objection to leading a joint life with orphans without departing from justice and propriety. (For more information look there) About the hint contained in saying ‘do not eat’, without saying ‘do not use their properties’, it has been explained before in Al-Baqarah. Using the properties of others - whoever's it may be - in a manner not in accordance with law is always forbidden and an offence indeed. When it is, moreover, that of orphans, it becomes more severe and grave. That is what the sentence **إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ حُوبٗا كَبِيرٗا** (surely, that is a great crime) signifies.
