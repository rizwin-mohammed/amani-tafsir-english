### Section – 9

*[Verses 60–63]*

*[p. 1043]* What are seen in these verses are some doings of the hypocrites, who utter with the mouth that they have believed in the Quran and in the earlier scriptures, and who go about wearing the guise of Muslims, and the reproach concerning them. Some Quran commentators can be seen to have quoted some causes that became the reason for the reproach. A quarrel arose between a man from among the Ansar and a Jew. The Jew said that it could be put to the decision of Muhammad, and the Ansari said that it could be put to the decision of a Jewish chief named Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf. This is one of them. Another is that some of the hypocrites used to try to make a settlement in their cases by approaching some judges and diviners of the Jahiliyyah. Whether the cause of the sending down was any of these or not, it is certain that all the hypocritical Muslims who, not being ready to submit to the decision of Allah and of the Rasul - that is, of the Quran and of the Sunnah - are satisfied with the decision of evil powers who are disbelievers, are subject to the reproach in these verses.

Concerning the meaning and the intent of **طَاغُوت** (taghut), it has already been described more than once *[p. 1044]* before. All the evil powers that defy the judgements and laws of Allah will be included in it. If one examines keeping these verses in front, it can be seen that a large section of today's Muslim community falls among such hypocrites. When some problem, or party quarrel, comes up, other than from where a judgement favourable to them will be obtained, on which side the truth and the right are, or what the judgement is that Allah and the Rasul command, is not at all a subject of thought for them. Perhaps, if they see that it is the judgement in Islam that will be favourable to them too, then they will also appear as great religious devotees. On the contrary, if they see that it is in obeying some government law, or court judgement, or arbitration decision that has no agreement at all with Islamic laws that there will be benefit for them, there, then, the commands and prohibitions of Islam, which they are bound to accept, will also be impracticable in their sight. Many laws that are made in our country, which is a secular state, in the name of the democratic system, and of public welfare - let their aim outwardly be indeed praiseworthy - will be ones not in accord with the principles and doctrines of Islam. It can be seen that they assign more value than to the divine laws to many such laws that help to appropriate the property or the rights of others without giving them up to their real owners, and to get by without paying off the debt liabilities that one is duty-bound to pay off.

Even if they are imposed by compulsion, to keep away as far as possible from such unjust legal proceedings is indeed, as far as the true believers are concerned, their duty. That being so, to throw out the religious injunctions, making them a weapon, even in a state where there is no compulsion, is utter hypocrisy. Or else it is defiance. What is more sorrowful is that this kind are wont to be seen not only among ignorant common people but even among individuals who are considered to be ones with some knowledge. If anyone says that the law of Islam is in such and such a way, surely - is not that what ought to be accepted -, what such people will do is wriggle out, saying some tricks and specious arguments. Yes, the very same practice that Allah stated in the 61st verse. As Allah has pointed out in the 59th verse, if they believe in Allah, and in the Last Day, they must necessarily change this stance. **والله المستعان**

As becomes clear from Al-Baqarah : 256, along with believing in Allah, and in the scripture, and in the Rasul, rejecting the ‘Taghut’s, and disbelieving in them, become indispensable. It is for that reason that they are reproached that, though they have been commanded to disbelieve in Taghut, they intend to go to Taghut and *[p. 1045]* seek judgement. That is, the import is that what they do is not only not performing what is obligatory, but also doing what is forbidden. When, after being satisfied with the judgement of satanic powers, aiming at some temporary gain, something unwelcome befalls them, it may come about that those hypocrites have to come to the Prophet ﷺ and present excuses. Then they will contend that what happened on their part before was not with mere ill intention *[TN: the print has ഉരുദ്ദേശ്യത്തോടു, which is not a word; ദുരുദ്ദേശ്യത്തോടു ("with ill intention"), the opposite of the following സദുദ്ദേശ്യത്തോടു ("with good intention"), is evidently meant.]*, that it was only with the good intention that good should come about, and that agreement should be brought about between the parties, and so on. But, Allah knows all their inside stories well. They are not at all strangers to Allah, even if not taking action against them at once, in due course Allah will indeed take the punitive action that they deserve. Therefore, it is enough for now to leave them alone, disregarding them, and to give good advice, and to say to them words fit to sink into the mind - such is what Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ.

To the word **فِيٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ**, to which the meaning in their own selves was given, the interpretations in the matters concerning them, and to them personally- that is, privately- have also been given. What is meant by **قَوۡلَۢا بَلِيغٗا** is indeed that words that touch their minds must be said to them, such as will serve to turn them back from such doings. The noble Prophet ﷺ is, surely, a person who has been given the ability to speak to all kinds of people in a style suited to the occasions. Allah says:-

*[Verses 64–65]*

*[p. 1046]* These verses too are the continuation of what was stated in the past verses about the hypocrites who, when division and quarrel come up among themselves, approach the Taghuts without trying to put it to the decision of the noble Rasul ﷺ. But, the principles contained in these verses are not ones that affect such people only. They are indeed ones that every Muslim who claims to be a true believer must accept as the foundation of his religious life.

*[p. 1047]* Supreme obedience in reality belongs to Allah alone. But, it is by means of His messengers (Rasuls), whom He commissions for that, that He conveys His messages and directions, and commands and prohibitions, all of them, to people. In that respect, obeying Allah is realised by way of obeying those messengers of His unconditionally. As for them, they will do anything only in accordance with His order and command. Therefore Allah has given permission for each messenger of God to be obeyed by his respective community, and has made it obligatory for each individual to obey them unconditionally. Then, while the Rasul ﷺ is present, obeying the Taghuts, who are his opponents, without obeying his judgement, becomes rejecting the Rasul. (After the lifetime of the Rasul, accepting judgements against his Sunnah is just the same.) Now, once such a wrong - and that is a huge wrong that one does to one's own self - has been committed, what is needed then is not making a justification for it, or presenting excuses. On the contrary, what they ought to do is to go to the noble Rasul ﷺ and express regret about the offence that has happened on one's part, and pray to Allah for forgiveness of sins, and request the noble one to pray for them. Along with that, in case the Rasul too, interceding for them, prays to Allah, surely Allah would forgive them - such is what Allah makes known in this verse.

It is very clear that what has been said here is concerning those who were in the time of the noble Rasul ﷺ. Going to the noble Rasul ﷺ, and his seeking forgiveness of sins, will, surely, be possible only in his lifetime. Some interested parties are wont to use this verse in order to make a justification for some superstitions, and evil customs, such as that whatever sin one commits, it is enough to go to the grave of the noble Rasul ﷺ and tell one's grief. They babble too that going to the grave of the noble one after the lifetime of the Prophet ﷺ, and going into his presence in the lifetime, are alike. Leaving aside that this is utterly meaningless, even if one supposes so, it is needless to say that the condition that the noble Rasul ﷺ seeks forgiveness of sins for these people will be fulfilled only in the lifetime. While the noble Rasul ﷺ is living, the disobedience that people who live connected with him show towards him will be a more serious crime. Likewise, having the fortune to receive directly the prayer that he makes is a very great blessing too. It is this that needs to be specially borne in mind here. *[p. 1048]* There is, surely, no way that the prayer that the Rasul ﷺ, whom Allah has commissioned and sent for the good of the community, makes to Him for them should fail to be acceptable with Him more than anyone else's.

When one looks at the style of speech adopted in this verse addressing the Prophet ﷺ, it ought to have said **وَأَسْتَغْفَرْتَ لَهُمْ** (and you seek forgiveness of sins for them). Leaving that style, what Allah has said is **وَٱسۡتَغۡفَرَ لَهُمُ ٱلرَّسُولُ** (and the Rasul seek forgiveness of sins for them). It is the said reality that this hints at. **الله اعلم**

It has been said, surely, that the sending of the Rasul is for obeying the Rasul. Then, on occasions when difference of opinion, or division, or party quarrel arises, a true believer has the right to look only at what the judgement of the Rasul is in that subject. Those who are in the time when the noble Rasul ﷺ is living can seek and inquire about the judgement directly. After his passing away, there is no means for that from anything else other than from the Kitab of Allah, which he has entrusted to us and gone, and from the Sunnah, which is his conduct. Those who argue that after his passing away only the Quran need be relied upon are in reality making out to be false, and rejecting, the Rasul and the Quran. Just reflect how strictly Allah has stated here the obligation of accepting the judgements of the Rasul:-

Firstly: **فَلَا**, a particle in the form of negation! It means ‘The matter being as stated above, no.....’, secondly: **وَرَبِّكَ**, a sentence of oath! It means ‘By your Rabb indeed - here I am saying it on oath’. Thirdly: repeating the particle of negation again, it presents the subject saying **لَا يُؤۡمِنُونَ حَتَّىٰ**… (they will not believe without making you the judge). That, without saying that appointing as judge is obligatory, or that otherwise there is punishment, or the like, it has said that as long as they do not appoint as judge true faith itself will not be right, deserves special attention. Fourthly: even appointing as judge and putting that judgement into effect is not enough; it has said that no hardship at all must be felt in the mind about that judgement either (... **ثُمَّ لَا يَجِدُواْ** ). Even that is not enough; fifthly it says that they must also submit to it ( **وَيُسَلِّمُواْ** ). Finally, even submitting is not enough; a sixth emphatic statement too, that it must be a submission that is with fully approving and consenting ( **تَسۡلِيمٗا** )! All this, can anyone in whose heart at least a little true faith has taken a place say that it is applicable only to the time of the Prophet ﷺ? Some sayings of the Prophet that contain the idea of this verse, *[p. 1049]* see them too:-

(a) **لاَ يُؤْمِنُ اَحَدُكُمْ حَتَّى اَكُونَ اَحَبَّ اَلَيْه مِنْ وَلَده وَوَلَده وَالنَّاس اَجْمَعِين (متفق عليه)** *[TN: the first وَلَده (after مِنْ) is evidently a slip for وَالِدِه ("his parent"); the Malayalam rendering below has ‘his parents, and children’.]*

(b) **لاَ يُوْمَنُ اَحَدُكُمْ حَتَّى يَكُونَ هَوِيَهُ تَبَعًا لِمَا جَئْتُ بِهِ (شرح السنة)**

(c) **ذَاقَ طَعْمَ الايمَان مَنْ رَضِيَ بالله رَبًّا وَبالاسْلاَم دينًا وَبِمُحَمَّد نَبيًّا(مسلم)**

(d) **عن عابس بن رَبِيعة قال رأيت عمربن اَلخطاب يقبِّل الحجرَ ـ يعني الاَسْوَدَ ـ وَيَقُولُ اَعْلَمُ اَنَّكَ حَجَرٌ مَا تَنْفَعُ وَلاَ تَضُرُّ وَلَوْ لاَ اَنِّي رَأَيْتُ رَسُولَ الله ﷺ يُقَبِّلُكَ مَا قَبَّلْتُكَ (متفق عليه)**

The consonants are certain.]*

The gist:- The Rasul ﷺ has said: (a) ‘For one of you, until I become one more beloved than his parents, and children, and all people, he will not become a believer.’ (Bu: Mu,) When the most beloved one is the Prophet ﷺ, no one else's judgement will, surely, be accepted against his judgement.

(b) Until the desire of one of you comes to be following what I have brought, he will not be a believer.’ (Sharh al-Sunnah) The import is that even desires against what the noble one has shown do not befit true believers.

(c) Whoever is satisfied with Allah as Rabb, and with Islam as religion, and with Muhammad as prophet, he will taste the flavour of true faith.’ (Mu.)

What is meant is that it is not enough to believe and to consent, the mind must also feel contentment, only then will the excellence of true faith be experienced.

(d) 'Abis ibn Rabi'ah (r) says: I saw 'Umar (r) kissing Hajar al-Aswad. He was also saying thus. ‘I know that you, are a stone that is unable to do benefit, or to do harm. Had I not seen the noble Rasul ﷺ kissing you, I would not have kissed you.’ (Bu; Mu.)

That if the Rasul ﷺ does or says anything, even if its cause and rationale have not been understood by us, we must obey it, and follow it, is indeed the import of what 'Umar (r) said.
