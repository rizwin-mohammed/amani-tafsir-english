### Section – 11

*[Verses 77–79]*

*[p. 1060]* In the beginning period of Islam - when the Muslims were in Makkah - itself, the prayer and Zakat had been made obligatory for the Muslims. Only, the Zakat of that time was not in the particular manner that is in force today. But, permission to fight with the enemies had not been given to them. The true believers had an intense desire that it would be good if they could wage battle, using what strength they could, against the wrongdoing and persecutions of the enemies. But, the command was to adopt patience and forbearance for the time being. In many respects the circumstances of that time *[p. 1061]* were not favourable for battle. The number and the strength of the Muslims being less, and that, since both parties were living in the surroundings of Makkah, it would come about that battle and clash would take place in the Haram, were the cause of that. After the Muslims went to Madinah, when a favourable atmosphere came into being, the command to fight, in the manner they had been desiring and demanding before, was revealed.

This command produced very great disquiet among the people who were hypocrites and those weak in faith. The fear of confronting the enemies, and the dread of death, troubled them. Rabb! Why was this battle made obligatory? Before our natural death itself, we will have to die at the hand of the enemies! Could You not leave us to live for some more time?! In such terms they came to have complaint and reproach. Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ to give a reply to this. The gist of the reply is this: However much comfortable life is obtained in this world, compared to the Hereafter it is only very paltry. For those who keep guarding against Him while obeying the commands of Allah, the life that is very much better than that is the life of the Hereafter. It will be everlasting and complete in comfort. The deeds that you do according to His command, or the sacrifices that you have to accept in it - nothing at all of them will be lost to you even as much as a whit. As for death, Allah has determined, of each one, when it is, how it is, and so on. It will not fail to happen just so. Even if, without taking part in any battle, you shut yourselves up inside huge forts, it will indeed happen. Do not think that through battle there will be a decrease in your lifespan, or through not fighting an increase in it. Therefore, your excuses and worries are out of place. Yielding to the command of Allah is best for you. Otherwise, its result will be that you bring irreparable loss upon yourselves.

Allah has mentioned in these verses another accusation of the hypocrites and the reply to it. It is said that not only the hypocrites but the Jews too had a share in this accusation. When some pleasant experiences such as well-being, comfort come, they will pride themselves, saying that it is a good obtained by them on the part of Allah. On the contrary, when some disagreeable things such as famine, grief and the like befall, they will also babble that it is an evil that happened on account of the Prophet ﷺ. Some commentators are seen to have said that, as soon as the Prophet ﷺ went to Madinah, famine befell there for a short time, and that that is what caused them to say thus. (Allah knows.) The Prophet ﷺ is commanded to give a reply to this accusation too. Whether it is good, or whether it is evil, all is what Allah determines and creates. For each one He has fixed some causes and occasions. These people *[p. 1062]* do not grasp or understand any such matters. In reality, although everything is indeed from Allah, there is a difference between good and evil. It is from Allah that good originates. Yes, it comes about through His favour and generosity. The origin of evil is from human beings themselves. That is, their evil of deeds and disobedience are the cause of it. Thus is the import of the reply.

Although good and evil both happen indeed from Allah, it is this difference between the two that is pointed out through the 79th verse. In it, because مَّآ أَصَابَكَ (what has befallen you) has been said addressing the Prophet ﷺ, one need not suppose that nothing that befalls others is so. In another place, making it somewhat more clear, Allah says: وَمَآ أَصَٰبَكُم مِّن مُّصِيبَةٖ فَبِمَا كَسَبَتۡ أَيۡدِيكُمۡ وَيَعۡفُواْ عَن كَثِيرٖ (Whatever disaster befalls you, it is on account of what your hands have done. And He pardons concerning many things.) (Shura - 30.) Continuing, it is pointed out that the Prophet ﷺ has no part in bringing about good and evil, and that Allah has sent the Prophet ﷺ making him His Rasul - his duty is only the preaching of His missions *[TN: printed ഭൗത്യങ്ങൾ, which is not a word; read as ദൗത്യങ്ങൾ ("missions").]* - and that, whether anyone accepts it or not, it needs no one else's proof or confirmation for it, and that Allah's testimony itself is sufficient.

Misinterpreting this verse, some other Quran verses too, some rationalists who deny ‘qada'qadr’ (the decree and disposition of Allah) are wont to give justification for their new arguments. Concerning this, it has been explained sufficiently in the explanatory note after Su: Hadid. Those who wish to know more, look there.

*[Verse 80]*

*[p. 1063]* The import of having said that obeying the Rasul is indeed obeying Allah has already been explained sufficiently before, in the explanation of the 59th verse and of many verses following. There is no need to repeat it here. He has no duty of making those who are not ready to obey the Rasul obey; what is meant by what was said afterwards is that the Rasul has as obligation only the fulfilling of the mission of Allah, and conveying it to the people.

*[Verse 81]*

This too is an evil trait of the hypocrites. When the Prophet ﷺ commands something, or makes them hear some divine messages, they will display readiness, saying that they are ready to obey it properly. *[p. 1064]* If they leave that place, they will, conspiring with one another, also plan programmes against it. Allah warns them that Allah knows all that, and is recording it, and that they themselves will have to experience its result in due course. But, it is enough if the Prophet ﷺ takes note of such doings of theirs concerning them. Apart from that, there is no need to assign any value to it or to take action; only entrusting to Allah is needed; He Himself will take the actions that are needed; the Prophet ﷺ is also alerted in such terms.

*[Verse 82]*

Allah reminds them that it is their not reflecting intently and thinking about the statements of the Quran that is the cause of such a perverted state of mind coming about in them, and that therefore what is needed is to think about the Quran attentively. This is not an announcement applicable only to the hypocrites of that time, or to the people of that period. This is an announcement that concerns everyone of every time. Allah's statement that, if it comes to be that this Book was not revealed from Allah - is the work of someone else -, surely many differences and disagreements could be seen in it, is enough to make everyone who has an impartial intellect, and a truth-seeking state of mind, sit and think. The plight that the constitutions and legal systems, which are formed by consensus after a group of people who are the very greatest intellectuals of a nation, and who have reached the summit in knowledge and learning, have come together and racked their brains for a period running to months and to years, have to be changed before long itself - on account of mutual contradiction and impracticability - at the hands of their makers themselves, is indeed an experience we are seeing today in most countries.

Concerning the Quran, in the principles that it conceives, or in its doctrines, or in its legal directions that realise those principles and doctrines - in none at all - until this time today, not one such *[p. 1065]* plight has happened. It can be said decisively that it will not happen hereafter either. But, it is only natural that for those who keep harbouring enmity and rancour towards the Quran, openly or covertly, and for those who feel contempt for its principles and doctrines because some of them do not suit their interests, and for those who give precedence to material interests, and the like, there is mental difficulty in acknowledging this reality. If they want, such people will also portray that there are some mutual differences, or disagreement, in the Quran. In reality, the responsibility for it lies indeed with their crooked way of thinking. One thing we can say firmly. That is:

It is not possible for it to be pointed out by anyone that a clear statement of the Quran ever goes against or is contrary to another clear statement. It is in some situations such as one statement being clear and another not being so clear, one being in detailed form and the other in general form, that it comes about that it seems to someone that there is some mutual difference outwardly. Interpreting the less clear part by means of the more clear part is what is proper and obligatory in such situations. Quite the contrary, only when, having made the less clear part the basis, and having determined for it the interpretation that each one sees - or else likes -, one interprets the more clear statements to fit that, will that contradiction be experienced in reality. It is to those who approach the Quran keeping some ideology or interest in mind beforehand that this experience usually comes. It is clear, is it not, that this is not a fault of the Quran. (Remember what has been explained before concerning this subject in the Introduction, and in Ali 'Imran-7 and in its explanation.)

*[Verse 83]*

*[p. 1066]* It is an evil that usually comes about from the hypocrites, and from some pious people who have no knowledge of affairs, that Allah points out. When some information that gives the people peace, or that produces dread, is heard, spreading it without considering - or else without knowing - its true state, and the consequence that may come about if it is made public. In the circumstances of that time, when the enemies of Islam were lying in wait on every side, it is natural, is it not, that many such reports and rumours are heard. The consequence that comes about through spreading such reports will sometimes be terrible. The hypocrites may venture upon it knowingly, deliberately. But, it will be because they do not know about the consequence that the pious people who are true believers do so. Either way, the result is indeed one and the same, is it not. If, on hearing any such reports, they inform the noble Rasul ﷺ, and the leaders who have knowledge of affairs enough to understand its merits and demerits, they will handle the matter as is needed, and will arrange to take the necessary actions on the basis of it. It is about spreading such reports at the very moment of hearing, without doing so, that Allah reproaches. An alert is also given that this practice is merely instigated by Satan, and that, if the favour and mercy of Allah had not been there, most people would have become subject to Satan's *[p. 1067]* evil promptings.

Publicising and spreading all the reports heard, without considering the true state and the consequence, is always a matter that needs very much care. In some situations it is extremely terrible consequences that will happen on account of a small baseless report. After it has happened, there is no use, is there, in knowing later that that report was not true. At the same time, all those who spread that report also become responsible for that crime. The Prophet ﷺ declares: ‘Telling all that one has heard is itself enough for falsehood to be in a man.’ (Mu; Da.) In a hadith that Mughirah ibn Shu'bah (r) narrated, it is seen: ‘The Prophet ﷺ has forbidden ‘qila’ and ‘qala’’. (Bu; Mu.) It is expressions such as ‘Someone said, it is so said. One hears people saying’ that are meant by ‘qila ( **قِيلَ**). Expressions such as he said, this one said’ are what is meant by ‘qala’ ( **قَالَ**). That is, it means that the Prophet ﷺ has forbidden saying ‘what one has seen and what one has heard’ and the like, without looking at the true state and the merits and demerits. The wording of a saying of the Prophet that Abu Dawud (r) narrated is this: **بئس مطية الرجل زعموا** (‘They say’ is a very evil mount of a man.’) That is, the import is that the practice of stating ‘they said, these said’ and so on when narrating reports is an evil trick that man uses in order to slip away from responsibility.

*[Verses 84–85]*

*[p. 1068]* Urging the true believers to battle and counselling them is the duty of the Prophet ﷺ. If they do not take part in battle, the Prophet ﷺ will not be asked why they did not take part. Allah alerts the Prophet ﷺ that only the matter of his own self will be asked about. Reassurance is also given that Allah may, before long, wear down the fighting strength and the vigour of the enemies, and that thus victory may come to the true believers.

It is made known in the second verse that, if a person makes some intercession of a good kind for a person, an allotment from its good, and if he makes some bad intercession, a part from its evil, will reach that intercessor. Counsels, encouragements, prayers and the like that are made for the sake of all good things are included in good intercession, and counsels and encouragements, *[p. 1069]* requests that are made for the sake of all bad things are included in bad intercession. Looking at the context, it may be considered that what is mainly meant here is the good intercessions of the kind that encourage fighting in the way of Allah, and the bad intercessions that are made against it. **والله اعلم**
