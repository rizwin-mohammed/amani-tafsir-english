*[Verses 126–127]*

The gist: your being given help by bringing down angels, and your being informed of this news, and so on, is only as good news and a congratulation for you. Through it you will obtain morale, and courage, and zest, and thus peace of mind will come about for you. Otherwise, it is not in order that you may obtain help and victory through them. Because, help is obtained only from Allah. It is not necessary that it be attained because of having brought down angels. It is not by number or by strength *[p. 848]* that victory comes to you. If He wills, you will obtain victory even without bringing down angels, and without even conducting a battle. If so, then, it may be asked why it is that He commands that you must fight, and must confront the enemies, and so on. Of the disbelievers who are your enemies, with a number of people perishing, and a number of people falling into captivity, and so on, their strength needs to decline, and they thus need to become defeated and disappointed ones. It is for that. Other than that, the intention is not to kill off the disbelievers entirely.

Advising that, on confronting the enemies, one must face them boldly, and so on, Allah says in Surat Muhammad: ‘If Allah wills, He would Himself take protective action *[DOUBT: "രക്ഷാനടപടി" is clearly printed (the amanithafseer.com text has the same) and reads "protective (or rescuing) action". The Arabic quoted (لَٱنتَصَرَ مِنۡهُمۡ) is rendered elsewhere as taking retribution, for which earlier parts have ശിക്ഷാനടപടി "punitive action"; this may be a printing slip for ശിക്ഷാനടപടി, or the author's own wording. Translated as printed.]* against them. But, it is in order to conduct a trial by means of some among you.’ ( **ذَٰلِكَۖ وَلَوۡ يَشَآءُ ٱللَّهُ لَٱنتَصَرَ مِنۡهُمۡ وَلَٰكِن لِّيَبۡلُوَاْ بَعۡضَكُم بِبَعۡضٖ** – **محمد : ٤** ) It can be seen that, following His telling the information of having brought down a thousand angels at Badr, in Su: Anfal too, indeed, Allah has repeated this matter stated in the 126th verse.

That the angels did not engage in fighting with the enemies in any of the other battles that were not the battle of Badr, and that at Badr the angels did take part in the fighting, is the opinion of the majority of scholars. That, in (the matter of) their having taken part in the fighting at Badr, the commentators of the Quran and the scholars of history are of one opinion, is indeed what Imam Razi (r) states. One reality needs to be remembered here. When it is said that the angels took part in the fighting, it must not be reckoned that their activity and involvement would be just like that of human beings. That angels have some hands in many matters that take place in this world is a thing established beyond doubt by the Quran and by Hadith. That being so, none of the questions of some, such as ‘Is not one angel enough to destroy the enemies altogether? Then, what is the need of angels by the thousand? If angels have fought, should not the enemies be seen falling dead otherwise than at the hands of human beings? Even though a thousand angels came down and fought, were not only seventy people killed.?’, have any relevance here.

Abu Bakr al-Asamm is one who, on the basis of some rational arguments like those indicated above, in former times flatly denied the coming down of angels, and (their) fighting. Giving reply and refutation to all his arguments, Imam Razi (r) and others say thus: ‘Only those who deny the Quran, and prophethood, can say so. It was never permissible for Abu Bakr al-Asamm to deny this matter that the Quran, and many Hadiths, have clearly stated’ About this we can speak more at Suratul Anfal. **إن شاء الله**

*[Verses 128–129]*

*[p. 849]* In the battle of Uhud many calamities befell the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions at the hands of the enemies, surely. It has come in Hadiths that, following that, the Prophet ﷺ prayed, or requested permission from Allah to pray, against some mushriks among the enemies who had committed more wrongdoing, and that it was in connection with that that this verse came down. The Prophet ﷺ has no authority to take the decision, or to pass judgement, that such and such people are ones on the right path, and are misguided ones. That matter belongs within Allah's right of authority. Perhaps, it may turn out that one who was thought to be a disbeliever and *[p. 850]* a misguided one afterwards regrets, turns back and becomes good, and Allah accepts his turning back. Or else it may also turn out that, since they are in the state of wrongdoers, Allah does punish them indeed. That the minute and final decision about each one is with Allah, and so on, is the gist of the first verse. The second verse strengthens this idea once more, too. That, as indicated above, some individuals who had been severe opponents of Islam afterwards believed in the Prophet ﷺ, and became prominent Companions, and many gains came about for Islam through them, is worth stating here. In another place Allah says: **إِنَّكَ لَا تَهۡدِي مَنۡ أَحۡبَبۡتَ وَلَٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَهۡدِي مَن يَشَآءُ** – **القصص : ٥٦** (You will not put on the right path those whom you have loved; but Allah puts on the right path those whom He wills.)

### Section – 14

*[Verses 130–131]*

After describing many fundamental subjects, and a number of matters relating to war, Allah gives a few injunctions and prohibitions, and counsels and directions, which the true believers must keep knowing and practising in daily life. It has been described in the Preface, surely, that the method that the Quran has adopted in the arrangement of *[p. 851]* subjects, and in dealing with them, is different from other books, and that it has a special method of its own. Therefore, we have also pointed out there that there need not necessarily be, outwardly, a connection by subject between each ayah and the ayah next to it. Even so, if examined as a whole, it is not that there is no connection by subject between these verses and the verses before, moreover. Following the references concerning the conflict between the true believers and their opponents, it would be appropriate, surely, to point out to them the ways needed to remove from the true believers the evil qualities that exist in the opponents, and to bring them up making them the best community.

Through this verse Allah forbids the true believers concerning eating interest property- or using it-. Many items of information about interest have gone past in Suratul Baqarah. Therefore, there is no need to explain much about it here. It is without any particular conditions or qualifications at all that Allah has declared interest forbidden there. Here, when He forbade that interest be used, He has said do not eat in a doubled and redoubled state **لَا تَأۡكُلُواْ ٱلرِّبَوٰٓاْ أَضۡعَٰفٗا مُّضَٰعَفَةٗ**. For this reason, this does not mean that only the interest taken at a double rate has been forbidden, or that there is no objection to ‘moderate interest’ in the language of the interest-dealers. When criticising, or forbidding, a vile or base matter, pointing out its worthlessness by mentioning qualifications that point to some of its corrupt aspects, and speaking about it in a style such that aversion to it is felt by the listeners, is the usual practice. This can be seen in all languages and literatures.

Belonging to that group is Allah's saying, without saying ‘Do not use interest’ or the like, ‘Do not eat interest ( **لَا تَأۡكُلُواْ ٱلرِّبَوٰٓاْ** ) here, and, instead of saying ‘Do not take and use the properties of orphans’, ‘Do not eat the properties of the orphans ( **وَلاَتَأْكُلُواْ أَمْوَالَ اليَتامى** *[DOUBT: these words are not found in this form in the Quran (the nearest are An-Nisa' 2, وَلَا تَأۡكُلُوٓاْ أَمۡوَٰلَهُمۡ إِلَىٰٓ أَمۡوَٰلِكُمۡ, and An-Nisa' 10, يَأۡكُلُونَ أَمۡوَٰلَ ٱلۡيَتَٰمَىٰ); the print gives no reference. The Arabic is transcribed as printed, its vowel marks read from a 600 dpi crop together with the PDF's text layer. Whether the author meant a free summary or one of these verses is for the reviewer.]* )’, and so on, too, just such a usage indeed is the usage ‘doubled and redoubled ( **أَضۡعَٰفٗا مُّضَٰعَفَةٗ** )’ too, taking and using another's wealth without right is wrong. The hint in it is that its being at a double rate beyond what was given out becomes a more terrible wrong than that. That it has come in Hadiths that the Prophet ﷺ said, when speaking in reproach about killing one's own children, **خشية ان يطعم معك** ( fearing that child's eating food with you), and, when speaking about adultery, **ان تزنى حليلة جارك** (to commit adultery with your neighbour's wife), is indeed included in this kind. No one with good sense will understand from this that, when there is no fear that the child will eat food with him, one may *[p. 852]* kill one's own children, or that there is no objection to committing adultery with women who are not the neighbour's wife, surely.

Some people of material interest who pride themselves as intellectuals, enlightened ones, progressives’ and so on, and some who work with a secret programme to estrange Muslims from Islam tactfully, under the pretence of being well-wishers of Islam, are wont to murmur off *[DOUBT: "തട്ടിമൂളിക്കാറുണ്ട്", clearly printed: the same idiom as part 05's തട്ടിമൂളിക്കൽ ("murmuring off"), for saying something offhand or evasively ("mumbling", "brushing aside"); "murmur off" keeps part 05's picture but is not settled English.]* that in Islam there is no objection to ‘moderate interest’, and that only ‘double interest’ has been forbidden. They will also hold up this word **أَضۡعَٰفٗا مُّضَٰعَفَةٗ** as a proof for themselves. For a person who accepts Allah's Quran, and the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ, as the authority of Islam, it is not possible to agree with this. Only that much is said about this for the present. From the words Allah used concerning interest in Suratul Baqarah, and from their explanation, this truth has already become clearer, surely.

There is another reason too for saying **أَضۡعَٰفٗا مُّضَٰعَفَةٗ** (in a doubled and redoubled state). What was more in vogue in Arabia at that time was the system of levying interest at double rates. Giving a sum on interest fixing a term; if the capital and the interest are not paid back at the term- mostly the transaction would have taken place with the certainty that that would not be possible-, adding to the capital the interest sum due until then too; when the next term ends, making the interest up to then too into the principal property and increasing it again, and adding interest to it. Thus, if the chance is got, doing in that same manner again and again. In the end, what is recovered will be many times over the capital given first. With this the debtor's story will also come to an end.

This system is one that the traders in interest keep adopting even today. In this age that is described as the ‘civilised age’- though not on the basis of Allah's having forbidden it-, for the reason that it is harmful to the economic policies of the country, many nations have instituted many controls on this double-interest system, that is all. At the same time, everyone also maintains moderate interest in their technical sense, as though it were an indispensable component of the existing economic policy.

When the true believers were warned that they must guard against Hell, that it has been said that it has been made ready for the disbelievers ( **أُعِدَّتۡ لِلۡكَٰفِرِينَ** ) is noteworthy. Hell has been prepared mainly with the disbelievers in view. Even so, that it must be remembered that true believers who commit crimes too will have to undergo the punishment in it, that if one exceeds the bounds in sinful deeds such as interest one must fear coming to fall among the company of the disbelievers, and so on, can be seen hinted in it. Besides, from its having been said, when speaking about Hell here, and in Al-Baqarah *[p. 853]* 24, and about Paradise in the next, 133rd verse and in Sura: Hadid 21,- that it has been made ready ( **أُعِدَّتۡ** ), what is understood is that Paradise and Hell have already been prepared beforehand. About this we have described earlier in Al-Baqarah.

*[Verses 132–133]*

It can be seen that in many places in the Quran it has been said, along with commanding to obey Allah, that one must obey the Rasul too. Obeying Allah is realised by way of obeying the Rasul, surely. Because, all of Allah's commands and directions are obtained through the Rasul. That is why Allah says in another place (in 4: 80) **مَّن يُطِعِ ٱلرَّسُولَ فَقَدۡ أَطَاعَ ٱللَّهَ** (Whoever obeys the Rasul, he has obeyed Allah). The partisans who argue that the Muslims need accept only the Quran as authority- that the Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ need not be made an authority- *[p. 854]* have forgotten this reality altogether. It was following His having forbidden concerning using interest, surely, that He commanded to obey Allah and the Rasul. Then, it is clear indeed that a special hint is also contained in this, that all the explanations and commands that the Prophet ﷺ has given concerning interest must be accepted.

Allah stirs the true believers to attain forgiveness of sins from Allah by obeying Allah and the Rasul, and, doing good deeds with the zeal of: I must be in front, I must be in front, to attain a blessed life in Paradise, wide and spacious as the heavens and the earth. Just as He commanded in the last verse that they must guard against coming to enter Hell, prepared for the disbelievers, He has counselled that it is for the God-fearing who observe caution that so wide a Paradise has been prepared, and that therefore they must strive to attain that Paradise by living as God-fearing ones. A verse rather like this (133rd) verse can be seen in Suratul Hadid 21 too. More explanation can be seen there. Allah points out the qualities of the God-fearing who become heirs of Paradise:-

*[Verses 134–136]*

*[p. 855]* It is three qualities of the God-fearing who are heirs of Paradise that have been stated in the first verse.

(1) Spending wealth in the state of happiness, and in the state of hardship. That is, spending in good things according to ability and according to the occasion, without distinction as to whether it be joy or sorrow, or plenty or dearth. Even those who spend rather open-handedly when there is comfort and happiness are wont at times, if some sorrow or straitness is met, then to change into misers for that reason, and, on the *[p. 856]* contrary, some who live as misers, when some calamity and hardship is met, to have a change of heart and venture to do almsgiving. The purport is that these will not be so. When speaking about interest, it was pointed out, surely, that it leads to Hell. As the first among the qualities that lead to Paradise, Allah has counted almsgiving, the nature directly opposite to interest.

(2) Keeping anger restrained. When anger comes, keeping it restrained in one's own mind without allowing it to intensify and without making a display of emotion.

(3) Giving pardon to people. That is, letting it go without taking revenge or doing wrong on account of the blunders and the errors and offences that come about from others, and not nursing grudge and enmity towards them.

After mentioning these three things, note that it has been said that Allah will love those who do good like this ( **وَٱللَّهُ يُحِبُّ ٱلۡمُحۡسِنِينَ** ). What other fortune is there for man to obtain above obtaining Allah's love?!

It can be seen that Allah has made statements in many places in the Quran concerning all these three things. Many Hadiths too are there to quote about them. About the first, we have quoted many things in Suratul Baqarah in the respective places. Only a few Hadiths concerning the second and the third matters may be quoted here.

(1). The Prophet ﷺ graciously said: ‘It is not by wrestling that one becomes mighty (strong). But, it is the one who subdues his own mind when anger comes who is indeed the mighty one.’ (A; Bu; Mu.)

(2) A man came to the Prophet ﷺ and requested that he should give him counsel, and that, for the convenience of his learning it, it should be only a few words. What the Prophet ﷺ said to him was **لاَ تغضب** (You must not get angry). He requested again and again. Every time, the noble one only repeated that very word. (Bu.) In one narration of Ahmad (r) there is this too: ‘That man is saying: When the Prophet ﷺ said that, I thought it over. When I looked, anger is one that includes all evils.’

(3) The Prophet ﷺ said: ‘If anger seizes one of you while he is standing, let him sit down; then if the anger goes, so (be it). Otherwise, let him lie down.’ (A; Da.) It has also come in a Hadith that Abu Dharr (r) acted according to this saying of the Prophet (A.) On the contrary, getting up and sitting if anger comes while lying, standing up if anger comes while sitting, and leaving the place where one stands if it comes while *[p. 857]* standing, will all indeed be things that give growth to anger.

(4) The Prophet ﷺ said: ‘If one keeps his anger restrained, though he has the ability to carry it out, Allah will increase refuge and faith within him.’ (Da.)

(5) The noble one said: ‘No wealth will diminish because of charity. Because of pardon, Allah will not increase a servant except in vigour (majesty). If anyone shows humility for the sake of Allah, Allah will raise him.’ (Mu.)

(6). The Prophet ﷺ said: ‘Anger is from Satan. And Satan, for his part, has been created from fire. Fire is put out only with water. Therefore, if anger seizes one of you, then let him perform ‘wudu'’.’ (A; D.)

What was stated (in the second verse) as the 4th quality of the God-fearing is indeed this: if one has committed some vile deed, or wrong against one's own self indeed, Allah coming to mind, and at once seeking forgiveness of sins. That is, if one has committed some sin, a sense of guilt coming at once, and the fear being born that one will have to answer before Allah, and thus repenting and asking for forgiveness. Errors and offences coming about is natural to man. At the same time, Allah, for His part, is the Merciful and the Ever-Merciful. Who else is there to forgive the errors and offences that come from His servants?! There is no one, surely. Therefore, it is man's duty, when he does wrong, to turn back at once and ask for pardon. On the contrary, committing an offence, and, knowing that it is an offence, standing firm in it without regretting and turning back from it, is ingratitude and defiance. That is why it has been specially mentioned: ‘They will not persist, knowingly, in what they have done, either ( **وَلَمۡ يُصِرُّواْ عَلَىٰ مَا فَعَلُواْ**.... )’. Even though one has committed an offence, if one at once repents and turns back from it, not only will the offence be forgiven.’ That turning back also becomes a meritorious deed.

The intention of having said that one should not persist in the offence committed is not that one should never afterwards repeat an offence once committed. Repeating an offence too is wrong indeed. But, if, when the offence is repeated, repentance too is repeated, it will not be persisting in the offence. Not repenting of the offence committed is persisting. The Prophet ﷺ says: ‘He who has sought forgiveness of sins has not persisted. Even if he repeats seventy times in a day.’ (Da; Ti.) The gist of the Hadith is indeed that even if a sin is repeated many times, one must every time regret and turn back, and seek forgiveness of sins from Allah; one must not despair that Allah will not forgive. In another Hadith *[p. 858]* the Prophet ﷺ says: ‘Greater than the joy at the time when the camel (mount) of one of you, having been lost (having gone missing) in a desert, is found by him, is the joy that Allah has in His servant's repenting.’ (Bu; Mu.) When speaking about the repentance of one who has sinned, the question that Allah asked even before that statement ended- as a parenthetical sentence **وَمَن يَغۡفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ** (Who is it that will forgive sins except Allah?!), ha, to which sinner does it not give hope in Allah's mercy?! To whom does it not give eagerness to seek forgiveness of sins from Him?! O Allah! Forgive us all our sins and bless us!
