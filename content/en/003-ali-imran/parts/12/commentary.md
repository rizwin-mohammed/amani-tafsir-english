### Section – 12

*[Verses 110–112]*

*[p. 827]* For the sentence **كُنتُمۡ خَيۡرَ أُمَّةٍ أُخۡرِجَتۡ لِلنَّاسِ** the meaning can come, as seen in the translation, as ‘You are the best community that has been brought forth for mankind’, and also as ‘The best among the communities that have been made manifest to mankind’. According to the first, the gist is that you are the best community, appointed in order to give example and leadership to the other communities, and according to the second, that among the communities of mankind it is you who are the most excellent and best community. Both, too, finally end up in the very same idea. The one that is best among the communities, and the one that ought to be an example to the other communities, is indeed the Muslim community. But, it is noteworthy that Allah has also placed some conditions for that. That is, ‘You command with good custom and forbid concerning evil custom, and believe in Allah.’ That is to say, the purport is that it is because of having these special features that you became the best community, and that only while those qualities exist in you will you be the best community. Qatadah (r) quotes that 'Umar (r), reciting this verse, said thus: ‘O people, if it is a joy to anyone to be included in the community just mentioned, let him fulfil the stipulation that Allah has fixed for it.’ (Ibn Jarir)

Having said ‘It is you who are the very best community’ is- as great men such as Ibn Kathir (r) have stated- indeed a ruling that includes the Muslim community in general. That is- it is not something said, as some have opined, intending only the Muslims who were in the time of the Prophet ﷺ. What Imam Ahmad (r) has quoted, that the Prophet ﷺ, in the course of specially mentioning some distinctive features that other prophets had not attained and that the Prophet ﷺ alone attained, stated **وجُعِلَتْ اُمتِى خَير الامم** (My community has also been made the best among the communities), is worth stating. There are other Hadiths too that give support to this. Ibn Kathir (r) can be seen to have quoted many of them. But, because the true believers who were in the time of the noble Prophet ﷺ and in the periods near to it were ones who stood foremost in special features like those said above, there is no doubt in the matter that it is they who are in the front rank of excellence. This fact, in a sacred saying, *[p. 828]* the Prophet ﷺ has also made clear. Saying **خَيرَ أمتِى قرنى الخ** (The best in my community are the people of my time. Then those who come near to them, and then those who come near to them, and then those near to them), the noble one continued thus: ‘Then after them there will be some people: they will bear witness, and they will not be asked for testimony, either; they will betray, and they will not be trusted, either; they will make vows, and they will not fulfil them, either. Corpulence (because of a life of luxurious comfort) will also appear among them. (Bu; Mu.) In one riwayah there is this too: ‘They will swear. They will not be asked to swear, either’.

The purport of having said **وَلَوۡ ءَامَنَ أَهۡلُ ٱلۡكِتَٰبِ**...... (If the People of the Scripture had believed, it would have been better for them) is that, although they say that they are the successors of the prophets, and believers, their belief and their practices have all gone astray; therefore, if they believe in the Prophet ﷺ and become real true believers, they too will become fortunate ones included in this best community. Although the word People of the Scripture will include both the Israelites and those who are not, when looked at in the light of the references seen below, what is understood is that here the Israelites have been specially intended. In any case, Allah has also pointed out that the People of the Scripture have not all become ones who have gone astray and disbelievers, and that among them there are a few good ones as true believers, and that the greater part are indeed ones who have gone astray. Those like 'Abdullah ibn Salam (r) are indeed among these few. Allah also makes known to the true believers that there is no need to fear the People of the Scripture on the ground that they are keeping up envy and enmity towards the true believers and towards Islam. He has also made clear that, except that because of them there may be, to some extent, nuisance and disturbance, they have not the ability or the boldness to bring harm to the Muslims, and that even if they come for war they will only turn back and run away, and that it is not possible for them to achieve victory. When the battles of Qainuqa', Nadir and Quraizah ( **قينقاع، نضير، قريظة** ) took place, the Muslims also saw the fulfilment of this prophecy in the Jews of Madinah, and at Khaibar in the Jews there.

The purport of having said in the 112th verse that wherever they may be, abasement- baseness- has been struck upon them ( **ضُرِبَتۡ عَلَيۡهِمُ ٱلذِّلَّةُ أَيۡنَ مَا ثُقِفُوٓاْ** ), and that they have returned with wrath from Allah ( **وَبَآءُو بِغَضَبٖ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ** ), and that upon them helplessness or degradation- has been struck. ( **ضُرِبَتۡ عَلَيۡهِمُ ٱلۡمَسۡكَنَةُ** ), has already been explained in the commentary of the verse Suratul Baqarah : 61. When the context is looked at, although it may seem that this is a general statement about the People of the Scripture, who comprise the Jews and the Christians, it can be understood from the verse in Al-Baqarah that this is a statement intending the Jews. The précis of what was said there is that abasement and degradation have become their *[p. 829]* inborn qualities in such a way that they never part from them, and that what they have gained in all in their life is the wrath of Allah. Look there for more information.

In this verse Allah has stated a fact that was not stated there when speaking about abasement: it is indeed ‘except along with some rope from Allah and some rope from mankind ( **إِلَّا بِحَبۡلٖ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ وَحَبۡلٖ مِّنَ ٱلنَّاسِ** )’. That is, the purport of this on the whole is that unless they get some hold or other from Allah and from mankind, it will never be possible for them to escape from abasement. Accepting true faith, entering into alliance or treaty with the Muslims, obtaining the alliance of other communities or of the enemies of the Muslims, and so on, can all be examples of this rope to hold on to.

As the reasons for the Israelites becoming so much the objects of the curse and wrath of Allah, it is the very same reasons stated in that verse of Suratul Baqarah that Allah has stated here too. That is, their having kept on rejecting the signs of Allah without believing in them, and their having kept on killing the prophets of Allah without any justification at all, and what impelled them to this, for its part, disobedience, and transgression, since the needed explanation about all this has already passed in Suratul Baqarah, there is no need to describe it further here.

If the history of the Israelites is examined, it will become clear that from whatever time they became victims of the curse and wrath of Allah, from that time abasement and degradation also settled in them. That can be seen manifestly in them both before the advent of Islam and after it up to today. Before Islam, being enslaved to the imperial powers of Iraq and of Rome. Suffering their oppression, and paying them tribute, they had to pass a long time. When Islamic rule came into existence, they had to live under it, paying tribute and making peace treaties. Even after Muslim rule broke up and scattered- in many places and in many states- they have been able to live only as base ones and degraded ones. In short, they have not been able, without the generosity, or help, of another party, either to stand on their own footing as an honourable and independent community, or to establish a ruling authority of their own. It is true that more than thirty years ago the Jews established a state in Palestine under the name the State of Israel. (*) But, the plain truth that its origin, and its continued existence, all indeed are in the hands of the great powers such as America, is widely known. This can also be taken as an example of Allah's having stated that, except along with the rope of Allah and of mankind *[p. 830]*, there is no escape for them from abasement. Many states have not recognised it as a state up to today. Let alone that the Muslim states, all of them, are in the utmost enmity and hostility towards it, it is also noteworthy that many even among the non-Muslim states are not living in sympathy towards it. Generally speaking. A state so much hated will not indeed be found anywhere else in the world.

In the month of May 1948 the State of Israel was also established in the portion of the Jews. It was by settling together the Jews who had been living scattered here and there in the world, and in many places- especially under the Nazis- as oppressed ones. It was the influence that the Zionist movement brought about in the European countries, and the vested interests of the allied parties comprising great powers such as America and England, which took part in the Second World War against the Nazis, that prepared the ground for this. The great powers who were its begetters themselves kept on rearing it, nursing it with milk. Today too it is indeed their help and sympathy that keep Israel in existence. Many clashes took place with the Arab states. They are still taking place. Among them the wars that took place in A.D. 1967 and 1973 are the main ones.

Another aspect too needs to be remembered: however accursed the State of Israel, or the Jewish community, may be, let alone wiping out that state, very small merely in size and in population (*), neither the Arab Muslim states numbering in tens, nor the Muslim states that are not Arabs, have been able up to now to hold it in check, and to defeat its moves against the Muslims. No way is seen, either, for a hope that they may be able to in the near future. The Arab states, for their part, are today, more than ever, in the front rank too in wealth and in prosperity. What is the reason for that? Although it is in the capacity of Muslim states that they are known, the real Islamic spirit has become rare in those places. Along with the increase of abundance of wealth, what keeps on increasing in those places is estrangement from the Islamic life, and interest in material comforts and luxuries. The disunity, and the mutual enmity, existing among the Arab states are also its inevitable results.

It is appropriate to the occasion here to remember some counsels that the Khalifah 'Umar (r) gave to Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (r), who was the commander-in-chief in the Iraq wars in the time of the Khulafa' al-Rashidin, and *[p. 831]* one of the prominent Companions, and the maternal uncle of the noble Prophet ﷺ. The gist of the wasiyyah that 'Umar (r) made to him when sending him off is this: ‘Sa'd, let not the fact that you are the maternal uncle and the friend of the Rasul of Allah deceive you. Allah does not wipe out evil with evil; only with good does He wipe out evil. Except by way of obeying Allah, there is no family relationship whatever between Allah and anyone. In the religion of Allah all are equals. Whatever thing you saw the Rasul ﷺ continuing in without letting go, you too hold fast to it without letting go.’ Afterwards, after Sa'd *[TN: the print has "സ്അ്ദ്" here, for "സഅ്ദ്" (Sa'd).]* (r) had gone, some sentences in a letter sent to him were thus: ‘You and those with you must always be ones having ‘taqwa’ (God-fearing reverence). That indeed is the greatest preparation, and the greatest war stratagem, against the enemies. You must be more watchful about yourselves indeed than about your enemies. For, it is the sins that the army commits that I fear more than their enemies. It is only because the enemies of the Muslims show disobedience to Allah that help is received by the Muslims against them. Although Allah has pointed out that it may perhaps become possible for them to obtain some escape from the state of abasement, Allah has not indicated that there is a way for them to get escape even once from the wrath of Allah, and from degradation. It is certain indeed that they will experience the result of the curse and wrath of Allah in the Hereafter. If the state in this world is to be told, a sense of inferiority, and a mentality of degradation, are indeed a characteristic nature of the Jews. It may be said that huge greed for wealth and excessive miserliness are a distinctive trait of theirs. What we ought to do from the many references of this kind in the Quran about the People of the Scripture- especially the Israelites-, is not only to memorise some historical information *[p. 832]*. What we ought to do is to take to heart the lessons contained in those histories, and to make use of them so that the harms that befell them may not come to us. May Allah help us to that. Amin. Allah says:-

*[Verses 113–115]*

*[p. 833]* About the People of the Scripture, it was said in the previous verse that they are subject to the curse and wrath of Allah. But, in these verses Allah makes known that, although that is the general state, they are not all such people, and that there are a few who stand firm in the right path and in true faith. Both before the noble Prophet ﷺ, and in the time of the noble one, there would have been a few such people among them. But, in the time of the Prophet ﷺ, and those after it, believing in the Prophet ﷺ is indeed indispensable, that is why many commentators say that what is intended by these are the individuals from the People of the Scripture who believed in the Prophet ﷺ and accepted Islam. The gist of a riwayah that Ibn Ishaq, Tabarani, Baihaqi, Ibn Jarir (r) and others have quoted as having been said by Ibn 'Abbas (r) is as follows: ‘When 'Abdullah ibn Salam (r) and others entered Islam, and believed in the Prophet ﷺ, the Jewish scholars, and the disbelievers among them, said: It is only the bad people among us who have believed in Muhammad, had they been good, they would not have left the religion of their fathers and gone to another. It was then that this verse came down.’ There is no need to say that those having the qualities that Allah has specially mentioned in these verses can, after the appearance of the Prophet ﷺ, only be ones who have believed in the Prophet ﷺ.

The things that Allah has said in praise about these righteous ones:

(1) They will recite the ayat of Allah in the times of the night, doing sujud. That is, the meaning is that they will pray at night and keep on carrying out recitation of the Quran in the prayer. Then, what is meant by sujud will be the prayer. It is customary, surely, in the Quran to use sujud, and ruku', for the prayer. Along with that, recitation of the Quran has not been prescribed in sujud, either. The Prophet ﷺ has also forbidden it For the word **وَهُمۡ يَسۡجُدُونَ** the meaning ‘They will also do sujud’ too can come. There is no difference in the idea, moreover.

(2) They believe in Allah and in the Last Day. Although the People of the Scripture are outwardly ones who believe in Allah and in the Last Day, they believe in Allah only with shirk *[p. 834]* mixed in. About the Last Day, for its part, what they have are some interpretations of meaning of their own. They are also ones not in accord with the clear descriptions of the Quran. Therefore, the purport of this is that these people are not such people they are ones who have accepted the proper belief-.

(3) They will counsel the people with the good thing, and forbid concerning evil things. The gist is that concealing the truth, twisting the truth to suit the wishes of the people and their own interest, keeping silence whatever indecency they see others doing, and so on, are none of them their habit.

(4) They will keep on advancing with haste in good things.

The vast majority among the People of the Scripture are ones who do not have the qualities just mentioned. It is indeed for that reason, too, that it was said about them (in the 110th verse) ‘Most of them are wayward ones’. On the contrary, about these Allah has certified ‘They are among the righteous’. Besides, He has also made known the good news that Allah will not waste any good thing whatever that they do- however trifling it may be-, and that He will accept it with gratitude and give reward.

*[Verses 116–117]*

*[p. 835]* Allah has made clear in more than one place that for the disbelievers- of whatever kind they may be- there is no merit at all to be received in the Hereafter on account of their deeds. Here too it is about that that it speaks. Wealth and children are, surely, what is most important in this world for the protection and the majesty of man. In the Hereafter they are not going to receive any benefit at all from those two. What they will receive there will be the everlasting punishment of Hell. Such, and so on, is the purport of the first verse. Then, room for a doubt arises: the disbelievers do spend wealth, surely, on good matters such as gifts and charities. Will no reward at all be received for that, either? It is the answer to this doubt that is contained, through an example, in the second verse. The state of their gifts and charities too is like the way the produce in the fields is lost if a storm of intense cold, which freezes and destroys the crops, strikes. That is, the gist is that the storm which is their disbelief and the evil works in accordance with it will destroy the fruits of their deeds too.

**صِرٌّ** (sirrun): it is ‘intense cold’ that most of the commentators of the Quran, and the scholars of language, assign as the meaning for this word. That is what we too have adopted. There are also those who have taken it to mean ‘fire’ and ‘poisonous wind’. When a fierce storm comes- whether it is a fire-wind, or a poisonous wind or a snow-wind, whichever *[p. 836]* it may be- the destruction and loss that happen because of it will surely be just the same. In this parable, the owners of the field have been described as **قَوۡمٖ ظَلَمُوٓاْ أَنفُسَهُمۡ** (a people who committed wrongdoing towards their own selves). That is, what is meant is those who did injustice to themselves indeed by way of disbelief and sins. While this parable would be complete even without this description, a secret can be seen to be contained in it. When the crop of those who are not wrongdoers- who are true believers- perishes by a storm, although it brings them destruction and loss in this world, in the Hereafter Allah may give a good reward in place of that destruction and loss. If the crop of the disbelievers perishes, it is indeed lost to them in this world and in the next world. Some are seen to have said that what is intended by ‘wrongdoing’ in this parable is not heeding the place, the season and so on suitable for sowing the crop. No justification is seen for this. For, it is by that very wrongdoing itself that that crop will end in loss. Then there is no need there for a wind of cold. Not only that, saying about it wrongdoing done towards their own selves does not seem so appropriate either. **الله اعلم**
