*[Verses 118–120]*

*[p. 838]* Garments worn on the outside are called **ظَهَارَة** (ziharat), and, as the opposite of that, garments worn inside are called **بِطَانَةً** (bitanat). For the special ones, attendants, close friends, intimates and such, who are closely connected so as to exchange internal matters and secrets with one another, too, **بِطَانَةً** is customarily said in a figurative form. It is in this meaning that it is here too. Just as, in the senses ‘regretted’ and ‘was amazed’, it is customarily said in Malayalam ‘bit the hand’ and ‘bit the tongue’ and so on, in the senses ‘regretted’ and ‘became angry’ it is customarily said in Arabic too **عض الاَنامل** (bit the finger-tips- or- the finger-). That here it is not because of grief- it is because of anger— is clear from the ayah, surely. The purport of having commanded to say to the enemies ‘Die with anger ( **مُوتُواْ بِغَيۡظِكُمۡ** )’ is the registering of dislike and protest towards them. A notice too is contained in it, that even if you die because of excess of anger, it is not possible for you to settle your grudge.

*[p. 839]* Because of the old alliance ties, and neighbourly ties, that had existed in the Jahiliyyah between the Jews and the Arabs of Madinah, as a continuation of it, in Islam too, some Muslims and they had kept up personal ties of love between them. It is natural, surely, that by this way the Jews get an opportunity to know some internal secrets of the Muslims. The Jews, for their part, will try to draw out the secrets by exploiting those ties, and will make use of it to create disorder among the Muslims. Besides, some Muslims will have close ties also with some hypocrites who had put on the guise of Islam for the sake of being able to stay on for the time being. The result of this too is the very same as said above. As far as the Muslims are concerned, they will be ones with an open mind. The state of the opposing parties was not that. As Allah has made clear, their hearts were polluted with poison. The consequence of ties and associations such as these is great, surely.

Therefore, in these verses Allah prohibits the true believers' establishing such close ties with anyone among those who are not. He has warned about its consequences, and has exposed that the mentality of the two parties is mutually contradictory. When the occasion of revelation is looked at, it may be said that what is prohibited in this is the establishing of close ties with the Jews and with the hypocrites. But, when the words that Allah used, and the reasons He explained, are looked at, and when the historical realities that have gone past up to now between the Muslims and the other communities who are in the field as their enemies are looked at, and when the rule that, even if they came down following a particular event, the ruling contained in the ayat will be general, is looked at, it is plain that these verses are not aimed only at the Jews and the hypocrites of that day. In whomever there are the characteristic states that Allah has pointed out as the dispositions of mind of those of the enemy side- whatever religion or section they may be of- establishing close ties with them is indeed forbidden and dangerous.

There is not any discord at all, indeed, between this prohibition and the Islamic principles such as that one must live with non-Muslims in love, in harmony and in affection, and must observe justice and righteousness towards all. Read the sentences in the ayah attentively. Even without any explanation they are self-evident. Pay special attention to the word that Allah said last to the true believers after telling this subject- in detail with this much seriousness. ‘If you are patient, and are on guard, their stratagem will not bring you any harm. Surely, Allah is One who keeps encompassing what they do- or, He is properly knowing it.’

*[p. 840]* In a Hadith that has come by more than one route from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (r) and others, the Prophet ﷺ says: ‘When Allah raises a prophet, or makes a khalifah, there will not fail to be for him two (kinds of) bitanats - a ‘bitanat’ that counsels with good and urges to it, and a ‘bitanat’ that counsels with evil, and urges to it. The person who does the work of writing under the government is likely, surely, to come to know the administrative secrets and betray them to the enemies.

### Section – 13

In the last few verses many states and conditions of the enemies of Islam were described. Warning was also given about the consequences of keeping up ties in which the minds are in accord with them. Finally, it was also brought to notice that, to get protection from the harms of their intrigues, patience and caution must be adopted. Afterwards, it is about an event- that is, the battle of Uhud- in which it befell the Muslims to see and know all that in experience, and in which many lessons were received for the future, that Allah makes statements in the next verses. Since it will be helpful for understanding the references in those verses that follow, it will be good to know beforehand a brief account concerning the said battle.

In the very famous battle of Badr that took place in the second year of the Hijrah, the army of the Quraish was very much greater than the Muslims, both in number and in equipment of arms. And yet, what happened was that they were utterly defeated. Many of their chief leaders were killed and many were *[p. 841]* taken captive. To take revenge for this, a huge army numbering about three thousand, under the leadership of Abu Sufyan, came down at a place near Madinah. That was at the beginning of the month of Shawwal of the third year of the Hijrah. When the news was known, the Prophet ﷺ held consultation with the Companions as to whether it was facing them inside Madinah itself that was needed? Or, whether it was facing them outside that was needed. The opinion of the Prophet ﷺ and of the experienced Companions was that, if they came into Madinah, it was better to fight there, and that there was no need to go out and fight. 'Abdullah ibn Ubayy, the leader of the hypocrites, also agreed with that.

But, the opinion of the young men- especially of those who had been regretful at not having been able to take part in the battle of Badr- was the opposite. Hamzah (r), the paternal uncle of the noble one, was also in this group. Because of the strong demand of these, who stood out in number and in bodily strength, in the end their opinion was accepted. In the Friday sermon on the tenth of Shawwal, the Prophet ﷺ gave many counsels about patience, courage and such. Putting on a coat of mail and such, early on Saturday the noble one got ready and set out from the house. When they saw this, it came about that some among those who had insisted that they must go outside and confront the enemy felt regret at their not having yielded to the wish of the Prophet ﷺ, and requested that he should do according to the noble one's wish. The noble one's reply was that it was not permitted for any prophet who had put on arms and set out to turn back until Allah carried out His decision.

Thus, the noble one set out with a thousand Companions. When they reached the place called Shawt ( **الشُوط** ), 'Abdullah ibn Ubayy and his followers numbering about three hundred turned back. It was saying that, without listening to our word, he- the Prophet ﷺ- set out accepting the opinion of the young men, that they turned back. The remaining 700 and the Prophet ﷺ moved forward. The turning back of this many people had caused a stir in the minds of many. When they reached Uhud, about four nazhikas' distance from Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions encamped in its valley, with Mount Uhud behind and the enemy army in front. At a vital spot where there was a likelihood of the enemies coming from behind over the mountain and attacking unexpectedly, the noble one also stationed fifty archers under the leadership of 'Abdullah ibn Jubair (r). The noble one had also specially commanded them not to allow the enemies to come by that way, and not to leave the place without getting permission, whatever might happen. After assigning places to the army divisions, the battle began.

In the beginning victory for the Muslims and defeat for the enemies began to be seen. The enemies began to run away, leaving the battlefield. Some also made use of that chance to collect the goods that they were abandoning and leaving behind. The enemies' turning back and running, and the Muslims' collecting ‘ghanimah’ items, the archers who were on the hill *[p. 842]* were watching. Thinking that the battle had ended, and that they too could share in the collecting of ghanimah, they left the place. They forgot the command of the noble one altogether. Only their chief Ibn Jubair (r) and a few others stood firm without leaving the place. But, Khalid ibnul Walid, who was the leader on the enemy side, understood this chance and made use of it. With a cavalry formation he came by that route through the back of the mountain and made a new attack on the Muslims from behind. With that, the enemies who had run away leaving the battlefield came back again and began to attack the Muslims also from the front. This produced great bewilderment and panic in the Muslims who were caught in the middle. Many left the ranks and scattered. Only a few people fought, standing firm in the battlefield without faltering.

Another reason that increased the alarm of the Muslims also came about. It was that a rumour spread that the Prophet ﷺ had been killed. If this was heard, then, what the state of the Companions at that time would be can also more or less be guessed, surely. The Prophet ﷺ, for his part, was standing firm valiantly, without straying from his place, or stumbling, or faltering in mind. Around the noble one, like a fortress, ten or twelve brave Companions stood resisting, receiving the weapons and arrows of the enemies upon themselves. Their brave feats were unmatched. Some of them, treating their own lives as straw, making their bodies shields for the noble Prophet ﷺ, had also received sixty and seventy wounds. The noble Prophet ﷺ received injuries, and his tooth was broken. It was after a while that the Companions were able to know that the noble one was alive. The relief that they had with that need not be told, surely. The noble one climbed up at a place on the mountain and was calling ‘Come here! Come here!’ With a new enthusiasm and courage the Companions ran together around and began to drive away the enemies. But, what they did was to leave the place quickly, beating the drum over the victory they had received first. This was a great favour that Allah did for the Muslims. Now let us enter into the ayat. Allah says:-

*[Verses 121–122]*

*[p. 843]* Those spoken of in saying that two sections among you attempted to show cowardice ( **هَمَّت طَّآئِفَتَانِ مِنكُمۡ أَن تَفۡشَلَا** ) are the Banu Salamah branch belonging to the Khazraj tribe, and the Banu Harithah branch belonging to the Aws tribe. It was they who had been stationed on both sides of the Muslim army as flank forces. The gist is that, because of the seriousness of the situation, their morale weakened. That it was not a disobedience or a misdeed that they committed deliberately is indicated by the sentence **وَٱللَّهُ وَلِيُّهُمَا** (And Allah, for His part, was the manager of their affairs, too). The reason for cowardice being felt can be guessed, surely, from what was described before. That the first verse has been ended with the sentence ‘Allah is One who hears and One who knows ( **وَٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ** )’, and the second verse with the sentence ‘Let the true believers rely upon Allah indeed ( **وَعَلَى ٱللَّهِ فَلۡيَتَوَكَّلِ ٱلۡمُؤۡمِنُونَ** ), is noteworthy. It is a speciality of the Quran, surely, that at the end of most of the ayat there are such concluding sentences, suited to the idea and to the context in those respective ayat. The principles contained in these concluding sentences must be specially taken to heart, looking at the context.

The battle of Uhud was a great event in which the true believers received, through experience, many lessons for understanding their shortcoming in obedience, courage, patience, tawakkul (relying upon Allah) and such, and for making it good. Of the fact that it is not according to great numbers of people, or strength of arms- it is according to faith and bravery- that Allah gives victory *[p. 844]* to the true believers, the battle of Badr was a sufficient proof. That is what the next verse points out.

*[Verses 123–125]*

*[p. 845]* The word **مُسَوِّمِينَ** has been read (with the ‘i’ sound and with the ‘a’ sound to the waw *[TN: printed "വാവി ‘ന്’", i.e. വാവിന് ("to the waw") with its ending in quotation marks; the round bracket opened before it is not closed.]* (as ‘musawwimin’, and as ‘musawwamin’) too. According to the first reading, the meaning will be that those angels will be ones who have adopted some special emblems for themselves, and according to the second reading, that they will be ones who have been given special emblems.

The battle of Badr took place on Friday, the seventeenth of Ramadan of the second year of the Hijrah. It was between the Quraish army, numbering about a thousand, which had come prepared with every kind of equipment, and three hundred and thirteen Muslims. Besides the number of the Muslims being small, there were with them, moreover, only a few of the weapons that Arabs usually keep in hand, and two horses and seventy camels. Thus, it is because they were so much less than the enemies in number and in strength and in everything that Allah described them as **أَذِلَّةٞ** (lowly ones). Although you were such a lowly band, Allah helped you at Badr and made you victorious. Then, it is not by number or by weapons that victory is attained. It is only from Allah that victory is attained ( **وَمَا ٱلنَّصۡرُ إِلَّا مِنۡ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ** ) Therefore, if you face the enemies without blunders occurring like those that came about at Uhud- guarding against Allah and entrusting matters to Him -victory is certain for you. Thus, you can rejoice, and be ones thankful to Allah. The battle of Badr is a proof of this, surely. Such things, and so on, Allah reminds the true believers of in the 123rd verse.

Afterwards, in the 124th verse, the occasion on which that great victory was received at Badr is described. That is, while they were engaged in combat with an army much greater than themselves, the Muslims heard a piece of news that an army of help was coming again to help the enemy army. This distressed them. On this occasion the Prophet ﷺ said to them: ‘Is it not enough if Allah helps you by bringing down three thousand angels? Yes, it is enough ! If you adopt patience and caution, and the enemy army arrives instantly, He will help you with five thousand specially prepared angels indeed.’ But, because of having received news that the enemy army had been utterly defeated in the battle, that army of help did not come. Therefore the five *[p. 846]* thousand angels were not brought down either. As announced first, only three thousand angels were brought down. It is in this way that many commentators of the Quran have stated here. Some riwayahs too can be seen in connection with this That there is no contradiction between what was said in the statement about the battle of Badr, in the Su: Anfal 9th verse, that He would help by bringing down a thousand angels, and what has been said here, and that, after first promising a thousand, He afterwards increased it to three thousand, and that, with some conditions, He increased it again to five thousand, and that, since the condition was not fulfilled because the enemy army did not arrive, the bringing down of five thousand did not take place- it was only three thousand that were brought down-, this too is pointed out here. **الله اعلم**

From what was stated above it has become clear, surely, that the word **إِذۡ تَقُولُ** (the occasion when you say) in the 124th verse is connected with the 123rd verse- that is, is concerning the bringing down of angels at Badr-. What is outwardly clear from the sentences of the ayah too is just so. There is another opinion too here. That what was stated as the Prophet ﷺ having said concerning bringing down angels was at Uhud- not at Badr- is indeed the opinion of some commentators. In their opinion the word **إِذۡ تَقُولُ** is connected with the 121st verse. The gist is that it is a quotation of what the Prophet ﷺ said at Uhud. However, these too say that, since the condition **إِن تَصۡبِرُواْ وَتَتَّقُواْ**.... (if you are patient and are on guard) was not fulfilled, angels were not brought down at Uhud. But, among these there are people of two opinions: that five thousand have not been brought down- the three thousand mentioned first have been brought down-, and that none at all have been brought down. The truth Allah knows. There is no need seen to lengthen this by quoting the opinions in this connection and their proofs. After describing them in general, it will be good to know the gist of the statement that Ibn Jarir (r) made. It can be quoted thus:-

‘The opinion that is nearer to the truth among these is this: Allah has quoted the Rasul ﷺ as having said to the true believers ‘Will it not suffice you that your Rabb gives you help with three thousand angels’. Thus, He promised that He would help with three thousand angels. But, that He gave them help with three thousand, or with five thousand, or that He did not, has not been stated in the ayah. Perhaps He may have given help. Or He may not have given help. Without proof, neither may be said. If there were proof, one of the two could have been accepted. But, it has been made known in the Quran that at Badr He gave help with a thousand angels. That is Su: Anfal 9th verse. As far as Uhud is concerned, that the giving of help by bringing down angels did not take place appears clearer than the opinion that it did. For, if help had been given in that way, defeat would not have befallen them, and calamities would not have *[p. 847]* struck them, either.’ ( **ابن جرير** )
