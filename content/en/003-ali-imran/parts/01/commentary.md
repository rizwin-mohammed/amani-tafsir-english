*[Verse 3]*

*[p. 712]* By **الْكِتَاب** (the scripture), what is meant is the Quran itself. Because all scriptures agree in the doctrines of belief and in fundamental matters, the Quran has been described as confirming the books before it and establishing their truthfulness. Among the former scriptures, the most important and best known are the Torah and the Injil, surely. Two communities exist, moreover, as their followers. Therefore both of them have also been mentioned specially. To separate and discriminate that the truth is such-and-such and that the untruth is such-and-such: that indeed is the meaning of the word **الفرقان**. It is not only making the former scriptures true and confirming them that Allah has done through the Quran. The import is that He has also discriminated and explained through it truths and untruths, and the just and the unjust. When the position that the Jews and the Christians adopted after the time of their prophets is looked at, it would have been difficult for the world to understand even whether two divine books, the Torah and the Injil, had really been sent down before. The Quran is also discriminating the truths and untruths that they adopted later.

By the Torah and the Injil – here and in all other places indeed – what is meant is not the Bible existing today, and the divisions in it that are the Old Testaments and the New Testaments. (It) is the real Torah sent down to the Prophet Musa (a), and the real Injil sent down to the Prophet 'Isa (a). The correct copy of these two- the very copy, without addition or omission- is indeed not in the possession of the Jews or the Christians or anyone else. But it is only that- in between many additions and alterations- in the books of the Old Testament there will be some portions of the very Torah, and in the books of the New Testament some portions of the very Injil. It is not possible to determine which those portions are. If their contents are examined, it can be seen that they are ones written by many, after Musa (a) and 'Isa (a), in the capacity of religious books, or history books, or memoirs, or messages. In a few of them even the names of the authors who wrote them can be seen. Some events after the time of the Prophet Musa (a) can also be seen in the Old Testament, and some events after the time of the Prophet 'Isa (a) in the New Testament. To put it briefly, as some interested parties keep saying, what the Quran confirms is not the Torah that is the Old Testaments in the existing Bible, or the Injil that is the New Testaments. It is only the real Torah and Injil that Allah sent down.

*[Verses 7–9]*

*[p. 715]* A chapter has been given in the Preface under the heading ‘Muhkam and Mutashabih’ ( **المحكم والمتشابه** ). Quoting this 7th verse, many subjects that ought necessarily to be known concerning both of them have also been explained there on its basis. In the commentary on these verses it would be good to remember those too. The gist of these verses can be understood briefly like this:-

It is Allah who has sent down this scripture that is the Quran. In it two kinds of verses are contained: ‘muhkam’ verses ( **ايات محكمات** ) and mutashabih ( **متشابهات** ) verses. The first division is the root and principal part of the Quran. The real interpretation and inner meaning of the second division only Allah knows. But people who are crooked-hearted go after this division in order to create disorder and to make misinterpretation. People of knowledge who do not lose their footing will not venture to give their own interpretation to this division and to make probing investigation into it. What they will do is both to firmly believe that all is what Allah has sent down and *[p. 716]* the true reality, and to leave the details to Allah. They will also keep supplicating that no crookedness or blunder may befall them on their part, and that they may obtain the mercy of Allah.

The word ‘Muhkam ( **محكم** )’ can be given meanings such as what is strong, what has been made firm, what is sagacious, what is very clear, what has the force of law, according to the context. ‘Mutashabih ( **متشابه** )’ means what has mutual resemblance, what is not distinguishable (on account of resemblance), what is equal to one another, in these ways too. Here, many Quran commentators can be seen to have explained in many ways about what is meant by ‘muhkam’. The summary of the opinion among them which is seen as more fitting and suitable, and which many great men who are men of precise knowledge have been adopting, is this:- All that whose meaning and idea are understood clearly in such a way that there is no room for doubt and confusion of ideas is ‘muhkam’, and those that are not are ‘mutashabih’. When this definition is elaborated, it can be seen that all the other opinions are either ones that stay confined within the scope of this definition, or ones that give importance only to some aspects of it.

It would be good to know some particulars that Imam Raghib (r) stated in his Quran lexicon ‘al-Mufradat’, explaining both these words. While explaining the root word ( **محكم** ), pointing to this (7th) verse, he says: ‘Muhkam’ means that in which there is no misgiving at all to arise, be it with respect to the word, be it with respect to the meaning. ‘Mutashabih’ is of many kinds. About it, it will be stated in its place. Later, while speaking about the root word ( **شبه** ), he can be seen to have given a very long explanation, fulfilling this promise. Its gem of a summary is like this:

‘It is the part in which, because of having resemblance to something else in word or in meaning, doubt gets entangled in interpretation, that is called ‘mutashabih’ in the Quran. If the Quran verses are examined, they will be of three kinds.

(1) That which is unconditionally muhkam.

(2) That which is unconditionally mutashabih

(3) That which is muhkam when looked at from one side, and mutashabih when looked at from another side. To put it in another way, the mutashabih can be divided on the whole into three.

(1) That which is mutashabih when the word is looked at: both because the meaning of single words is not well-determined, and on account of the special character of the sentence structure, *[p. 717]* this comes about.

(2) That which is mutashabih when the intention is looked at. The attributes of Allah, the particulars of the Day of Qiyamah and the like belong to this. Because they are beyond our conjecture, and inference, and experience.

(3) That which is mutashabih both when the words are looked at and when the intention is looked at. There are many examples of this too. Is the ruling (in the ayah) general, or particular? Is it in the form of obligation, or in the form of counsel? Did it come down first, or come down later? To which situation is the ruling applicable? What are the conditions of the ruling? The reason for this will be the unclarity faced in such subjects. When these facts have been understood, it can be seen that all the different interpretations that Quran commentators have given to mutashabih are contained in what has been stated above, ( **من المفردات** )

Imam Raghib (r) has also quoted examples for each of the said mutashabih that he pointed out. Out of fear of lengthiness we have left it out. From the explanation of mutashabih, the intention of muhkam is also clear, surely. From what was explained in the Preface quoting from Imam Shah Waliyullah ad-Dahlawi (r) and others, and from the quotation seen above, anyone can understand the most necessary information about muhkam - mutashabih. ( **ان شاء الله** )

It would be suitable to the occasion to know also the gem of a summary of what Sayyid Rashid Rida (r) stated in Tafsir al-Manar after explaining in detail the differences of opinion concerning ‘muhkam’, mutashabih and ta'wil ( **المحكم والمتشابه والتأويل** ). It can be quoted like this:-

‘When we closely examined the part in which Shaikh al-Islam Ibn Taimiyyah (r) spoke about ‘mutashabih’ and ‘ta'wil’ in the commentary on Suratul Ikhlas, we became convinced that that is the most real and correct explanation. He has established in it that there is no sentence in the Quran whose meaning is not comprehensible, and that ‘mutashabih’ is merely relative- what is mutashabih for the weak (and for ordinary people) will not be mutashabih for firmly grounded scholars-, and that what is meant by the things whose ‘ta'wil’ (inner meaning) no one other than Allah knows is matters such as the reality of the attributes of Allah and of the unseen worlds such as Paradise and Hell. Allah's, ‘what is the ‘Qudrah’ (power) like’, ‘how is it that Allah ascends upon the 'Arsh’, what is the real form of the favours that the people of Paradise receive and of the punishments that the people of Hell receive – none of such matters does anyone know other than Allah, surely. *[p. 718]* (About the people of Paradise) ‘What has been kept hidden in store for them as a coolness of the eyes, no one knows’, Allah says (32:17) The fire in Hell is not like the fire in the earthly world. It is another kind of fire. The fruits and milk and honey and so on in Paradise are not the fruits and milk and honey that we have become familiar with in this world. They too are of another kind.’

It was said, surely, that the muhkam verses are the root and principal part of the book - of the Quran ( **هن ام الكتاب** ). The import of that is indeed that the doctrines, injunctions and prohibitions, counsels and directions, modes of customs and observances and the like that the Quran preaches are all in the muhkam verses. Then, so far as the mutashabihs are concerned, only an approach that takes them as the basis and does not go outside the bounds of their ideas is permissible. Going beyond that and immersing oneself in the mutashabihs, or trying to search through them, to gauge their depth and to give them interpretation - explanation, is not good. That will become a cause of harm more than of good. Often it may also end in danger. That is what Allah points out **فاماالذين في قلوبهم زيغ....** (But, those who have crookedness in hearts, intending disorder and intending interpretation, go after the ones from it that are mutashabih). Yes, it is those of a crooked state of mind and those who nurture evil intention who will immerse themselves in them. The gist is that to create disorder by making confusion of ideas and division in religious doctrines and eternal principles, and in Islamic laws, and to misinterpret them for that purpose – this will be its result. To put it in another way, the blameless ones, who have good intention and devotion to the truth, will not venture to enter immersing themselves in the mutashabihs, or to analyse and interpret them. Imam Bukhari (r) and others have narrated thus from 'A'ishah (r), that the Prophet ﷺ said, reciting this verse: Then, if you see those who go after what is mutashabih in it – from the Quran –, those indeed are the group whom Allah has named (as those who have crookedness in hearts), therefore you must be on your guard about them.

Going after the mutashabihs, and questioning the accepted principles in Islam by devising misinterpretations for them, is a character seen in all the deviant parties. If examples of this are lined up, that itself will become a big book. Of the crooked-minded, who, holding up one of the many purports of meaning that are possible to come to any one word or sentence in the Quran, create confusion of ideas in some other matters that have been set forth in a clearer manner than it, and on its strength propagate their own interests, about many *[p. 719]* misinterpretations too we have often been pointing out in this book.

The opinion was stated before, surely, that some verses of this surah came down following the conversation that the Christian deputation of Najran held with the Prophet ﷺ. It is worth stating that it has come in some riwayahs describing that event that some in that band ventured to maintain that what has been said in the Quran about the Prophet 'Isa (a) (as can be seen in 4: 171), **كلمته.....وروح منه** (Allah's word, and a spirit from Him), was the Quran's having accepted his godhood. Let that be; today many Christian padres, misinterpreting both those words, keep maintaining that the Quran has accepted the divinity of 'Isa (a). That too is one of the many new tricks that they are adopting today to attract the Muslim masses to the Christian religion.,

The reason why going after the mutashabih, and seeking interpretation for it, are this dangerous can be understood from the sentences that follow. Before that, it would be good to know some matters.

(1) The very meaning of the word ( **تأويل** ) is to make (something) from one state into another state ( **التصيير من حالة الى حالة** ). It is used in many meanings: such as interpretation, explanation ( **التفسير والبيان** ), and such as inner essence, real inner meaning, principle, aim, final outcome, explanation ( **الحقيقة العلة والحكمة العاقبة ما يؤل اليه و نحوها** ). Examples of all these will be seen in the Quran itself too. Here, it is the meaning (interpretation-explanation) for that word that one section of the Quran commentators adopts. Another section adopts meanings such as inner essence -inner meaning too.

(2) In the opinion of some, the mutashabihs are the sentences that refer to matters beyond our conjecture and experience, such as the attributes of Allah and the tidings of the Hereafter, and the mere letters at the beginning of some surahs, like Alif-Lam-Mim. Their real inner meaning and the inner essences contained in them only Allah knows, surely.

(3) The opinion of a large section of the scholars is indeed that **وما يعلم تأويله الاالله** (its interpretation-or inner meaning- none will know except Allah) is one statement, and **والراسخون في العلم يقولون .. الخ** (people firmly grounded in knowledge will say....) is another statement. Accordingly, those holding this opinion make waqf at the place **الاالله** (read with a stop.) (\*) *[p. 720]* In the translation it is this opinion that we have adopted. In the opinion of another section of scholars, all that is from **وما يعلم** up to **في العلم** together is one statement. And the next statement begins from **يقولون**. According to this, the meaning of that statement will be: its interpretation, none will know except Allah and those firmly grounded in knowledge.(\*)

> **Note:** (\*) It has been explained in the Preface about waqfs ( **الوقف** ).

> **Note:** (\*) The reason for these opinions is that the particle ‘wa’ ( **الواو** ) in **والراسخون** may possibly be one that is to be joined to the noun before it ( **واوالعطف** ), and may possibly be one that marks the beginning of a new statement ( **واوالاستيناف** ).

It is those who assign to the word **تأويل** (ta'wil) the meaning ‘real inner meaning -or inner essence’, and those of the opinion that what is meant by ‘mutashabih’ is matters known only to Allah, who make waqf at the place **الاالله**. It is those who adopt for **تأويل** meanings such as interpretation, explanation, and those of the opinion that among the mutashabihs there may be both what is known only to Allah and what is known to Allah and to people skilled in the sciences, who, without making waqf there, begin the second verse from **يقولون** *[DOUBT: വചനം is rendered "verse" throughout, but here it plainly means the second statement of the verse (from يقولون); it may be a slip for വാചകം ("statement"), or used loosely.]*. For neither opinion are there any records worth mentioning. It is for that very reason too that- as pointed out in the Preface, Shah Waliyullah (r) and others, among explaining examples of mutashabih verses, have specially shown these sentences too as an example.

One reality needs to be specially remembered. By saying those firmly grounded in knowledge ( **الراسخون في العلم** ) and men of good reason ( **أولوالالباب** ) and so on, what is meant is not- as some are used to supposing- those who have acquired a number of technical sciences, religious or worldly, and those who are adept in philosophies or in scientific thoughts. What is meant is those who have attained sciences that increase true faith and God-fearing reverence, and who have awareness and thought about Allah and the Hereafter. This matter can be understood clearly from what Allah quoted as their words in verses 8 and 9, and from what is stated in verses 190-194 below about people who are men of reason. Remember also what we explained before in the commentary on Al-Baqarah 269 about the purports of meaning contained in the word ( **أولوالالباب** ). The gist of a Hadith that Ibn Abi Hatim (r) quoted is like this. “Whoever keeps what he has sworn to, and whose tongue speaks the truth, and whose mind is upright, and whose belly and private parts are decent, he is among the people firmly grounded in knowledge:” Ibn Mundhir (r) quotes Nafi' ibn Yazid (r) as having said: ‘Those firmly grounded in knowledge is said of people who show devotion to Allah- who offer humility for His pleasure- who do not show arrogance to those above- who do not belittle *[p. 721]* those below.’

The Holy Quran is a scripture that all ought to grasp, surely. That being so, the main justification of those of the other opinion is indeed that it is not fitting that the real idea of some verses in it should come to be known to no one other than Allah. Or, that is the reason for their giving preference to reading together **الاالله والراسخون في العلم** (except Allah and those firmly grounded in knowledge). At first sight this justification may seem right. But the actual truth is that there are in the Quran some matters of which, even along with the meaning being understood, the creatures cannot ascertain what the very inner meaning is and how the reality is. That is also merely natural. The linguistic meaning of the words and sentences of the Quran is understood in general indeed; there is no doubt. Even so- by knowing the linguistic meaning and the outward interpretation- it does not follow that it must become clear that the real inner meaning of everything is such-and-such. The attributes of Allah, the experiences in the Hereafter, the descriptions about Paradise and Hell, many matters such as the balance that weighs deeds, and statements such as that Allah has ascended upon the 'Arsh, are indeed examples of this. What is the real form of all these? How is it? and so on- who knows other than Allah? It needs also to be remembered that in human languages there are not even words fit to describe such matters in their true form.

Some words that many Quran commentators have quoted as said by Ibn 'Abbas (r) are worth stating here. He says: ‘Tafsir (Quran commentary) is of four kinds thus: what the Arabs can grasp through their language; what contains religious rulings which no one has the means not to know; what scholars understand; what no one other than Allah can know’. That none other than Allah knows the unseen matters is something Allah has said repeatedly, and is beyond dispute, surely. Then, how can man grasp the realities of unseen matters like those mentioned above! It is because of all this, too, that the majority of the great Quran commentators, such as Ibn Jarir (r), have adopted the first opinion (the opinion that one should make waqf at the place **الاالله** and begin another statement from **والراسخون**). This is also what is right.

Some great men have pointed out some secrets of sagacity contained in some part of the Quran being mutashabih, without the whole of the Quran being muhkam. Since most of them can be found in the Quran sentences that follow, it is not quoted here. Whichever opinion seen above is adopted in the *[p. 722]* definition and explanation of the mutashabih, it is certain that, for those who have knowledge and discretion, it will increase faith and intent reflection. Yes, they will say **آمنابه كل من عند ربنا** (We have believed in it, all is indeed from our Rabb) **ومايذكرإلا أولوالألباب** (None will reflect intently, moreover, except those who have good intellect.) As a result of their faith and intent reflection, they will not venture to misinterpret them -the mutashabihs-. Because, in their hearts, there will not be any crookedness. That they must not go astray from the straight path that Allah gave them, that they must be worthy of the mercy of Allah, that on the Day of Qiyamah they must not become worthy of the wrath of Allah, and so on, will be their aim. Yes, they will keep supplicating for that.
