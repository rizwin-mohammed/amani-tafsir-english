*[Verses 23–25]*

*[p. 733]* The People of the Scripture, who claim to be followers of the Torah and the Injil, are bound to accept, in the matter of obeying Allah and the Rasul, the judgements that have been stated in those same books. Even so, if they are invited to that, they withdraw without accepting that invitation. They also know that this is deserving of punishment and wrong. That, whatever faults and offences we commit, at the most we will have to suffer the punishment of Hell only for fixed days, and so on: this they have fabricated by themselves beforehand in the name of religion. In the course of time they accepted all that as principles of religion and have become deceived too; this is the reason. There is no need to keep holding such blind and baseless notions. A day is to come on which, gathering everyone together, fitting action is taken on the doings of each one without any injustice at all; that day they will have to regret. By that regret no benefit at all will be obtained either. Therefore it is better for them to seek the way of deliverance even now: so Allah warns them. (The import of their having said that the punishment of Hell will not affect us except for a few days has been explained already in Al-Baqarah 80 and in the commentary).

*[Verses 26–27]*

*[p. 734]* The People of the Scripture are those who reckon prophethood, majesty, kingship and the like as their monopoly right, and who take false pride in nobility of birth. That is also one main reason that urges them to denial of the truth and to defiance. These verses are a refutation of such claims. While the true believers keep suffering poverty and hardship, their enemy camps live in comforts and luxuries and in majesty. If it is the true believers who are pleasing to Allah, is it not they who ought to receive *[p. 735]* more of life's comforts and conveniences and ability? The answer to the question that usually comes from the disbelievers is also contained in these verses.

Although outwardly these verses are in the form of a supplication made addressing Allah, there is no doubt that the matters pointed out above are contained in it. Yes,through these verses Allah teaches the true believers a great supplication, and a number of realities of principle. It is also worth stating that Tabarani (r) and others have quoted some Hadiths that show that, if supplication is made through these verses, it will not fail to get an answer.

Even without the help of a commentary, the idea in these verses is clear. Their gem of a summary can be understood thus. O Allah, it is You who graciously bestows all kinds of ranks and positions and good things, and who takes them away, and who controls night and day, and who creates and sustains all things, that are living beings and lifeless things. Everything is included in Your ownership and Your authority of control alone. Nothing will happen except according to Your will and Your plan. No one other than You has any share or right in any of it. Therefore do shower upon us Your generosity and Your gracious regard!

The gist of having said ‘making the night enter into the day and the day into the night’ is: when the night decreases, increasing that decrease in the day, and, when the day decreases, increasing that decrease in the night. It can also be meant as the day merging into the night following sunset, and the night into the day following dawn. (See what has been explained about this in places such as Surah Hajj 61, Luqman 29) About making lifeless things from living beings, and living things from lifeless things, nothing in particular needs to be said. Man from semen, and semen from man, the plant from the seed, and the seed from the plant, the egg from the bird, and the bird from the egg, the worm from manure, and manure from the worm: so we keep seeing countless examples daily. Although mankind may be able to enumerate some outward causes for all this, in reality the cause of those causes, and the One who arranges and controls them, is Allah, surely. For the very statement that He will give to whomever He wills, and that He will take away from whomever He wills, if the history of past and present times is examined, examples in plenty can be seen - without distinction of individual, or distinction of community -. Communities that had continued for hundreds and thousands of years with extreme majesty and fame and heritage suddenly turning into the most abject of the abject, and great ones who had gained the seal of renown throughout the whole world suddenly turning, in the eyes of the world, into the lowest of the low, and the opposite of this happening: these are sights usually seen in experience at all times, surely. Yes, everything is Allah's creation. Everything is His command ( **أَلَا لَهُ ٱلۡخَلۡقُ وَٱلۡأَمۡرُ** )

*[Verse 28]*

*[p. 736]* Allah strictly forbids the true believers' accepting disbelieving individuals or groups as friends and managers, establishing ties of affection with them. He also gives a heavy warning that those who do so have no connection at all with Allah- or that he has no mercy and love to expect from Allah -. But He also makes known that, in case it is feared that some danger, or unbearable wrong, or persecutions may come about from their side, there is no objection to establishing friendship and ties of affection with them outwardly, as a precaution for obtaining deliverance from that. But He has also reminded, in addition, that it must be guarded against that that relationship exceeds the limit, or becomes more than necessary, and that otherwise Allah will take action. That a close relationship with people of the enemy camp will become harmful to Islam and to the Muslims is the reason for so strict a warning in this matter. Not only that, that one does not set enough value on the good of one's own religion and community, and that one is in agreement with the stand of the enemies: that, surely, is *[p. 737]* the meaning of regarding them as friends.

Allah says: ‘O you who have believed, do not make the Jews and the Christians friends. Some of them are indeed the friends of some (of one another among themselves). If anyone from among you makes them friends, he will be of them,(Ma'idah :51) ‘Some of those who have disbelieved are the friends of some’ (they are not friends to you) In case you do not do so, there will be disorder and great ruin on the earth’. (Anfal:73) ‘O you who have believed, do not make those who are My enemy and your enemy friends, showing them affection......’ (Mumtahanah :1) It will be fitting to the occasion to look at the full portion of this verse and its commentary in Sura: Mumtahanah: Imam Bukhari (r)quotes that Abud-Darda' (r) stated; we will show our teeth (smiling) to the faces of some people. Our hearts will also be cursing. The gist, that is, is that when we see some of our enemies, in our minds there will be hatred and disgust towards them. As the interpretation of having said **تقية** (showing caution) in this verse, Imam Thawri (r) and others quote thus from Ibn 'Abbas(r): Caution is not by deed. Caution is only by the tongue’ That is, the gist is that, other than showing affection in speech and in behaviour, there must not be affection with the heart in harmony, or activities based on ties of affection. Allah has made clear (in Surah: Nahl 106 ) that, at a stage when there is no other way out, if one has to say some words of kufr while keeping true faith firm in the heart, there is no objection to that.

It ought to be remembered specially that all that was stated above is about disbelievers who keep up enmity with the Muslims, and who keep plotting to harm and to wrong them if they get the opportunity. Towards disbelievers who go on behaving in a good manner with one another, without hiding enmity in the mind or keeping on showing it outwardly, the Muslims, on their side, ought to behave in affection and in friendliness indeed. Allah has stated this matter clearly in verses 8, 9 of Suratul Mumtahanah. More information concerning dealing with non-Muslims can be seen in those verses and in their commentary. Look there.

*[Verses 29–30]*

*[p. 738]* Looking grammatically, it is possible for everything from **يَوۡمَ تَجِدُ** up to **مِن سُوٓءٖ** to come together as one sentence, and for the second sentence to begin from **تَوَدُّ**. Then the meaning of the first sentence can be stated thus: ‘The day on which each and every soul -or person- finds what it has done as good, and what it has done as evil, brought present *[p. 739]*! It need not be said that this is meant of the day of Qiyamah. That is, surely, the day when one sees in front all the good and evil deeds done in this world. The meaning of the second sentence can also be stated thus: ‘If only there had been, between it and it, or between the soul and that day - a far-off distance (it would have been well), so it will long’ Either way, the gist is that those who have done evil will, on the day of Qiyamah, come to be in endless grief because of it.

### Section – 4

*[Verses 31–32]*

The People of the Scripture, who are the Jews and the Christians that claim that we are the children and the favourites of Allah ( **نَحۡنُ أَبۡنَـٰٓؤُاْ ٱللَّهِ وَأَحِبَّـٰٓؤُهُۥ** ), those who, under the pretence that it is for gaining nearness to Allah- worship and supplicate to those other than Allah, directly or indirectly, those who are called ‘Ahlul Quranis’, who, claiming that it is we who in reality obey Allah, disregard and throw out the practices of the Prophet and the sayings of the Prophet: for all such as these, a fitting refutation is contained in these verses. Those who do not obey the Rasul and do not follow the way of the Rasul are *[p. 740]* disbelievers whom Allah does not like: that indeed is what the last verse shows. The matter of obeying the Rasul is not - as some light-minded people think - merely some trivial subject. All the prophets and all the Rasuls who passed before would have been bound to walk following the Prophet ﷺ. That subject is so serious. More information about this can be seen below in the 81st verse.( **ان شاءالله** )

As was explained before in Suratul Fatihah, it is from love that God-fearing reverence, and obedience, and respect originate. Everyone who claims that he loves Allah is bound to accept the way that the noble Prophet ﷺ showed. That is why the Prophet ﷺ said thus in a Hadith: ‘If anyone does a deed that is not in the manner in which our matter is, it will be rejected’ (Bu). The claim that one loves Allah is realised by way of accepting the practice of the noble Rasul ﷺ. It is those who do so that Allah too will love. Not only that, He will also forgive them their sins. Obeying Allah and obeying the Rasul are not different. Both are one and the same. That is the gist of what Allah has said. Elsewhere Allah says: **مَّن يُطِعِ ٱلرَّسُولَ فَقَدۡ أَطَاعَ ٱللَّهَ** (Whoever obeys the Rasul, he has obeyed Allah. (Nisa' : 80)

*[Verses 33–34]*

As was pointed out at the beginning of the surah, whether or not all the more than eighty verses from the beginning came down following the Christian deputation that came from Najran, in these verses too *[p. 741]* and in a few verses that follow, it is subjects connected with the said event that are contained. Prophet Adam (a) is, surely, the father of mankind and the first prophet. The first known Rasul after him is Prophet Nuh (a). The eminent prophet who received great ranks and positions in many respects in the history of the Rasuls appointed thereafter is Prophet Ibrahim (a). His lines of offspring are indeed the Isra'ilites and the Arabs. One main branch belonging to the Isra'ilites is the family of 'Imran (Alu 'Imran) The gist is that Allah has given all these superiority over the other peoples of their respective times.

The opinion of some is that by 'Imran ( **عمران** ) is meant 'Imran, the father of the prophets Musa and Harun (a), and that both of them are included in the word the family of 'Imran. Another opinion is indeed that what is meant is 'Imran, the father of Maryam Bivi, the mother of Prophet 'Isa (a), and his family. When the fact that the main subject treated in the verses that follow is about 'Isa (a), and the beginning of the next verse, ‘When the woman of 'Imran said’, are looked at, precedence is seen for this second opinion. By the woman of 'Imran too, those of the first opinion mean a woman belonging to the family of 'Imran. This does not seem so good. In any case, these verses stand as a preface, as it were, to the statements about the prophets 'Isa, Zakariyya and Yahya (a) in the verses that follow. A hint that Allah knows in full detail all the histories from Adam (a) up to the family of 'Imran and after it can be seen to be in the last sentence, Allah is One who hears everything and One who knows ( **وَٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ** ). *[TN: the Arabic here is printed in plain script with the article on the middle word (السميع for سميع); verse 34 reads وَٱللَّهُ سَمِيعٌ عَلِيمٌ, which is given here as the Quran text.]* ( **والله اعلم** )

*[Verse 35]*

*[p. 742]* The wife of 'Imran is the mother of Maryam Bivi (a). Her name is known to be Hannah ( **حنة** ). She had not had children born to her; she supplicated for a child. When she became pregnant, she vowed a vow to make the child in the womb, if it was born, an offering for the service of Bait al-Muqaddas, and for carrying out acts of worship there; the child's father had died while she was still pregnant. Many things such as these are seen stated. It was customary at that time to make boys an offering for the service of Bait al-Muqaddas. Accordingly that noble lady too did so. She may have expected that the baby she would give birth to would be a boy. She may also have had the wish that it should turn out a boy; that it must be for that reason that she made it a vow so even before giving birth is what is understood from the next verse. The intention of having said making one who has been set free ( **مُحَرَّرٗا** ) is indeed to make one who does not turn attention to anything other than the service of the mosque and worship.
