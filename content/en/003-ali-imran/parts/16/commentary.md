### Section – 16

*[Verses 149–151]*

*[p. 868]* After advising that the true believers who stood firm in the footprints of the prophets must be made an example, He is warning about obeying the disbelievers and making them managers. He also alerts that, if you obey them, they will turn you back to the religion of shirk itself, and you will become ones who have lost everything; that your Master is Allah- it is He who is your best helper-; and that it is obeying Him that is your liability.

Exploiting the defeat that befell the Muslims at Uhud, the Jews and the hypocrites made an attempt to create confusion and doubt among them. It is the warning that if you obey them you will come into hardship *[p. 869]* that is contained in this. Continuing, for the true believers, also a piece of glad tidings like this. Although for the time being these disbelievers have obtained an occasion for false pride, it is not permanent. In the near future itself the situation will turn right over and strike back. Their hearts will fill with terror. Because, those who keep worshipping things other than Allah without any authority or justification, none of those things will help them. Allah's help will not be obtained by them at all, either. Hell will be their destination. Therefore, you who are true believers need not be in terror and worry about them.

Jabir (r) quotes that the Prophet ﷺ said: ‘Five things have been given to me that were not given to any one among the prophets before me. I have been given help by terror (that comes about to the enemies about me) as far as the distance of a month's way. The earth (everywhere) has been made for me a place of prayer and a thing for purification. ‘Ghanimah’ properties have been made lawful for me. ‘Shafa'ah’ (the permission to intercede in the Hereafter) has been given to me. The (other) prophets had been commissioned to their own people only; I have been commissioned to all mankind indeed.’ (Bu; Mu.) The gist is that the enemies as far as a month's travelling distance had terror and fear about the Prophet ﷺ and the followers, and that this is one of the special features of the Prophet ﷺ. The pages of history bear witness to this, too. That is indeed the fulfilment of this sacred saying of Allah.

*[Verses 152–153]*

*[p. 871]* In the first stage of the war it was a huge victory for the Muslims. The standard-bearers of the mushriks were killed, and they began to leave the place and run off. What was said, that Allah made His promise true to you when you were killing and destroying them, is about this. Watching for the occasion when the battlefield was becoming empty because of the enemies' turning and running, many among the Muslims became engaged in collecting the weapons and so on that they had left behind. When they saw this, the archers whom the Prophet ﷺ had stationed on the hilltop- except their head and a small few individuals- came down to the battlefield and joined in collecting the wealth. It was that the war had ended, and that the enemies had been defeated, that they presumed. Nor did they take into account that the Prophet ﷺ had commanded them not to leave the place without permission, whatever might happen. It is this that paved the way for all the calamities. That is, it is the enemies' making proper use of this gap that became the cause of their striking hard again and of calamities befalling the Muslims. What was said, ‘when you showed cowardice, and became divided with one another, and, after He showed you the thing you like, showed disobedience again...’, is about this scene. Yes, abandoning bravery they showed cowardice, abandoning unity they became divided, and abandoning obedience they showed disobedience. All this happened after the first victory, surely. The gist is that with this, the help from Allah stopped and all that was to happen happened.

That what was said, that they fell out with one another- or became divided-, and that they showed disobedience, is about the archers can be understood from what was said above. What they did was to act against the command of the Prophet ﷺ, and the command of their leader. Saying such things as, now why must one stand here, *[p. 872]* they also carried on a dispute. It was in order to collect the wealth that the archers left the place, surely. Many who were on the battlefield became engaged in it. But, the Companions had not all taken part in it. That is what Allah said, that among you there are those who intend this world and those who intend the Hereafter. It has been reported that Ibn Mas'ud (r) said thus: ‘I had not thought that there was anyone among the Companions of the noble Rasul ﷺ who intended this world, until this verse came down about us in the Uhud event.

Thus many blunders came about among the Companions. Owing to that, they also experienced a great many hardships and losses. Although in the second attack there was a situation in which the enemies could, if they wished, have destroyed the Muslims, what they did was, without making use of it, to quickly sound the drum of victory and leave the place. This is indeed a great favour that Allah did for the Muslims. It is about this that it was said afterwards, He turned you away from them ( **ثُمَّ صَرَفَكُمۡ عَنۡهُمۡ** ). All this is in order to put you to trial; therefore hereafter you need to learn a lesson from this event; what happened this time Allah has given you pardon for. Hereafter do not repeat it like this; you are true believers, surely; Allah is One who does great generosity to the true believers; it is because of that that He gives you pardon and does not take any punitive action. Such things are what is made known in the verses that follow.

Then, in the 153rd verse, He calls to mind the stage when the course of events reached its climax. With their seeing the enemies' return and re-attack, unthought-of and unexpected, the Muslims became bewildered with fear. They came to not know what to do. Some ran straight to Madinah. Some left the place without any particular direction. Some climbed upwards along the mountain. At this time the noble Rasul ﷺ was standing firm without moving from his own position. O servants of Allah, come here! (‘Ilayya 'ibadallah) ( **إِلَى عِبَادُ اللهِ** )’ *[DOUBT: the Arabic is transcribed as printed (300 dpi crop): إِلَى ("to") and عِبَادُ with damma. The author's Malayalam transliteration "ഇലയ്യ ഇബാദല്ലാഹ്" ("ilayya 'ibadallah") gives إِلَيَّ ("to me"), and the call as usually reported is إِلَيَّ عِبَادَ اللهِ, with fatha on the vocative عِبَادَ. Either a printing slip, or the author's own spelling; kept as printed.]*, the noble one kept calling out from behind. But they, running for their lives, do not look back anywhere. A headlong flight indeed. Meanwhile, also a false report that the Prophet ﷺ had been killed! There is no clue at all as to what the future is. If that were not enough, also the fear whether the enemies might go straight into Madinah and destroy houses and families and all! Altogether, grief through grief! Distress with distress! All this happened through their own deeds indeed. At the same time, some secrets were also contained in it, moreover. Yes, through it Allah was training the true believers to hold to patience and endurance in calamities. Whatever might happen, or be lost, hereafter they must not grieve or feel pain over any of it. To match that, their faith must become firmly established, and their strength of mind must grow strong. ( **لِّكَيۡلَا تَحۡزَنُواْ**.... ) All the deeds of everyone, and all the *[p. 873]* emotions that motivate them, Allah is One who knows properly and minutely, surely. ( **وَٱللَّهُ خَبِيرُۢ بِمَا تَعۡمَلُونَ** )

*[Verse 154]*

*[p. 874]* Although the Companions at Uhud had to experience a great deal of trouble and hardships, afterwards, on the battlefield itself- Allah gave them tranquillity and peace of mind. Their hearts obtained ease, as if nothing had befallen them. Yes, at that time Allah cast upon them a most wonderful *[p. 875]* drowsiness of slumber. With that, the anxious thought about the past calamities went away from them. When the noble Prophet ﷺ and a small few Companions became isolated, and the enemies made their most fierce attacks, one among those who, for the protection of the Prophet ﷺ, without regard even for their own lives, stood resisting the enemies was Abu Talhah al-Ansari (r). He is saying: ‘At Uhud, while we were standing in our ranks itself, a drowsiness of slumber befell us. I too was one among those whom that drowsiness befell that day. My sword kept falling from my hand, and I kept taking it up in my hand.’ This is what many, such as Bukhari, Muslim (r), have reported. In some reports it has come that he also said: ‘Owing to the drowsiness, not even one- my head has not failed to droop below his shield’ *[DOUBT: printed "മയക്കം നിമിത്തം ഒരാളും തന്നെ എന്റെ തല തന്റെ പരിചയുടെ താഴേക്ക് ചരിയാതിരുന്നിട്ടില്ല", literally "owing to the drowsiness, not even one (person) — my head has not failed to droop to below his (own) shield". The sentence does not agree with itself: ഒരാളും തന്നെ ("not even one") and തന്റെ ("his own") call for "there was not even one whose head did not droop below his shield", but എന്റെ തല ("my head") is printed. Either a printing slip (for ഒരാളുടെയും തല, "anyone's head"), or the author's wording; translated as printed.]*. That the drowsiness of a sleep came about like this at this stage is indeed an extraordinary favour that they obtained from Allah, certainly. But we do not feel any wonder at some rationalists who have lost spiritual sight portraying and seeing this drowsiness ( the word **نُّعَاسٗا** ) only as a simile. Because, that is something that does not digest with their reason, surely.

This drowsiness had not befallen everyone on the Muslim side. It had befallen only the true believers in whose faith hypocrisy and taint had not touched. That is why Allah said, ‘it was enveloping a section from among you’. That the section of people who did not experience the drowsiness, and the mental tranquillity obtained through it, were hypocrites is clear from the description about them. What came about for them, instead of peace of mind, was disquiet and agitation. Except about their own affairs, they have no concern about Islam, or about the Prophet ﷺ, or about the true believers. Islam has collapsed and fallen flat with that, now it has no survival, Allah's promise that there would be victory for the Muslims has not been kept, and so on- such notions are what is in their minds. Yes, the thoughts and attitudes of the Jahiliyyah period themselves. ‘Muhammad did not listen to anything we said. Do we have any share in the conduct of affairs?! If there were, nothing like this would have happened. Our people would not have been slain like this, either.’ They kept saying many things like this. That is only some of what sits in their minds. The gist of the reply that Allah gives them to that is this: The affairs all belong to Allah. Everything happens only according to His authority and control and intention. The matter of death too is not exempt from this. He has already determined that such and such a person will be killed at such and such a place at such and such a time. It will not fail to happen like that. Even those who shut themselves in the house without going out will not fail to come out at that time and place, and be killed. Besides, all this is also a trial and a purification to show separated the hypocrites and the deceivers among you.

*[Verse 155]*

*[p. 876]* When war takes place between the Muslim band and the enemy band, the Muslims are ones who must stand firm, resolved on either victory, or else a heroic death (‘shahadah’). That many among the Muslims at Uhud turned back and met defeat, and that some ran away leaving the place, and all, is because they fell into the snare of Satan. Allah reminds the Muslims again that the cause of that is the faults and offences that came about from themselves indeed. In any case, along with that He also consoles them, adding that Allah has given them pardon for those faults that have gone past. Allah is One who forgives much, and One who has much forbearance, surely.
