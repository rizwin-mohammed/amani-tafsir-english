### Section – 18

*[Verses 172–174]*

*[p. 895]* Although defeat was experienced at the beginning of the battle, after striking back again owing to the slips of hand that occurred on the part of the Muslims and bringing about huge harms, what the mushriks did was to leave the place quickly, arrogantly. The Prophet ﷺ felt that perhaps they might come back again and attack Madinah and bring destruction. It is also said that such a piece of news had spread. Therefore, on the very next day, the noble one invited the people to pursue the enemies. The intention of the Prophet ﷺ was to make the enemies understand that the vigour of the Muslims had not perished, and to produce fear in them. The noble one also commanded that no one other than those who had taken part in Uhud must come along with him. A band of Companions accepted the invitation *[TN: the print has ക്ഷണനം, evidently a slip for ക്ഷണം ("invitation"), as in Ali 'Imran part 06 (p. 765); so again on p. 896.]* and got ready. That, although he had not taken part in Uhud, by the special permission of the Prophet ﷺ Jabir (r) too was in that group, *[p. 896]* Ibn Ishaq (r) and others have stated. In his house there were seven women, as his sisters. As men, only he and his father 'Abdullah ibn Husam (r) *[DOUBT: printed "അബ്ദുല്ലാഹിബ്നു ഹുസാം" (Husam), clear on the image, and the same in the amanithafseer.com text; Jabir's father is generally known as 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr ibn Haram, so "ഹുസാം" may be a printing slip for "ഹറാം" (Haram). Translated as printed.]* were in the house. It was after entrusting Jabir (r) with looking after them that the father went to the battle. He also became a martyr in the battle. Jabir (r) requested of the Prophet ﷺ that this was the reason for his not taking part in the battle, and that therefore he must be permitted to take part in this journey. The noble one also permitted it. The Prophet ﷺ and the band reached Hamra' al-Asad, situated eight nazhika away. What the noble one had guessed was right. At having returned quickly without entering Madinah and carrying out an attack following their winning in the battle, the enemies came to feel regret midway on the road. Thus, they had got ready to turn back and return. Owing to this coming of the Prophet ﷺ and of the band, they became afraid. They thought that many who had not taken part in the battle yesterday would be in that band, and that the Muslims would have obtained new strength and influence they also quickly continued the return journey to Makkah.

The 172nd verse is one praising the Companions who went and took part in this band. It was without valuing the calamities and injuries that befell them in the battle the day before that they accepted the invitation *[TN: ക്ഷണനം again, as above.]* of the Prophet ﷺ, surely. It has also come in some riwayahs that it was while being unable, owing to the injuries some Companions had sustained, even to walk properly, supporting one another and helping, that they went. It is so that such great readiness for sacrifice of theirs may become an excellent model for the true believers of all times that Allah has preserved this memory of them in the Quran. It is about another exemplary event of the Companions that it is stated in the 173rd verse. so indeed many Quran commentators say. The gist of that event is thus:-

When, the battle of Uhud being over, the enemy army was going back sounding the drum of victory, their commander-in-chief Abu Sufyan (\*) had called out that the next meeting would be at Badr next year, and the Prophet ﷺ had agreed to it. Badr was a place where a huge trade fair used to take place year after year. When the fixed time came, owing to the Quraish having been seized by famine, they were *[p. 897]* not able to make war preparations. To escape from the disgrace of breaking the promise, Abu Sufyan employed a ruse. To carry out propaganda in Madinah that the Quraish were carrying out huge war preparations; thus to produce fear in the inhabitants of Madinah and to make the Muslims lose heart so that they would not get ready for battle. This was the ruse. For this, fixing a huge prize too, he also instructed and sent a man called Nu'aim ibn Mas'ud. Then, it can be understood that the saying **قَالَ لَهُمُ ٱلنَّاسُ**.... (men said to them: those people have gathered persons to face you) will have been with reference to this false propaganda carried out under the leadership of Nu'aim. It is also understood from some riwayahs that a caravan of the 'Abd Qais tribe, and the hypocrites of Madinah, had a part in this propaganda. Anyhow, the Prophet ﷺ and the true believers attached no value at all to that evil propaganda. Yes, **فَزَادَهُمۡ إِيمَٰنٗا وَقَالُواْ**.... (it both increased faith for them, and they said: Allah is sufficient for us! How very good One relied upon He is!) The Prophet ﷺ, for his part, did keep the word indeed. That is:

> **Note:** (\*) Abu Sufyan was one of the chief ones among the Quraish leaders it was he who led many of the battles the Quraish carried out against Islam. At the time of the conquest of Makkah he became a Muslim. Afterwards he took part in the battle of Hunain. After the Prophet ﷺ, he also took part in the famous battle of Yarmuk. Amir Mu'awiyah (r) is his son.

Leading an army of one thousand five hundred people, in Sha'ban of the next year the Prophet ﷺ went and alighted at Badr. The Quraish did not come. Abu Sufyan supposed that that propaganda carried out in Madinah would have taken effect. Even so, to make it appear that he had kept the word, Abu Sufyan, setting out from Makkah with an army, did also turn back after a journey of one or two days. Since it was the time when the trade fair of Badr takes place, the Muslims thus also obtained an opportunity to carry on huge trade and gain profit in such a way as not to face the rivalry and enmity of the mushriks. This event is known by the name of the Lesser Badr ( **بَدْر الصَغِير** )’.

As described above, the opinion of the majority of the commentators is that the event indicated in the 172nd verse is the Hamram al-Asad *[TN: printed "ഹംറാംഉൽ അസദ്" here; on p. 896 the name is printed "ഹംറാഉൽ അസദ്" (Hamra' al-Asad).]* event, and that what is indicated in the 173rd verse is the Lesser Badr event. They also say that, although the Lesser Badr event took place after one year had passed, because the battle of Uhud and it are connected with each other, all have been described together. That both these verses are about the Lesser Badr event itself is indeed the opinion of some commentators. But- as Ibn Jarir (r) and others have pointed out- the phrase **مِنۢ بَعۡدِ مَآ أَصَابَهُمُ ٱلۡقَرۡحُ** (after wound happened to them) in the 172nd verse gives strength to the opinion stated first. The Lesser Badr event was after one year had passed, surely. By then the wounds sustained at Uhud the year before would mostly have healed, surely. The cause of this difference of opinion must be that the subjects referred to in both verses are seen to be more or less in agreement with both events, and that the statements in the riwayahs that have come concerning both are found intermixed with one another. The statements in the 174th verse, that it was with *[p. 898]* favour and generosity from Allah that they returned, and that no evil at all befell them, and so on, do not go against either of these two opinions, moreover. **والله اعلم**

Bukhari (r) quotes Ibn 'Abbas (r) as having said: ‘**حَسۡبُنَا ٱللَّهُ وَنِعۡمَ ٱلۡوَكِيلُ** (Allah is sufficient for us. How very good He is to be relied upon.) the Prophet Ibrahim (a) too, on the occasion when he was put into the fire, and, when the people said **إِنَّ ٱلنَّاسَ قَدۡ جَمَعُواْ لَكُمۡ**.... (men have gathered persons to face you), the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ too, did say.’

*[Verse 175]*

The Quraish are carrying out a huge gathering of troops to attack Madinah, they may destroy this land. You must be afraid of them, threats in this manner like those seen above, and pieces of false propaganda and all, are of Satan's making. It is he and his followers who are its spokesmen his aim is to produce fear in you about the disbelievers, who are his kinsmen, make you devoid of vigour, and take advantage. You who are true believers must not yield to any such threat at all. The purport is that, since you are true believers, it is only Allah whom you must fear.

*[Verses 176–178]*

*[p. 900]* These verses are applicable to all who, though plenty of proofs and signs needful and prompting for accepting true faith and the straight way were in front of them, without valuing any of that, hurry to fall into the very path of the rejection of the truth with a spirit of rivalry and a defiant mentality. Statements like this can be seen in many places in the Quran. (See 9: 55, 85; 23:55, 56; 68:44, 45 and so on.) Seeing the people standing obstinately in disbelief though there were sufficiently clear proofs, the noble Prophet ﷺ used to have great grief. (See 18:6; 26:3 and so on.) There is no need to grieve thus; owing to their defiance, no harm at all is going to befall Allah, or His religion; Allah has let them loose with the thought that they may go on doing all that they do: they themselves will have to experience its evil result. With such things Allah is consoling the Prophet ﷺ.

*[Verse 179]*

*[p. 901]* It will not do without there coming about a set of circumstances in which, the position in which the real believers and the hypocrites are not distinguished having changed, the two groups can be seen taking clear shape apart from each other. Until that comes about, Allah will keep conducting some trials like those that happened at Uhud. Concerning each individual, Allah knows well all his secrets and open matters. But that is among the unseen matters. Knowledge of the unseen, for its part, belongs only to Allah. None of the creatures- not even the prophets- know it creatures can know external and visible matters. But, it may be that He makes known some unseen information to His Rasuls. That too will be only for those whom He wills, and only the matters He wills. Those Rasuls, and through them their followers, can know those matters. That is all. Therefore, it is necessary that you believe properly in Allah, who knows all the unseen things, and in the Rasuls who are His message-bearers. For those who go on believing thus, and observing caution, a huge reward from Allah will also be obtained.

About the meanings and intents of the word **غَيْب** (unseen matter), it has been explained in the commentary on Su: Al-Baqarah: the 3rd verse. Many explanations concerning the knowledge of unseen matters can be seen in Su: An'am: 50; Naml: 65; Jinn: 26; and such verses, and in their commentaries.

*[Verse 180]*

*[p. 902]* Many things were stated before this about sacrifice with the body in the path of Allah. In this verse it encourages sacrifice with wealth, and warns about miserliness. There is no doubt that the intended sense of ‘showing miserliness in what Allah has given by His favour’ is chiefly being stingy in spending wealth. Even so, not using at the time of need favours such as knowledge, health, that Allah has given, is also indeed included in reprehensible stinginess. As Ibn Kathir (r) and others have indicated, it is this stinginess that will on some occasions become more serious than stinginess in wealth. Likewise too, all kinds of stinginess in carrying out financial duties, zakah and other than it, and in doing good causes such as almsgiving, are reproached. But, all stinginess in obligatory matters will be culpable and subject to punishment. As regards a Muslim, besides fixed duties like zakah, there are also, moreover, many other financial duties to be carried out according to the occasion and the opportunity, and according to the measure of ability.

*[p. 903]* Allah warns that the properties locked up and kept without spending in good ways and without paying off duties will be put on, on the Day of Qiyamah, as neck ornaments upon the misers who are their owners. That the property on which zakah has not been paid off will be made a huge serpent and, on the Day of Qiyamah, its owner too will be garlanded and punished (Bu.) and that on the one who encroached on another's land, that land will be put as a garland on the Day of Qiyamah. (Bu; Mu.) and so on, what the Prophet ﷺ has stated are explanations about some aspects of this verse. None of those are merely some figurative usages, or similes. Only for those who measure and reckon the realities of the Hereafter and material experiences with the same criterion will there come the plight of having to count all that as figurative usages. For those who know that the punishment that is experienced in the Hereafter for each offence will be in a form suited to that offence, and that many realities which it is not possible even to imagine in this world will take place in the Hereafter, it is possible to accept all such statements of Allah and of the Rasul in their clear meaning itself.

Allah has given man a temporary right and opportunity to manage wealth. But, its real owner is Allah. In the end, all kinds of management of everything also become vested in Him alone. Therefore man has entitlement in wealth only to carry out management according to Allah's commands and directions. Otherwise, man has no authority to lock it up and keep it, and not to spend it in the matter He commands. As in wealth, so indeed in the other favours Allah has given. How man manages all of them, and what all he does, Allah also keeps knowing and watching minutely. Such things are what Allah reminds of at the end.
