### Section – 17

*[Verses 156–158]*

*[p. 878]* The disbelievers, surely, are those who have no belief in Allah's decrees and determinations, and in that everything will happen only according to His intention. Therefore, when their friends or those related to them die on some journeys, or, in some war, are killed by enemies, they may say, ‘Ah! Had he been right here, this would not have happened!’ and so on. They do not know at all, surely, such things as that life and death are what Allah determines, and that even if they do not leave the house, death will indeed seize them at the fixed time. Their notion will be that had they left the house they would live for some more time *[DOUBT: printed "വീട് വിട്ടുപോയിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ" ("had they left the house"), as also in the amanithafseer.com text. The sense of the passage seems to call for "വിട്ടുപോകാതിരുന്നെങ്കിൽ" ("had they not left the house"). Either a printing slip, or the author's wording in a sense not clear to the translator; translated as printed.]*. Thus, they will come to be in great regret and lamentation over the ones who died having met an untimely death, and over their having left the house so as to give occasion for it. Quite the opposite, for the true believers who firmly believe that every matter takes place according to Allah's determination, there is no right in any of that to regret or to lamentation; nor is that befitting for them; that being the situation, such thoughts or words must not come about among the true believers on account of their own people, or their dear ones, having been lost at Uhud or elsewhere. Allah alerts the true believers to such things. Notions and words like these are usually displayed among some of the common Muslim people too. Those who are like that need especially to take this 156th verse to heart.

Whether death happens in a natural form, or because of causes like killing- provided that it is in the path of Allah- it is the forgiveness for sins and the mercy that are to be obtained from Allah that will be better than all that can be earned in this world. Therefore, that one's dying is in the path of Allah must be regarded only as a good fortune. Whoever, however, whenever one dies, it is to the presence of Allah indeed that all return. Then, He will examine the circumstances of each one and take fitting action in respect of each one, too. Such is the gist of what was said afterwards.

*[Verses 159–160]*

*[p. 880]* At Uhud, many blunders and disobedience came about among the Companions. Owing to that, many calamities were experienced too. Even so, the Prophet ﷺ did not deal with them harshly, or severely. It was with great gentleness and mildness that he dealt. As their leader and superior, he had not dealt in a tone of authority and a tone of reproach. Had he done so, it would have produced weariness and aversion in the Companions and would have become a cause for their keeping away from the Prophet ﷺ. Allah is praising this excellent character of the Prophet ﷺ. This, surely, is a rank that other leaders cannot attain. He also alerts that such a great quality of character having come to be in the Prophet ﷺ is a great favour that Allah, owing to His great mercy, gave to the Prophet ﷺ , and to the true believers. To those who have had the good fortune to become acquainted even a little with the conduct and history of the noble Prophet ﷺ, it will not be impossible to understand more or less about the exceptional excellences of character of the noble one. It can be seen that in more than one place Allah has praised in the Quran the excellent traits of character of the noble one. That the noble one has declared, ‘It is only to complete the excellent traits of character that I have been commissioned.’ is also worth mentioning.

That the Prophet ﷺ was one extremely gentle-natured, and one who dealt with tact and gentleness, does not mean that he will never deal boldly, or that whatever he sees he will indeed shut his eyes. In gentleness where gentleness is needed, and in boldness where boldness is needed, the noble one was unequalled. Plenty of examples of each can be seen in the history of the noble one. In the Uhud event itself there are many examples of this. This, surely, is not the occasion to describe that here. As a complement, as it were, to the gentle disposition and character of the noble one, Allah advises him two or three things:

*[p. 881]* (1) That he must give them pardon. It was said before (in 155), surely, that Allah has pardoned them the fault that came about at their hands in the obligations towards Allah. The gist of this is that the Prophet ﷺ too must pardon the lapse that came about at their hands in the obligations towards the Prophet ﷺ. Although this command is a general command, it need not be said that it is made with particular intent on the matters connected with Uhud.

(2) That he must seek forgiveness of sins for them. The prayer that the prophets make for their followers will be more acceptable with Allah. In one place, in the midst of stating about some corrupt traits of character of the hypocrites, Allah says: ‘When they committed wrong against their own selves, had they come to you, and then they sought forgiveness of sins from Allah and the Rasul too sought forgiveness of sins for them, they could have found Allah One who accepts repentance much and Ever-Merciful.’ (4: 64.)

(3) That he must hold consultation with them in affairs, When one person holds consultation with another person on a subject, besides being able to know that person's opinion too in that matter, the according of a respectable position to him also comes about by means of it. That being the situation, the Rasul ﷺ's consulting with them will produce, surely, more closeness, attachment, and readiness to obey in their minds. It is matters such as public affairs like war, diplomatic affairs, affairs of government, worldly subjects, that are meant here. That is, in religious matters- in how they must be - no one's opinion need be sought. In that only the decision that Allah and His Rasul take has a place. Likewise too, once the Prophet ﷺ has received wahy that on any one of the subjects indicated above it must be in such and such a way, in that matter too there is then no seeking of others' opinion.

When even the noble Prophet ﷺ, who keeps receiving wahy from Allah, is commanded to hold consultation with others in affairs, it can be guessed how great is the necessity for leaders who are mere non-prophets, and rulers and all, to hold consultation with those under them. In the midst of describing some qualities of the true believers who come to be recipients of Allah's favours, Allah says in Su: Shura: **وَأَمۡرُهُمۡ شُورَىٰ بَيۡنَهُمۡ** (their affair will also be one consulted upon among them (Shura: 38) In a hadith that Tirmidhi (r) has quoted, it has come thus: If those in authority over you are the best among you, and your rich men are the open-handed among you, and your affairs are ones on which consultation is held among you, then for you, rather than the inner part of the earth (dying), its upper part (life) *[p. 882]* will be better.... A fuller explanation can be seen in Su: Shura.

That it was said that consultation must be held with others does not mean that the opinions of those consulted must be accepted unconditionally. It is in order, after knowing the opinions of others, to choose from among them what is more reasonable and just, and what is more practicable, and to bring about agreement and satisfaction for them in the decision. It must not be understood from this verse that before this verse came down the Prophet ﷺ did not have the habit of holding consultation with the people in affairs. The fact that before and after it was a quality of the noble one is famous in history. That in the battle of Badr, in the battle of Khandaq, in the Hudaibiyyah event and so on, the noble one held consultation with the Companions on many subjects is indeed well known. Let that be; in the Uhud event itself we saw an example of it, surely. Not as a new command- it is in the manner of praising that excellent character of the Prophet ﷺ, and advising that hereafter too the noble one must keep showing the people an example in it- that this command stands.

Without saying that, after holding consultation, once the decision on how the matter must be has been taken, one must just carry it out, it is, rely upon Allah ( **فَإِذَا عَزَمۡتَ فَتَوَكَّلۡ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ** ), indeed that Allah said. On whatever basis the decision was taken, for carrying it out, and for its final end to be good, Allah's help is indispensable, surely. Therefore, what it shows is that one must move forward believing in His help, and hoping for and asking for it. Along with that, it also indicates that ‘those who do not do tawakkul must not hope for His help. Besides all this, He alerts once again, repeating it, ‘Let the true believers do tawakkul upon Allah’. How great is Allah's interest in the good of His servants!

*[Verses 161–163]*

*[p. 883]* The verb forms **غَلَّ،يغل** (ghalla, yaghullu) are from the root **غلول** (ghulul) to embezzle, to defraud, to cheat, such is its meaning. Although its use is mostly in defrauding from the ‘ghanimah’ properties obtained from the enemy side in war, it is also usual for it to be used in defrauding from public property, or from another's right. Whether it is public property, or property belonging to individuals, embezzling is a severe wrong and a great sin. That being so, *[p. 884]* no prophet will ever do it. Nor is it possible for him to do. Now, if anyone- even if it is a prophet himself- embezzles anything, it is with that thing he has embezzled that he will come on the Day of Qiyamah. Thus, owing to that deed that he did privately without anyone knowing, he is disgraced to the utmost at the great assembly of the Mahshar. Not only that, the punishment for it will also be given to him with the account settled. But, no injustice at all will be shown to him, by giving a punishment exceeding the offence or otherwise, either. Such is the purport of the first verse.

Two kinds of riwayahs can be seen as the occasion of the revelation of this verse:

(1) It was, surely, when they saw the Muslims who were on the battlefield collecting the goods that the enemies had left behind, on the occasion when the enemies at Uhud were at first defeated and ran, that the bowmen who had been standing on the hilltop, seeing that, left the place and came down, and took part in the collecting. The Prophet ﷺ may leave to each, to themselves, the things that each collects, or may not distribute the ghanimah properties equally to all: therefore let us too go and collect what we get, it was saying so among one another that they came down leaving the hill. Afterwards, when the Prophet ﷺ asked them the reason for leaving the place, they also hesitated to say it openly. Then the noble one said to them thus: ‘Or else, what you supposed is that We would deceive, and that We would not distribute properly. It is on this occasion that this verse came down, indeed, is what is seen in some riwayahs. As the subject of reference in these verses is matters connected with the Uhud war, it is this riwayah that is seen to fit more.

(2) In the battle of Badr, a red blanket that had been obtained from the enemies went missing. Some hypocrites said that the Prophet ﷺ must have taken it and put it aside for himself. That the revelation of this verse came about in connection with this is the second riwayah. When the route of report is looked at, it is this that is seen to have more strength. Whichever of the two it is, the purport of the ayah is that no one may suspect that the Prophet ﷺ would not handle the ‘ghanimah’ properties properly, or would not apportion them justly to all; that no prophet will do so.

In the place of **يَغُلُّ** (yaghullu), it has also been read **يُغَلَّ** (yughalla). ‘No prophet may be deceived’ -that is, that no one may do anything by defrauding a prophet, will then be the meaning. This reading is also usually interpreted in the sense that no prophet may be supposed to be one who embezzles. In any case, the intent of all of them is clear, indeed. People who, aiming at Allah's pleasure, and following the path by which pleasure is obtained, *[p. 885]* have become recipients of His pleasure, and people who, following a corrupt path such as deceit that becomes a cause of His anger, have earned only His anger, are not alike. Those of the first section will be deserving of the favours of Paradise and those of the other section of the punishment of Hell. But, within each section itself, according to the stand that each has adopted, there will be difference between one another in position and in ranks. Such is the gist of the verses that follow.

The Quran verses and the sayings of the Prophet that have come about deceit are plentiful. By way of example, let us recall here some sayings of the Prophet:

(1) Abu Humaid al-Sa'idi (r) is saying: ‘The Prophet ﷺ appointed and sent a man of the Azd ( **اَزَد** ) tribe to the work of collecting sadaqah (zakah). When he came, he said, ‘This is for you, this is what I obtained as a ‘hadyah’ (gift)’. Then the Rasul ﷺ went up on the minbar (the pulpit) and said thus: ‘What is the state of some workers?! We appoint him to a work. Then he says: this is for you, this is what I got as hadyah. Should he not look whether, sitting in the house of his father and his mother, he gets hadyah, or not?: He in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, by Him indeed (I swear)! If any one of you brings anything like that, he will not fail to come on the Day of Qiyamah carrying it on his neck. If it is a camel, it will have a roaring, if it is a cow, it will have a lowing *[DOUBT: printed "അകറിച്ചയും" (clear on the image; amanithafseer.com has the same), not a known word. It may be a printing slip for അമറിച്ചയും (from അമറുക, "to low, to bellow"), or a word or spelling not known to the translator. "lowing" is given from the context (the cow's cry, as the footnote says). The reviewer may know its exact sense.]*, and if it is a goat, it will have a crying (\*).’ Then the noble one, holding both hands raised so that the colour of the armpits could be seen, said thus three times: ‘O Allah! Have I conveyed (to the people)?!’ (A; Bu; Mu.) The gist is that the noble one called Allah to witness that he had fulfilled his charge. This hadith is also an explanation of the sentence **وَمَن يَغۡلُلۡ يَأۡتِ بِمَا غَلَّ يَوۡمَ ٱلۡقِيَٰمَةِ** (whoever embezzles, he will come on the Day of Qiyamah with what he embezzled.). It is clear from this hadith that its meaning is that it is bearing those very things that they embezzled that the deceivers will come into the Mahshar on the Day of Qiyamah.

> **Note:** (\*) The intent is that each will utter its own cry of wailing.

(2) The gist of a hadith quoted from Abu Hurairah (r) is this: One day in a sermon, the Rasul ﷺ spoke very severely about those who embezzle. Then he said: ‘Let me not see one of you coming on the Day of Qiyamah carrying on his neck a camel that keeps roaring and crying out, or some other animal! Then he may say: O Rasul of Allah, help me! I will say: ‘I have no power to keep back from you anything that comes from Allah. I have already conveyed (the information beforehand).’ (A; Bu; Mu.)

*[p. 886]* (3) 'Adiyy ibn 'Umairah ( **عدى بن عميرة الكندى ـ رضـ** ) says: The Rasul ﷺ said: ‘O people, if any of you does a work for Us and conceals from it a needle, or anything above it (bigger than it), it is deceit. He will come on the Day of Qiyamah with it.’ Then, a black man from among the Ansaris- it was Sa'd ibn 'Ubadah (r)- stood up and said: ‘O Rasul of Allah, may you take your office (back) from me!’ The noble one asked: ‘What is that?’ He said: ‘I heard you saying such and such. (That itself)’ Then the noble one said: ‘I say it even now. If We entrust a man with a work, he must bring (onto the scene) all, small and great, in it (in what is obtained through it). What is given to him from it he may accept. And he must keep away from what he has been forbidden.’ (A; Mu; Da.) So many examples of honesty without equal, which the Companions showed in following this Quran verse and the advices of the Prophet ﷺ based on it, can be seen in the histories of the Companions.
