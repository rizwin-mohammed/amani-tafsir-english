Israel is the Prophet Ya'qub (a), the father of the Israelite tribes and the grandson of the Prophet Ibrahim (a). Until his time all foodstuffs were, in general, permissible to the Israelites. On the basis that they were what Ya'qub (a) had made forbidden, his descendants too went on reckoning those things as forbidden. Thus, when the Torah came down, either- as can be seen in Nisa', 160 and in An'am, 146- in the course of some things being prohibited to them on account of their wrongdoing, those too were prohibited to them. Or, although there is no clear prohibition about them in the Torah, the Israelites went on observing that prohibition, which they had received traditionally, as a permanent religious law of theirs (there are two opinions thus). This is the doubt. That this is something that was not in the original edition of the Torah, and that belongs among the many things that were later written into it, is indeed the reply to this. If this, or some statement like this, had been in the real Torah, there would be no warrant for this challenge. No evidence worth mentioning is seen as to which things the Prophet Ya'qub (a) had made forbidden, or what the reason for it was. *[p. 810]* What the People of the Scripture had been acknowledging as their centre of pilgrimage and holy place was Bait al-Muqaddas. Therefore, one of the reproaches that they had been putting out about the Prophet ﷺ was that he had been adopting the Ka'bah as the qiblah, and as the centre of pilgrimage. As this reproach comparatively deserves some importance, it can be seen that a reply has been given to it in the Quran in more than one place. One of them, indeed, is this place too. Not only is it that it was established at the hand of the Prophet Ibrahim (a), it is also the first house of worship established for the people in order to worship Allah, and one that contains many signs and excellences. Therefore it is more excellent than all other houses of worship. For all who are able to come there and perform the acts of worship relating to the Hajj, performing it is the duty of mankind to Allah. For all who have entered there, it is also a place of refuge. That being so, the People of the Scripture especially ought to acknowledge it as their centre of worship. Such, in brief, is the *[p. 811]* purport of the reply stated here. That those who are not ready to accept this reality are disbelievers. And it has also reminded that, whoever indeed disbelieves, no harm whatever is going to befall Allah by that.

From having said that the house of worship established first for the people is the Ka'bah, situated in Makkah, which is called Bakka (\*), it is clear that before that there had not been a centre of worship thus. The opinion of many great men is that the Ka'bah had been established even before the Prophet Ibrahim (a), and that what he did was, after it had perished in the course of time, to build it up again from its foundation and to reconstruct it. Bait al-Muqaddas, for its part, is what was established in the time of the Prophet Sulaiman (a). (\*\*) That was after four hundred or five hundred years had passed after the Prophet Musa (a).
