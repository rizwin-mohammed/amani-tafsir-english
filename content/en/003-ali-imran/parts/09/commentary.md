### Section – 9

*[Verses 81–82]*

*[p. 798]* For any prophet whatever who has received the scripture and the teachings of wisdom from Allah, in case another Rasul comes afterwards confirming as true the guidances and the principles of truth that he received, to believe in that Rasul, and to give him help and cooperation in his preaching, is obligatory. Since the fundamental principles of mission of all the Rasuls are one, the mission of one will never, surely, be opposed to that of the other. Allah has taken an assurance from all the prophets that they will do in this way. All have also, acknowledging it as their liability, borne witness to it. There is no need to say specially that this grave liability, which is inescapable even for the prophets, applies to their communities too. That being so, no community that is People of the Scripture has the right to say that we will believe only in our prophet and in our book - that we need believe only (so) -. To do so is defiance and licentiousness. Such is the gist.

The noble Prophet ﷺ being the last prophet, since no other Rasul is to come either in the time of the noble one or after it, the noble one is naturally exempt from this liability. But, for those who stand as the followers of any prophet before the noble one, to believe in the noble one, and to follow the noble one, is obligatory moreover. It is with the communities that are People of the Scripture specially in view that Allah makes the statement about this pact. It is on the basis of this pact, too, that the Prophet ﷺ has said thus in a hadith: ‘If Musa (a) had been among you, and then you had followed him, and had abandoned me, you would have gone astray indeed’. The wording in a hadith of Abu Ya'la (r) is thus *[p. 799]*; ‘If Musa (a) had been living among you, there would have been no way out for him except to follow me.’

*[Verses 83–84]*

*[p. 800]* The religion that was preached by all the prophets is the one and only religion that is the religion of Allah (that is Islam). Besides that too, it is while being subject to the systems of law that Allah has determined that all indeed who are in the heavens and the earth, including human beings, stand. Whether it be by one's own wish, or whether it be under compulsion, there is no alternative for anyone except to become submissive to that system of law. It is to Him indeed, too, that finally all return and end up. That being so, is their demand that some other religion, which is not what Allah has appointed and commanded, must be accepted?! If so, that will never be acceptable. This is the idea in the first verse. In the second verse this idea has been strengthened once more. That is, Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ to proclaim that we are ones who have accepted the fundamental reality that what all the prophets have brought is that very religion which is Allah's, and that therefore it is not possible to believe in the mission of some, and not to believe in that of some. In the next verse He makes this supreme truth clear once more again, in such a way that there is no room for doubt about the meaning.

The intent of having said that all who are in the heavens and the earth have become submissive to Allah, obediently or compulsorily. It is that it is not possible for anyone to stand, even for the space of a wink, in a manner that differs from it even slightly, otherwise than with all being completely subject to His systems of governance and to the systems of nature that He has appointed. Without distinctions of type such as true believer, disbeliever, naturalist, polytheist, atheist, or distinction of kind such as human being, jinns, angels, animals, lifeless things, all things are, in this sense, ones subjected to Allah. (Further information can be seen in places such as Ra'd, 15: Ha Mim Sajdah: 11) That being so, there is no need to say, surely, that not becoming submissive to His religious laws, which He has graciously commanded, and which are the source of the everlasting good of man, is not just- that it is injustice and defiance -.

Prophets have passed away in lakhs. Among them, it is because they are in many ways more well familiar and more honoured among the Arabs and the People of the Scripture, and because they are more memorable in the historical and religious fields, that the names of some prophets such as the Prophet Ibrahim (a) have been specially *[p. 801]* picked out and stated. It is intending the prophets who arose in the twelve lines of descendants of the Prophet Ya'qub (a) that **ٱلۡأَسۡبَاطِ** (progeny) has been said. When speaking about the Prophet Ibrahim (a) and others, ‘what has been sent down upon them’ ( **مَآ أُنزِلَ** ), and then, when speaking about Musa, 'Isa (a), ‘what has been given to them’ ( **مَآ أُوتِيَ** ): such are the words that Allah has used.- In receiving the wahy and message from Allah, and in being given the signs necessary to establish the truthfulness of each one, almost all the prophets will have a share. But, so much fame as the Torah of the Prophet Musa (a) and the Injil of the Prophet 'Isa (a), and as the divine signs that appeared at their hands, has not come about in the midst of the community for the books, or for the signs, of other prophets. It may be because of that that it was stated differentiating thus. (Allah knows)

*[Verse 85]*

It was stated in the previous verses that what man is by nature obliged to accept, and what all the prophets preached, is only the one religion that is the religion of Allah. That indeed is the religion Islam. It was stated in the 19th verse too: **إِنَّ ٱلدِّينَ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلۡإِسۡلَٰمُ** (Surely, with Allah the religion is Islam). Therefore Allah makes clear in this verse, in a way that leaves no room for any doubt or interpretation, that whatever religion other than Islam anyone at all accepts or demands, it is not acceptable with Allah, and that he will be one who has lost everything in the Hereafter.

‘No objection whatever the religion, it is enough if man becomes good’, such a novel principle- no, a sweet, charming meaningless theory heard from some who suppose themselves to be enlightened ones and intellectuals *[p. 802]*- is in circulation among the people today. Some who are Muslims in name only do not fail, either, to take it up and sing it. That word cannot come out from the mind, or through the mouth, of a Muslim who knows at least the elementary principles of Islam, or who has belief in the Quran even to the extent of an atom. Their aim is only that they must live in harmony in material life with other communities without difference of opinion, and that they must earn their love. Its meaning, surely, is that there is no objection whatever stand one adopts in beliefs and practices, and in procedures- it is enough if it does not become an objection to the reproach and opposition of others *[DOUBT: "മറ്റുള്ളവരുടെ ആക്ഷേപത്തിനും എതിർപ്പിനും വിരോധമാകാതിരുന്നാൽ മതി", literally "it is enough if it does not become objection (or hostility) to the reproach and opposition of others"; the sense seems to be "if it does not run against, or provoke, the reproach and opposition of others", but the exact force of വിരോധമാകുക here is not certain.]*. In reality, between the definition of ‘religion’ and of ‘becoming good’ with these people, and the definition that Allah and the Rasul give to those two, there is not even a nominal connection. Certainly.

*[Verses 86–89]*

*[p. 803]* These verses are a heavy warning intended for the People of the Scripture who, even after conviction and belief had come about, through the scriptures and through the proofs, in the truthfulness of the Prophet ﷺ and of what he has brought, became disbelievers without believing in the Prophet ﷺ, and for those who, after having accepted true faith, went off as outcasts from Islam. The purport of having said that the curse of Allah, and of the angels, and of mankind, all, will be upon them has been explained in the explanation of Suratul Baqarah: 161 it is not repeated here.

*[Verses 90–91]*

*[p. 804]* In case people who are disbelievers die while being disbelievers indeed- without having turned back from it-, Allah proclaims that there is no salvation whatsoever for them in the Hereafter; that, whether by having expressed regret at the time of death, or by means of some atonement, or by means of any good things that they may have done while being disbelievers- by nothing at all-, there is not any benefit, either, to be obtained for them there. This subject has been made clear in many places in the Quran. It can be understood from this that the final decision as to whether a person is a believer, or a disbeliever, one on the right path, or one on the wrong path, is settled only with his death.

*[p. 805]* The Prophet ﷺ has graciously said: ‘On the day of Qiyamah, of a man who is of the people of Hell it will be asked: Here, look: if all that is on the earth were yours, would you (now) give it as a ransom? He will answer, Yes. Then, Allah will say: It was a much more trifling matter than this that I had intended from you. In the back of your father Adam I took a pact from you that you would not associate anything with Me. You, refusing that, did indeed associate partners with Me.’ (A; Bu; Mu)

### Section – 10 · Juz' – 4

*[Verse 92]*

It was seen in the previous verse, surely, that none of the good things that disbelievers do- while they are disbelievers- is acceptable with Allah. All the good things that true believers do- however trifling it may be- will be acceptable with Allah. But, if what is spent is to be meritorious and deserving of more reward, Allah makes known in this verse how it ought to be. What is spent ought to be from things that are agreeable to them. Only then does it become a meritorious deed. That a thing is liked need not be on account of the greatness of its price. It may be because of some other reason too. Therefore, in case their owner spends, so as to spend, in place of such things, even things more valuable than them *[DOUBT: the clause "അത്തരം വസ്തുക്കൾക്ക് പകരം അവയെക്കാൾ വിലപിടിപ്പുള്ള വസ്തുക്കളെപ്പോലും ചിലവഴിക്കുവാൻ അവയുടെ ഉടമസ്ഥൻ ചിലവാക്കുന്നപക്ഷം" is printed so (amanithafseer.com has the same) and does not construe smoothly. It may mean "in case their owner, (who would be ready) to spend even things more valuable than them in place of such things, spends them", that is, the owner gives the very things he likes; a word may have dropped out. The English follows the printed words.]*, that shows, surely, that what he attaches more importance to than that is the pleasure and the reward of Allah. It is this state of mind, too, that makes it meritorious. The more the fondness for the thing that is spent increases, the more the merit in spending *[p. 806]* it also increases. Quite contrary, it is clear from this that the practice of keeping aside, for spending, things that are not dear and that are poor is not good. (Remember what was stated about this in 2: 267)

When this verse came down, see a few examples that show how great the reactions were that it brought about among the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ:

1. Anas (r) says: Among the Ansaris, Abu Talhah (r) (\*) was a man with very much wealth Of his properties, the one dearest to him was the garden called ‘Bairuha’ ( **بَيْرُحَاء** ). It was in front of the masjid. The Prophet ﷺ was used to entering it, and drinking the good water in it. When this verse, ........ **لَن تَنَالُواْ**, came down, he said: ‘O Rasul of Allah, Allah says that you will not get merit without spending from what you like. Of my property, the dearest to me is ‘Bairuha. It is a charity for Allah. I hope for its merit and (reward) deposit with Allah. Therefore, may you decide it (in the matter that is needed) as Allah causes you to deem fit.’ Then, the Prophet ﷺ said: ‘Ha! Ha! That is profitable wealth! Profitable wealth! (I have heard what you said. What I advise is that you set it aside (as charity) for the near families.’ Abu Talhah (r) said: ‘I will do so.’ So, he divided it among his near families and his paternal cousins. (A; Bu; Mu)

> **Note:** (\*) He had married the mother of Anas (r).

2. For 'Umar (r), among his properties: the one more valued *[DOUBT: the word is വീര്യപ്പെട്ടത് (from വീര്യം, "potency, vigour"), so "more valued, more precious"; it may be a slip for പ്രിയപ്പെട്ടത് "dearer", the word used in the other examples. amanithafseer.com has the same word.]* was a garden that he got as his share when the ‘ghanimah’ of Khaibar was divided. After consulting the Prophet ﷺ he made it a ‘waqf’ in the way of Allah and declared it. (Bu; Mu.)

3. When this verse came down, Ibn 'Umar (r), on considering his things, found that it was a Roman slave woman of his that was the property dearer to him among them. He set her free and let her go. Out of fear lest it would turn out to be taking her back again, he did not venture even to marry her. (Bazzar)

4. Of the properties of Zaid ibn Harithah (r), the dearest to him was his horse. He made it a charity in the way of Allah. The Prophet ﷺ also accepted it. Afterwards the Prophet ﷺ permitted his son Usamah (r) to ride on its back. When he saw this, displeasure at it showed on the face of Zaid (r). The Prophet ﷺ *[p. 807]* said to him: ‘Allah has accepted your charity. (No harm will befall it because the son rode on it) thus (Ibn Abi Hatim)
