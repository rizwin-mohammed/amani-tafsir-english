*[Verse 164]*

*[p. 887]* After they had fallen into misguidance for a long time and spun about in bewilderment, it is reminding the true believers about the great good fortune obtained from Allah by way of commissioning for them a Rasul who keeps conveying from Allah the principles of teaching, and the guidance, by which they may live devoted to right guidance- very convenient to ask and learn from, to take as a model, and to deal with closely- born among them and living right along with them-. The ideas contained in this verse have already been explained before in the commentary on Su: Baqarah 129. A fuller explanation can be seen in Su: Jumu'ah.

*[Verses 165–168]*

*[p. 889]* Following the huge hardships and losses experienced by the Muslims at Uhud, some common people said: We are fighting for Allah. Allah has also made the promise that He will help us. His Rasul too is with us. As for the disbelievers, it is against Allah that they are fighting. That being so, what is it that has happened thus?! and so on. To this Allah gives a reply. The gist of the reply can be explained thus: That some calamities befell you is right indeed. Before, on an occasion when there was not this much strength and preparation, at Badr you brought on the disbelievers calamities double this, surely. Seventy of their main pillars were killed, and as many became captives. You drove them back pitiably. Now, only half that calamity has happened to you. Even so, you wonder, saying how this happened. In reality, the causes for this happening came about from you yourselves. You made the Rasul compelled to go out of Madinah and enter into battle. You disregarded the command that you must stand firm boldly without leaving the ranks. Before the battle concluded, you became engaged in collecting the ‘ghanimah’ properties. The bowmen stationed on the hill violated the command and left the place. Many such things are what became the cause of it. Allah has the power to do anything. According to the occasion He will do what is needed. In that He cannot be questioned. Not only that, Allah has also kept in view some wise secrets in this happening. It is necessary to see distinguished in the open who are the ones who stand firm in true faith without their feet faltering whatever happens, and who are the reactionaries and hypocrites who go about pretending to be believers. This is also for that. That has now also become clear, surely.

Under the leadership of 'Abdullah ibn Ubayy, three hundred hypocrites turned back from the way, surely. Some Companions made an attempt to call them back and bring them. Either come and fight together with the Prophet ﷺ, or else at *[p. 890]* least stand resisting for the Muslims. They tried telling them that they must do one of the two. In case they were not taking part in the battle, if they at least made a show of men on the side of the Muslims, that would serve to bring about a loss of courage in the enemies, surely. Perhaps, too, it is not impossible that the enemies may enter inside Madinah and attack the townspeople and the helpless women. If that happened, the families of these hypocrites themselves too would not get exemption from destruction. Therefore, if not actively, at least outwardly you must stand with the Muslims, and so on, they advised. The reply the hypocrites gave to this was this: ‘We do not think that there will be a battle. If at all there is one, we will not leave you to the enemies. Then we too will join along with you. That this is merely a trick and a mockery need not be said, surely.

A huge army that had come prepared to take revenge for the irreparable loss that befell them last time at Badr. Those hypocrites know very well that they would not just go back idly without clashing with the Muslims, who were fewer both in number and in bulk. The real inner intent of them, who until now had been passing their time acting out the guise of Islam through the Muslims *[DOUBT: printed "മുസ്ലിംകളിലൂടെ" ("through the Muslims"), clear on the image; the sense may be "among the Muslims" (as മുസ്ലിംകളുടെ ഇടയിൽ). Either the author's wording or a printing slip; translated as printed.]*, is only now openly displayed. It is about that that Allah said, ‘It is to disbelief rather than to true faith that they had more closeness that day’ and that, they say with the mouth what is not in their hearts. As Ibn Kathir (r) pointed out, the great ones have understood from the sentence **هُمۡ لِلۡكُفۡرِ يَوۡمَئِذٍ**.... that a man's stand will undergo change according to his circumstances, and that thus sometimes his closeness to true faith, and at other times to disbelief, will be more and less.

The hypocrites had said that it was because they went out to battle without listening to their word that many had to die as martyrs in the battle. If that is so, Allah commands the Prophet ﷺ to tell them: let them stay shut up in the house without taking part in any battle and just escape from death; we can just see that, surely. Yes- as was said in the 154th verse- death is what Allah has determined. When its time comes, it will indeed happen. The purport is that this is what is in reality. Anyhow, quite a few people have died, surely; is that not indeed a loss that cannot be made good? it may be asked. The answer to that can be known from the next verse. Allah says:-

*[Verses 169–171]*

*[p. 892]* That it is in the matter of those who became shahid (died as martyrs) in the battle of Uhud that these verses have come down is indeed what the majority of the Quran commentators hold. When the context is looked at too, the reasonable thing is for that to be right. Whatever the cause of the sending down was, it is clear that Allah has stated in these verses generally about all the shuhada'. In Suratul Baqarah: 154, what was stated was that one must not say about the shuhada' that they are ones who have died, and that they are ones living with Allah, but that you cannot comprehend it. Here their life has been explained a little. It will be good to remember here too the things explained there in its commentary.

Apart from our being able to understand about life and about death through some signs, neither scholars nor scientists have been able till this day to define precisely and objectively what life is, or what death is. It is only that some have some opinions. In the inference that it may become possible in the future, science keeps toiling for it too. But, those two will remain indeed as one of Allah's secrets. Anyhow, it is definitively known by the Quran and by hadith that after death, which marks the end of that material life, in the interim before the resurrection, man has a kind of spiritual life in which joys and sorrows can be experienced. It is not possible for science or for reason to do research about it and to conduct experiments. Reason does not render it impossible, moreover. It is this life that is called the life in the ‘Barzakh’ ( **بَرْزَخ** ), and the life in the Qabr ( **قَبَر** *[DOUBT: the 600 dpi crop shows a fatha on the qaf and a fatha on the ba (قَبَر); the word the author names, his "ക്വബ്റ്" (qabr, grave), is usually vowelled قَبْر. Either a printing slip or the printed vowelling; transcribed as printed.]* ). Although it will be different in joys and sorrows, no one at all will be exempt from it. But, the life that Allah has stated those who die as martyrs in the path of Allah have, cannot possibly be meant as a life of the very same manner as this just mentioned. It can only be a life that is given to them specially, and that is of a higher standard than that of the other true believers. Otherwise, the statements in these verses would not have any special meaning, surely. But, as seen in the ayah in Al-Baqarah, we cannot infer anything clearly about what the reality of that life is. About it, we can understand only what Allah and the Rasul have told us.

It is the following things that Allah has specially mentioned about the martyrs who are killed in the path of Allah:

(1) That they need not be deemed ones who have died- they are ones living with Allah-. With their being killed, that the death well familiar to us has befallen them, and that the life well familiar to us *[p. 893]* has come to an end for them, is certain indeed. Then it becomes clear that that life in which Allah also says that they are ones living is not the material life familiar to us- it is a kind of life in a special sense-.

(2) Livelihood- or provision- keeps being given to them. Only this, about this too, can we say: that it will be a system of provision suited to that special life. That too Allah has stated as a favour they obtain. What some are used to saying, that the purport of its having been said that livelihood will be given is that they will obtain many favours, is not in keeping with the clear statements of the Quran and of the sayings of the Prophet.

(3) They will be joyful with the favours they obtain. The indication in this is that besides being given livelihood, they will attain many other experiences too.

(4) It is not only becoming filled with happiness with the favours they obtain that they do; they will be ones who feel great happiness also seeing the favours that their brothers - who are to come and join their company in the future - who become martyrs - may obtain there. Yes, they will have interest not only about themselves, but also about the brothers who are to arrive after them.

(5) Besides the generosities and favours that they have obtained, and are obtaining, from Allah, there is one more thing too that thrills them with delight. They will keep being happy in the huge rewards that are to be obtained generally by the true believers- whether they be martyrs, or be they ones who are not-, and in that they will attain them after their death without any of them being lost, surely.

Many sayings of the Prophet that give light on the ideas of these verses can be seen in the hadith books. Only some of them can we recall here:

1. Anas (r) quotes the Prophet ﷺ as having declared: ‘For not even one soul that, having died, obtains good from Allah, will returning to this world be a happiness- except the shahid. Because he sees and experiences the excellence of the shahadah (of dying as a martyr), it will be a thing of happiness to him to return to this world and be killed one more time’. (A; Mu.)

2. Jabir (r) is saying: When my father was killed in the battle of Uhud, I kept weeping, and kept removing the cloth from his face. The Companions kept forbidding me. The Rasul ﷺ did not forbid. He said: ‘There is no need to weep; until he is raised *[p. 894]* (until his janazah is taken up) the angels keep giving him shade with their wings.’ (Bu; Mu; Na.)

3. Ibn 'Abbas (r) says: The Rasul ﷺ has said thus: ‘When calamity befell your brothers on the day of Uhud, Allah has put their souls into the insides of some birds of green colour. They drink water from the streams of Paradise. They also eat from its fruits. They also go and gather in the golden kinadi (lamp)s *[DOUBT: printed "സ്വർണക്കിനാദി (വിളക്കു) കളിൽ"; കിനാദി appears to be the Arabic قناديل (qanadil, lamps), which the author glosses "(വിളക്കു)" ("lamp"). Kept as printed, "kinadi"; the reviewer may prefer "qanadil".]* in the shade of the ‘'Arsh’. Thus, when they saw the excellence of their food and drink, and the excellence of their place of rest, they said: How good it would have been if our brothers had seen what Allah has done for us! Then they would have been without showing lack of interest in the struggle, and without being reluctant for battle! Then, Allah said: On your behalf I will convey this (this information) to them. Thus, Allah sent down these (169- 171) verses.’ (A; Da; Ha; Ibn Jarir)

4. In a hadith that Ahmad and Ibn Jarir (r) have reported from Ibn 'Abbas (r), it has also come that the shuhada' will obtain their provision from Paradise morning and evening.
