*[Verses 137–138]*

Ever so many confrontations, with the prophets and their followers on one side, and the deniers of the truth who were their opponents on the other side, have gone past before this. If you travel through the earth and inquire into how the final end of those communities that went past before came about, you will obtain plenty of evidence for this. It is in order that mankind in general may understand that matters are explained to you like this. At the same time, as regards the true believers who observe caution, it is guidance and good counsel for them, too. They, surely, are the ones who make use of it. Such is the gist.

*[Verse 139]*

*[p. 859]* The fact being as stated above, on account of having experienced some temporary defeat or hardships, as happened at Uhud, you must not lose courage and become weak ones, or keep grieving about it. As long as you become real true believers, the high position in this world and in the next world will be yours indeed. It is weakness coming about in your faith that you must give special attention to. If that does not befall, you need not fear about the future: with such things the true believers are being exhorted. Allah continues:-

*[Verses 140–141]*

*[p. 860]* If much injury and hurt have befallen you at Uhud, the enemies too have already been struck likewise at Badr. As soon as something or other like that befalls at times, lack of patience and loss of courage must not come about in it. Victory and defeat may come to befall everyone. It is Allah who manages all that. At one time good may be seen for one party. At another time the reverse too will be seen It is with certain aims in view that Allah does so. The real believers and the hypocrites must come out into the open separated, true witnesses ready to make any sacrifice for the truth must emerge among you, the weakness and deficiency in you must be removed and you must be purified, and thus, gradually, all the strength and majesty of the disbelievers must be wiped out and removed. Many such things are what Allah intends by this trial: that is the gist.

To **يَتَّخِذَ مِنكُمۡ شُهَدَآءَ** (making witnesses from among you) interpretation has been given in two ways:

1\. Making martyrs among (from) you. The position that martyrs obtain with Allah is very great, surely. That the Prophet ﷺ has graciously said that those who became martyrs, when they see the great favours they obtain after their death, will keep longing that it would have been good if they had been brought to life again and again and made to be killed by enemies, is worth stating. At Uhud, for its part, seventy people did become martyrs.

2\. True witnesses- or model men- purified so as to embrace any sacrifice for Islam, coming into being. It can be seen that both opinions finally end in the same principle indeed.

From usages like those seen in the 140th verse, **لِيَعۡلَمَ ٱللَّهُ** (in order that Allah may know), and in the next verse below, **وَلَمَّا يَعۡلَمِ ٱللَّهُ** *[p. 861]* (while Allah has not known)- as some rationalists are wont to argue- no one should come to suppose that Allah does not know about matters before they happen. That Allah is the one who knows all matters, without distinction of past- present- future time, and without distinction of small and large, is a reality established beyond doubt by numerous Quran verses, and by sayings of the Prophet. Intellect and logic, too, proclaim its necessity. The purport of those words is indeed that those matters that Allah knows beforehand come to be seen made manifest through experience, so that others too can see. This can be understood more from the 179th verse below itself. **إن شاء الله**

*[Verses 142–143]*

Before an opportunity is made to distinguish, in experience, those who struggle for the Din and those who adopt endurance and patience in confronting the enemies- merely by the fact that you have accepted the true faith- if you think that you can *[p. 862]* enter Paradise, that is not to be. (Look at the verses 2: 214; 29: 2 too.) You too need to be subjected to many trials like those that took place among the former communities. Only those who adopt steadfastness, and endurance, in trials will obtain victory in this world, and Paradise in the next world. You had greatly longed, surely, to battle with the enemies and attain a hero's death for Islam. Now you have seen it in experience. Therefore, what you must do is to get ready to face it bravely: that is the gist. As to the purport of ‘in a state where Allah does not know ( **وَلَمَّا يَعۡلَمِ ٱللَّهُ** )’, remember what was said in the explanation of the last verse.

Even before war was ordained, owing to the persecution and wrongdoing of the enemies, the desire to confront them had arisen among the true believers. (Look at Sura: Hajj 39th verse and its explanation.) It was while many were regretting, after the battle of Badr was over, that they had not been able to take part in it, that the Uhud event took place. It is these things that Allah is pointing out. It was not only the craving for revenge against the enemies that had urged them to it. Their yearning was that they should attain merit by embracing sacrifice, and martyrdom, for the Din. The Prophet ﷺ has graciously said thus: ‘You must not yearn to meet the enemy. You should ask Allah to spare you (from it). Then, if you have met them, then you must hold to patience. Know you; that Paradise is in the shade of swords.’ (Bu; Mu.)

### Section – 15

*[Verses 144–145]*

*[p. 863]* It was said earlier, surely, that at the time when the battle scene at Uhud reached its climax a rumour spread that the noble Prophet ﷺ had been killed. This *[p. 864]* caused great bewilderment among the Muslims. Many said such things as: ‘If Muhammad has been killed, why should we fight any more.’ ‘Now, we may surely return to our old religion itself’. It need not be said that the spokesmen of this would be the hypocrites, and those weak in faith. On the contrary, some also said: ‘If Muhammad has been killed, no matter. He has conveyed to us what he had to convey to us; let us fight for our religion’. Many left the battlefield. In short, that news demoralised the Muslims in general, and plunged them into fear. It is about this that Allah speaks.

Muhammad is merely a Rasul of Allah only. He is not God or the son of God or anything, that being so, he may die or be killed. Even so, will you leave the religion and the faith and return to the old position?! Is your faith so weak?! Rasuls attaining the Hereafter is nothing new. None of the Rasuls who went past before remain, surely. Some among them have been killed, too. True faith is not something tied only to the life of the Rasul. Now, whatever the reason may be, if anyone goes back, abandoning the true Din, to the old shirk and kufr, they will have to suffer the consequence of that ingratitude. There is no harm to befall Allah by it. There is one thing. To those who stand firm in that very path, showing thanks for the favours that Allah has done for them, such as right guidance, Allah will later surely give a huge reward,.

As regards death, Allah has reckoned beforehand a fixed term, that the death of each individual- there being no distinction as to whether a prophet or those who are not- will be at such and such a time. It will not happen before or after that term. No one need suppose that because of a cause such as war it may happen before the fixed time, or, that by running away one can escape from it. Getting a short life, and getting a long life, and all, is only according to that determination. (35: 11) Another thing; the reward obtained for deeds such as taking part in war will be obtained according to the intentions and aims of each. If someone's intention is worldly gains such as acquiring wealth, it is worldly achievements that they will attain. If someone's intention is otherworldly achievements, what they obtain will also be otherworldly achievements. To the thankful, for their part, more will be given, too. Such things are the ideas contained in these verses.

When the noble Rasul ﷺ passed away, the event in which the Companions were stunned, and many, including the brave 'Umar (r), denied the noble one's death, is well known. Abu Bakr (r), who was not there at that time, came and, after looking at the noble one, he said: ‘If anyone worships Muhammad, Muhammad has died. If anyone worships Allah, *[p. 865]* He has remained.’ Continuing, he also recited this (144th) verse. It was on hearing this that 'Umar (r) and others got their senses back. ‘It seemed as if this ayah had not been heard before’, they also said.

The sentence **قَدۡ خَلَتۡ مِن قَبۡلِهِ ٱلرُّسُلُ** (Rasuls have gone past before him) some people of vested interest are wont to exploit as proof for a crooked argument of theirs. They are ones who argue that 'Isa (a) has not been raised up to heaven, and that he has died as is ordinary. They say that what this sentence denotes, surely, is that all the Rasuls before the noble Rasul ﷺ have passed their time- that is, have already died-. The meaning of the verb **خلا** (khala) is ‘went past, became alone, became free ( **مضى، انفرد، فرغ** )’, and so on. Imam Raghib (r) has stated that, although it is used with reference to time and with reference to place, because it is past time that is considered in it, the scholars of language are wont to give as its meaning ‘time went past ( **مضى، الزمان، وذهب** )’. Then, the word has no meaning that they died, other than that the Rasuls passed their time, or else that they left the place (from this world), indeed. That can happen by means of death or by means of leaving the earth. Now, even if it is granted for the sake of argument that the meaning of that term is indeed ‘died’, Allah has not said ‘all the Rasuls’. He has only said ‘Rasuls’ ( **ٱلرُّسُلُ** ) in general. Then, even if one, or more than one, are exempt from it, there is nothing incongruous in it. At the same time, that the word **خلا** does not have the meaning ‘died’ is also something anyone can understand if one looks at the contexts in which that word has been used in many places in the Quran. (Example: 2: 14, 76; 3: 119, 137.) The word **انْقَلِبْ عَلَى عَقِبَيْهِ** *[DOUBT: the Arabic is transcribed as printed, its vowel marks read from a 600 dpi crop and the PDF's text layer: kasra under the lam and sukun on the ba of انْقَلِبْ (the form of a command, "turn back!"), and عَلَى without a dagger alif. The author's gloss "തിരിച്ചു പോയി" ("went back") is past tense, which would fit انْقَلَبَ; the vowelling may be a printing slip. It is not a Quran quotation in this form (3:144 has يَنقَلِبۡ عَلَىٰ عَقِبَيۡهِ, 2:143 يَنقَلِبُ عَلَىٰ عَقِبَيۡهِ).]* (went back on the heels) is a usage in Arabic like what is wont to be said in Malayalam: ‘went back the moment he came’, ‘went back on the very feet he came on’, and so on.

*[Verses 146–148]*

*[p. 867]* Of **ربى** (ribbiyy) the plural is **رِبِّيُّونَ** (ribbiyyun). This too is indeed a word rather like the **ربانى** (Rabbani) seen in the 79th verse. Meanings such as ‘priests, learned ones, holy men, meritorious ones, followers’, and also ‘bands’, are wont to be given for it. There is also another opinion, that those in authority will be called ‘Rabbani’s and their subordinate staff ‘ribbis’. In any case, the purport of the ayah is clear indeed.

In former times, along with ever so many prophets, good men in large numbers, who were their followers, have battled with the enemies for the true Din. Many unpleasant events have also befallen them from the enemies. Even so, they had not shown impatience, or lack of courage, or weakness. Nor did they become submissive to the enemies and surrender. What they did was to pray for the forgiveness of their sins, and for help to stand firm without stumbling before the enemies and attain victory, and to entrust everything to Allah. Thus, Allah gave them victory and peace in this world. In the next world He gave great rewards too. You too must adopt their example. You must remember that Allah will not fail to help the patient, and the meritorious ones, either: that is the gist.

In place of **قَٰتَلَ مَعَهُۥ**, it has also been read **قوتل معه** *[DOUBT: printed قوتل معه (unvowelled), clearly. The variant reading of this verse that is usually reported is قُتِلَ مَعَهُۥ ("were killed along with him"), which is what the author's Malayalam gloss "കൊലചെയ്യപ്പെട്ടു" ("were killed") gives; قوتل is the passive of قاتل ("was fought against"). This may be a printing slip for قتل or the author's own spelling; kept as printed.]*. Then the meaning will be ‘along with them - along with the prophets -many ribbis were killed’. According to this, the gist of the ayah can be understood thus: Ever so many prophets, and the meritorious ones who fought along with them, have been killed in war before. Then their followers who remained, who were true believers, stood firm without weakness, or loss of courage, befalling them. Should that not be an example for you? It is not as you did, when you heard a report that Muhammad, the Prophet ﷺ, had been killed, and some died, cowardice came upon you and your vigour went out, that the followers of the prophets ought to do. What Ibn Jarir (r) is seen giving preference to is this reading, mentioned second. **والله اعلم**
