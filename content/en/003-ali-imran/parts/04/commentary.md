*[Verse 36]*

When the wife of 'Imran gave birth and looked, the child had been born, contrary to expectation, female. ‘What I have given birth to is a girl! In the matter intended, the boy and the girl are not alike!’ so she grieved. *[p. 743]* Since she had vowed a vow of the child that was in the womb, whether it was male or female, that vow had to be fulfilled, surely. And she fulfilled it. She named the child Maryam, and she supplicated that none of the harms of Satan should affect Maryam and the offspring that might come from her. The sentence **وَٱللَّهُ أَعۡلَمُ بِمَا وَضَعَتۡ** (Allah is the One who knows best about what she gave birth to) is what Allah said in between. Whether the child she gave birth to was male or female, and what the difficulties were, in the matter intended, because of the child being a girl, and that, along with that, many signs were to happen to that girl in the future: all this Allah knows very well. That it was indeed knowing that that He made it born as a girl: such hints are contained in that sentence.

*[Verse 37]*

*[p. 744]* Allah accepted the supplication of that noble lady. He also guarded and protected Maryam (a) from the harm of Satan. Allah brought her up with qualities such as good character, firmness of faith, knowledge, God-fearing reverence, discipline and purity of chastity. So as to be a help to all this, He also entrusted the responsibility of protecting Maryam (a) to Zakariyya (a). That is, as can be seen in the 44th verse- in the drawing of lots that took place among the priests in order to decide who was to take charge of Maryam, the lot came to the Prophet Zakariyya (a). He was also the head of the priests of Bait al-Muqaddas and the husband of Maryam's mother's sister. So Maryam grew up in his protection, and in his special regard, and in the general affectionate care of the priests. It was in the mihrab of the mosque - in the prayer-chamber - indeed that a place had been arranged for Maryam. (\*)

> **Note:** (\*) What we now call ‘mihrab’ is the special place made specially for the imam who leads the prayer in mosques to stand in. That is not what is meant here. What is meant by it is the prayer-chamber that is specially set up in Christian churches and the like for their priests and attendants to carry out acts of prayer, and that stands a little raised above the other places.

For purposes such as giving Maryam the advice and directions she needed, enquiring after her condition, and bringing her the things she required, it would have been usual, surely, for Zakariyya (a) to go to her in the prayer-chamber. Whenever he went, he would see there food items that had no right to be there in the ordinary course. At that, feeling astonished, he asked: O Maryam, from where do you get this? ( **يَٰمَرۡيَمُ أَنَّىٰ لَكِ هَٰذَا** ). Her answer was: This is what is got from the side of Allah, ( **هُوَ مِنۡ عِندِ ٱللَّهِ** ). What were these things that astonished Zakariyya (a). What were the ways by which they were got? Nothing is seen stated in the Quran about this. What there is to point out here is two riwayahs that are quoted from the great ones of ancient times.

(1) It is what Ibn Jarir (r) quoted in his Tafsir from many, such as Mujahid, 'Ikrimah, Sa'id ibn Jubair, Qatadah and Rabi' (r), and what most commentators of the Quran usually quote. Its summary is this. *[p. 745]* He used to see with Maryam the fruits of the hot season in the cold season, and the fruits of the cold season in the hot season.

(2) The second is what Ibn Jarir (r) quoted saying ‘Some others say’; it is like this: ‘When Zakariyya (a) went to Maryam in the mihrab, he used to see there more food than the man who bore the expense of her food used to give her. Following on, he has also quoted, as the basis for this, a riwayah from Muhammad ibn Ishaq (r). Its summary is this. At that time a great famine came about. The Prophet Zakariyya (a), or others, became unable to bear her expenses. In the end they cast lots. The lot fell to a man named Juraij. When she saw his difficulty, Maryam (a) consoled him. After that he kept getting the means for each day's needs. Every day he kept giving from it the means for Maryam. When he went with it, Allah would make it grow and increase. When Zakariyya (a) went there, he kept seeing there more things than the measure that Juraij used to give. Whether any one of these two, or both indeed, turn out to be right or not, one thing we can say decisively: those food items must have been obtained through some way by which Maryam was not likely to obtain them in the ordinary course, and which the Prophet Zakariyya (a) was unable to understand. That is why he was astonished and asked that question. His question was not a mere enquiry, pointing to some food item seen there, as to who the man was who had brought it. Maryam's answer too indeed shows that there was some kind of extraordinariness in the way by which it was obtained. As is understood from the next verse, it is when he saw Allah honouring Maryam with such extraordinary honour that he supplicates for a child for himself, even though his wife was barren and he was old.

**إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَرۡزُقُ**.... (Allah gives to whomever He wills without account): it is possible for this last sentence to come as Allah's own sentence, and as a sentence belonging to Maryam's answer. Either way the intention is clear indeed. Not only that Allah will increase things without account for whomever He wills; that He will give through a way that others do not obtain and that they have no right to expect is also included in the extent of meaning of that sentence. Connecting it with the subject of Maryam, Allah says:

*[Verses 38–39]*

*[p. 746]* The story of Maryam Bivi (a) is, surely, the subject in the past verses. Its continuing part is described below from the 42nd verse. In between, as an event connected with it in many ways, the event in which the Prophet Zakariyya (a) supplicated for a child, and that supplication was fulfilled, is being described.

As is said in the next verse, Zakariyya (a) has become very old. His wife, for her part, is a barren woman who does not give birth. As he has no heir to succeed him, he keeps grieving, he saw Maryam being born from a mother who was merely old *[DOUBT: കേവലം വൃദ്ധയായ ഒരു മാതാവിൽനിന്ന്: കേവലം usually means "merely"; here it may instead strengthen the adjective, "a quite (utterly) old mother", as in "കേവലം സംഭവ്യമല്ല" on p. 750 ("not possible at all"). The English keeps "merely".]*, and Allah bringing Maryam, who grew up under his protection, to a very excellent and pure standing, and Him honouring Maryam by giving her food through some way that was not ordinary, and so on. For Allah, who creates wonders and signs such as these, it is no difficulty at all, surely, *[p. 747]* to grant him a child. When he saw all this, he supplicated to Allah to bless him too with a child. He also requested: May that child be an excellent and pure one! His need was not that a successor should come about who would become heir to his wealth or properties. What he wanted was that there should be an heir to succeed him who would carry out the duties of preaching the true Din after him. (19:5,6). Allah accepted his supplication, and, while he was praying in the prayer-chamber, Allah informed him through the angels of the glad tidings that that supplication had been accepted. He informed him that the child who was going to be born to him would be male and that the name would be Yahya (19:7) Not only that, He also informed him beforehand of the special qualities of that child.

That is: (1) He will be one who confirms as true a word from Allah. That what is meant by ‘word ’ will be the Prophet 'Isa (a) can be understood from the 45th verse. More information can be seen there. **إن شاءالله** He was the person who first believed in 'Isa (a) when he received prophethood, and the person who spread the principles of his preaching. It has been narrated from Abu 'Ubaidah (r) that what is meant here by **كلمة** (word) is a scripture, or wahy (divine message). And moreover, speech containing many words is also usually called **كلمة**.

(2) He will be a leader; he was famous in all such as religious knowledge, eminent character and God-fearing reverence; he was also the religious leader in the community. That is why he is known by the name ‘John the Baptist’ (\*).

(3) He will be a person who has self-control; he did not engage in play and amusement, or in sins, or in matters that are not respectable.

(4) He will be a righteous prophet. This is indeed a qualification higher than all the others.

The word to which we have given the meaning ‘one who has self-control’ is **حصور** (hasur); many commentators have given it the meaning one who does not have relations with women. Some are also seen to have said that by nature he did not have the ability for it. Many eminent great men have also stated firmly that this is not right, and that it was by his self-control. One of these is Qadi 'Iyad (r). The gist of what he said in his book named Shifa is this. That Yahya (a) was a person who was incapable of relations with women, as some say, is not right. Many worthy ones included among the commentators of the Quran and the scholars have refuted it. They have made clear that it is a defect, and that it is not fitting to the rank of prophets. What is meant by **حصور** is one who is protected from sin. *[p. 748]* Meanings such as one who has no bodily desire, and one who shows no longing towards women, and so on, have been given for it. From this it can be understood, surely, that being incapable of marriage is a defect, and that the qualification lies in adopting self-control even though having the ability. But having the ability for it, and bringing it into practice, and observing its proprieties and duties, and, along with that, its not causing any interruption to the duties towards Allah: that is a qualification superior to that. That is the state of our Prophet ﷺ. Even though the noble Prophet ﷺ had many wives, his acts of worship and observance did not do anything but increase. He also did not set value on worldly life.’ Ibn Kathir (r) has also confirmed this statement of Qadi 'Iyad.

> **Note:** (\*) In the Bible, what are called John the Baptist, and **يحيي المعهدان** *[DOUBT: the second Arabic word, on a 600 dpi crop, reads المعهدان; the usual Arabic for "the Baptist" is المعمدان, so it may be a printing slip for المعمدان (al-Ma'madan). Transcribed as it appears.]*, and John Baptist, all refer to the Prophet Yahya (a). About the Christians' ‘baptism’ it has already been explained in the commentary of Al-Baqarah 138.

*[Verses 40–41]*

*[p. 749]* Allah has power over every matter. He usually brings about many extraordinary events here. He will not fail to fulfil the promise He has made: all this the Prophet Zakariyya (a) knows well. Even so, it is a wonder, surely, for a child to be born to him, who had become an old man, from a wife who was barren and advanced in age. He has come to know from the words of the angels that the child who was going to be born to him would be a boy. That is why, on that occasion full of joy and causing wonder, he asked **أَنَّىٰ يَكُونُ لِي غُلَٰمٞ** (How will a boy be born to me). Perhaps he may also, surely, have had a doubt whether it would be by bringing about some change in the bodily nature of himself and his wife, or the like. But Allah's answer was that it would happen without any such changes indeed, and that I do whatever I will. His eagerness increased. He requested that it would be good if a mark were made known to him beforehand as to when the child would be born. The answer came that its mark was not speaking to anyone for a period of three days, except giving some indication to the people by means of some gesture, and that, if that happened, he could be sure that a child was going to be born. Along with that, He advised him to keep remembering Allah much, and not to omit carrying out singing of praise morning and evening. Accordingly, coming out from the prayer-chamber, he made known to the people by way of gesture that they should carry out the morning and evening singing of praise. (See Su: Maryam 11)

The gist of ‘not speaking ( **أَلَّا تُكَلِّمَ** )’ has been interpreted in more than one way: *[TN: the print writes the Arabic here in plain script, with أن and لا written apart instead of joined; verse 41 has أَلَّا تُكَلِّمَ, which is given here as the Quran text.]*

(1) Being unable to speak for three days without any bodily defect at all affecting him; and moreover there would be no hindrance to prayer, tasbih and the like. This interpretation is what is understood from verses 10, 11 of Suratu Maryam, and what most commentators have adopted.

(2) Stopping other speech of his own accord for three days, except tasbih and the like. If he did so, from then on it could be reckoned that a child was going to be born.

*[p. 750]* (3) Observing a fast for three days; the practice of observing silence during the fasting period, without speaking with people, was usual at that time. In any case, it was not, as some are seen to have said, a punishment decreed on account of his having asked for a mark for it even after the angels had announced the good news about the child. The reason for their saying so may be that it was based on some statements of Luke (1:20) in the Bible. As regards a prophet, not being satisfied with the angels' message of good news is not possible at all.

Suratu Maryam is another chapter that has described somewhat more about the events of the birth of Yahya (a) and of 'Isa (a). For further explanation, it would be good to look at that surah too and its commentary.

### Section – 5

*[Verses 42–43]*

While describing the event of Maryam (a), in between, as something connected with it, some accounts of the Prophet Zakariyya (a) *[p. 751]* having been described, the event is being continued again. Although Maryam Bivi (a) has no position of prophethood, it is clear from many statements of the Quran that angels used to go to her, and used to make known to her many messages. It is stated in this verse that the angels gave her two or three messages of congratulation. That Allah has chosen her as excellent, and has made her pure: that is, the gist is that Allah has raised her up, giving her excellent qualities such as worship, God-fearing reverence, good character, self-sacrifice and knowledge, and purity from bad qualities and from impurities. The angels also made known that Allah has blessed her, giving her a rank superior to the other women in the world. That what is meant by this is a rank higher than the other women of that time is indeed what is to be understood from the quotations seen below.

In a saying of the Prophet that Bukhari and Muslim (r) quoted, it has come thus. ‘Maryam, daughter of 'Imran, is the best of the women of her (time), and Khadijah, daughter of Khuwailid, is the best of the women of her (time)’. In some Hadiths that Tirmidhi (r) and others quoted, besides these two, Asiyah, the wife of Fir'aun, and Fatimah (r), the daughter of the Prophet, and in some others, besides these four, the wife of the Prophet, 'A'ishah (r), too, can be seen counted as the best among women. Of the two model women, believers in the truth, whose names have been mentioned in Suratut Tahrim, the first is the wife of Fir'aun, and the second is Maryam.

Besides the messages of congratulation seen above, the angels also advise Maryam (a) to do sujud and ruku'. Sujud is the prayer in full prostration with the face placed on the ground. Ruku' is the prayer done bowing down and bending. Although there may be some little difference in the observances of the act of prayer of that time and of the prayer now in practice, sujud, ruku' and qiyam (standing) are indeed the main components of prayer, then and now. That is why, concerning prayer, these three words of the said components are seen used in the Holy Quran and in Hadith. The intention of having commanded to bow down together with those who bow down has been interpreted in both ways, thus- that she must join with those who pray and pray together, and that she too must be included in the company of the righteous who carry out prayer. Allah says:

*[Verse 44]*

*[p. 752]* After Maryam (a) was born, when her mother took the child to Bait al-Muqaddas to fulfil her vow, there was a dispute in the circles of priests and attendants there as to who should take charge of that child, the daughter of 'Imran, who had been their leader. In the end they cast lots. It was to the Prophet Zakariyya (a) that the lot fell. So he took charge of her. When all these events took place, the noble Prophet ﷺ was not there, surely. That being so, as regards the Prophet ﷺ, all that is news of the unseen. The gist is that it is only because Allah made it known through wahy that the Prophet ﷺ is able to know it. Nothing is seen stated in the Bible about this drawing of lots. Nor is it a subject widely known among the People of the Scripture. The Prophet ﷺ, for his part, has no acquaintance with the scriptures either. That being so, when the Prophet ﷺ publicly proclaims such accounts in front of the People of the Scripture, it is clear that their not denying any of it is a proof of the truthfulness of the Prophet ﷺ.

The practice of making a decision by means of lots, when a dispute and division arise in some matter, is widespread even today. Many people usually use many ways for casting lots. It would be by using some one form among them that they cast lots by means of their pens -or arrow-shafts-. How it was cast, we do not know. Some are seen to have stated that what they did was that everyone cast one each, as his own, into the river Jordan ( **نهر الاردن** ), and whosever remained without flowing away in the water, he was reckoned as the one to whom the lot fell. (Allah knows)
