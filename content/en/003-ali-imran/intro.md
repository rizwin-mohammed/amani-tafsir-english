## 3. Suratu Alu Imran

**سورة آل عمران** · Revealed in Madinah – Verses 200 – Sections (Ruku') 20 *[p. 709]*

Since the family of Imran has been mentioned in this surah, it is called Suratu Alu Imran (the family of Imran). Some Hadiths containing the excellences of this surah have been quoted before, at the beginning of Suratul Baqarah. In Al-Baqarah very many matters were described making reference to the Jews and the Israelites, surely. Likewise, in that surah quite a lot of statements concerning the Christians too are contained. *[DOUBT: ആ സൂറത്തിൽ is printed ("in that surah"); from the sense it most probably means this surah (Ali 'Imran), set against Al-Baqarah in the sentence before, but it could also be read as Al-Baqarah itself.]* It is following the event of a Christian deputation of the people of the land of Najran * coming to the Prophet ﷺ in the ninth year of the Hijrah that the eighty-odd verses from the beginning of this surah came down; so indeed many Quran commentators say. For understanding some ideas contained in those verses, knowing about that event will be useful. The summary of the said event, which has been quoted in Islamic history books and in some Hadith books, is like this:-

> **Note:** (\*) A country between Hijaz and Yemen.

It was a Christian band of sixty people. In it there was a leadership band of fourteen people who were their famous leaders and scholars. It was immediately after the 'Asr prayer was over that they entered the Madinah mosque. When it was the time of their prayer (rite of supplication), the Prophet ﷺ gave them permission to perform it in the mosque itself. They performed it, turning to the east according to their custom. Three people who were the greatest leaders in the group held a conversation with the Prophet ﷺ. Talk also took place about the mutually contradictory Christian beliefs: that 'Isa (a) is God, and that he is the son of God, and that God is the combination of the three, the Father, the Mother(\*\*) the Son. *[p. 710]* That some superhuman signs, such as bringing the dead to life, had become manifest at the hand of the Prophet 'Isa (a) was because he was God; and that his being born without a father was because he was the son of God; and that, in the scriptures, in the place where it ought to be said in the singular form ‘I created, I commanded ‘ thus, saying in the plural form We created, We commanded, and so on, was because God is more than one person; and so on, they maintained(\*). The Quran came down refuting their claims and arguments, and they were stuck for an answer before the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ invited them to Islam. They claimed that they had already become Muslims before. The Prophet ﷺ replied that, since you carry on the claim that Allah has offspring, the eating of pig's flesh, the worship of the cross and the like, you have not accepted Islam. The Prophet ﷺ called them, who were not ready to return to it even though the truth had become clear to them, to ‘Mubahalah’ (to performing a mutual prayer of curse), as can be seen in the 61st verse below. What they did was to back away from that. In the end they went back, making an agreement that they would pay tribute. At their request that a trustworthy person suitable for taking decisions in some internal disputes that were among them be sent along with them, the Prophet ﷺ also sent along with them Abu 'Ubaidah (r), who is known by the title of honour ‘the trustworthy one of the community ( **امين الامة** ). This is the summary of the event.

> **Note:** (\*\*) In the Trinity doctrine of the Christians, nowadays the Mother (Maryam Bivi) has no place. Instead, it is the Holy Spirit that they have accepted. About this it will be explained later, as the occasion arises. ( **ان شاء الله** )

> **Note:** (\*) All such Christian claims – even though with some frills and fringes suited to the times – are common even today among the padres.

Since some verses of this surah have been sent down making reference to the Christians and their claims, it can be seen that many of the Christian beliefs and practices have been refuted and criticised in this surah. At the same time, they have been treated in a manner that includes also many other erroneous beliefs and practices equal to them. It is good to remember one more fact as well: although many Quran commentators say on the whole that the first 80-odd verses are ones sent down following the event of the Najran deputation mentioned above, when the subject and the style of speech in some verses among them are examined, what is understood is that they are ones sent down even before that. The 13th verse, concerning the Battle of Badr, is pointed out as an example of this. There also exist, moreover, some riwayahs showing that the revelation of some verses belonging to the said 80 did not take place following the coming of the Najran band. That being so, it is not possible to state it with certainty on the basis of the riwayahs to the effect that the 80 ayahs are about the people of Najran. Therefore, the reasonable thing is to think that most of the verses in those 80 came down concerning them *[p. 711]*, and some others on other occasions as well. ( **الله اعلم** )
